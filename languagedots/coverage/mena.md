# MENA — language coverage (sweep 2026-10-03)

- **Morocco is the one real source, and it is good.** The 2024 census workbook from HCP, the statistics office (`hcp.ma/file/242671/`, xlsx), gives a column for each local language used daily (Darija, Tachelhit, Tamazight, Tarifit, Hassania) for every commune and urban arrondissement, about 2,000 rows. It covers the Moroccan-run part of Western Sahara too. The catch is that people can tick more than one language (national shares add up to about 117%) and the item counts daily use, not mother tongue. A one-dot-per-person map needs a rule for splitting bilinguals. The IPUMS 2014 sample gives the first-listed language per person, but it is gated.
- **Everywhere else has no current census language data.** Algeria, Tunisia, Libya, Egypt, Sudan, Palestine, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, Yemen and the six Gulf states have no language question in their latest census. I checked the forms or IPUMS for Palestine 2017, Jordan 2004/2015, Israel 2008 and Egypt/Sudan in IPUMS; the rest come from reports or are guesses.
- **Old censuses that did count language:** Algeria 1966 (mother tongue, Arabic vs Berber, by wilaya and daira), Iraq 1957 (mother tongue by liwa) and 1997 (language spoken, IPUMS, without the three Kurdistan governorates), Sudan 1955/56 (language and ethnicity by province), Israel 1972/1983 (main language, IPUMS).
- **Surprise: Iraq's 2024 census, its first since 1997, deliberately left out ethnicity and home language** because of the disputed territories. The best Iraqi substitute is MICS6 2018: mother tongue of the household head (Arabic, Kurdish, Turkmen, Assyrian), representative by governorate. I checked the questionnaire. Algeria's MICS6 drops that question, and Tunisia's only codes French, Arabic or other.
- **Israel's 2008 census has no language question.** I checked the form. The last census with one is 1983. Open data on Jewish/Arab population group and on former-USSR immigrants by locality would make a passable Hebrew/Arabic/Russian stand-in.
- **Gulf states:** they publish nationality, not language, and only split nationals from non-nationals below the national level. Saudi 2022 gives detailed citizenship (Bangladesh, India, Pakistan...) for the whole country only. GLMM (gulfmigration.grc.net) collects these tables. Turning nationality into language is rough: India and Pakistan are themselves multilingual.
- **Survey fallbacks:** Arab Barometer waves III-IV ask "first language" (open answer) and record governorate, but say they are not built to be representative below national level. Afrobarometer home language covers Algeria, Morocco, Tunisia, Egypt and Sudan. The Berber question (Algeria, Libya, Tunisia) and the Kurdish question (Iraq, Syria) are politically sensitive, which is why no census asks them.
- **Biggest gaps:** Sudan (very diverse, nothing since 1956), Algeria's Berber geography (nothing since 1966), Syria's Kurds, and the Modern South Arabian languages (Yemen, Oman).
