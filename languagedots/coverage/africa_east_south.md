# East and southern Africa: language coverage

24 countries, swept 2026-10-03/04. Six have an open census language table below province level, three more have only ethnicity, and the rest have no census figures (or are effectively one language anyway).

**Best sources**
- **Zambia 2022** is the find of the region. The "widely spoken language of communication" is tabulated for 1,853 wards in 9 language groups, and about 75 languages by province (`Languages-Descriptive-Tables.pdf` on zamstats.gov.zm). The same layout exists for ethnicity.
- **South Africa 2022**: home language by electoral ward (about 4,468) on Stats SA SuperWEB2, which needs a free account. 2011 goes down to small areas (about 84,900).
- **Ethiopia 2007**: mother tongue by woreda (738), CC BY, boundaries in the same USCB file on HDX. The data is 19 years old and there is no newer census.
- **Botswana 2022**: home language and a new "early childhood language" question, by census district (28), from PDF appendices.
- **Namibia 2011**: open PUMS microdata with household main language and constituency (107). The 2023 PUMS dropped the language variable.
- **Mozambique 2017**: both mother tongue and home language, published as xlsx by province. The 10% public microdata may reach district (161). The INE site timed out, so this is still unconfirmed.

**Ethnicity only**
- Malawi 2018: tribe by district, 13 groups. It maps nearly 1:1 to languages.
- Kenya 2019: national only. 1979 and 1989 have district tables.
- Uganda 2014 and 2024: national only.

**Gaps**
- No census language data: Tanzania, Zimbabwe, Somalia, South Sudan and Eritrea (Eritrea has never held a census).
- Questionnaires not checked: Djibouti, Madagascar, Comoros, Lesotho and Eswatini.
- Seychelles, Mauritius and Angola: the home-language question is confirmed, but the geography of the published tables is not.
- Afrobarometer (free download, home language by region) is the realistic fallback for Tanzania and Zimbabwe.

**Surprises**
- Zambia publishes language down to ward level as an ordinary PDF.
- Namibia's question is asked once per household, not per person.
- Angola's 2014 shares look like more than one language per person was allowed.
- Botswana 2022 added a mother-tongue-like question.
- Kenya's ethnicity has been published only nationally since 1989.
