# Papua New Guinea (pg): record

Drawn 2026-10-05 (session edd42a8c-pg). **A language-area model, not a count.** No PNG census
publishes first languages by area. Built on the estimate route Egypt used (`sources/eg.md`;
ask 019 on that route is still open, so every row is `modelled`). 10,185,363 people (2024
census Final Figures, religiondots' `pg_lookup.csv`), 22 provinces, 779 languages. 9,808 dots at
1:1000; 3.7% of people sit in languages under one dot nationally and draw none.

```
python sources/pg_build.py        # pg.csv, pg_languages.csv, taxonomy/tree.d/pg.txt
python taxonomy/build.py
python tools/check_country.py pg
python scatter.py --country pg
```

Files: `sources/pg_build.py`, `taxonomy/pg2024.py` (identity), `taxonomy/tree.d/pg.txt`
(generated by the build script), `countries/pg.py`, `data/normalized/pg.csv`,
`data/normalized/pg_languages.csv`, `data/raw/pg/joshuaproject_pgic.csv`.

## 1. What exists

- **Censuses.** 2011 and 2024 ask literacy in Tok Pisin, English, Hiri Motu and "tok ples"
  only. 1966 and 1971 asked competence in the three lingua francas; 1971 also asked "language
  usually spoken at home" but tabulated only the lingua francas. The 1980 long form (urban areas
  and a village sample) asked the language spoken most at home, from a short list (English,
  Motu, Pidgin, Enga, Tolai, Melpa, Kuman, Huli, Mendi, Kate, Yabem, other); Laycock, "Tok
  Pisin and the census" (Pacific Linguistics C-70, 1985, CC BY-SA) says unpublished. Paliwala,
  "Language in Papua New Guinea: the value of census data" covers 1980-2000 (academia.edu, 403).
- **Speaker figures.** Joshua Project's people-group file (`joshuaproject.net/resources/datasets/1`,
  keyless, the same file religiondots used): 883 PNG groups, 10.79M people, with an ISO 639-3 code
  each. Its populations follow Ethnologue. Summed by code, they are the speaker estimates.
- **Locations and classification.** Glottolog 5 (`data/raw/glottolog`, CC BY): one point per
  language, the classification path, and the endangerment status.

## 2. How the counts are made (`sources/pg_build.py`)

- Languages: every Glottolog language listed in PG or carrying an ISO code Joshua Project has in
  PNG (900). Left out: pidgins, sign languages, Bookkeeping/unclassifiable and Indo-European
  (Tok Pisin, which Glottolog files as an English creole, and English): 29; extinct, nearly
  extinct or moribund by Glottolog's status: 84; points outside PNG with no PNG estimate: 8
  (Marind, Yetfa, Mandobo Bawah, South Muyu in Indonesia; Touo and Bilua in Solomon Islands;
  Mandarin, Tagalog). 16 points outside every province polygon (coast, small islands, a few
  just over the Indonesian border with a PNG estimate) snapped to the nearest province, all
  within 42 km.
- Each province's 2024 people are shared among the languages whose point is in it, in proportion
  to their estimate. 765 languages have one; 14 take the median of their province's others.
  Drawn against estimate across languages, log r = 0.996.
- **NCD (Port Moresby)** holds no point (Motu's and Koitabu's are in Central). Its 756,754
  people are shared across all 779 languages by estimate: a migrant city with no published
  origin mix. Motu villages inside NCD (Hanuabada) are not separated.
- **Tok Pisin not drawn.** No cited share of town people speaking it first was found (searched:
  Laycock 1985, APiCS's Tok Pisin chapter, Wikipedia's national L1 "130,000 (2004-2016)",
  which has no place attached). Joshua Project's 2.0M "Detribalized, PNG" and "Neo-Melanesian"
  Tok Pisin groups are dropped with it, so those people sit on local languages instead.
- Placement (`countries/pg.py`): inside a province, a language's dots go to the Kontur hexes
  nearer its point than to any other drawn language's point in that province, by population;
  4 languages whose cell is empty fall back to population x exp(-d/20 km) around their point.
  NCD on plain population. Counts never move.

**Checks.** Every province sums to its 2024 figure (asserted). Families as drawn: Trans-New
Guinea 57.3%, Oceanic 24.3%, Sepik 4.3%, Torricelli 2.8%, isolates 2.3%, South Bougainville
1.7%, Ramu-Lower Sepik 1.6%, Eleman 1.6%. Largest: Enga 478k (estimate 440k), Huli 345k (293k),
Kuanua 266k (170k), Melpa 203k (147k). A preview render (Highlands) shows each language in its
own area along the valleys.

## 3. Tree (`taxonomy/tree.d/pg.txt`, generated)

- `papuan.<family>[.<subgroup>].<language>`, `austronesian.oceanic.<subgroup>.<language>`,
  `isolate.<language>`.
- **Trans-New Guinea** as Pawley & Hammarström (2018) accept it (Wikipedia's TNG page, read
  2026-10-05): Glottolog's Nuclear TNG plus Angan, Dagan, Mailuan, Bosavi, Koiarian, East
  Strickland, Kiwaian, Suki-Gogodala, Turama-Kikori, Yareban, Manubaran, Kutubuan and Kunimaipan.
  Anim, Eleman, Kamula-Elevala, Teberan stay apart (P&H exclude Anim; the others are not in
  their list as quoted). **Sepik** includes Ndu; **Ramu-Lower Sepik** joins Glottolog's two.
  Glottolog's isolates (Wiru, Duna, Fuyug, Tauade, Pawaia, Kuot, Yele...) go on `isolate`, as
  the brief says, though several are TNG in Ross 2005.
- A family with more than 40 drawn languages (the colour grid has 45 slots) is cut at the
  Glottolog level that brings each group to 40 or fewer: TNG into 24 groups (Chimbu-Wahgi,
  Enga-Kewa-Huli, Kainantu-Goroka, Angan...), Oceanic into 13 (Glottolog's "linkage" dropped
  from labels), Sepik into 9, Torricelli into 10. Kiwaian lands on au.txt's `papuan.kiwai`
  (reused node), not under TNG; and Glottolog names P&H's Kutubu family "East Kutubu", so the
  fold list's "Kutubuan" matched nothing and it stays `papuan.east_kutubu` (Foi, Fiwaga). Both
  small; fix in the script's TNG_FOLD/REUSE if wanted.
- Colours: TNG 0.72 0.14 135, Sepik 0.80 0.15 95, Torricelli 0.68 0.14 70, Ramu-Lower Sepik
  0.84 0.12 160, all near `papuan`'s 120; Oceanic is uk.txt's 225 blue; the rest generated.

## 4. Calls someone might reverse

- The estimate route itself (ask 019 is open on it).
- Tok Pisin left off. A cited urban share would add it in NCD, Lae, Madang.
- A language spanning provinces is drawn wholly in its point's province (Kewa, Enga, Kuman...);
  provinces with few points (Enga 2, Western Highlands 4, Hela 5) give each a large share.
- NCD as the national mix.
- Moribund languages dropped; their people go to the province's other languages.
