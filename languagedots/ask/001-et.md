# Nilo-Saharan as one root, Omotic inside Afroasiatic

status: closed
country: et
summary: Ethiopia drawn with a new root nilosaharan (Nilotic, Surmic, Koman, Berta, Gumuz, Kunama) and Omotic under Afroasiatic, the conventional grouping. Glottolog splits both into separate families. Keep, or split?

## The decision I already took

Ethiopia needed two families the tree did not have. In `taxonomy/tree.d/et.txt`:

- **`nilosaharan` is a new root** (blue, hue 255), holding Nilotic (Nuer, Anuak, Nyangatom),
  Surmic (Me'en, Suri, Mursi and others), Koman (Komo, Gwama, Opo), Berta, Gumuz and Kunama.
  876,000 people in Ethiopia, mostly in Gambela and Benishangul-Gumuz.
- **Omotic sits inside Afroasiatic** (`afroasiatic.omotic`, pinks), next to Ethiopian Semitic
  and Cushitic. 5.9 million people (Wolaytta, Gamo, Kafa, Dawro, Aari and others).

Both follow the usual reference books for the region (Bender, Ethnologue). Glottolog accepts
neither: it gives Nilotic, Surmic, Koman, Gumuz, Berta and Kunama a family each, and splits
Omotic into four families outside Afroasiatic (Ta-Ne-Omotic, South Omotic, Dizoid, Blue Nile
Mao). Every language's branch below those levels does match Glottolog.

## What reversing it would cost

Small for now. Only Ethiopia uses these nodes. Splitting them means renaming ids in
`tree.d/et.txt` and `taxonomy/et2007.py` (about 40 lines), choosing colours for six new roots
instead of one, and re-running the build and the scatter (a few minutes). It gets more expensive
once Sudan, South Sudan, Kenya, Uganda or Chad are built on the same nodes.

## What I need from Anita

Keep one Nilo-Saharan root and Omotic inside Afroasiatic, or follow Glottolog? I'd keep them:
one root reads better in the legend, and it is how most readers have seen these languages
grouped.
