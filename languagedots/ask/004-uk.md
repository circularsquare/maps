# Wales: the census's one 'English or Welsh' box

status: closed
country: uk
summary: Wales's 2.9M 'English or Welsh' answers are drawn as Indo-European, language not named. Would you allow a Welsh-speaking-ability proxy instead? Also one new root, Kartvelian (Georgian, 11 people).

## The decision I already took

The 2021 form in Wales asked "What is your main language?" with one tick box reading "English
or Welsh" and a write-in for anything else. ONS prints the answer as "English (English or
Welsh in Wales)", 2,917,644 people, and no table anywhere splits it. Under spec §3.2 an answer
naming two languages sits on the narrowest node holding both, which is `indoeuropean` (English
is Germanic, Welsh Celtic), so all of Wales is drawn in Indo-European's washed-out "language not
named" colour, beige beside England's near-white. England's 7,363 people who wrote in Welsh are
drawn as Welsh. `note_public` says why Wales looks different. The call lives in one place,
`taxonomy/uk2021.py` (`WALES_ENGLISH_OR_WELSH`).

## What reversing it would cost

Two ways out, both cheap to build (one function in `sources/uk_census.py`):

- **Draw it all as English.** One line. Misstates the Welsh-speaking north-west: Gwynedd is
  about 64% Welsh speakers and the map would show it as English as solidly as Kent.
- **Split it with a proxy, tier `modelled`.** Census 2021 TS032 gives "can speak Welsh" for
  every Output Area in Wales (538,300 people, 17.8%). Speaking Welsh is not the same as Welsh
  being the main language; the Welsh Language Use Survey 2019-20 (Welsh Government) publishes
  the share of Welsh speakers who speak it daily or who are fluent, by region, which could
  weight it. That fits your proxy rule (the non-matching part is a published number), but the
  survey measures fluency and daily use, not "main language", so the weighting is itself an
  assumption. I have not built it.
