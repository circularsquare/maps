# Afghanistan: leave undrawn, or allow a village-majority proxy?

status: closed
country: af
summary: No open source asks Afghans their first language. The one survey that did (Asia Foundation) was withdrawn in 2021 for the respondents' safety. Undrawn unless you allow a c.2005 village-majority-language proxy or a data request.

## The decision I already took

Afghanistan is left undrawn (queue status stays `ruling`). Details in sources/af.md.

- The best source, the Asia Foundation's Survey of the Afghan People (2004-2021, all 34
  provinces, about 15,000 respondents a year, asks the language spoken at home), had its microdata
  at the Australian Data Archive (doi:10.26193/VDDO0X). By October 2021 the archive's page reads
  "Due to the changing security situation on the ground and new risks, this dataset is currently
  unavailable for use." The depositor pulled it to protect the people in it. I did not look for a
  copy elsewhere, and would not draw from one: that withdrawal is the clearest signal there is
  about §7 here.
- DHS 2015 is reported to have asked respondents' native language, but I found no published
  table of it by province; the microdata needs DHS registration, which is off.
- REACH's Whole of Afghanistan Assessments (2019-2026) are on HDX only "by request" (HDX
  Connect, an account and a stated purpose). I did not confirm they ask a first language.
- NSIA's ALCS/NRVA microdata is request-only; I found no published language table. The
  UNFPA/CSO provincial surveys (SDES, 2011-2016) ask literacy, not language.
- CLEAR Global has no Afghanistan dataset on HDX; its Afghanistan page is national figures from a
  2020 survey of 65 radio broadcasters.

What does exist openly: the Ministry of Rural Rehabilitation and Development's provincial profiles
(undated, probably about 2006-07; reprinted in the US Army CALL handbook 11-16), which give per province the share of
people living in villages whose majority speaks each language ("Dari is spoken by 77 percent of
the population and 80 percent of the villages"). That counts every villager as the village's
majority language, so it changes the counts and is a proxy: yours to allow. I'd advise against
it: prose figures, uneven across provinces ("about half"), around 20 years old, and it erases
minorities inside mixed villages.

## What reversing it would cost

Nothing to undo. Drawing from the profiles is about a day: transcribe 34 provinces' prose,
reuse religiondots' province hex layer as is. All rows would be `modelled` and Hazaragi would
not appear (the profiles say Dari).

## What I need from Anita

Nothing, if Afghanistan stays off the map (my recommendation). Otherwise one of:

- a yes to the province-level village-majority proxy, knowing its flaws above;
- or a request you make yourself: HDX Connect for REACH WoAA 2024 or 2025 (first check the
  questionnaire asks household language), or DHS 2015 microdata. Province is the finest grain I
  would draw from either, given the Asia Foundation's reason for withdrawing its own data.

## Ruling (2026-10-05)

Look for the Asia Foundation microdata elsewhere first; the village-majority proxy is very inaccurate but a vague picture of what is where is still useful (for asia1m), so it is the fallback.
