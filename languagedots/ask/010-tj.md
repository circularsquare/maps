# Tajikistan: Pamiri languages drawn as Tajik, as the census counts them

status: closed
country: tj
summary: The census files Shughni, Rushani, Wakhi and other Pamiri languages under Tajik, so Gorno-Badakhshan is drawn Tajik and the note says so. Drawing them from an outside estimate would change counts, and is political since 2022.

## The decision I already took

Tajikistan is drawn from the 2010 census (the 2020 language volume is unpublished), whose
nationality list has no Pamiri entry and whose native-language table counts the Pamiri peoples'
languages as Tajik. So Gorno-Badakhshan comes out 94% Tajik and 5% Kyrgyz (Murghob). Nothing
Pamiri is drawn. `gap` names the Pamiri languages, and `note_public` says plainly that the census
counts them as Tajik and that they are spoken at home across much of the region. Some of the
13,581 Tajiks who named "other languages" may have named a Pamiri language, but the table does not
say which language, so they sit on `other`.

## What reversing it would cost

No measured source places Pamiri speakers. Drawing them would mean a proxy that changes the counts:
for example, the five Pamiri districts' population (Khorog, Shughnon, Roshtqal'a, Ishkoshim,
Rushon: 154,623 in the 2020 census's Volume I table 1, which religiondots holds) drawn on a new
`indoeuropean.iranian.pamiri` group, or split by district into Shughni, Rushani, Wakhi and the rest
from published speaker estimates. About an hour's work: a new fragment, a rule in
`sources/tj_census.py`, every such row `modelled`, a rewritten note.

There is also the people drawn. Since the Khorog protests of May 2022 the state has pressed hard on
Gorno-Badakhshan; the Pamiris' status as a people apart from Tajiks is itself contested, and the
census's choice to count them as Tajiks is part of that. Religiondots kept the region's Ismailis
undrawn for the same want of a measure (its ask 051, recommended leaving it).
