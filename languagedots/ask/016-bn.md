# Brunei: ask DEPS for the 2021 home-language table, or leave it undrawn?

status: closed
country: bn
summary: BPP 2021 asked home language (E27, five answers) but no publication tabulates it. Parked undrawn. An email to DEPS could get it by district or mukim; otherwise Brunei stays off the map.

## The decision I already took

Brunei is parked undrawn. The 2021 census form asked "language mainly spoken at home, one answer"
(item E27: Malay, English, Chinese, Arabic, Others please specify), and the 2011 form asked the
same (D18). Neither census's answers are published anywhere I could find: not in the 2021 report
(94 pages), its Annex A-C workbook, the 2021 Education Characteristics report, the two 2021
"Insights" reports, the 2016 mid-term survey annexes A-E, the 2011 reports, or the 2021 statistical
yearbook. What DEPS does publish is literacy by language (which languages people can read and
write, national only), which is a skill question, not a first language, so not drawn. UNSD's
language table is unreachable (data.un.org now serves another app). Details in sources/bn.md.

I did not draw Brunei from the race table (Malay / Chinese / Others by district) as a proxy,
because a proxy that sets the counts is yours to allow.

## What reversing it would cost

Nothing to undo. With an E27 table by district (or mukim), an agent builds Brunei in about an
hour: religiondots' district placement layer is reused as is. The map would be shallow: Malay is
one node (Brunei Malay, Kedayan, Dusun, Iban and Murut speakers all answer Malay or Others), so
Brunei would read mostly Malay with Chinese and English along the coast.

## What I need from Anita

Only if you want Brunei on the map, one of:

- an email to DEPS (Department of Economic Planning and Statistics, deps.gov.bn, "Contact Us")
  asking for BPP 2021 "language mainly spoken at home" (E27) by district, or by mukim if they will,
  persons, all residents. Drop the file in `languagedots/data/raw/bn/`.
- or a yes to drawing it from race by district (Malay as Malay, Chinese as Chinese, Others as
  other), which I'd advise against: race says nothing about the English-speaking households or
  which Chinese language.

Doing neither is fine; Brunei stays off the map, which at 0.46 million people is a small hole.
