# Taiwan: split the 169k indigenous-language speakers by each township's registered indigenous peoples?

status: closed
country: tw
summary: Census names only 'indigenous languages' (169k). Each township's registered indigenous population by people (Amis, Paiwan, Atayal...) could share it out as a proxy, tier derived. Drawn unnamed for now.

## The decision I already took

Taiwan is drawn from the 2020 census, table 7 (language learned first), by 368 townships. The
census prints indigenous languages as one answer, "原住民族語", 169k people. They are drawn on a
new group node `austronesian.taiwan` with Glottolog's 15 Formosan languages as (empty) children,
so they show washed out as "language not named". Nothing is guessed into a named language.

## What reversing it would cost

Small: the Council of Indigenous Peoples publishes registered indigenous population by people
(Amis, Paiwan, Atayal, Bunun, Truku ...) by township. Share each township's indigenous-language
figure across the peoples registered there, in proportion, tier `derived`, said in `how` and
`note_public`. It reads ethnicity as language, which overstates the languages of urban
indigenous people who mostly speak Mandarin, but the census figure already counts only people
who learned an indigenous language first, so the proxy only decides which one. Roughly an hour.
