# Afrobarometer countries: draw English and Swahili 'home language' answers as given, or move them to the respondent's ethnic language?

status: closed
country: ke
summary: Nigeria and Kenya move them (English 21M->122k in ng; Swahili 28%->2.7% in ke), Ghana keeps English (555k). Needs one rule for every survey-built country.

## The decision I already took

Countries built from Afrobarometer (ng, gh, ke so far, more to come) meet respondents who name
a lingua franca as their home language. The agents differed:

- **Nigeria:** 514 "English at home" answers moved to the language of the respondent's own ethnic
  group (English drawn 122k, not ~21M; Cross River would be 47% English). Reason: English was not
  on the answer list in R4/R6 and nearly every English answer came from an English-language
  interview, so it looks like an artefact.
- **Kenya:** Swahili and English county shares taken from R4/R6 ("home language"); R7-R9 asked
  "language spoken in home", and Swahili jumped from 3.6% to 28% (half of Nairobi, 74% of
  Mombasa), not an interview-language effect. Those R7-R9 answers moved to the ethnic language:
  Swahili drawn 2.7% nationally, Nairobi 4%, Mombasa 7.6%.
- **Ghana:** English kept as answered, 555k, mostly Accra.

- **Tanzania** (added by the supervisor): drawn with Swahili as given from R7-R9 (on instruction),
  65.8% nationally, 97% Dar es Salaam. Swahili by round: R4 9.0%, R5 3.4%, R6 5.5%, R7 73.0%, R8
  61.4%, R9 70.4%. R4/R6 give 6.3%, close to the Languages of Tanzania project's 2009 count of
  first-language Swahili speakers (~7%). So R7-R9's wording seems to capture the language *used* at
  home, not the first language. Switch: `SW_ROUNDS` in `sources/tz_afro.py`.

- **Malawi** (added by the supervisor): the rounds disagree the other way here. R4 and R6 record
  the tribe's own language far more often (Chichewa 47-51% nationally, against 66-74% in R5 and
  R7-R9). R5 and R7-R9 match the 1998 census's household-language figures (Chichewa ~70%), so the
  agent dropped R4 and R6. The wording change is not one clean "first language vs use" split in
  every country, so each country needs its own check against a census figure where one exists.

## What reversing it would cost

One line per country (`SE_ROUNDS` in `sources/ke_afro.py`; the English switch in
`sources/ng_afro.py`), a re-scatter, a build tail. Future agents follow whatever rule you set.

## What I need from Anita

One rule for every survey-built country. It comes down to what the map shows: first language
(take R4-R6 wording; Swahili ~6% in Tanzania, ~3% in Kenya, matching LOT) or language used at
home (take R7-R9; Swahili ~66% in Tanzania, ~28% in Kenya). The supervisor first suggested the
second; Tanzania's LOT check suggests the first matches what the rest of the map draws (mother
tongue, first language). Nigeria's English is an interview artefact either way.
