# Australia (taxonomy/au2021.py). Census 2021, language used at home, ASCL 2016. Most migrant
# languages were already added by us.txt, uk.txt and ca.txt; this file adds the rest and the
# Australian Indigenous languages.
#
# AUSTRALIAN INDIGENOUS LANGUAGES. One root, `australian`, as the ABS draws the line ("Australian
# Indigenous Languages", ASCL broad group 8): Pama-Nyungan plus the twenty-odd small families of
# the north (Gunwinyguan, Maningrida, Iwaidjan, Mirndi, the Daly families, the Kimberley families,
# Tangkic, Garrwan...) plus the Torres Strait's languages and the Indigenous creoles. Not a
# family: an areal root, as `americas_other` is, because (1) readers know these as "Aboriginal
# languages", (2) a root per small family would put two dozen roots in the legend for a few
# thousand people, and (3) the census's unnamed remainders ("Australian Indigenous Languages,
# nfd", "Torres Strait Island Languages, nfd") mix Pama-Nyungan, non-Pama-Nyungan, Papuan
# (Meriam Mir) and creole speakers, and must sit on a node containing all of them (spec §3.2).
# Under it, families and the main Pama-Nyungan subgroups follow Glottolog
# (data/raw/glottolog): Yuulngu (Yolngu Matha), Arandic, Wati (Western Desert), Ngumpin-Yapa,
# South-West and Southeastern Pama-Nyungan. Pama-Nyungan languages outside those six sit
# directly under Pama-Nyungan. The Daly languages (Southern, Western and Northern Daly, three
# families in Glottolog) are one group here, "Daly", for the same readability reason.
# Kriol, Yumplatok (Torres Strait Creole), Gurindji Kriol and Aboriginal English sit in
# `australian.contact`, not under `creole`: the ABS counts them as Indigenous languages and its
# unnamed Indigenous remainders can hold their speakers. Tiwi, an isolate, stays under the
# Australian root rather than `isolate` for the same reason. Meriam Mir is Eastern Trans-Fly
# (Papuan) in Glottolog; it is under the Australian root as a Torres Strait language.
# Languages with no speakers in 2021 (zero in Table 5) get no node.
#
# Colours: Pama-Nyungan's desert subgroups take the warm side (Western Desert reds and oranges,
# Arandic yellows, Ngumpin-Yapa crimsons and pinks) because they meet around Alice Springs and
# the Tanami; Yolngu greens; the Arnhem Land families violets, blues and magenta (Gunwinyguan
# violet, Maningrida blue, Daly magenta, Tiwi teal), since Kunwinjku, Burarra and the Yolngu
# languages border each other. Kriol, spoken across the whole Top End and Kimberley, is a pale
# yellow-green that differs from all of them.
