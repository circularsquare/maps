# Namibia (taxonomy/na2011.py). Census 2011, main language spoken in the household. The census
# offers thirteen answers, six of them named as groups ("Oshiwambo languages"). Parents and
# languages other fragments already define are repeated identically (label and colour), so a
# change there shows up as a clash, not a dangle.
#
# KHOISAN, a new root. Glottolog has no Khoisan family: Khoekhoe is in Khoe-Kwadi (khoe1240),
# the Ju and !Xun languages in Kx'a (kxaa1236), Taa and !Xoon in Tuu (tuuu1241). Readers know
# them as one family, and the brief's rule (families as most readers know them, Anita
# 2026-10-04, as for Nilo-Saharan) gives them one root. Under it, Khoe (khoe1241) holds
# Khoekhoegowab, the Nama/Damara language (Glottolog nama1264, North Khoekhoe); the Kalahari
# Khoe languages (Naro, Khwe) belong under Khoe too when a census names them. The census's "San
# languages" spans Khoe, Kx'a and Tuu, so it sits on the root (na2011.py).
#
# Oshiwambo and Otjiherero hang from Bantu directly, as Venda does: Glottolog's Ndonga (R.20)
# and Herero (R.30) groups are under Kunene / Southern Njila, levels no other fragment uses.
#
# Colours, for the neighbours on the Namibian ground, using the whole wheel as za.txt does:
#   Oshiwambo a full orange across the north; Otjiherero a magenta (Kunene, Omaheke,
#   Otjozondjupa, beside Oshiwambo and Khoekhoegowab); Khoekhoegowab a light yellow (Kunene,
#   Erongo, the south), apart from Oshiwambo's orange by lightness and from Setswana's olive in
#   Aminuis; Afrikaans stays us.txt's blue. The Khoisan root, where the San languages are drawn
#   washed out, is a green, so the washed San in Tsumkwe reads apart from the washed Bantu
#   (Kavango and Caprivi languages) next door.
