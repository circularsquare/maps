# Thailand 2010 census, language usually spoken in the household (taxonomy/th2010.py). Nodes other
# fragments already define are repeated identically (label, and colour where one gives it), so
# Thailand does not depend on them being in the build.
#
# Three new leaves and one new areal root:
#   * Lao Khrang (Glottolog laok1241, a dialect of Lao, laoo1244): the census's "ลาวครั่ง/ลาวขี้ครั่ง".
#     Beside `lao`, not under it: a child would make Lao a group node and wash out every Lao dot
#     drawn elsewhere (hk.txt's Teochew, for the same reason).
#   * "Tai Khün, Thai Loei or Lao Loei": the census's one answer "ไทยขึน/ไทยเลย/ลาวเลย", which
#     puts Khün (khun1259) of the north and the Loei speech of Loei province in one box. Both
#     are Tai, so it is a Kra-Dai leaf; nothing published separates the two.
#   * Pattani Malay (patt1254, a dialect of Malay): the census's "มลายูถิ่น/นายู/ยาวี", local
#     Malay, Nayu, Yawi. Beside `malay`, for the same reason as Lao Khrang.
#   * `seasia_other`: the census's two unnamed boxes, "ภาษาถิ่น" (local language) and
#     "ภาษาพื้นเมืองและชาวเขาอื่นๆ" (other indigenous and hill-tribe languages). What they hold
#     crosses Austroasiatic (Northern Khmer, Kuy, Lawa, Khmu), Sino-Tibetan (Lahu, Akha, Lisu),
#     Hmong-Mien (Mien) and Kra-Dai (Phu Thai, Tai Lue), so no family node is narrow enough; an
#     areal root as `indonesia_other` and `americas_other` are (AGENT_BRIEF §3).
#
#
# The regional Thai varieties (2026-10-06, session 5d7dac7e-th; countries/th.py splits the
# census's Thai by World Values Survey shares, sources/th_wvs.py). `kradai.thai` stays "Thai" and
# now holds Central Thai (standard Thai, Thai Korat included). Three leaves beside it, as
# Glottolog has them (each a language under Southwestern Tai, taik1256):
#   * Isan (Glottolog "Northeastern Thai", nort2741, a sibling of Lao, laoo1244, in its Lao-Phutai
#     group). Labelled "Isan" because that is the name readers and Thai speakers use; Glottolog's
#     and Ethnologue's "Northeastern Thai" goes in brackets. Its own leaf, not under Lao: it is the
#     speech of Thai citizens written in Thai script, and a child would wash out Laos's Lao.
#   * Northern Thai (nort2740, Kham Mueang): the node Laos already defines for its Nhuan
#     (la.txt), repeated with the same label.
#   * Southern Thai (sout2746, Pak Tai).
#
# Colours. Thai is nearly everyone, so each minority is set off from Thai's light green: Pattani
# Malay a mid teal-blue (Malay's own 150 green sat on Thai's 125 in the deep south), Lao Khrang a
# pale yellow-green (beside Hmong's teal in Phetchabun), and `seasia_other` a low-chroma
# grey-lilac so it reads as a remainder (552,000 of it in Surin). The regional varieties:
#   * Isan a warm orange, well away from Thai's light green to the west and Lao's olive across
#     the Mekong, and from Khmer's pink to the south;
#   * the Khün/Loei box moved from amber to a pale cream, because amber sat on Isan's orange in
#     Loei;
#   * Northern Thai a darker green-teal, apart from Thai to the south and Lao to the east (this
#     also colours Laos's Nhuan, which had a generated one);
#   * Southern Thai a darker olive-gold, apart from Thai to the north and from Pattani Malay's
#     blue and Malay's green to the south.
