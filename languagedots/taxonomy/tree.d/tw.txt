# TAIWAN'S INDIGENOUS LANGUAGES. An areal group, as `australian` and `americas_other` are: the
# census files every one of them under 原住民族語 and names none. They are not one branch: the
# Formosan languages are several primary branches of Austronesian (Glottolog), and Yami (Tao) on
# Orchid Island is Malayo-Polynesian (Batanic). The group keeps the census's indigenous-language
# speakers whose people the household register does not name (尚未申報), drawn as "language not
# named". The members are drawn since ask 012 (Anita, 2026-10-05): each township's census figure
# shared across the peoples registered there (countries/tw.py, sources/tw_cip.py).
# Members: the sixteen languages of Taiwan's sixteen recognised peoples. Glottolog's fifteen
# (aust1307) plus Truku, which Glottolog files as a dialect of Seediq (taro1264) but Taiwan
# recognises as its own people and language; the register counts Truku (32,717) and Seediq
# (10,602) apart, so they are two leaves. Names as most readers know them (Wikipedia's).
# Colours hand-picked across green, teal, blue and lime (Austronesian's side of the wheel, clear
# of the three Sinitic reds that fill the plain), for contrast where peoples meet: Amis on the
# east coast against Truku, Kavalan and Sakizaya in Hualien and Puyuma in Taitung; Atayal,
# Saisiyat, Seediq and Truku across the north and centre; Bunun in the central mountains against
# all of them, Tsou, Thao, Kanakanavu and Saaroa; Paiwan, Rukai and Puyuma in the south.
