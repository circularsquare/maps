# Canada (taxonomy/ca2021.py). Census 2021 mother tongue: Canada's Indigenous languages in
# StatCan's own classification, and the migrant languages the other fragments mostly already had.
# Families and branches checked against Glottolog (data/raw/glottolog/languages.csv):
#   * Michif is Algic there ("Heritage Michif", algi1248), not a mixed language, so it sits
#     under algic.
#   * Kutenai is an isolate (Is_Isolate true). Haida is a two-language family there (haid1248);
#     it goes under br.txt's `isolate` root, the conventional reading, because Canada's census
#     has one Haida row.
#   * Tlingit is Athabaskan-Eyak-Tlingit (atha1245), so it is under nadene, beside Northern
#     Athabaskan, as StatCan also files it.
#   * Iroquoian, Salishan, Wakashan and Tsimshianic are Glottolog families with no node yet: new
#     roots, as us.txt made Algic and Siouan (ask 003 asks whether families-as-roots is right for
#     North America; these four follow whatever it decides).
#   * Sango is Atlantic-Congo (sang1328), not a creole, though StatCan lists it with the creoles.
#   * Wojenaka (woje1238) and Bamanankan are Mande; Mwani (mwan1247) is Bantu.
#   * Morisyen sits under French-based creoles, as br.txt and us.txt file creoles.
# Mina is placed where StatCan places it, under Chadic (Glottolog's Mina (Cameroon), mina1276).
# Many Canadians who answer "Mina" may speak Gen (Gbe, Togo); the census's classification is
# followed because nothing published says which.
#
# Colours (L C h): chosen for the neighbours on Canada's ground. English is near-white and French
# violet everywhere; in the north Cree (yellow-green), Ojibwe and Oji-Cree (greens) meet across
# northern Ontario and Manitoba, Inuktitut (pale blue) and Dene (green-teal) meet at the
# treeline, and in British Columbia the Salishan (gold), Wakashan (pink) and Tsimshianic (salmon)
# languages sit beside Athabaskan Dakelh and Tsilhqot'in.
