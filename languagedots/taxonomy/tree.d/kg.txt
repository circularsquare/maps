# Kyrgyzstan, 2022 census native language (taxonomy/kg2022.py). Checked against Glottolog
# (data/raw/glottolog/values.csv classification):
#   Dungan (dung1253) is Sinitic; Glottolog files it under Mandarin (mand1471 > zhon1236,
#   Zhongyuan), but `sinitic.mandarin` is a leaf other countries draw on, and a child would turn it
#   into a group drawn as "language not named". Dungan is written in Cyrillic, standardised apart
#   and asked as a language of its own by the census (it has its own column in Naryn's volume),
#   so it is a sibling of Mandarin, as the usual handbooks list it.
#   Aghul (aghu1253) is Lezgic (lezg1248), beside Lezgian.
#   "Languages of India and Pakistan": the census's own language of the "peoples of India and
#   Pakistan", which names no language; a named leaf under `other`, as Azerbaijan's "Jewish".

#
# Colours. Generated, Kyrgyz, Uzbek, Kazakh and Uyghur all came out the same mid pink round
# Turkic's hue, and Kyrgyz and Uzbek are THE pair that meets on the ground (Osh, Jalal-Abad,
# Batken, the Fergana valley edge). Hand-picked here: Kyrgyz a full red (the country's 80%),
# Uzbek a lighter magenta-pink, Kazakh a pale rose (it meets Kyrgyz in Talas and Chui), Uyghur a
# light orange (Chui, Bishkek, and Osh beside Uzbek), all still in Turkic's warm region of the
# wheel. Elsewhere these four are a few thousand migrants (ca, cz, pl,
# ua), so nothing there depends on the old colours. Russian (green), Dungan (violet) and Tajik
# (sand) already stand apart from all three.
