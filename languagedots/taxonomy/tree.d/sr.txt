# Suriname, Census 7 (2004), language most spoken in the household (taxonomy/sr2004.py).
# Families from Glottolog (data/raw/glottolog):
#   Sarnami: Caribbean Hindustani (cari1275; Sarnami Hindustani sarn1238 is its Suriname
#     dialect), Indo-Aryan > Bihari > Bhojpuric. A leaf beside Bhojpuri under Bihari.
#   Saramaccan (sara1340), Ndyuka / Aukan (ndyu1242) and Pamaka / Paramaccan (para1317, which
#     Glottolog files as an Aukan dialect) are Surinamese Creole English, like Sranan: leaves
#     under English-based creoles. Pamaka is a sibling of Ndyuka, not its child, because the
#     census prints it as its own answer and a child would wash Ndyuka out as a group.
# Nodes other fragments define are repeated only as parents or reused leaves, with the identical
# label and no colour, except Sranan Tongo (pl.txt's node, which carried none).
#
# Colours. On the coast Dutch (blue, cz.txt), Javanese (violet-blue, id.txt), Sarnami and Sranan
# sit side by side in the same polders, and Lokono (pale green, ve.txt) and Kari'na (pink,
# br.txt) villages sit among them in Para and at the Marowijne mouth. Sarnami takes an orange
# near Bhojpuri's; Sranan a lime green, kept off Lokono's pale green. In the interior the
# Maroon creoles meet along the rivers (Saramaccan and Ndyuka in Brokopondo, Ndyuka and Pamaka
# on the Marowijne), so they step apart in lightness and hue inside the creole greens:
# Saramaccan olive-yellow, Ndyuka a deep teal, Pamaka a pale cream (a pale cyan sat 0.07 from
# Lokono, whose villages are near Albina too).
