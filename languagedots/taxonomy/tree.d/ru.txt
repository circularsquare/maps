# Russia, 2021 census native language (taxonomy/ru2021.py). Checked against Glottolog
# (data/raw/glottolog/languages.csv):
#   Nakh-Daghestanian (nakh1245): Andi, Akhvakh, Bagvalal, Botlikh, Godoberi, Karata, Tindi and
#   Chamalal under the existing Avar-Andic branch; Tsez (dido1241), Hinukh, Hunzib (hunz1247),
#   Bezhta (bezh1248) and Khvarshi as a Tsezic branch; Lak (lakk1252) a branch of its own beside
#   Dargwa; Tabasaran, Rutul (rutu1240) and Archi (arch1244) under Lezgic, beside Aghul.
#   Chukotko-Kamchatkan (chuk1271), Yeniseian (yeni1252: Ket, Yugh) and Yukaghir (yuka1259) are new
#   roots, one per family (AGENT_BRIEF §3). The census's "Yukaghir" names neither Tundra nor Kolyma
#   Yukaghir, so it is a leaf `yukaghir.yukaghir`, not the root. Nivkh is a family of two in
#   Glottolog (nivk1234) but read as an isolate almost everywhere: `isolate.nivkh`.
#   Uralic stays flat under `uralic`, as every country before has it. Mordvin (mord1256) and Mari
#   (mari1278) are pairs in Glottolog; the census prints "Mordvin" beside Erzya and Moksha, and
#   "Mari" beside Hill Mari and Meadow-Eastern Mari, so each of the four unspecified or specified
#   labels is a sibling leaf (a child would turn Erzya's parent into a group drawn "not named").
#   Siberian Yupik (cent2128) and the census's "Yuit" under Eskimo-Aleut; Mingrelian (ming1252)
#   under Kartvelian; Church Slavonic (chur1257, South Slavic in Glottolog) directly under Slavic, so
#   that South Slavic gains no uncoloured member (which would move Croatian's and Bosnian's generated
#   colours); Russian Sign Language (russ1271).
#   Turkic stays flat. Teleut, Tofa and Chelkan are dialects in Glottolog (of Southern Altai,
#   Tuvan-ish Tofa, Northern Altai) and Nogai-Karagash and Yurt Tatar are Nogai dialects in the
#   Institute of Linguistics' list; the census prints each as a language, so each is a leaf.
#   "Ahiska" is the census's "Turkic" (Tyurksky), which the Institute of Linguistics' list says is
#   the Meskhetian Turks' name for their language. "Bulgar" is the census's own label: the Institute
#   files most of it as Tatar, the rest as "other answers"; drawn as what it says, under Turkic.
#   "Dagestani" names no language (the Institute splits it over nine Daghestanian and Turkic
#   languages), so it is a leaf under `other`, as az.txt's "Jewish" is.
#
# COLOURS. Hand-picked for the places where languages meet on the ground:
#   Volga-Urals: Tatar (coral red), Bashkir (amber), Chuvash (magenta), Mari (light cyan), Udmurt
#     (blue), the three Mordvin labels (teals), Komi-Permyak and Komi (blues) beside Russian green.
#     Crimean Tatar keeps ua.txt's deep red; Tatar is lighter and more orange so the two differ.
#   Dagestan: Avar (az.txt's periwinkle), Dargwa dark teal, Lak pale lavender, Kumyk pink, Lezgian
#     (az.txt's blue), Tabasaran pale aqua, Rutul lilac, Nogai orange, Chechen pale sky; Ingush a
#     mid blue against Chechen; the Circassian languages violet and rose; Karachay-Balkar a darker red (beside Nogai orange in Karachay-Cherkessia).
#   Siberia: Yakut, Tuvan, Altai, Khakas in Turkic's reds and oranges; Nenets violet against Komi;
#     Khanty teal and Mansi pale blue; Chukotko-Kamchatkan orchid; Yukaghir pale yellow; Ket tan.
#   Turkic, Uralic and Nakh-Daghestanian gain many siblings here, which moves every generated
#   colour among them; the ones other countries draw are frozen at their old colours below.
