Click anywhere to set the start. Each colour is how long it takes to walk there. A day is 8 hours of walking.Each colour is how long it takes to get there with a canoe. A day is 8 hours of paddling or carrying.

Walking speeds follow the Malaria Atlas Project's recipe (Weiss et al., Nature Medicine 2020, CC BY 4.0): a pace for each kind of ground, slowed by height and slope. Ground is from Copernicus Global Land Cover 2019 (CC BY 4.0), and roads and paths from OpenStreetMap (ODbL). The whole world is worked out on cells of about 10 km, and the area in view on cells of 3 km and then 1 km as you zoom in, down to 100 m in towns and mountains.

Slopes are walked on the Copernicus 90 m height model, slower uphill than down. The canoe goes 5 km/h on still water and is carried overland at half walking speed. Rivers come from RiverATLAS and lakes from HydroLAKES (HydroSHEDS, CC BY 4.0). Which desert rivers stop flowing is from Messager et al. 2021, Global prevalence of non-perennial rivers and streams (CC BY 4.0). River lengths, and river slopes in tropical forest, are from MERIT Hydro (Yamazaki et al. 2019) through Hydrography90m (Amatulli et al. 2022, CC BY-NC 4.0), changed to a length factor and a slope per river. Falls, dams and weirs are from OpenStreetMap (ODbL), the coast from Natural Earth. Sea currents are from the NOAA Global Drifter Program, and summer waves, water temperature and sea ice from ERA5 (Copernicus Climate Change Service, CC BY 4.0).

Danger on land is thirst, cold, ice and height. Temperatures and rain are from CHELSA (Karger et al. 2017, 2021), and each place takes January or July, whichever is safer. Drinking water is rivers from RiverATLAS and lakes from HydroLAKES (HydroSHEDS, CC BY 4.0), and wells, springs and villages from OpenStreetMap (ODbL). Ice is from Copernicus Global Land Cover 2019 (CC BY 4.0), the Randolph Glacier Inventory 6.0 (CC BY 4.0) and Natural Earth, and height from GMTED2010 (USGS). Places with under 1% survival are left blank.

Hill shading is from the Terrain Tiles on AWS, built by Mapzen from SRTM, GMTED2010 and 3DEP (USGS), ETOPO1 (NOAA), EU-DEM (Copernicus) and national models, credited in full here. River lines are the ones the canoe uses, wider for more water and faint where it has to be carried.

Seas and lakes are crossed only on bridges. Ponds under 5 km² are waded at 1 km/h. Weather, disease, borders and rest are not counted. Basemap from OpenFreeMap and OpenStreetMap, drawn with MapLibre GL.
