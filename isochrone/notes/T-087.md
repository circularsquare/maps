# T-087 — the road share factor on a farm grid and other patterns

`notes/T-087/cross.mjs` was deleted at M2's doc tidy (T-078, 2026-11-07; in git history); the Reproduce scripts are kept.

2026-11-06. Rule under test: `docs/roads.md` (e = min(1, 3 s), road speed 5 km/h x height x slope
of min(slope, 10%)). Scripts in `notes/T-087/`, results (compare.mjs output) in `notes/T-087/res_*.json`,
plot `maps/artifacts/isochrone/t087_factor.png`. No page, tools/ or data/ change.

## Answer

- **Factor 3 is right for road nets that wind, branch or are dense, and too low on straight sparse grids.**
  Iowa's section-line grid wants 5-6, Ethiopian and Sahel farm tracks 5-6 or more; Cluj 3-4, Kentucky,
  the Alps, Piedmont, Nepal and northern France 2.5-3.3. One factor for all cannot fit both.
- On Iowa, factor 3 leaves the 1 km map 11-18% slower (median per cell) than a 100 m walker on the same
  roads. Factor 6 fits Iowa (1-3%) but is 6-8% too fast on every dense or winding tile.
- **What separates them is how straight a cell's roads are** (longest run of road cells across the cell,
  divided by the road cells in it; Iowa 0.62-0.71, Ethiopia 0.62, Sahel 0.66, Cluj and Kentucky 0.53-0.54,
  Alps, Piedmont, Nepal, France 0.30-0.37). Not road kind, not the road's share of the cell, not whether
  a road crosses opposite edges, not how much the road beats off-road walking.
- **Proposed rule** (`strs_3_20_0.5`): e = min(1, a s), with a = 3 + 20 x max(0, q - 0.5), where q is the
  straightness from the cell and its 8 neighbours (sums of span and of road cells). Same as factor 3 where
  q <= 0.5 (every winding and dense tile), up to 6 at q = 0.65, 8 at q = 0.7. Against the same 100 m solves
  the worst tile falls from 17-18% to 3.6% (roads only) or 11.6% (full land cover); see tables.
- It needs one new number per 1 km cell in the road layer (`l1/` holds count and OR of kinds; add the longest
  run, a byte, at `build.py reduce`, then `build_tiles.py` computes q). The page's road byte format is
  unchanged. If that is more than wanted, **factor 4 is the cheapest single change**: worst tile 14% (full) or 10.6%
  (roads only), mean 6.1% against 6.9%; it makes dense tiles 3-6% too fast.

## Method

Like T-010 (100 m solve with roads as truth, 1 km rules compared with the page's own search, `js/search.js`
run in node; same 16 moves, same cost rules), with these differences:

- Twelve 1 degree tiles, all with `data/roads/bits/` and our surface. Iowa n41w094 and n42w093 (section roads);
  Sahel n13w002 (Burkina Faso, sparse tracks); Ethiopian highlands n11e038 (sparse, hilly, tf 0.54); northern
  France n50e002 (dense village net); Piedmont n44e007 and Valais n46e007 (hilly, dense); Nepal n27e085 (hills,
  mixed); Kentucky n37w083 (winding hollow roads); Cluj n46e023 (T-010's tile, a replication); Finland n61e025
  (forest tracks and lakes); Mekong n10e105 (rice delta, canals).
- **Truth** (`sample.py`): 100 m cells of LC100 class speed x the L1 terrain factor; a road cell (path, local or
  main) walks at the better of that and the **1 km road speed** (5 km/h x height factor x slope factor of min(slope,
  10%)); water is blocked except under a walkable bridge. This is the speed the 1 km rule gives its roads, so a
  difference is the rule's blend, not the grade cap (the page's L0.1 still walks roads at the full terrain
  factor; below, "page 100 m"). Second truth `f_uni` (`uni.py`): the same with every off-road 100 m cell walking at
  its 1 km cell's off-road speed, which removes the land cover mosaic and leaves only the roads. Third, the
  floor: the plain 100 m (no roads) against the no-roads 1 km map.
- **Pairs** (`compare.mjs`): 12 random land starts a tile (fixed seeds), the 1 km search started in the 1 km cell
  holding the start. Every third 100 m cell (rows and columns) reached at both with a 1 km time over 0.5 h,
  about 2 million pairs a rule and tile. Measures: **rel** = median |t100 - t1| / t1; **ratio** = summed
  100 m time / summed 1 km time (below 1: the 1 km map is too slow; above 1: too fast); bias = median ln(t100/t1).
- Rules built in `rules.py` from per-cell features (`sample.py`: share s, shares by kind, longest road run,
  edges touched, components) on the page's blend: `1 - e + e x min(1, off-road / road speed)`, bridges at road speed.

## What did not help

(`res_cap.json`; all worse than the straightness rule.) Road kind was not run as a rule: the tiles' shares of
path, local and main roads (`featstats.py`) do not line up with the best factor (Piedmont has 0.21 paths and
wants 2.8-3, Iowa 0.04 and wants 5-6, Kentucky 0.04 and wants 2.5). Through-roads (`thru`), edges touched
(`cross`) and the longest run alone (`span`) raise e on every cell with a road and overshoot dense tiles by 7-12%. `pay` (A rising with how much a road beats off-road walking) fits Iowa,
Kentucky and Cluj at 1-3% but overshoots Piedmont, Valais and France by 6-9%: the dense tiles gain as much from
roads as Iowa does and still want 3. A detour on the road speed (`det`) is 2-8% worse than none where
off-road is already fast. Saturating forms (sat_8, sat_12) trade Iowa for the dense tiles like a bigger A.

Why straightness: factor 3 reads a cell's road cells as three times their area because a walker strings roads
together. A long straight road or a mile grid is usable whole, a village street net or a hollow road with
branches is not (cells are touched by several streets, dead ends, the wrong direction). On straight roads one
road cell is worth about 6 cells of area, on tangled ones 3 (or 2.2 in Finland, 2.5 in Kentucky).

## What it moves

Mean time per km over land cells, strs_3_20_0.5 against factor 3 (`shift.py`): Iowa -9% (n41w094, 3.40 to
3.7 km/h) and -15% (n42w093, 3.04 to 3.6 km/h); 38% and 59% of cells change speed by over 10%. Ethiopia -2%,
Sahel -2%; Cluj -2%, Kentucky -1%, France 0%, Piedmont -1%, Valais -2%, Nepal -1%. A plain factor 4 would move
dense tiles by 5-7% with 20-36% of their cells over 10%, which the 100 m times do not support (3-6% too fast
there). Iowa against MAP (T-084: 1.34x slower at L10) would fall to roughly 1.2x (a rough division, not rebuilt);
what stays is the 10-14% land cover mosaic floor (above), which the 100 m walker beats and the 1 km map does not.

## Caveats

- Mekong (delta) and Finland (lakes) are water-confounded: a 1 km land cell can hold canals or lakes that the 100 m
  solve must walk around, so the 1 km map is too fast whatever the roads (Mekong ratio 1.24 with no roads at all,
  1.7-2.0 with roads); they say nothing about the road factor. Mekong is excluded from the means; Finland is in
  the tables and excluded from the means.
- Twelve tiles, one 1 degree tile each; the straightness knee (0.5) and slope (20) were set by eye on them
  (neighbours strs_3_15_0.45 and strs_3_25_0.5 score the same, 3.8% and 3.7%), so a new tile type (a rice
  terrace region, a Brazil cerrado grid, a Canadian prairie grid) is worth one more check before the world
  build. Prairie grids (Canada, Kansas, Pampas) are the case the rule most extends to.
- The 100 m truth is OSM at the bits' date; fine-level roads of a different date agree to 0.01-0.05%.
- Sampling every third 100 m cell and 12 starts a tile, one seed set; no other seeds were run, so differences
  under about 1-2% between rules are not established.

### Cost

Node, one core, this machine (`cross.mjs`; the 100 m grids are in memory; reading LC100, terrain and bits for
a tile is 0.03-0.05 s):

| Margin | us per land cell per grid | Tiles' difference from margin 5 (six tiles) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 (half a cell, T-080's) | 333 | none |
| 2 | 152 | up to +0.2 points |
| 1 | 107 | up to +0.6 points (Saskatchewan 5.0 to 5.4%) |

(margin 0 has no source row and is not valid.) The page needs two grids per cell (with and without roads, for the
road byte), so a world build is 250 M land cells x 2 grids x the per-cell time: **margin 5 ~46 core-hours,
margin 2 ~21, margin 1 ~15** (on the 4 isochrone cores 11, 5 and 3.7 hours; on 10 cores 4.6, 2.1 and 1.5).
Roads-free windows (no road bit within the cell and margin, a large share of the Sahara, Siberia, Amazon,
Antarctic-edge land) need one grid, not two, which could take a third to a half off in the world, not
measured. The search is JS Dijkstra with a heap; a numpy or C port is likely several times faster, not
tried. The 100 m inputs for the whole world come from the LC100 COG, `data/roads/bits/` (16,183 tiles) and
`terrain_factor.tif` with `sample.py`'s assembly (a second or two a tile, so ~10 core-hours extra, none of it
measured at that scale).

### Recommendation

Prefer the crossing rule, built with margin 1 or 2, over any share factor, if a 15-25 core-hour world build
is acceptable: one rule takes the road factor, road straightness and the mosaic floor at once, with the
worst tile at 7% (Pampas) against 21% for factor 3 and 12% for the straightness rule, needs no fitted
constants (the straightness rule's knee and slope were set by eye) and keeps the page's byte format
(speed = `xminplain`, road byte = `xmin / xminplain`, capped at 1). It also moves every no-roads L1 speed
(the floor correction: the no-roads mean rule is 10-20% slow against the 100 m grid in most tiles) so the walks in roadless country change too, and Anita
should see the map before and after; that is T-080's change for L10 and L3, applied one level down.
If a rebuild that size is not wanted now: the straightness rule (one extra byte in `l1/`) is the cheaper
second choice, factor 4 the cheapest; neither touches the floor. Open before choosing: delta and canal
country (Mekong), whether the page's water rules (small water waded at 1 km/h, the quarter rule, canoe data)
still agree with crossing speeds built on blocked 100 m water, and a prairie check on more than three tiles.
