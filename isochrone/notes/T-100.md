# T-100 small-river lengths, and T-101 canopy read as rapids

Both measured against MERIT Hydro (Yamazaki et al. 2019), read from Hydrography90m (2026-11-07).
Rules now in `docs/canoe.md` 2 and 4; code `tools/canoe/h90.py` and `tools/canoe/rivers.py`
(`length_factor`, `merit_reaches`).

## Source and licence

MERIT Hydro's own download is a Google Form registration that mails a password (no account made;
URL for Anita: https://global-hydrodynamics.github.io/MERIT_Hydro/). Its licence is CC BY-NC 4.0
or ODbL 1.0, the user's choice.

Hydrography90m (Amatulli et al. 2022, doi:10.5194/essd-14-4525-2022, data doi:10.18728/igb-fred-762.1)
is MERIT Hydro's network with per-cell layers, on a public Nextcloud share with no login. Its
global layers are cloud-optimised GeoTIFFs and the share answers HTTP range requests, so GDAL
reads only the windows used. **Its licence is CC BY-NC 4.0 only** (README/LICENSE.txt on the share).
Used here: `outlet_diff_dw_basin` (m above the basin outlet along the flow path, i.e. MERIT's
hydrologically adjusted elevation less the outlet's), `outlet_dist_dw_basin` (m to the outlet along
the flow path), `accumulation` (km²), `basin` (id). See research/canoe.md 1.

## Method

Sample boxes (34, ~1.5-8 square degrees each; `T-100/fetch_all.py` reads them into
`data/raw/hydrography90m/`, `analyse.py` pairs them to `pairs.npz`, `report.py` and `table.py`
print the tables, `points.py` the checks below): for every paddleable reach whose midpoint is in a box, the
stretch from its midpoint to the midpoint of the first downstream reach at least 10 km on (the
gradient window of T-031). Each midpoint is snapped to the highest-accumulation MERIT cell within
4 pixels (360 m); a pair is kept when both ends are in one basin, the lower end is nearer the
outlet, and Q / upstream area at each end is within 4x of the box median (else the snap found
another river). 21,974 pairs. Per pair: RiverATLAS km, MERIT km (difference of flow distance),
MERIT drop, GMTED drop on the same two points (3 x 3 minimum).

Reading: in tropical forest GMTED's drop is high by 0.1-0.5 m/km, about a canopy step of a few
metres over the 10 km window, and it is enough to tip small rivers into rapids. MERIT removes most
of it but not all: round Manaus MERIT still has Q 1-10 streams at a median 1.3 m/km, which is the
terra firme's real relief (streams cut 50-80 m down from the plateau to the Negro's ~20 m), not
canopy. In boreal and temperate forest (Siberia, Canada, Poland) GMTED and MERIT agree, so only
tropical forest needs MERIT.

**Tiles**: 5-degree tiles with at least 20% of their land in LC100 class 112 (closed evergreen
broadleaf forest): 132 tiles, ~1,840 square degrees of land (10%: 156 tiles; 30%: 111).

## Coarse steps counted every twig (fixed on the way)

With T-100's factor alone, big-river trips at L10 got 11-17% slower (Kisangani to Kinshasa 30.8
-> 35.6 days), though their own factor is ~1.01. Cause: T-082's contraction gave a coarse step
half of each piece's whole river length, and a piece is every L1 river step in the cell joined to
it, so the twigs of small tributaries inside an L10 cell were paddled along the big river; their
v also pulled the step's v down. Kisangani to Kinshasa was 242 h paddled for ~1,730 km, against
~162 h at p + v. Now a coarse step counts the piece's main river only (L1 steps with v >= 0.9 x
the piece's fastest). On the lower Mississippi the open steps with v >= 1.35 m/s then sum to
1,128 km at L10 against 1,147 km at L1 (684 km at L10 before: the twigs' v pulled steps below the
cut). The fix alone (`tfix`, served L1 contracted again), and with T-100 (`t100`), ONLY=rivers at
L10, K = 1000:

the river 1.2-1.5 x longer, poling at 2.5 is already slower per valley km than carrying, so a lower
speed would change nothing here. Left as it is.
