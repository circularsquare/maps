minimum round each end): Manaus 83/17/0 GMTED, 90/10/0 GLO-90; Belem 71/29/0, 74/26/0; St Louis

49/30/21; Kinshasa 48/48/4, 47/49/4; Iron Gates 46/53/1, 46/52/2. Mean m/km on Q >= 300: the same
to 0.1 everywhere but the Glomma (0.99 / 0.84) and Kinshasa (0.32 / 0.40). Both are surface models
with the canopy in them; GLO-90 would need the 100+ GB world stream again for a few points of
class share. Not worth it.

**Decision: GMTED over 10 km everywhere**, one rule, no seam at 60N. It closes about as much as
before (23% of km south of 60N, was 24%): the brief's "24% closed too much" does not hold up. Both
DEMs agree that small streams in mountains are steep (Alps 67% closed in all three sources, the
upper Indus 77-79%); what moves is lowland rapids to flat (Amazon 64% -> 48%, Finland 56% -> 27%)
and canyons opening (the Grand Canyon). 10 km rather than 20: 20 smooths lowland noise a little
more but averages short gorges away (the Grand Canyon 2% closed, the Livingstone stretch 5%).

### Big rivers: the closing gradient falls with Q

The Livingstone Falls average 0.8 m/km (Kinshasa ~270 m to Matadi ~5 m over ~350 km), flat by any
gradient limit, and GMTED shows them as runs of 0.5-1.5 m/km between calmer stretches. A river of
39,000 m³/s at 0.8 m/km has a stream power over its width of ~200 W/m²; the old limit (2 m/km
from Q = 1000) is ~86 W/m² there. Rule: closing gradient 2.5 x sqrt(1000/Q), between 0.3 and 2
(about 100 W/m² over W = 7.2 Q^0.5). The first try, 2 x sqrt(1000/Q), also closed four Brahmaputra
reaches near Tezpur (0.51-0.59 against 0.50), which are flat water; 2.5 has none there.

With it (GMTED 10 km), new closures on big rivers: the Congo from Kintambo to Manyanga and Isangila
to Matadi (148 km), leaving the Manyanga-Isangila stretch open, which was navigated between the
portages in the 1880s; the Columbia at Celilo (-121.10, 45.64) and the Cascades (-121.93, 45.65);
the Mekong at Khone (105.99, 13.95) and in northern Laos; the Zambezi at Cahora Bassa (Kebrabasa);
the Yangtze's Three Gorges (a reservoir today, so closed already); 5 km of the Danube at the Iron
Gates (dams). None on the Amazon, Mississippi, Ob, Brahmaputra or the middle Congo. The 0.3 floor
keeps a 2 m GMTED step over 10 km (0.2 m/km) from closing the Amazon.

## T-083: desert permanence from GIRES

GIRES (Messager et al. 2021; licence in research/canoe.md 1) gives each RiverATLAS reach the
probability that it stops flowing at least 30 days a year (`predprob30`). Of the 84,800 km of hot
desert rivers T-079's rule kept, 33,600 km have predprob30 >= 0.5 and 20,200 km >= 0.7.

Rule, hot desert only: a reach whose predprob30, averaged over it and 25 km up and down its river,
is 0.6 or more is dry and passes no flow on. Kept now: **57,300 km** of hot desert rivers (was
84,800); 215,600 km of hot desert reaches dropped in all.

- 0.5 (GIRES's own class) cut the lower Senegal from Podor to the sea (0.48-0.55 there) and,
  without the averaging, the Niger below Timbuktu: three reaches at 0.51-0.52 (-3.6 to -3.5 E,
  16.1 N) among 0.2s, and a dry reach passes nothing on.
- Dropped: Cooper Creek below the mask edge (mean 0.88), the Diamantina and Georgina (0.72, 0.65),
  the Finke (0.90), the Draa past Zagora (0.64), most of the lower Gila. The Rio Grande below El
  Paso stays (0.53). The Thomson at Longreach is outside the hot
  desert mask and still paddles (seasonal rivers outside the desert run, the paddler picks the
  season).
- Kept whole: Nile, Atbara (3,183 of 3,241 km), Niger, Senegal, Euphrates, Tigris, Indus, Helmand,
  Amu and Syr Darya, Colorado, Orange, Kunene, Ili.
- **The Darling** crosses the mask's edge below Bourke: its hot desert reaches read 0.64-0.77 and
  drop, the others stay, so it is broken into pieces between Wilcannia and Menindee (Bourke to
  Wentworth 16.8 -> 20.0 days, carried 13 -> 54 h). Today it does stop for months in droughts;
  left as the rule gives it.
- Glacier-fed rivers (lower Tarim, Hotan, Keriya) were already dry from T-079 (WaterGAP has no
  glacier melt); GIRES changes nothing there. Noted in docs/canoe.md 8 for 10,000 BC (T-012).

The land hazard reads the same flags for drinking water in hot desert: vertices dropped there
1,180,039 -> 1,216,320. Windorah to Lake Eyre now carries the canoe with no river water: survival
99.2% -> 95.4%.
