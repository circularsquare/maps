## Design

T-082 split coarse rivers into pieces; sea and lake water in a coarse cell stayed one node. The
same idea for water:

- **Water pieces** (`tools/canoe/pieces.py`): 8-connected runs of L1 sea or lake pixels inside
  one coarse cell, labelled strip by strip with one 3-D `ndimage.label` over the cells' blocks
  (connectivity only inside a block). A cell is *split* when it has two or more, or any while it
  is river water (kind 2: its own node has no grid edges, so its lake bits were no node at all).
  Only split cells get piece ids; elsewhere the cell bands already say everything.
- **Units** (`water_units` in `rivers.py`): in a split cell the water pieces and T-082's river
  pieces are joined where a river piece's L1 cell is a sea or lake pixel of a piece, so a river
  running from one lake to another inside the cell makes one unit of the two (L1 paddles it). Slot
  0 is the unit with most pixels of the cell's own kind (sea or lake); in a river-water cell the
  longest river, one touching water first (T-082's rule there). Cells not split keep T-082's slots
  exactly.
- **Links**: L1 pixel pairs that touch across a cell border (8-neighbours; a diagonal through the
  corner pixels, as `water.py`'s `make_links`), where one side is in a split cell, give unit
  pairs. A pair of the two cells' own nodes stays a `links` bit (the band is rewritten unit to
  unit, so grid moves and knight moves run as before); every other pair is a listed water link.
- **Worker**: listed units become further nodes (`extraN`); listed links become edges in the
  river edge lists, both ways, coded `P_GRID + move` and costed as the search costs a sea or lake
  grid move (current from each unit's own shore, hazard of each end). `canoeFrom` picks, for a
  grid code in a cell with several nodes, the water link whose cost gives the node's weight
  exactly, else the cell's own node. `search.js` needs no change: further nodes already had no
  grid moves and read the edge lists.

Why not other shapes: one node per water pixel group with explicit edges everywhere (no bits) would
be ~20 M records at L10 for the open ocean; patching only split cells keeps the data small (0.7 MB
at L10) and the ocean on the fast grid path.

- **Splitting water alone moved little** (Jyvaskyla 1.16 -> 1.14). Only lakes of 5 km² and more
  are water (smaller ones are land, docs/resolution.md), so few L10 cells hold two: 35,615 split
  cells at L10, 23,083 at L3.
- **The routes** (`probe.mjs`, Jyvaskyla 40-160 km, four bearings): L10 paddled about as many
  hours as L1, but switched between water bodies through transfers that carry nothing: a river
  node to the land node and on into a lake node of the same cell for 15 min plus the river's access,
  where L1 carried several km. T-082 had left water nodes on land cells with no access.
- **Water access**: the same as a river piece's, the mean straight distance from the cell's L1 land
  pixels to the unit's water (median 2.9 km at L10, 0.9 at L3), carried at `cfg.access` (0.5). It
  is the bigger part of the gain. It also lands on the start's cell: a fjord town's start cell
  pays its mean access at mountain walking speed (Sogndal: getting in 0.3 -> 3.3 h), which is why
  fjords and the Amazon floodplain went slow. Exempting the start's own cell recovers most of
  that but gives back part of the lake gain and is a second rule; not kept (DECISIONS.md).
- **What is left** (docs/canoe.md 8): a coarse node on a land cell stands at the cell's centre for
  transfers to neighbouring cells, wherever its water is, so chains of lakes and rivers stay a
  little cheap at L10; and the slow side is T-080's coarse walking bias (T-081 would cut it, and
  `cfg.access` should be re-measured then, as T-082 said).

## T-075

- **One-pixel ponds** (fixed): a sea or lake cell with no land, no linked open water, no river and
  no water link beside it counts as river water off the line (`buildCanoe`, every level), so T-036's
  depth rule reaches it and the river water round it. Sundarbans L1 box 4 holes -> 0; no ktrips
  change.
- **Water round an island no river line comes near** (left): not part of this design. The depth
  of river water off the line counts from the island's shore as well as from the line, so the water
  between them climbs from both sides and has no way down to the island. A fix would count depth
  from canoe nodes first (land only for patches with none): the paddler could then cross from the
  line to any bank, still never along the water (depth only rises away from the line). That lets
  canoe trips reach banks across wide river water everywhere (the Amazon, the lower Danube), so it
  is a model change with its own measurement, not a small case.
