## Sources checked

- **CHELSA V2.1**: EnviDat page says CC0 1.0 (research/friction.md 6 had "opendata.swiss open
  terms"; corrected). Files are uint16 K x 10 (scale tag 0.1), 84 N to 90 S; north of 84 N the
  build uses the 84 N row.
- **FM 21-76 (1957) fig. 196**, the desert survival table, read from the archive.org OCR (p. 224).
  The OCR drops single-digit cells; every surviving cell matches the usual table, so the table is
  taken as checked. The 10-quart walking column drives the thirst rule.
- **Altitude**: Weinbruch & Nordby 2013 ranges confirmed on the Himalayan Database page (no
  period given); Shlim & Gallie 1992 added for trekkers (14 deaths per 100,000, 1987-91).
- **Ice**: Greenland crossings (Colgan's blog from Danish Polar Centre counts: ~24 crossing teams
  a year 2010-12, ~3% evacuated). Cold exposure: no usable source; stays a guess.
- **Heat with water**: Hajj 2024 (1,301 deaths, 51.8 C); attendance ~1.8 M from memory.

## Water: what counts

First pass: every RiverATLAS reach with mean Q >= 0.1 m³/s and every HydroLAKES lake. The plot
showed the Sahara's wadi network as water: WaterGAP gives big dry catchments a mean flow over 0.1
(99th percentile 2.3 m³/s in the central Sahara), so most of the Sahara read 1e-4 to 1e-3 an hour.
Fixed with a **hot desert** mask (annual rain under 250 mm, CHELSA bio12, and the warmer of the
January and July day highs at least 25 C): there only perennial rivers count (Q >= 1 with at least
0.1 m³/s in the driest month, RiverATLAS `dis_m3_pmn`), and lakes do not (mostly salt or
seasonal). 1.07 M of 33.9 M river vertices dropped; 7.7 M of 55.6 M land cells of the 2 km grid
are hot desert. Cold deserts (the high Arctic, 100-290 mm) keep their streams: they melt each
summer.

K = 300 and 3000 (osm): the same routes as K = 1000 within a day, except Greenland (300: 79 d,
76%; 3000: 95 d, 86%) and Khartoum at 3000 (63.5 d, 99.3%).

**Cluj's whole reach** (osm, K = 1000 against hazard off): 368,357 land cells reached against
372,462 (the stop rule drops the worst of the Sahara and Arabia at the far edge); the time to
cells reached both ways is 0.1% longer on average, 0.2% of them over 5% longer.

Reading the table:
- **Cluj, Siberia, Lhasa, Darwin barely move**: the season rule takes Siberia in July and the
  steppe in January, so cold almost never fires; altitude costs Lhasa ~1%.
- **Thirst does almost all the work**, in hot deserts, and only far from water: with OSM points
  most of the Sahara is crossable; without them only the Nile and Niger corridors are.
- **Greenland** dies of thirst (no liquid water on the ice, 280 km to the middle) more than of
  the ice rate; K = 1000 walks round the south tip.
- **Altar to Tucson** was meant to check the 0.2%-a-crossing border death rate; the walker here
  follows the road through settlements (water every few km), so the rule gives ~0 even in July.
  Migrants die because they avoid exactly those places. Not a contradiction, and not a check.
- **K = 1000 is not absurd on land**: a day or two for several points of survival in deserts and
  the Himalaya, nothing in temperate country. The worst is In Salah in July, a 127-day loop at
  37% where the direct line is under 1%; that follows from K's 10 h per 1% and is the right shape.
- Solve times here mean nothing (a JS-array heap); stage 3 measures the real loop.
