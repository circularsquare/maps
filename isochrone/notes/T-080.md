# T-080: does zooming in make trips slower?

Its scripts were deleted at M2's doc tidy (T-078, 2026-11-07; in git history); `checks/levels.mjs` is the standing check.

Anita (T-080): "if we zoom in i think the trips get strictly slower cuz we are simulating more
barriers. not sure if thats true. but is there a way we could like predict the rough scale of this
bias so it doesnt feel quite as directional"

## Answer

- **Between L10, L3 and L1 it is the other way round: zooming in makes walks faster**, by about
  a fifth from L10 to L1 (median L1/L10 = 0.81), the same at every distance from 10 km to 300 km.
  Mountains most (0.63), cities and lake country least (0.84-0.88).
- **The cause is the averaging, not barriers.** A coarse cell's speed is the mean time per metre
  of its 1 km pixels; a walk at 1 km keeps to the fast pixels (MAP's 5 km/h road pixels, valley
  floors, passes) and the mean charges it for the slow ones too. Water, the quarter rule and the
  16-move geometry add almost nothing on average.
- **Where zooming in does make trips slower**, it is one of three other things:
  1. **L0.1 against L1** (Cluj, New York, the default start): 100 m times are ~21% longer than L1.
     That is the source, not the resolution: L0.1 is LC100 + OSM roads, L1 is MAP, whose road
     model walks a whole 1 km pixel at 5 km/h when a road crosses it (T-047). Against L1 made from
     the same 100 m data, L0.1 is ~10% faster, as every other step.
  2. **Rivers and lakes that cut land at 1 km but not at 10 km** (the Amazon: 29 of 57
     destinations round Coari reached at L10 and not at L1; New York's rivers at 100 m): rare,
     but large.
  3. **The canoe in river country** is 1.2-1.9 times too fast at L10 (below), so zooming in on a
     canoe start there slows it.
- **The fix**: make each coarse cell's speed the speed at which the finer grid crosses it (the
  best crossing, east-west and north-south). Prototyped on 25 regions: L1/L10 goes from 0.81
  (0.56-0.98) to 1.01 (0.89-1.14), L1/L3 from 0.89 (0.71-1.00) to 0.99 (0.91-1.04), and window
  seams disappear. Same file format and size, no page code changes, a ~15-30 minute build.

## Method

Scripts in `notes/T-080/` (run from that folder; `lib.mjs` loads the worker's modules in node over
the real data, as `checks/ktrips.mjs`).

- **Pairs** (`regions.mjs`, `measure.mjs`): 25 starts in 7 kinds of land (farmland: Poltava, Ames,
  Pampa, Ludhiana; mountains: Brig, Cusco, Pokhara, Dillon; lakes: Jyvaskyla, Sudbury, Bemidji;
  desert: Tamanrasset, Alice Springs, Dalanzadgad; forest: Kisangani, Coari, Ust-Ilimsk; cities:
  Cluj, New York, London, Lagos, Delhi; coast and delta: Khulna, Utrecht, Sogndal). From each,
  destinations at 5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160 and 300 km (+-15%), 12 bearings, kept when on L1 land:
  1,971 pairs.
- **Pure levels**: each level solved on its own from the start only: L10 the world (as the page),
  L3 and L1 a box +-3.3 degrees round the start; destinations at least 0.3 degree inside the box.
  The time read is the cell holding the destination (what the page paints and hovers; a water
  cell takes its 3 x 3's least). Walking with land hazard on and off, and the canoe (on a copy of
  `data/canoe/` from 2026-10-26, taken before tonight's river rebuild).
- **As served** (`served.mjs`): the page's own windows (`windowFor`, `solveWindow`, seeding as
  `refineWindows`) for a 1400 x 900 view centred on each destination at zoom 4, 6 and 8, the time
  read from the finest level holding it. 12 destinations a start (20, 80, 300 km).
- **L0.1** (`fine.mjs`, `finex.mjs`): four starts in the Cluj and New York tiles (two towns, two
  rural), 16 bearings at 5-80 km, the L0.1 window against L1 (MAP) and against L1 and L10 made
  from L0.1 itself.
- **Causes** (`variants.mjs`): the L1 box altered, then made into L3 and L10 by the build rule
  (`upscale` in `lib.mjs` reproduces the built L3 tiles exactly for 90-99.7% of cells, the rest
  one step of rounding), so only one thing differs at a time.
- **Fixes** (`proto.mjs`, `proto2.mjs`, `homog.mjs`): coarse grids made from the L1 box by other
  rules, solved and read as above, against the L1 time.

Ratios are fine time / coarse time: under 1 means the finer level is faster. Tables give the
median and the 10th-90th percentile; "within 10%" is the share of pairs between 0.9 and 1.1.
Trips under 20 km are left out of most tables: the start and the destination are snapped to cell
centres, which at L10 (7-9 km cells) is most of a 5 km trip.

## 2. Where it comes from

Each variant changes the L1 box, then L3 and L10 are made from it by the build rule
(`variants.mjs`). L1/L10, trips 20-300 km:

| Variant | What is left to differ | all | farmland | mountains | forest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| real | everything | 0.81 (0.56-0.98) | 0.81 | 0.63 | 0.73 |
| flat: every cell 3 km/h, no water | geometry and cell snapping | 1.00 (0.93-1.09) | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| uni: land 3 km/h, water kept | + water and the quarter rule | 1.01 (0.93-1.11) | 1.00 | 1.00 | 1.01 (0.92-1.35) |
| fill: water filled at the median speed | speed contrast only | 0.81 (0.55-0.98) | 0.81 | 0.63 | 0.74 |
| fcap: fill, capped at the median | slow patches only | 0.88 (0.67-1.02) | 0.81 | 0.79 | 0.98 |
| ffloor: fill, raised to the median | fast corridors only | 0.98 (0.71-1.07) | 1.00 | 0.96 | 0.78 |

- **Speed contrast is all of it.** Filling the water changes nothing (fill = real); with water
  and land cover gone (flat) the bias is gone. Averaging time per metre keeps the cost of walking
  straight through a cell, but a 1 km path does not walk straight through: it takes the fast
  pixels. The coarse cell charges every path for the slow ones.
- **Which pixels**: the median L1 speed is 5 km/h (MAP's road and open-ground speed) in farmland,
  cities, lake country and the Alps and Rockies: there the fine path avoids the slow pixels
  (fields, slopes, marsh: fcap = real). In forest and most desert (median 1.5-3 km/h) it follows the
  fast ones, the roads and tracks (ffloor = real). Cusco and Pokhara have both.
- **Water and the quarter rule** cancel on average (1.01) with a long tail where rivers cut (Coari
  p90 2.1): a coarse cell that is a quarter land is walked across at its land speed, a cell under
  a quarter land is water and blocks. Small at L10/L1, large at L0.1 in New York.
- **Geometry and cell snapping** (16 moves, start and destination at cell centres) is unbiased,
  +-7-9% a trip at L10. No fix of the speeds can remove it; it is the floor for any coarse rule.
- **Hazard**: none. Hazard on and off give the same ratios to 0.01.
- **Seeding** from a coarser ring: carries the coarse level's bias into a window that does not
  hold the start (section 4), and makes the steps at window edges; it adds none of its own.

## 3. Fixes

### Options tried (L1 / corrected coarse, 20-300 km)

| Rule for a coarse cell's speed | L1/L10 | within 10% | L1/L3 | within 10% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| now: mean time per metre | 0.81 (0.56-0.98) | 22% | 0.89 (0.71-1.00) | 44% |
| now x one global factor (0.81, 0.89) | 1.00 (0.69-1.21) | 43% | 1.00 (0.80-1.12) | 61% |
| now x a factor per kind of land | 1.00 (0.76-1.24) | 46% | 1.00 (0.83-1.12) | 68% |
| geometric mean of the pixel speeds | 0.87 (0.66-1.02) | 35% | 0.92 (0.78-1.00) | 58% |
| mean speed (km/h, not time per metre) | 0.91 (0.73-1.05) | 47% | 0.94 (0.83-1.01) | 72% |
| crossing, mean over the far edge | 0.94 (0.77-1.07) | 59% | 0.94 (0.80-1.00) | 67% |
| crossing, between best and mean | 0.97 (0.83-1.10) | 70% | 0.96 (0.86-1.01) | 79% |
| **crossing, best** | **1.01 (0.89-1.14)** | **73%** | **0.99 (0.91-1.04)** | **88%** |
| floor: cell snapping alone (flat) | 1.00 (0.93-1.09) | | | |

The factors were fitted on the same pairs, so they flatter themselves; they still leave 0.69-1.21
at L10 because the bias runs from 0.57 to 0.98 between regions of one kind (Kisangani against
Ust-Ilimsk, Brig against Pokhara).

**Crossing, best** ("xmin"), per cell: search the 1 km grid inside the cell and a margin round
it, from the column just west of the cell to its east column, and from the row just north to its
south row (the page's own search: 16 moves, the knight-move water rule). The fastest arrival on
the far edge over the cell's width is its east-west time per km; the cell's speed is from the
geometric mean of the two directions (the one that exists, if only one does; the mean rule's
value if neither). Land and water stay as now (the quarter rule), so the canoe water data, the
hazard tiles and the page code are untouched; hazard per km follows the speed as it does now.

L3/L10 with both corrected: 1.02 (0.94-1.15) (now 0.92 (0.76-1.02)). Pictures:
`maps/artifacts/isochrone/t080_ratios.png` (all kinds, now against crossing), `t080_cusco.png` and

Left after it: desert and forest are still ~4% fast at 1 km, sparse tracks a single crossing
can't credit fully; rivers that cut (Coari, Midtown: a blocked direction is averaged with the
open one); and the snapping floor.

**Two values a cell** (east-west and north-south kept apart, a move costed on the ellipse they
make; `proto2.mjs`, `dirsearch.mjs`) gains a little, nearly all of it in desert and forest, where
tracks run one way: L1/L10 1.01 (0.91-1.14), 77% within 10% (one value: 73%); L1/L3 1.00
(0.93-1.05), 92% (one value: 88%); desert at L3 90% against 70%. It doubles the data and changes
the search in every mode, so it is a later step if the desert tails come to matter.

### Cost

- **Build** (node, measured on three boxes): L10 0.45-0.5 ms a land cell with a margin of half a
  cell, 1.0-1.2 ms with a whole cell (the best crossing is the same either way: the gzipped grids
  differ by bytes); L3 18-22 us a cell with a 1-pixel margin. The world has 2.25 M L10 land cells
  and ~20 M L3: ~19 min and ~7 min on one core, under 10 minutes on the 4 isochrone cores, plus
  reading the tif in strips with a margin row of coarse cells above and below.
- **Size**: the same uint8 grid and tiles. Gzipped, the corrected boxes were 0.5-1.06 times the
  current ones (smoother in lake country).
- **Page**: no change. `tools/build_grid.py` and `tools/build_tiles.py` change their averaging.
  The search could later be ported to numpy or kept in node as `tools/crossing.mjs`.
- **L1 itself** stays MAP's 1 km pixels. Once T-028/T-010 make L1 from 100 m inputs, the same rule
  builds L1 from them: from L0.1 in the Cluj and New York tiles it gives 1.01 (0.98-1.15) against
  0.90 (0.80-1.10) for the mean rule.

**Portals** (T-007): crossing times between several points on each edge, a graph search over
them. 10-100 times the data and a new search. Measured here, the best-crossing grid already takes
the bias out and the two-direction version adds a few points; what portals would add is the blocked
direction (a river crossed only at a bridge, a ridge with one pass), which is the Coari and Midtown
tail. Not needed for T-080; T-007 stays where it is.

## 4. Window seams

`seam.mjs`: for each start, four L1 windows of about 170 x 110 km, centred 120-200 km away and
not holding the start (97 windows in all), seeded on their ring from L10 as `windowSeeds` does, against L1 solved whole.

| L10 built | window / whole L1, inside | window / L10 a quarter in from the edge |
|---|---|---|
| now | 1.09 (1.01-1.39) | 0.91 (0.75-1.00) |
| crossing, best | 1.00 (0.98-1.05) | 1.00 (0.98-1.02) |

Now, a window that does not hold the start starts from L10's slow times on its ring (9% slow, up
to 39%), and inside it times grow at L1's faster rate, so the bands step at the window edge (the
window 9% faster than the coarse level next to it). With the corrected L10 both go to 1.00: the
seams docs/resolution.md describes as small steps should all but vanish, and a window's colours
no longer depend on whether it holds the start.
