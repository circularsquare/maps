# T-011 — 90 m terrain, stage 1: design, pilot, downloader

2026-11-06. Folds in T-089 and T-081 (one world rebuild). Stage 1 changes no page code, no
`tools/build_tiles.py` and no served data. New: `tools/terrain/` (glo90.py, walk.py, cross.mjs,
plan.py, world.py), `data/terrain/plan.json`. Pilot scripts in `notes/T-011/`; their outputs in the
session scratchpad (`t011/ev/`, not kept).

## Answer

- **Rule** (no roads): each L1 cell gets four directed crossing times (E, W, S, N) from the 100 m
  directed walker. Each is its one-cell crossing (T-080's best crossing, margin 1) times its 3-cell
  strip's consistency ratio: the time across the cell and its two neighbours along the direction,
  over the sum of the three one-cell crossings. **3.0% mean, 7.9% worst** median error against the
  100 m walker on 17 tiles (ratio 1.01). The one-cell crossing alone is 7.7% / 19.6% and 8% too
  fast (1.08). Today's L1 is 19.2% / 35% and 19% too slow (0.81).
- **With roads**: the same on the grid with roads, with a strip lane 3 fine cells wider than the
  cell each side (a road may wander off the cell's rows), on cells with a road in their 3 x 3;
  elsewhere the no-roads value. **3.2% mean, 7.2% worst** (ratio 0.99); one-cell 3.9% / 8.9%;
  today 15.9% / 30%. Roads keep their own four directions: one road byte for all four is 3.5% /
  7.3% on average but 4.8% against 2.2% on the Saskatchewan section grid.
- **Directed is worth it** once the strip correction is in: one symmetric value a cell is 6.6% /
  18.5% (no roads), and mountain trips uphill and downhill come out even under the rule (ratio
  1.02 up, 0.99 down; one-cell 1.15 / 1.10). Before the correction, directed values only
  exploited the crossing's slack more.
- **What it moves**: mountains get faster, not slower. Today's L1 charges MAP's uphill slope
  factor on every 1 km cell in every direction; the 100 m walker contours, keeps to valleys and
  descends faster. Mountain tiles' times become 0.64-0.83 of today's without roads (mean 0.72),
  0.69-0.91 with (0.80); hills 0.84-1.03; flat land 0.82-0.89 (T-087's land cover mosaic floor
  removed again). Per tile below.
- **World**: 18,984 tiles, ~70-75 GB streamed at the ~7 MB/s measured here (about 3 h). Compute
  is the bottleneck: ~50 core-hours at today's machine load (the core ledger full), perhaps half
  that on a quiet machine, so 6-12 h on the 4 isochrone cores. Disk peak ~6 GB; kept: L1 planes
  ~1.9 GB, elevation for the fine level ~2.4 GB (3 GB budget).

## 1. Walking on a slope (the 100 m walker)

`tools/terrain/walk.py` `move_costs`, `cross.mjs` the same.

- **Slope**: directed Tobler normalised to the flat (research/friction.md 2),
  `exp(-3.5 (|g + 0.05| - 0.05))` on the move's grade g from its two end cells (a knight move's
  too): 1 on the flat, 1.19 at 5% down, 0.35 at 30% up, 0.50 at 30% down. Steeper than 45 degrees
  (|g| > 1) is not walked.
- **Height**: MAP's factor `1.016 exp(-0.0001072 h)` at the mean height of the move's end cells
  (the geometric mean of theirs, as it is exponential).
- **Graded cells**: a move between two cells that both have a walkable way or bridge (roads grid)
  or are urban takes its grade clipped to 10% (ROAD_GRADE, T-084; docs/fine.md stage 2).
- **Cost**: length x the mean hours per km of the cells it touches (two, four for a knight move,
  the page's rule) / (height x slope factor). Cells: LC100 class speeds; with roads a walkable way
  makes a land cell at least 5 km/h and a walkable bridge makes a water cell 5 km/h; water blocks.
- **Tobler capped at 1** (no faster than flat on gentle descents) was tried: it moves truth and
  L1 alike and changes the fit little (one-cell rule 7.7% to 6.7% without roads, 3.9% to 3.5% with,
  17 tiles). The full curve stays, as research/friction.md recommends.

## 2. GLO-90 on the fine grid

GLO-90 is pixel-is-point (`AREA_OR_POINT=Point`): its posts sit on multiples of 1/1200 degree,
the corners of the fine cells, not their centres (n46e023's bounds start at 22.99958 E, 47.00042 N:
half a post out). A fine cell's height is bilinear at its centre, the mean of its four corner posts
south of 50 degrees; north of it the posts are 1.5, 2, 3, 4 times wider in longitude (800, 600,
400, 300 columns at 50, 60, 70, 80 N) and the same bilinear fills the 1200 columns
(`glo90.read_fine`). A tile holds no south post row or east column (those are the next tiles');
the last fine row and column repeat the tile's last posts, a half-post smoothing on 1 row in 1200.
Ocean posts in coastal tiles are 0 m. GLO-90 is a surface model: tree and roof tops (the grade
cap keeps towns flat; forest edges are steps of 10-30 m, part of what the walker pays).

## 3. The crossing rule

T-080's best crossing (search inside the cell plus a margin, from the fine line just outside to
the far edge, least arrival time over the width) leaves each cell free to choose where it is
entered and left. Chained cell to cell at L1 that freedom is credited again at every cell edge:
T-087 saw it as a 3% fast bias in roadless country; on 90 m terrain, with much more variation
inside a cell, it is 7-20% in mountains (Sogndal 1.20, Valais 1.16, Annapurna and Mont Blanc 1.13).

**Strip consistency.** For each direction, a second crossing over the cell and its neighbours
before and after it (3 cells along, the cell's rows plus the margin across; source and target the
same rows). Its time is what three cells cost walked consistently; the three one-cell crossings'
sum is what they cost each chosen freely. Their ratio (at least about 1) scales the cell's own
one-cell crossing: `x = x1 * x3 * 3 / (x1(prev) + x1 + x1(next))`. The cell keeps its own
character (a road or a valley in it), the strip removes most of the slack. Where a neighbour is
not crossed (water, a tile edge without data) the cell keeps its one-cell value.

## 4. The per-cell product and the page

**Stored by the run** (`data/terrain/l1/<tile>.bin.gz`, `walk.tile_l1`): 8 planes of 120 x 120
uint8 km/h x 40 (0 = not crossed): no roads E, W, S, N, then with roads E, W, S, N; ~100 KB a
tile gzipped, ~1.9 GB for the world. Roads are solved only on cells with a walkable way or bridge
in their 3 x 3 (40% of land cells); elsewhere the with-roads planes repeat the plain ones.

**For the page** (stage 3, build_tiles.py and js/; designed here, not built):

- `l1/` speed byte: the geometric mean of the four no-roads directions, as now one number per
  cell for everything that wants one (land hazard per km, paint, corridor speed classes).
- `r1/` road byte: unchanged meaning (time with roads over without, x 250), from the geometric
  means; `applyRoads` unchanged.
- new `d1/`: four int8 planes, `32 ln(v_dir / v)`, each direction against the speed byte (3%
  steps, |value| <= 1 written 0); new `e1/`: four int8 planes of the with-roads directions against
  the no-roads ones, fetched only when roads are on. L3 `d3/`, `e3/`; L10 `grid_dir.bin.gz`,
  `grid_dir_roads.bin.gz`. Sizes on the pilot tiles, gzipped a 1 degree tile: today l1 + r1
  20.8 KB; speed 11.3 + d1 27.8 + e1 22.9 = 62 KB (x64 steps without the dead zone: 78 KB).
  World L1 ~530 MB against 176 MB now (T-016 publishing should weigh it).
- **js/search.js**, walking moves: the worker turns the planes into four hours-per-km arrays per
  level (Float32 x 4N). Move k from cell u leaves along (dx, dy) km; its east-west part uses E or
  W by the sign of dx, its north-south part S or N by the sign of dy, weighted by
  `wx = |dx| / (|dx| + |dy|)`, `wy = 1 - wx` (precomputed per row and move, like `stepKm`):
  `c(i) = wx s_x(i) + wy s_y(i)`, and the move costs `stepKm x mean of c over the cells it
  touches` (two, or four for a knight move), the same mean as now. With all four equal this is
  today's search exactly. The ellipse form `sqrt((s_x dx)^2 + (s_y dy)^2)` scored the same on the
  pilot to 0.1-0.2 points; the linear one has no square root in the inner loop. Hazard per km,
  carrying (x2 the directed walk), getting in and out are unchanged.
- **Corridors, route, speeds panel**: read the search's times as now; `edgeSpeeds` may keep the
  symmetric speed.

**L3 and L10 (T-081)**: the same rule one level up, on the directed L1 grid with the page's
directed move cost: each coarse cell's four directions with and without roads, by crossing the
L1 cells inside it with margin 1, scaled by the 3-cell strip, land by the quarter rule as now.
~1% of the L1 work (2.25 M L10 cells, ~20 M L3). Checked in stage 3 against L1 as T-080 did.

## 5. Engine

The crossing is solved for all cells of a tile at once by Gauss-Seidel sweeps over the blocks
(forward along the crossing, then back, each with passes both ways across): the same times as a
Dijkstra per block (checked on 200-300 random cells a tile, to 1e-7), one forward sweep already
exact for 99% of one-cell blocks, three sweeps leave 0.02% mean error on 3-cell strips (max 10% in
a few switchback blocks). T-087's Dijkstra per cell would be ~15-21 core-hours for one direction
pair and one grid more than this. `walk.py` (numpy) and `cross.mjs` (node) give the same times
(to 4e-7); node is ~1.5-2.5x faster on the strips and the run uses it.

Time per road-dense tile (`walk.tile_l1`, node, 2 sweeps), measured with the machine's core ledger
full (12 of 12 claimed), so a quiet machine should be faster, perhaps twice:

|---|---|---|---|
| costs, no roads / roads | 1.2 / 1.1 s | 1.4 / 1.2 s | |
| one-cell crossings, 4 directions, no roads | 1.6 s | 1.7 s | ~110 us |
| 3-cell strips, no roads | 3.4 s | 4.0 s | ~260 us |

World: 218 M land cells x ~370 us + 86.5 M road cells x ~410 us + ~3.4 s a tile of costs and

before stage 2, not tried: costs only for nodes inside land blocks (coastal tiles), a tighter
inner loop in cross.mjs (it runs ~7 ns a relaxation now; 2-3 is usual for such a loop), or the
cheaper road rule (one-cell with roads: 3.9% / 8.9%, ~20% less work). The 4-core isochrone cap is
Anita's; more cores while she is away would shorten it in proportion.

## 6. Stage 2: the run

`python isochrone/tools/terrain/world.py run --workers N` (N from `cores.py claim`), tested on a
3 x 3 block (n47-49, e024-026: 9 tiles fetched, computed, raw deleted, work files cleared, 108 s on
one worker under load).

- **Order**: `data/terrain/plan.json` (`plan.py`), north to south, west to east: 18,984 tiles
  with L1 land and a GLO-90 tile (8 land tiles have none), 218 M land cells, 86.5 M near roads,
  5,376 that keep elevation (4,671 town tiles by block mask, 158 water country, 822 mountains,
  the test regions).
- **Download**: `glo90.Stream`, 6 GETs at once, at most 2 GB downloaded and not yet used, each
  checked to leave 22 GB free (else it waits and logs, every 10 minutes); length checked, written
  to `.part` and renamed; 5 retries with backoff, then the tile is logged as failed and left for a
  rerun. Measured: 6.5-8 MB/s with 6 or 12 threads (the connection's limit, not the threads').
- **Per tile**: elevation at fine centres to `data/terrain/work/<tile>.npy` (decimetres), raw
  deleted at once; keep tiles' elevation to `data/terrain/elev/<tile>.bin.gz` (int16 m, column
  deltas, gzip 9; outside a town tile's blocks zeroed; 0.3-1.6 MB a whole tile). A tile is computed
  once it and its 8 neighbours have elevation (16 fine cells of halo, for the strips), on a pool;
  a work file is deleted when every plan tile next to it is done. Peak work files ~1-2 rows of
  tiles, under 1 GB.
- **Resume**: `data/terrain/done.txt`; a restart skips done tiles and fetches only what a tile
  still to do needs (itself and neighbours without a work file).
- **Liveness**: `data/terrain/world.log` (a line a tile: seconds, MB/s, free GB),
  `data/terrain/world.pid`; `python isochrone/tools/terrain/world.py status` prints both, the done
  count and the log's age.
- **Disk**: peak ~2 GB buffer + <1 GB work + outputs (1.9 + 2.4 GB at the end): ~6 GB.
- **After it** (stage 3): build_tiles.py reads the planes, writes the page format above and L3,
  L10 by crossing; the page's search goes directed; today's `data/tiles` kept as a backup to
  compare; re-measure the canoe's `cfg.access` (T-082), checks/ktrips.mjs and the smoke check.
