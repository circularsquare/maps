# T-036 working notes: unreached river water on canoe starts

2026-11-05. Rule: docs/canoe.md 1 ("River water off the line"); code: `buildCanoe` in
`js/canoe.js`. No data rebuild. The scripts named below were one-offs in the agent's scratchpad

## What the dark cells were

L1 water cells of kind 2 (river water) with no open river step, so no canoe node. Counted in
L1 windows (`diag.mjs`, cells of 1/120 degree):

| Window | Water | Nodeless | Line in the next cell | Only closed steps in or next to the cell | No step near (ponds) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phongsaly, Laos (21.7 N 102.1 E), 270 x 180 | 851 | 562 | 228 | 205 + 111 | 18 |
| Danube mouth (45.1 N 29.3 E), 270 x 150 | 19,494 | 232 | 29 | 0 | 188 + 15 beside a lake |

In Laos 72% of the river steps are class unrunnable (steeper than 5 m/km), so most of the dark
chains were rivers whose steps are all closed, not only lines that miss a cell. Every nodeless
cell was within 2 cells of land or a canoe node (patches under 5 km² are land already,
`tools/smallwater.py`).

## Rule chosen, and why not still water

Plain still water (lake edges between these cells) would paint them but make a current-free
channel beside the river: up the Amazon at 5 km/h where the line's own upstream is 3, and up an
unrunnable river's water where the model portages. So each cell is a dead end, depth = 8-neighbour
steps to the nearest land or canoe node; edges into it from a canoe node beside it (depth 1) or
a cell one depth less (len / 5 km/h, the flat-river hazard), getting in from the shore as any
water; out only by getting out. Deeper cells are next to no land, so a path that goes deeper
never comes out: the only routes through are in and out at one depth-1 cell (crossing river
water one cell wide, paying both 15 min transfers) or from a canoe node to the shore one cell on.

A first version with no getting out left every other node's time exactly unchanged, but left
islands in wide rivers unreached (and their ring of water: 73 of 6,061 new cells in the
Amazon window) and kept one-cell river water a wall for carrying; getting out was kept.
