# T-073 working notes: small rivers both ways

Question: is a small river too fast in the model, upstream (4.3 km/h at Q = 1) and downstream? The
records and their sources are in research/canoe.md 4 ("Small rivers"). This note compares them with
the model on the same rivers, on the served data (rivers rebuilt by T-031 today).

## Outcome

- **Up rapids is now 2.5 km/h on any river** (`js/canoe.js`, the upstream twin of a class-rapids
  step; `cfg.upMin` reused, no new number). Flat water upstream keeps max(p - 0.4 v, 2.5).
- **Downstream unchanged, no depth limit.** Q >= 1 already meets a canoe's least depth at mean flow.
- **Found, not fixed:** RiverATLAS lengths of small rivers are 1.27-1.47 x short (below), which makes
  them fast both ways. Needs a world-wide measure before a fix; proposed as a new task.

Reading:
- **Upstream on small and medium rivers the model was 2.5-3x too fast.** Every record is slower
  than 4 km/h; the accounts put the slowness in the riffles: the men wading and dragging (Lewis and
  Clark, Schoolcraft above Cass Lake, Mackenzie on the Bad River, Allagash Stream), poles up the
  rapids (Mackenzie, Thoreau). Still reaches of the same small streams were paddled (Thoreau's
  deadwater, Schoolcraft's "thread wound across a savannah valley").
- **The rapids class tells the two apart** with data the model already has, so the rule is
  per-step: rapids upstream at the poling speed, flat water as before. 2.5 km/h is the tracking and
  poling speed T-071 fitted on big rivers. The small-river records are lower still (1.2-1.4), but
  they are heavy dugouts or low water, while the model runs mean flow with a light canoe; and a
  slower rule would mostly hand the trip to carrying (below).
- **Downstream is close.** The Jefferson 1806 descent is within ~10% of the model on the published
  length. Schoolcraft's descent took ~2.3x the model's time: 1.47x of that is RiverATLAS's short
  length, the rest axe work at "almost every sudden bend", rapids where the men waded and led the
  canoes down, and a capsize. Logjams and beaver dams are not modelled.
- **No depth limit.** Hyra (1978) puts a canoe's least water at 0.15 m deep and 1.2 m wide at the
  shallowest section. Hydraulic geometry gives Q = 1 a mean depth of 0.27 m and width of 7.2 m, so
  the Q >= 1 cut is about where a loaded canoe still floats at mean flow. Schoolcraft's canoes ran
  the Itasca outlet, "ten to twelve feet broad ... twelve to eighteen inches" deep, RiverATLAS Q
  1.1, downstream fast in a wet July (the DNR recommends May water for it today). A depth limit
  would only matter in a low-water season, which the model does not have.

Up the Beaverhead the search now carries the canoe up the valley for most of the way: carrying on

slower upstream rule here, which is one reason not to go below 2.5.

## RiverATLAS lengths are short on small rivers

The model counts a reach's `LENGTH_KM` (docs/canoe.md 2), traced on the 15 arc-second network, so
meanders smaller than a few hundred metres are lost; a small river's meander wavelength is ~10-14
channel widths (70 m at Q = 1). Against published river miles (`reachtrips.py length`):

| Stretch | Q | RiverATLAS | Published | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mississippi, Itasca - Knutson Dam (MN DNR RM 1341.6-1258.2, with four lakes) | 1-13 | 92 km | 134 km | 1.47 |
| Beaverhead + Jefferson (69 + 83 mi) | 18-69 | 193 | 245 | 1.27 |
| Jefferson (83 mi) | 51-69 | 104 | 134 | 1.28 |
| Illinois (RM 273-0) | 176-3613 | 457 | 439 | 0.96 |
| Ohio (RM 0-981) | 967-7860 | 1611 | 1579 | 0.98 |
| Missouri, Three Forks - mouth (2,341 mi) | 119-2976 | 3766 | 3767 | 1.00 |

Whole-river lengths from source (`reachtrips.py whole`: DIST_UP_KM at the mouth against Wikipedia
lengths) are too noisy to use, since DIST_UP follows the longest branch: 0.54-1.37.

So small rivers are fast both ways by about the ratio, on top of the upstream question. Not fixed
here: three US stretches are not a world-wide factor, and a fix (a length factor by width, or
lengths from a finer network) touches every river step. Proposed task: measure RiverATLAS against a
finer network (MERIT Hydro's 3 arc-second lines, or OSM waterways) on a sample of rivers by size
and region, then scale small-river lengths in `rivers.py` or the worker.

## Thin spots

- Moving hours are guessed from day lengths; distances of the historical trips use today's
  published lengths.
- No small-canoe upstream log from the tropics was found (Amazon igarapés, Congo creeks).
- 60% of Q 1-10 river length is class rapids, and in the Amazon lowlands much of that is GMTED
  reading the forest canopy (docs/canoe.md 8): those streams are now poled upstream too, which may
  be right in the dry season and slow at high water.
