## Why

Routes traced at each level (`probe2.mjs`, Coari, 80 and 160 km, four bearings): at L10 they
paddle end to end with ~0.3 h of getting in and out; at L1 the same trips carry 5-19 h, plus
0.5-11 h of transfers. Two things in the coarse graph:

1. **Access for free.** A coarse cell with a river has one canoe node; getting in or out within the
   cell costs 15 minutes, so the whole 10 km cell is reached from its river, and a start anywhere
   in it is on the river.
2. **Rivers joined where they share a cell.** A cell had one canoe node for every river in it, so
   two rivers passing through one 10 km cell without meeting were joined there for free. At L10,
   246k of the 963k cells with river hold more than one separate piece of river; at L3, 203k of
   3.9 M.

- Pieces alone make some places faster (Utrecht, London, Sudbury): the old coarse rivers dropped
  one of two rivers on the same (cell, direction) and lost joins in rasterising; the contraction
  keeps every L1 join, and switching pieces through the cell's land node then costs only 30 min.
- **Access per piece**, not per cell, is what prices a switch between two rivers in one cell: the
  distance to the nearest water anywhere is near zero in a delta, the distance to that river is not.
- Painting a coarse river cell at its node's time plus the access (in place of the least of its
  nodes) changed medians by under 0.02, so it was left out.
- **Half the access** leaves the least bias in river country today. All of it is the cleaner
  idea (the land node is the whole cell, the river a line through it) but overshoots (Coari 0.88);
  part of that is the walking bias of T-080: access hours use the coarse cell's walking speed,
  which is ~20% slow (T-081 not applied), and carrying between rivers at L10 is slow too.

## Built

**Data** (`tools/canoe/rivers.py build`, `contract_level`): L1 from the reaches as before; L3 and
L10 are the L1 steps contracted. A piece is a run of L1 river cells in one coarse cell joined by
open L1 steps; each piece an open step touches gets a slot (0 the cell's own node: the wet pieces,
on a sea or lake node, or else the longest), a coarse step is an L1 step between two coarse cells,
piece to piece, length half of each piece's river plus the step's. Each end carries its slot and
its piece's access (mean straight distance from the cell's L1 land pixels, 0.1 km), as four uint8
columns after the old five (old parsers read on unchanged). 214 s, ~8 GB, as before.

| | L10 | L3 |
|---|---|---|
| steps | 1,241,769 (was 1,019,259) | 4,105,392 (was 3,629,861) |
| river km in steps | 6.84 M | 7.21 M (L1 8.44 M: half a piece is lost at each source and mouth) |
| file | 6.7 MB (was 4.1) | 20.8 MB, 906 tiles (was 15.0) |
| cells with river pieces / with several | 963k / 246k | 3.94 M / 203k |
| pieces off slot 0 (further nodes) | 336k | 228k |

**Worker**: `js/canoedata.js` reads the columns; `js/canoe.js` builds a cell's further river
nodes (`extra0`, `extraN`), river edges by node, each node's access (`acc`), and
`eachCanoeNode`/`canoeFrom`; `js/search.js` gets in to every canoe node of a cell, adds
`cfg.access` (0.5, `js/config.js`) x carry x access at the cell's walking speed to getting in or
out within the cell, reads river edges by node, and keeps further nodes off sea and lake grid
moves; `js/levels.js` (`sampleNode`), `js/windows.js` (ring seeds), `js/route.js` and
`js/corridors.js` (predecessor of a node in a cell with several, river km by node) follow, and so
does `checks/ktrips.mjs` (and `CANOE=<folder>/` for an A/B). With the old data the new code gives
exactly the old times (954 of 954 at Coari and Cluj). L10 canoe build 1.05 -> 1.10 s, nodes 0.76 M -> 1.17 M
beyond the cells; solve times unchanged (ktrips 0.43-0.92 s a start before and after).

**What is left**: (1) lake country and some coasts (Jyvaskyla 1.16, New York 1.15, Utrecht 1.08):
sea and lake water in one coarse cell is still one node, so lakes an isthmus parts at L1 are joined
and a river touching any of them joins them all. The same contraction applied to water bodies
(pieces of L1 sea and lake per coarse cell, their links piece to piece) is the general fix; it
needs grid edges between pieces, a bigger change than rivers. (2) L10 now ~10% slow in forest
(Kisangani 0.85, Coari 0.90): mostly the walking bias (Kisangani walks at 0.57 in T-080), which
T-081 would take out.

case gets a little worse (1.20), since walking round the lakes then costs less at L10 too.
