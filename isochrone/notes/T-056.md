# T-056 Corridor rays along grid directions

Done 2026-11-05. Rule: `docs/corridors.md` (Near-equal land parents); code `reparent()` in
`js/corridors.js`. The search, the time map and the hover route are unchanged.

## Cause

- **On truly uniform ground the search's tree is 16 spokes.** With every land cell set to one
  speed, the corridors from Ghat are exactly the 16 move directions: a destination between two
  move directions has many equal-cost paths (any order of the two moves), and the tree takes
  "all of one move, then all of the other", so a whole 22.5° sector rides one spoke and peels off
  in parallel strands of the other move.
- **On the real Sahara and steppe the ties are not exact, but nearly.** MAP speeds there vary a
  few % cell to cell (Ghat: 2.6-3.0 km/h within 600 km, roads and tracks at 3.1-5). Only 0.4% of
  nodes have two parents within 0.01% of the move's time, 24% within 1%. The trunks are not
  worth much: slowing every trunk cell (count over the threshold) by 5% moves 93% of trunk cells
  elsewhere and costs a median 0.8% of time (p99 2%). Their place is set by noise.
- **But routes there do bend.** A straight line from Ghat is a median 4.4% slower than the
  fastest route (p10 2%), from the Kazakh steppe 9.8%; a far route crosses the 300 km ring a
  median ~10° off its destination's bearing in both the old and new trees. So the fan is not
  even by bearing in the model, and the fix does not force it: it removes the grid bunching and
  parallel strands, not the real bends.

## Fix

Re-choose the parent of each land node among near-equal land parents (the search's move and the
one either side round the 16 directions): spread every trip over them in proportion to
(1 - slack / 3% of the node's time)², then each node takes the parent the most spread trips
pass through, keeping its route through the tree within 1% of the fastest. Everything else
(transfers, rivers, sea, seeds) keeps the search's parent.

Tried and dropped:
- Tie-break towards the straight line from the start (or a parent's anchor): any rule that
  looks only at the node pulls routes onto fixed attractor directions (the bisectors between
  moves, or the axes); in tests it made more straight parallel strands, not fewer.
- Spreading counts weighted by the number of lattice paths (Brandes style): with any tolerance
  the path counts swamp the time differences and trips pool into wide "rivers"; without it
  nothing changes on real data.
- Drawing the spread counts directly: a band of trips draws as many side-by-side lines.
- A streamline trace along the spread's mean direction: even fan on synthetic ground, but on
  real data it falls back to the old tree and routes ran up to 11% over.
- A per-move 2% bound instead of the 1% route budget: kept the Ghat south trunk dominant.
