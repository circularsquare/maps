# T-007 — coarse walking across water (fjords, rivers): stage 1, design

2026-11-07. Stage 1 changes no page code, tools or data. Scripts in `notes/T-007/`; their outputs
in the session scratchpad (`iso-t007/`, not kept). Plots: `maps/artifacts/isochrone/t007_l10_world.png`
(one pixel an L10 cell) and `t007_l10_crops.png` (Sogndal, Coari, Khulna, Jyvaskyla).

## In short

**What is wrong.** L10 and L3 cells are each one node. When a fjord, a river, a strait or a lake
cuts a cell's land in two, the node still joins both halves, so a coarse walk steps in on one
bank and out on the other where L1 has to go round, sometimes hundreds of km. Two smaller faults
point the same way: a direction a cell can't be crossed in is given the speed of the directions it
can, and two cells side by side are joined even when a river runs along the border between them.
Coarse times only ever come out too fast from this.

**How often.** Worldwide 41,777 L10 land cells of 2.23 M (1.9%) hold two or more pieces of land
that L1 keeps apart inside the cell, and 20,113 pairs of neighbouring L10 cells have no land across
their border. At L3 it is 42,356 cells of 24.5 M (0.17%) and 238,681 pairs. By the water in the cell
at L10: lakes 47%, sea inlets and fjords 29%, rivers 14%, straits 10%. They sit in fjord, lake and
delta country (Norway, Finland, Canada, the Amazon, the Sundarbans); elsewhere there are almost none.

**The fix: split a cell into its land pieces**, as T-088 did for the canoe's water. Every L1 land
piece of a coarse cell that touches a neighbour's land becomes its own node, with its own four
crossing speeds (crossed on that piece alone) and its own links: which pieces of each neighbour its
L1 cells touch across the border. Cells with one piece stay exactly as they are. A start goes in the
piece holding the L1 cell it snaps to.

**What it does** (`checks/levels.mjs`'s starts, roads on; median L1/coarse time and 10-90%):

**What it costs.** About 5 MB of new data in all (both levels, roads on and off; data/tiles is 735
MB). 52,000 more nodes at L10 (2.3%). The search stays as fast: an L10 click measured the same
within the machine's noise (median 174-308 ms against 155-332 ms on four starts), because a move
mask per cell replaces the land checks the search does now. The build's coarse pass gets up to
~30 min longer on 3 workers with the prototype's plain loops, less once tightened.

**Recommended**: build it (stage 2), at both coarse levels, every piece its own node. Measured
against it: blocking the directions a cell can't be crossed in leaves most of the error (Sogndal
1.26, Coari's tail 14); joining pieces that meet within the next cell needs a third of the nodes
but keeps a good part of the error (Sogndal 1.05 (0.87-1.25), Coari 1.06 (0.96-1.35)); keeping only each cell's
biggest piece strands starts on the small ones (Coari's town is on one). Portals would mean 10-100
times the data and a new search, for what is left after splitting (a few percent, the detours
inside one piece).

**Nothing here needs Anita's call** beyond going ahead. One visible effect: a split L10 cell is
painted one colour, its earliest piece's, so the far bank's part of a 10 km cell shows the near
bank's time until a finer window covers it (as now, but no longer leaking further).

## 1. The failure

L3 and L10 are made by crossing L1 (T-011, docs/resolution.md "Directions"): each coarse cell is
crossed E, W, S and N over its L1 cells, and a coarse cell is land when a quarter of its L1 cells
are. Three things join land that L1 keeps apart:

1. **Split cells.** The cell's L1 land (8-connected inside the cell) is in two or more pieces that
   each touch a coarse land neighbour's land. The node joins every neighbour to every other through
   the cell, so a walk enters on one piece and leaves from another. At Coari the Solimoes runs
   through a line of L10 cells and every one of them is a free crossing; at Sogndal it is the
   fjord and its arms.
2. **Directions not crossed.** A direction no path crosses takes the geometric mean of the ones
   that are, and a cell crossed in no direction takes the mean rule, the same every way. A cell cut
   by an east-west river is then crossed north-south at the speed of walking along it. In the
   tiles round levels.mjs's 25 starts (10,090 split L10 cells) 40% of split cells are crossed in
   all four directions anyway (each piece crosses some), 51% miss some (S and N together 3,447, E
   and W 771, the rest one or three), 9% none. At L3 (8,784 split cells): 30%, 49%, 21%. So
   blocking the missing directions (option A) can reach at most about half the split cells.
3. **Borders with no land across.** Two coarse land cells side by side whose L1 land doesn't touch
   across the shared border (a river along it, or for a diagonal, water at the corner and on both
   side cells). The move joins them anyway.

**How bad**: a split cell is a fault only where L1 can't join its pieces close by. "Barrier" below
is the stricter count: the pieces are not joined inside the 3 x 3 coarse cells round the cell. The
prototype (section 4) shows that the milder ones still matter (merging them leaves Sogndal at 1.05,
Coari's 90th percentile at 1.35).

## 3. The options

- **A. Block the directions a cell is not crossed in** (a reserved direction byte; no new data, no
  search change). Fixes fault 2 only, where a cell has some direction it can't be crossed in. A
  cell crossed in no direction can't be blocked without making its land unreachable (**A2** makes
  such cells water).
- **B. Split cells into their land pieces** (T-088's shape). Each piece is a node with its own four
  crossings, made with the cell's other pixels blocked (margin and neighbours as they are, and its
  3-cell strip scaled by the neighbours' one-cell crossings as now); a piece crossed in no direction
  takes the mean rule over its own pixels. Links per piece and plain move: the neighbour's pieces
  its L1 cells touch across the border (8-neighbour pixels; a diagonal through the corner pixels, or
  through a common side cell's piece by two straight links). Fixes faults 1 and 3; fault 2 goes
  with 1, since each piece is crossed alone. Variants: every piece a node (**B**); pieces that join
  within the 3 x 3 block one node (**B merged**, a third of the nodes); only the biggest piece
  (**B lite**, no further nodes, just links).
- **C. Portals** (HPA*, docs/resolution.md "Thin barriers"): crossing points on each cell edge with
  L1 times between them. One portal per land run on an edge is ~4 nodes and ~50 directed costs a
  cell: ~110 M costs at L10 against 2.2 M cells now, and paths that bend at edge midpoints (an
  8-neighbour metric, up to ~8% slow on diagonals, where the 16-move grid is ~1-2%) unless each
  edge gets several portals. Used only in split cells, it is B plus where on the edge a piece is
  entered. Not prototyped: what it adds over B is the detours inside one piece, and after B those
  are within the spread every kind of land has (section 4).
- **D. Finer cells where split**: an L10 cell with pieces replaced by its 100 L1 cells. 4.2 M more
  L10 nodes (2.9x the graph) for the split cells alone. Not prototyped.

## 5. Search cost

`p7.mjs` runs a copy of `solveWalk` with one 32-bit mask a node: bits 0-15 the 16 moves to the
neighbour's main piece (a knight move needs the four straight links through the two cells it
passes, as the canoe's sea moves do), bit 16 that the node has listed edges to further pieces
(kept in a small map, as T-088's water links). The mask replaces the search's `grid[v]` and
knight-cell land checks. Its answers equal a plain copy of `solveWalk` exactly.

| L10 click, node, median of 9 (ms) | Jyvaskyla | Coari | Cluj | Sogndal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| js/search.js today (one run of 7) | 615 | 190 | 467 | 213 |
| the copy, no patch | 317 | 155 | 332 | 168 |
| the copy, B patched round 6 starts | 308 | 183 | 303 | 174 |

The machine was loaded (the core ledger at 14 of 16), so only the two lower rows compare: the same
within noise. Making the masks for the world L10 takes 0.5-1.0 s in node, once a session; the build
can write them instead. Per window (L3, L1 untouched) only the listed cells need masks. A first
version that looked up pieces per move (no mask) was 30-35% slower: the mask is the part that
matters. Memory: +4 bytes a node (30 MB at L10; or a 1-byte flag and the mask only for listed
cells).

## 6. Data and build

Measured on the prototype's own layout (`size.py`, gzipped): an L10 split cell is ~25 bytes (its
pieces' four speed bytes and eight link masks each, and its 100-pixel piece map at 4 bits a
pixel), a cell whose links only change ~2.3 bytes; L3 ~13 and ~2 bytes. World, one roads setting:
L10 ~1.1 MB of split cells and ~0.2 MB of changed links, L3 ~0.6 + ~0.7 MB: about 2.6 MB, so ~5 MB
for roads on and off (the with-roads pieces differ near bridges, so both are kept, like d and e).

Build: `proto.py` takes 2-6 s a tile per level and roads setting, including the full crossing the
build already does; the pieces, links and masked crossings of split pieces are ~1-2 s of it in
plain Python loops over cells. `count.py`, much the same labelling for both levels and both roads
settings, took ~85 min for the world on one core. So as written ~+30 min on 3 workers over pass
2's 19 min (notes/T-011.md); doing the per-cell loops only for cells next to water, or in numpy,
should take most of that away.

## 7. Stage 2 (what changes where)

- `tools/build_tiles.py` pass 2: pieces, links and per-piece crossings per coarse tile, roads off
  and on (the roads structure on the land with bridges); written as a pieces file per level like
  `data/canoe/pieces*` (L10 whole world, L3 per tile), only split cells and cells with changed
  links. Unsplit cells' bytes unchanged, so the old page reads the new data as today.
- `js/tiles.js`, `js/levels.js`: load the pieces with the level and the roads setting; the move
  mask (or flag); further nodes after the cells (as the canoe's `extraN`, `extra0`); per-piece
  four hours per km beside `L.sd`.
- `js/search.js`: both loops read the mask; further nodes walk plain moves only, from the listed
  edges. The canoe loop carries over the same land graph, so the canoe's coarse carrying stops
  crossing fjords too; a further land node gets in and out like its cell's land node.
- `js/levels.js sampleNode` and `snap`: a split cell's node by the piece holding the L1 point
  (the piece map); `cellTimes`: a cell's time is its earliest piece's (paint and hover).
- `js/route.js`, `js/corridors.js`: pred codes for moves into or out of a further piece (codes
  42-169 are free: 42 + 8 x piece + move), or T-088's pick-the-edge-that-gives-the-weight rule.
- `checks/levels.mjs`: read a destination's own piece (as p7.mjs); smoke, corridors, ktrips.
- The canoe: docs/canoe.md 8 says coarse walking across the fjord makes Sogndal's canoe ~5% fast
  where it would be ~13% slow; after this it should go slow there (T-092's access question). Not
  measured here.
