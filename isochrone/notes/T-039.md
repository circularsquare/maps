# T-039 — fine level stage 2: GLO-90 heights and directed slope at L0.1

2026-11-06. The rule is in docs/fine.md ("What a tile holds", "Terrain"); this note has the
measurements. The bucket queue of stage 2 is T-093.

## What changed

- `.land` tiles hold GLO-90 heights (whole metres, int16, plane deltas) in place of the 120 x 120
  terrain factor; `index.json` carries `elev` and walk.py's `SLOPE_RULE` (`tools/fine/build.py`,
  heights from T-011's `data/terrain/elev/`, so no download).
- The page: `js/tiles.js fineDecode` gives speeds without terrain, heights and graded cells;
  `js/levels.js setSlope`; `js/search.js` costs each L0.1 move with walk.py's rule in both loops.
  walk.py and cross.mjs read the same named constants (`SLOPE_RULE`), so the page, the L1 walker
  and its node twin share one set of numbers.
- `?fine=<name>` reads `data/fine_<name>/` (cfg.fineBase); with roads off the fine tiles no longer
  fetch or fold in their roads (they did before: the L0.1 level ignored `cfg.roads`; the page has
  no roads switch yet, so only the checks see it).
- `checks/fine.mjs`: the seam check below, kept as a standing check.
- Info panel: the GLO-90 credit is now the licence's own wording for modified data plus its
  no-liability sentence (research/friction.md, "GLO-90 licence"). LC100's CC BY credit was there
  already.
- Layout: plane deltas (`z - w - n + nw`) gzip 15% smaller than deltas along rows (n46e023 0.90
  against 1.08 MB, n40w074 0.20 against 0.22, n47e011 0.74 against 0.88; byte planes split
  helped rows a little and plane deltas not at all).

## The page against walk.py

The page's modules in node over the whole of n46e023 (1200 x 1200) from Cluj centre, against
walk.py's `move_costs` and scipy's Dijkstra on the same classes, roads and heights: t(page) /
t(walk.py) median 1.0003 (1-99%: 0.9998-1.0007, worst 0.9993-1.0010) with roads, 1.0009
(0.9998-1.0014) without; every cell reached by both. The differences are the speed bytes (km/h x
40) and the 0.1% grade steps of the Tobler table. Both search loops (`solveWalk` and the general
`solve` with zero hazard) give the same.

walk.tile_l1 after its constants were named gives n46e023's planes again: 98.7% of inner bytes
equal or one step off, the rest from reading heights in whole metres (elev/) where the run read
decimetres.
