# T-043 lake water temperature

Done 2026-11-07. Rule: docs/canoe.md 3 and 4; source and licence: research/canoe.md 2.

## What was wrong

The task line said lakes take the nearest sea's summer temperature. That held only for lakes
with no ERA5 water point. ERA5's SST field has values at every ERA5 water point, inland lakes
included (FLake's mix-layer temperature on most lakes, something else on the Great Lakes), and
sea.py averages the four ERA5 points round each 0.25° cell before filling gaps from the nearest
cell. So every big lake in the brief already read its own ERA5 temperature (one test hour,
2010-07-15 12 UTC, `sst` = `lmlt` to 0.1 C on Great Bear, Great Slave, Baikal, Ladoga, Victoria,
Titicaca, Malawi, Winnipeg, Issyk-Kul; Superior 5.9 against 13.8, Michigan 15.9 against 17.7).

The gap was lakes with no ERA5 water point in their cell, mostly 100-2,000 km²: they took
whatever water the fill reached first, often far away. Tibetan and Pamir lakes read 16-26 C,
Yellowstone Lake 24 C.

## Change

`sea.bin.gz` gets a sixth band, lake temperature: the four-point summer SST where ERA5 has
one, else the four-point summer mean of `lmlt` (ERA5 has it at every point), with no fill from
elsewhere. The worker (js/canoe.js) reads it for lake cells and lake water units; sea cells keep
`sst`. Every lake takes it, not only those over 100 km²: lakes under ~350 km² have Hs under 0.5 m,
where the wave rate is 0 and the cold factor changes nothing, so a size cut would have no effect.
Bands 1-5 came out byte-identical to the old file (the drifter files were fetched again for the
rebuild and deleted after).

    python tools/canoe/sea_fetch.py era5lake 2     # 601 chunks, 0.97 GB, 135 s
    python tools/canoe/sea.py [--out DIR]
