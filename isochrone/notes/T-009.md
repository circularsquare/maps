## Agreement with MAP (off-road)

`compare.py`, second run (the table as now in `common.py`). Pixels where MAP is exactly
5.000 km/h are roads (MAP's walking surface puts every road at 5 km/h) and left out, as is
water on either side: 49.3M road pixels out of 214.3M land pixels both sides call land, leaving
165.0M off-road. Ratio = ours / MAP.

| Group | n (M) | median | p10 | p90 | within 10% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all off-road | 165.0 | 1.003 | 0.72 | 1.18 | 53% |
| same land cover speed both sides (terrain only) | 53.4 | 1.003 | 0.997 | 1.017 | 100% |
| different land cover speed | 39.4 | 0.98 | 0.67 | 1.24 | 17% |

**Terrain reproduces MAP.** Where both sides give a pixel the same land cover speed, the
elevation and slope factors from GMTED 30" agree within 2% at the 90th percentile. So the
formulas, GMTED and 4-neighbour slope at 1 km are what MAP did. Every larger difference is
land cover.

Water: MAP land that we call water 4.2M pixels (2.0% of land), MAP water we call land 2.8M
(1.3%). MAP draws rivers as lines (CIA World Data Bank II) and keeps lakes only when a pixel is
fully water; ours is water at half cover. So rivers narrower than ~500 m that MAP draws as
unbroken lines (Amazon tributaries in t009_amazon.png) come out broken or missing in ours,
and become walkable; T-028 has to settle the water rule (HydroRIVERS lines, as the canoe will
use, are the likely fix).

## Why they disagree

- **Land cover vintage and classifier**: MODIS 500 m (MCD12Q1, around 2015-2019) against
  LC100 100 m (2019). Grass against open shrub and savanna against forest flip often, at
  4.2 / 4.86 / 3.24 km/h, giving the 0.67-1.24 spread on "different land cover speed".
- **Wetland**: LC100 maps herbaceous wetland (Sudd, Okavango edges, Siberian bogs) that MODIS
  calls grassland, so ours is 2.0 km/h where MAP has 4.2-4.86. Ours is the more correct; kept.
- **Greenland**: MAP has 1.62 km/h flat over Greenland's ice with no elevation or slope factor
  (Iceland and Patagonia ice do get it). We apply the factors, so at 2,000-3,000 m we are
  0.73-0.78 of MAP. Ours follows MAP's stated recipe; kept.
- **Resolution**: a 1 km pixel holds ~70 LC100 pixels against ~4 MODIS ones; mixed pixels in
  ours are blends by crossing time, MAP's are one class. Cropland edges (class 40 p10 0.52)
  are mostly this.
- **Tibet and the Himalaya** (t009_himalaya.png): on the plateau (85-90 E, 29-30.5 N, median
  0.91) a fifth of the off-road pixels are LC100 bare (3.0) where MODIS has grass (4.86). In the
  steep high ranges a third of the pixels have a MAP speed that fits no table value over our
  terrain factor: there the 1 km slope is sensitive to how the elevation was sampled (GMTED
  mean, bilinear, half a pixel shifted here; whatever Earth Engine did for MAP). T-011
  replaces this slope anyway.
- **Australia** ours ~13% faster (120-140 E, 30-20 S): two thirds of the pixels are LC100
  herbaceous (4.86) where MODIS has open shrubland (4.2).

## Table changes made from this check

The first run used the draft table in research/friction.md 3. Classes with no MODIS match
were set to what MODIS mostly gives the same pixels: shrubs 3.6 -> 4.2, open deciduous
broadleaf and open unknown forest 4.0 -> 4.86, moss and lichen 4.2 -> 4.86. Land with no
LC100 class (north of 80 N, and class 0 over Svalbard and some Arctic islands, which had come
out as water) is now snow and ice where GMTED is above 0 m. Effect: off-road within 10% from
48% to 53%, "different land cover speed" median 0.86 -> 0.98, MAP land we called water
6.1M -> 4.2M pixels.
