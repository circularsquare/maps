# T-030 — a 100 m level: measurements

Working notes for `docs/fine.md`. Probe scripts were in the session scratchpad (`t030/`) and
are not kept; everything they found is here. 2026-10-26.

## LC100 at 100 m round New York and Cluj

1 x 1 degree windows (New York 74.5-73.5 W, 40.2-41.2 N; Cluj 23.1-24.1 E, 46.3-47.3 N),
LC100 native 1/1008 degree, 1008 x 1008 pixels.

| Class | New York | Cluj |
|---|---|---|
| 50 urban | 31.2% | 3.0% |
| 200 sea | 26.8% | 0 |
| 114 closed deciduous broadleaf | 16.3% | 27.2% |
| 126 open forest, unknown | 11.6% | 20.2% |
| 116 closed forest, unknown | 6.6% | 1.3% |
| 80 inland water | 3.0% | 0.09% |
| 40 cultivated | 1.2% | 29.8% |
| 30 herbaceous | 1.6% | 7.7% |
| others | 1.7% | 10.7% |

- **Streets are not in LC100.** A city is one class (50); streets, blocks and parks under ~100 m
  are all inside it.
- **Bridges are water.** Brooklyn, George Washington, Queensboro and Verrazzano bridges: every
  pixel along the span is class 80 or 200. Without a road layer, Manhattan at 100 m is an island.
- **Narrow rivers are not water.** The Somes through Cluj (30-50 m wide) is almost absent:
  0.09% inland water in the whole tile.
- Resampled to 1/1200 degree (nearest), one class byte a cell: 1.44 MB raw, **161 KB gzipped
  (New York), 204 KB (Cluj)** per 1 degree tile.

## OSM roads at 100 m

Overpass, all `highway=*` ways with geometry in 0.1 x 0.1 degree boxes (Brooklyn and lower
Manhattan 40.66-40.76 N, 74.02-73.92 W: 40.5 MB of JSON, 36 s; Cluj centre 46.72-46.82 N,
23.54-23.64 E: 9.0 MB, 42 s), rasterised to 1/1200 degree cells (any segment through a cell
marks it). Kinds: path = footway, path, steps, track, bridleway, pedestrian, cycleway;
local = residential, service, unclassified, living street, tertiary; main = secondary to trunk;
motorway.

| | New York box | Cluj box |
|---|---|---|
| ways (path / local / main / motorway) | 56,686 / 10,380 / 4,525 / 843 | 6,754 / 6,797 / 1,179 / 0 |
| km (path / local / main / motorway) | 2,942 / 1,237 / 388 / 134 | 585 / 738 / 111 / 0 |
| land cells with a walkable way | 97.4% | 67.2% |
| urban (LC 50) cells with a walkable way | 98.4% | 91.2% |
| non-urban land cells with a walkable way | 86.5% | 48.5% |
| land cells with only a path, no road | 4.5% | 16.5% |
| L1 (1 km) cells with a walkable way | 99.3% | 100% |
| water cells a walkable way crosses | 332 of 2,207 (107 tagged bridge) | 6 of 6 |
| road byte raster, gzip | 4.3 KB (0.43 MB per 1° if all city) | 3.9 KB |

- New York mapped its sidewalks: footways are 80% of the ways. So in a city nearly every 100 m
  cell has a street, and urban land cover is already 5 km/h, the same as a road in MAP's model:
  **in a city roads add almost nothing to walking time at 100 m except bridges.** Outside cities
  they matter: half of Cluj's non-urban cells have a way, and cropland is 2.5 km/h off it.
- Most of the 332 water cells a way touches are shore promenades, piers and banks that spill into
  a cell LC100 calls water. Only bridge-tagged ways may turn a water cell into a crossing.
- 1 degree counts (Overpass `out count`): New York 1,046,880 highway ways, 15,448 bridges, 3,755
  tunnels, 111 ferry routes. Cluj 53,408 ways, 2,571 bridges, 303 tunnels, 0 ferries. A 1 degree
  New York tile by Overpass would be ~1-2 GB of JSON: Overpass is fine for 0.1 degree checks,
  not for tiles.

## GLO-90

- **No CORS** on `copernicus-dem-90m.s3.amazonaws.com` (a ranged GET with an Origin header
  answers 206 with no `Access-Control-Allow-Origin`), so the browser cannot read it directly.
  The LC100 COG mirror at nrp-nautilus.io does send `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`.
- Tile sizes (1 x 1 degree COG, float32): N40 W074 (New York, half sea) 1.9 MB; N40 W075
  5.6 MB; N46 E023 (Cluj, Apuseni) 5.3 MB; N45 E007 (Alps) 5.2 MB; N27 E086 (Everest) 5.3 MB.
- As a browser tile, int16 metres, 1200 x 1200:

| Tile | gzip | row deltas, gzip | row deltas, brotli 11 | xz |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| N40 W074 New York | 0.31 MB | 0.27 | 0.23 | 0.22 |
| N46 E023 Cluj | 1.68 | 1.18 | 0.98 | 0.96 |
| N45 E007 Alps | 1.99 | 1.38 | 1.14 | 1.12 |

- **It is a surface model.** Midtown Manhattan (40.74-40.77 N, 74.00-73.97 W) has elevations
  -1 to 171 m and 40% of 90 m steps steeper than 10% grade: the towers. Low-rise Brooklyn: 0.9%
  of steps over 10%. Cluj centre: 8.7%. Forest edges round Cluj: a step from field into forest
  rises ~3.7 m on average (4% over 90 m), small.

## Solve times at 100 m

The search as committed before T-022 (binary heap, 16 moves), in node 22 on one core, on
LC100 speeds at 1/1200 degree round Cluj (all land, so every cell is reached), median of 3.
"Directed" adds MAP-style directed Tobler from a lookup table on the grade of each move
(elevation from the Cluj GLO-90 tile, mirrored to fill the grid).

| Window | Cells | Heap, plain | Heap, directed | Bucket queue, plain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 850 x 425 | 0.36 M | 163 ms | 233 ms | |
| 1463 x 731 | 1.07 M | 500-633 ms | 979 ms | 390 ms |
| 1710 x 855 | 1.46 M | 930 ms | 1139 ms | |
| 2000 x 1000 | 2.00 M | 1090-1307 ms | 1491 ms | 704 ms |
| 2922 x 1461 | 4.27 M | 2532-3001 ms | 5245 ms | 1968 ms |
| 3418 x 1709 | 5.84 M | 4596 ms | 5645 ms | |

- ~0.6 µs a cell with the heap, in line with L1's 0.85-1.5 s for 2 M cells in headless Chrome.
- Directed costs 15-90% more (one table lookup and a division a move).
- A bucket queue (Dial's, buckets 0.01 h wide, under the shortest 100 m move at 5 km/h of
  0.0126 h) is ~1.3-1.6x faster and agreed with the heap to 0.3% (max 0.02 h). It helps every
  level, not only 100 m.
- The same runs round New York reached only 31 k to 676 k of the cells from midtown: water
  without bridges cuts the start off.

## Hex grids, the arithmetic

The worst extra length of a straight line walked on a grid is 1 / cos(half the largest angle
between allowed moves): squares with 8 moves 8.2% (45° gaps), squares with 16 moves 2.7%
(26.6°), hexes with 6 moves 15.5% (60°), hexes with 12 moves 3.5% (30°).
