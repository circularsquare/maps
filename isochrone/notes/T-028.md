## Water rule

Measured over the world, 1 km pixels, against MAP's mask (km²): both land / MAP land only / ours
only / both water.

| Our water at LC100 water share >= | MAP land, ours water | MAP water, ours land |
|---|---|---|
| 0.25 | 3.13 M | 1.62 M |
| 0.5 (chosen) | 1.97 M | 1.68 M |
| 0.75 | 1.28 M | 1.73 M |

- **Half or more** is what `walk_today.tif` uses and what a 100 m view shows as more water than
  land. 0.75 agrees more with MAP, but where they differ MAP is often the one wrong:
- **MAP's "water" on land**: MAP gives exactly 0.06 min/m to water and also to land as slow as
  1 km/h, so steep and icy ground read as water (Greenland's ice margin 67-70 N: MAP 36% land in a
  tile, ours 99.7%); and it misses the dry Aral bed (44-45 N 59 E: 3,400 km²). Our speeds on
  MAP's 0.06 pixels: 1.68 M km², most of it 1.25-3 km/h.
- **LC100's sea where there is land**: LC100 has some islands and coasts as class 200 outright:
  Samoa, Tahiti, Kerguelen, South Georgia and the Society and Marquesas islands as sea in whole
  tiles, and much of the Chilean archipelago, the Sundarbans, Chongming (Shanghai), Raja Ampat,
  south-west Korea's islands and the Bothnian archipelago in part. A pixel 98% sea or more in
  LC100 with GMTED above 0 m: 164k km² (MAP land on 136k); above 2 m: 98k km² (MAP land on 87k,
  89%). Most of the rest at 1-2 m is GMTED's bilinear edge on sea next to land. Chosen: above
  2 m, walked at 3 km/h (LC100's bare or sparse, the middle of the table) times the terrain factor;
  no MAP data needed.
- **Small water** is T-013's rule on our water: 8-connected patches under 5 km², 1 km/h.
- **Coarse cells**: the quarter rule, unchanged. L10 lost 21,146 land cells and gained 1,722
  (of 2.25 M): mostly coast and island fringe cells now under a quarter land, since LC100's 100 m
  coast is tighter than MAP's MODIS one (Aegean, Philippines, Patagonia, the Canadian Arctic,
  north Greenland); gains are Greenland's ice margin and the Aral bed.

## What depends on the land/water mask

Canoe water (`tools/canoe/water.py`, data/canoe) and the river build (`rivers.py`, its
`load_water`) read the speed tiles' water, and the land hazard band (`tools/hazard/build.py`)
reads MAP's mask directly. None were rebuilt; the mismatch, in cells:

| | L10 | L3 | L1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| our land | 2,225,607 | 24,463,173 | 218,158,332 |
| our water, canoe land | 372 | 38,808 | 1,693,026 |
| our land, canoe sea or lake | 134,442 | 488,263 | 1,510,247 |
| our land, canoe river water | 833 | 21,890 | 1,733,371 |
| new land with no land hazard | 1,722 | 36,560 | 1,932,128 |
| ... next to a hazard code over 160 | 0 | 5 | 245 |

How the search takes each (`js/canoe.js`, `js/search.js`):

- **Our water, canoe land**: `buildCanoe` makes the cell a sea canoe node (kind 0 off the speed
  grid becomes sea), shore 0. At L1 links are implicit, so it joins the water round it; at L3 and
  L10 it has no links, so it is a dead end reached by getting in from the land beside it. On a
  walking start it is water, as intended.
- **Our land, canoe sea or lake**: a land node and a canoe node in one cell, as the straits MAP
  walked over always were. **Our land, canoe river water**: a land node only. Painted as water on
  canoe starts (PAINT_WATER follows the canoe kind).
- **No holes**: unreached cells with a node and 6 or more of 8 neighbours reached, before and
  after, from Constanta, Shanghai, Dhaka, Belem, Nukus (Aral) and Nuuk, walking and canoe, at L10
  (world) and in 2.4 x 3.2 degree boxes at L1 and L3 (Danube delta, Shanghai, Sundarbans, Aral,
  Nuuk): L10 0-56 such cells a start before, 0-51 after, at the edges of the time and survival
  limits as before; the L1 and L3 canoe boxes reach every node before and after (one L3 cell in
  the Sundarbans now unreached among reached ones). No stranded cells.
- **Hazard**: cells that were MAP water have code 0 (no land hazard). Almost none border serious
  hazard (245 L1 cells next to a code over 160, a rate of 1% an hour); Greenland walks got less
  safe, not more (below). Rebuilding the band on our land is a one-line change in
  `tools/hazard/build.py` (`land` from our L1, as `build_tiles.py` makes it) and a ~7 min run;
  canoe water (`water.py`, ~11 min, 6 GB) and the river steps after it. Not done here (not this
  task's files).

## Road byte as built

As docs/roads.md designed, with one change: a water cell's byte is its bridge's speed over 5 km/h
x 250 instead of the single code 255, so the bridge keeps its terrain factor (5 km/h x terrain, as
at 100 m) without a second band. On land, 250 (no change, towns) is stored as 0. The worker folds
the byte into the uint8 speed at load (`applyRoads`): speed x 250 / byte on land, byte x 0.8 on
water. Carrying the byte cost 67 MB of files and two fetches a tile position; the fold is one pass
over a window (a few ms at 2 M cells). Baking roads in would save the bytes but make "no roads"
a second set of tiles; not worth it.

Bridges at L1 (walking, hazard off; roads on / off): the Bosporus 2.9 h / 11.4 h (at L1 it is
cut at its narrowest, where pixels fall under half water); Manhattan to Staten Island 5.1 h /
35 h; Brooklyn to Staten Island 5.3 h / not reached (the Verrazzano is not walkable in OSM, the
walk goes round by New Jersey).

## Time changes

**L1, rural walks** (the T-080 destinations, 10-80 km, L1 after / before): Cluj 1.28 (1.15-1.37),
Beclean 1.31 (1.24-1.37), Middletown NY 1.11 (1.06-1.22), Midtown 1.03 (1.01-1.09). T-047 had
100 m 1.20-1.52 slower than MAP's L1 on the Romanian tiles and 1.05-1.22 round New York: this is
that gap closing.

**L10 by region** (median cell time with roads, new / old): Romania 1.13, north-east US 1.17,
India 1.20, Siberia 1.06, Congo 1.02, Sahara 1.00, Amazon 1.00, Alps 1.64. Off-road alone: Romania
1.32, US 1.36, India 1.37, Alps 2.0.

**The Alps** get the most: L1 median 1.22-1.28 slower (p90 3x), L10 1.64. MAP walked every road
pixel at a flat 5 km/h on any slope, and its 1 km/h "water" pixels on steep ground were left out
of coarse means. Ours walks hillside roads at 5 km/h times the terrain factor (docs/roads.md,
"Open": the street grade cap of T-039 would bring roads back up). The Himalaya box (30-35 N
75-80 E) is unchanged in median (1.00, p10 0.74, p90 2.24), though 40% of its cells are now under
1 km/h, where MAP had 1.4% at its 1 km/h.
