# T-035 — canoe search speed

2026-11-07. Outcome: no change to the search. The time of a canoe L10 click is not in the sea edges'
table reads, and no exact trim of the canoe paths made a measurable difference. 16 moves at sea
still costs 10-25% a click in Chrome (Constanta ~0.97 s median, up to 1.22 s), so 8 moves past
20 km from shore stay (docs/canoe.md 7, DECISIONS 2026-11-07).

## Where the time goes (node, Constanta, L10 canoe, hazard on)

Work per click: 787 k nodes settled (391 k land, 387 k canoe, 178 k of them 20 km or more out),
13 M edges tried:

| Edge kind | Tried | To a node already settled, or no node | Costed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land (walk or carry, 16 moves) | 6.25 M | 3.14 M settled, 0.03 M water | 3.08 M |
| Get in | 2.55 M | 1.85 M (no canoe node there, or settled) | 0.70 M |
| Get out | 1.69 M | 0.86 M | 0.83 M |
| Sea and lake grid moves | 2.49 M | 1.12 M settled | 1.14 M |
| River steps and water links | 0.27 M | | |

Lisbon: 535 k nodes, land tries 3.2 M, sea 2.3 M; Jakarta 520 k, land 2.0 M, sea 2.5 M; Cluj
778 k, land 5.6 M, sea 2.8 M. A CPU profile of `solve` by line (Constanta): land moves 36%, getting
in 14%, getting out 7%, sea moves 15%, river edges 5%, pop and stop checks 6%, the queue's set and
pop 10%. No line takes more than 2.4%; the heaviest are the directed-walking read of `L.sd` and the
settled check on land moves.

So the sea is about a sixth of the work, and per settled node the cost is flat, ~0.9 us.

## What was tried (node, wall time, a forced GC between solves, medians)

Wall time with a GC forced between solves is much steadier than the process CPU time T-093 used
(spread 680-740 ms against 780-1270 ms for the same solve); without the forced GC a solve is ~45 ms
slower (749 against 705 ms).

| Change | Exact? | Constanta ms (base -> changed) | Lisbon |
|---|---|---|---|
| Get in and get out over a 9-bit mask per cell of which neighbours have a canoe node or land (skips 72% of get-in tries) | yes | 706 -> 719 | |
| Sea moves test the cell's own link bits before reading the other cells' words (16 moves at sea) | yes | 767 -> 753 | 548 -> 525 |
| Ablation: each sea edge uses its own cell's current and hazard, not the mean of both cells | no | 708 -> 702 | |
| Ablation: no directed walking on canoe starts (`L.sd` not read) | no | 708 -> 643 | |
| Result arrays reused from three solves back (timing only; unsafe as written), Chrome | yes | Cluj 706-1029 -> 645-683, Jakarta 471-702 -> 414-584 | |

The first two are within the run-to-run spread, so neither was kept. Reading the neighbour's
current and hazard costs nothing measurable, so packing the sea tables or precomputing sea costs per
direction (the brief's ideas) can't help: those lines are in cache by the time they are read. The
largest single piece is the land loop's directed-walking reads (9%), which is T-007 stage 2's code.

What it would take to get a canoe click near 0.6 s (none started, each bigger than this task):

- the land loop, after T-007 stage 2 rewrites land moves (directions read per move, 16 moves,
  half of them to settled nodes);
- one interleaved record per node (weight, time, hazard, queue position) so a neighbour check
  touches one cache line, not four; a guess of ~10%, not measured;
- reusing the result arrays across solves, which needs `session.js` and `windows.js` to hand back
  a replaced result; ~5-10% in Chrome at best on the runs above, unclear under load.

**What 8 moves costs on the map.** `checks/ktrips.mjs trips 1000` with 16 moves against 8 (same
data): every trip the same except two that cross open water diagonally. Marseille to Barcelona 9.7
-> 8.2 days (with 16 it cuts across the Gulf of Lion, 70 km out, survival 99.5% -> 98.4%; with 8 it
hugs the coast); Bergen to Reykjavik 45.5 -> 43.7 days (survival 6.1% -> 7.6%). Shots, Constanta
start, Black Sea (`maps/artifacts/isochrone/t035_blacksea_live.png` 8 moves,
`t035_blacksea_sea16.png` 16): with 8 the open-sea bands are octagons and the corridors straight
45-degree runs; with 16 they are round and the corridors curve. Lisbon over the Atlantic
(`t035_atlantic_*.png`): the same, fainter.
