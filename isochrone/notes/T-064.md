# T-064 Corridor speeds are noisy (done 2026-11-05)

## Cause

Traced in Node over the real L10 data from a canoe start at Manaus (-60.02, -3.10), back from the
main stem near Obidos and from Manicore on the Madeira.

1. **Straight length against river length.** T-062 measured an edge by its straight cell-to-cell
   length (9.3 km, or 13.1 km diagonal at L10). A river step's time is its own river length over
   the river speed, and that length is the run of the river inside the upstream cell: 0.2 to 15 km
   per step. Adjacent main-stem steps read 9.4 to 290 km/h by straight length; by river length
   every main-stem step reads 11.9 km/h down (river 6.9 km/h + paddle 5).
2. **Routes hop between rivers sharing cells.** Even by river length the route mixes main-stem
   steps (11.9) with steps on small rivers in the same cells (6.9-8.9 down, 3.9 up), so per-edge
   colour still flickered. Fixed by the 6-edge window each way, and cuts only 0.4 km/h past a
   class edge.
3. **Transfers.** A get-in to a river cell next door carries the whole step (9.3 km in ~5 h);
   it had no speed of its own and borrowed the paddling speed. Now carried, with its own length
   and time. Same-cell transfers travel nothing and take the speed round them; no line reads 0.
4. **The hover** took the first feature in a 9 x 9 px box, often a faint thin line beside the
   one under the pointer; the "2.8 km/h on the main stem" in t062_amazon.png was the pointer
   ~38 px off the main stem, on a faint tributary line. Now the nearest line.

The search and the river data were not at fault: each step's time matches its length and speed.

Upstream on the main stem itself the model gives 1.5 km/h (the `upMin` floor) and the route takes
it only for short stretches: going up the Madeira or Solimoes the cheapest route weaves between
small rivers that share L10 cells, paddling down some (6.9) and up others (3.9), so those lines
read 4-6 km/h. That is the route the search finds, not a corridor artefact; it comes from small
rivers sharing cells with a big one (docs/canoe.md 8, "two rivers sharing an L10 cell are joined
there").
