# Passing tracks and overtaking: background for T-2228

Written 2026-11-03 by a research helper. Every claim has a link. Items marked **unconfirmed** could not be pinned to a source. Web fetches were read through a summariser, so quotes are as that tool returned them; the Japanese Wikipedia station table was flattened and some layouts below are inferred from its cell order (marked).

## 1. Tokaido / Sanyo Shinkansen

### Which stations have passing tracks

The Tokaido line has 17 stations (Tokyo, Shinagawa, Shin-Yokohama, Odawara, Atami, Mishima, Shin-Fuji, Shizuoka, Kakegawa, Hamamatsu, Toyohashi, Mikawa-Anjo, Nagoya, Gifu-Hashima, Maibara, Kyoto, Shin-Osaka). Per the [Japanese Wikipedia article on the line](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東海道新幹線):

- Stations with a through line (tsuuka-sen): 10 of 17. Odawara, Shin-Fuji, Shizuoka, Kakegawa, Hamamatsu, Mikawa-Anjo ("2 platforms, 2 tracks + through line"); Toyohashi and Maibara ("2 platforms, 3 tracks + through line"); Gifu-Hashima ("2 platforms, 4 tracks + through line"); Mishima ("1 island platform, 2 tracks + through line").
- "Most of this type put the loop and platform outside the main line." So the common layout is: two through tracks in the middle, one platform track on a loop on each outside, a platform beside each loop. This is the 2+2 layout the design agent asked about.
- Stations where every train stops use the plain "2 platforms, 4 tracks" layout (2面4線): Shinagawa, Shin-Yokohama, Nagoya, Kyoto (these four inferred from the flattened table; the text states the rule "全列車が停車する駅では「2面4線」の構内配線が基本となる"). Tokyo has 3 platforms / 6 tracks and Shin-Osaka 5 / 8. Atami has 2 platforms / 2 tracks on the main line, no through line.
- So no overtake is possible at Shinagawa, Shin-Yokohama, Nagoya, Kyoto or Atami. That is 5 of the 15 intermediate stations. Between Tokyo and Odawara (Shinagawa, Shin-Yokohama) there is no passing option at all, so the Tokyo end of the line is where the timetable is tightest. Kyoto, the last stop before Shin-Osaka, also has none. (This tightness is my inference from the layouts, not a sourced statement.)
- A general English description agrees: [Kodama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodama_(train)) trains wait at most intermediate stations for faster trains; stations are described elsewhere as four-track with "two central fast lines and two on loops with side access platforms" (Odawara example, from a search snippet; page not opened).

### Timetable pattern

- Since March 2020 up to 16 trains an hour: Nozomi up to 12, Hikari 2, Kodama 2 ([ja Wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東海道新幹線)). Eight of the Nozomi slots are mainly for extra (rinji) trains. 2 to 3 Nozomi an hour run the whole way to Hakata.
- Kodama: basically one Nagoya-bound and one Shin-Osaka-bound an hour ([ja Wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東海道新幹線)).
- Stop counts: Nozomi 6 stops against Kodama 17 including both ends (a search summary; source not opened, so treat as **unconfirmed**).
- Since 2003 Tokyo trains had to stop at Shinagawa or Shin-Yokohama; from 2008 Nozomi stop at both ([ja Wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東海道新幹線)). Those two have no passing line, which is one reason every train stops there.

### How long Kodama waits and how often

- Standard Kodama Tokyo-Shin-Osaka: 3 h 54 min (March 2020 timetable). With no overtakes at all it would be 3 h 12 min, "unrealistic under current conditions". The article estimates two fewer waits would save "just under 10 minutes", so roughly 4-5 minutes per wait ([Traffic News, page 3](https://trafficnews.jp/post/97342/3), [page 1](https://trafficnews.jp/post/97342)).
- Kodama 719 (10:57 from Tokyo): total stopped time to Shin-Osaka is 57 minutes, much of it waiting for Nozomi and Hikari to pass; the article does not split dwell from waiting ([Traffic News, page 2](https://trafficnews.jp/post/97342/2)).
- Nozomi Tokyo-Shin-Osaka is 2 h 21 min ([ja Wikipedia](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/東海道新幹線)).
- Inference, not sourced: the 42-minute gap (3:54 minus 3:12) divided by 4-5 minutes gives roughly 8-10 waits per trip, which fits the 10 stations with a passing line. No source gives the count directly (**unconfirmed**). The article says the March 2020 timetable has more waits than the 2017 one, without numbers.
- At opening in 1964, Hikari overtook Kodama only once, at Hamamatsu ([Traffic News](https://trafficnews.jp/post/97342)).
- A daytime Kodama in the 2019 timetable took 3 h 57 min against about 3 h 20 min for morning and evening ones that are overtaken less ([ja Wikipedia, Kodama](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/こだま_(列車))).
- Sanyo section: Mizuho and Sakura can also pass Kodama, sometimes several in one run ([ja Wikipedia, Kodama](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/こだま_(列車))).
- A travel guide claim that Kodama stop "3 to 6 minutes" to be overtaken could not be traced to a page I could open: **unconfirmed**.

## 2. Japanese commuter lines (taihi-eki)

- Two overtake types ([ja Wikipedia, taihi-sen](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/待避線)):
  - Connecting overtake (setsuzoku oikoshi, kankyuu setsuzoku): both trains stop, passengers transfer across the platform, the express leaves first. Needs an island-platform 2 platforms / 4 tracks layout. Example: Keikyu Kamiooka.
  - Passing overtake (tsuuka oikoshi): the express runs through while the local waits on the loop. Examples: Shin-Fuji on the Tokaido Shinkansen, Keikyu Samezu.
- Cross-platform transfer upside: riders from small stations reach the express. Downside: crowding on the express, and the local's trip gets longer. At rush hour operators often drop the connection and keep the trains apart ([ja Wikipedia, kankyuu setsuzoku](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/緩急接続)).
- Tokyu Toyoko: four overtaking stations, Yutenji, Jiyugaoka, Motosumiyoshi, Kikuna ([search summary of a rail media article](https://trafficnews.jp/post/65710/)). Yutenji's passing track was added in March 2017. Before that there was nothing for expresses to pass locals between Shibuya and Jiyugaoka (7.0 km); expresses caught up with locals ahead and lost time. Yutenji is the third station from each end of that section, so the spacing is about every third station. The article gives no minutes saved ([Traffic News](https://trafficnews.jp/post/65710/)).
- Jiyugaoka, August 2023: the rule "outer platform for locals, inner for expresses" was dropped for the morning peak towards Shibuya. Either kind of train may take either platform, so a train can enter as soon as one clears. Trains had been waiting at the signal just outside because their own platform was still occupied. 20 of the 38 trains between 7:20 and 8:51 changed. No minutes given ([Traffic News](https://trafficnews.jp/post/127444)). Design hint: platform choice matters as much as the track count.
- Passing waits are said to be frequent on Keikyu, Meitetsu, Keio and the Kintetsu Yamada line (reader comments, not data) ([Traffic News Q&A](https://trafficnews.jp/questions/614)). I found no figures for wait minutes or for how many stations apart overtaking stations sit on Keikyu, Odakyu or Keio: **unconfirmed**. A typical local wait of 1-4 minutes is common knowledge but I have no source.
- Research on choosing which stations get an overtake: fewer overtaking stations with no simultaneous overtake at a stopping station worked best in one simulation (no figures in the abstract) ([WIT Press, Shortening average trip times by adjusting stopping and overtaking train stations](https://www.witpress.com/elibrary/wit-transactions-on-the-built-environment/135/27103)). A Chinese suburban rail study (UIC 406 based) reports allowing overtaking cut total transit time by 23.4% in its simulation (summary only, [SPIE](https://nanolithography.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/12591/1259116/Capacity-optimization-of-suburban-railway-based-on-improved-UIC406-method/10.1117/12.2668529.full)).

## 3. Amtrak Northeast Corridor

Not much could be confirmed. What I have:

- The line has "2-6" tracks ([Wikipedia, Northeast Corridor infobox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor)). More than 2,200 trains a day use some part of it.
- New Jersey, Trenton to Newark area: four tracks, inner pair for express, outer pair for local. At Metropark the platforms flank the outer pair; Amtrak long-distance trains and most Keystone trains go by on the inner tracks ([Wikipedia, Metropark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropark_station); a 2013 construction notice is quoted in the search summary). This is the fixed fast/slow split, the quad layout the game already has.
- Connecticut: two tracks between New London and Westerly; Amtrak, Shore Line East and Metro-North share them; track priority is a friction point ([CT DOT capacity appendix](https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/dot/documents/dpolicy/ecrts/appe_corridorcapacity.pdf), snippet only). Acela being held up by commuter trains is reported by riders on forums only (**unconfirmed**).
- The NEC Master Plan found 24 of 66 segments at 75% capacity or more and 8 in northern New Jersey and New York at 100% ([Northeast Alliance for Rail, 2011](https://northeastallianceforrail.org/2011/07/the-acela-story-part-2-planning-for-the-not-so-distant-future.html)). FRA's NEC FUTURE plan proposes four tracks "at most locations" ([Mass Transit summary](https://www.masstransitmag.com/rail/press-release/12289017/federal-railroad-administration-fra-upgrades-to-allow-for-more-frequent-reliable-and-faster-rail-service-along-the-northeast-corridor-recommended-by-fra)).
- Harold Interlocking project: lets Amtrak trains from New England avoid NJ Transit and LIRR trains ([Wikipedia, NEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor)).
- I could not find a source on where Acela overtakes regional trains at interlockings, or on the share of the line that is four-track: **unconfirmed**.

## 4. Swiss practice

- Swiss freight overtaking tracks are built about 740-750 m long: Wettingen (740 m, [BAV planning document](https://www.bav.admin.ch/dam/bav/de/dokumente/infrastruktur/SIS/ob-1-4-limmattal.pdf)), Pfaeffikon SZ (750 m, needed for a half-hourly Zurich-Chur InterCity, [Gruner](https://www.gruner.ch/en/projects/freight-overtaking-track-and-reduced-train-headways)), Lausanne-Geneva (750 m, CHF 73 million, so passenger trains can pass freight and two freight trains an hour per direction keep running, [Transport Journal](https://transportjournal.com/home/news/artikeldetail/fewer-trucks-between-lausanne-and-geneva.html?L=1%27)). 750 m is the usual European reference length for freight trains.
- The loop is built where the timetable needs the pass, not by distance: a forum poster says extensions are planned from the timetable, wherever it has a crossing ([ice-treff forum, anecdotal](https://www.ice-treff.de/index.php?id=56419)). The downside noted there: the timetable becomes hard to change and delays upset it quickly.
- Single-track Swiss lines: capacity depends on the spacing of crossing stations, the speed differences, and the mix of train types, more than on average speed (study of 88 lines, [Emery, STRC 2010](https://www.strc.ch/2010/Emery.pdf)).
- An InterCity overtaking an InterRegio on the parallel track at Arth-Goldau is described by a forum poster ([JNS forum](https://jnsforum.com/community/topic/11721-swiss-intercity-overtaking-interregio)); anecdote only.
- I found no source for the Taktfahrplan overtake pattern itself (for example, minutes of regional dwell in a timed pass): **unconfirmed**.

## 5. Capacity literature

- UIC 406 compresses the timetable and describes capacity through four things: number of trains, stability, heterogeneity of train types and average speed ([Emery, STRC 2010](https://www.strc.ch/2010/Emery.pdf)). Capacity peaks for one train type at an average speed of about 80-120 km/h with good block lengths, since braking distance grows with the square of speed (same paper). The commonly quoted 60% peak / 75% daily occupancy limits are not in the pages I read: **unconfirmed**.
- Mixed fast and slow service is the main loss: "Line capacity decreases with speed difference", and more overtakes lower the slow trains' average speed and make the fast trains dependent on others ([Lindfeldt, WCTR 2010](https://wctrs-society.com/wp-content/uploads/abstracts/lisbon/general/03217.pdf)). In that simulation, freight speed and the frequency of the fast trains had the most effect on delays; distance between overtaking stations did not matter by itself, only through interaction with other factors (same paper).
- No loss when all trains stop at all stations; mixing local and express "causes a substantial reduction in capacity" ([Wikipedia, Route capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_capacity)).
- "Lost capacity" is the unusable time between paths of different speeds; one proposed fix is to put an overtaking train on the opposite track ([Czech paper](https://ojs.cvut.cz/ojs/index.php/APP/article/download/3836/3712), search summary; page would not load).
- Overtaking-position studies find capacity falls the farther the overtaking point is from the middle of the line ([Mahidol/Hasanuddin study](https://murex.mahidol.ac.th/en/publications/analysis-of-appropriate-overtaking-position-under-equal-block-len/), abstract only).
- A rough rule from a game wiki, not a rail text: quad track is often needed beyond about two slow plus two fast trains an hour ([NIMBY Rails wiki, Track layout guide](https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/Track_layout_guide)).
- A simple formula I could not find in a source. My own derivation, labelled as such: an express leaves h minutes behind a local, speeds v_e and v_l. It catches up after distance d = v_e * h * v_l / (v_e - v_l). From there to the end (D - d) it loses (D - d) * (1/v_l - 1/v_e) if it must follow. A passing loop at or before d removes that loss and costs the local its wait (the time for the express to clear the loop plus buffers). Real data point: Kodama about 4-5 minutes per wait (section 1).

## 6. NIMBY Rails

- Signals: path signals (a train stopping at one traces its path to its destination or the next blocking signal; if that path touches another train's path or reservation the signal turns red and re-checks), one-way, no-way, block balises. Old block signals were removed. Default setting blocks same-direction paths only ([NIMBY wiki, Signal](https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/Signal), [devblog](https://carloscarrasco.com/page/50/)).
- Local/express guide: with no signals in the passing stations, "the Express service will not leave the signal until the path in front" is clear. Passing tracks let expresses run round station platforms. Removing exit block signals makes locals wait for expresses to pass ([NIMBY wiki, Path signal setup](https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/Path_signal_setup)).
- Rule of thumb in the track layout guide: "Passing loops at stations and timed overtakes can allow increasing the capacity of lines"; quad track is often needed beyond roughly two slow and two fast trains per hour. A timed overtake is "a short passing track for the slow train to stop in while the fast train is allowed to pass in front". Or time the slow train to leave after the fast one so no overtake is needed ([Track layout guide](https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/Track_layout_guide)).
- Platform choice: stop assignment is basic (platform given) or advanced since version 1.9 (exact stopping point, trains may share a platform if they do not overlap) ([wiki](https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/How_to_have_multiple_trains_share_a_platform)). The pages read do not say a train picks a free platform by itself, and do not describe an "any platform" option: **unconfirmed**. The guide says "being smart with platform assignment can reduce path crossings".
- So the player builds every track and signal by hand; the sim decides who waits by path reservation, never by timetable priority.

## 7. OpenTTD

- Official tutorial: a passing siding "is a short section of track that is partly doubled, guarded by signals"; make it long enough for the longest train; use one-way path signals (two-way ones let trains "sneak ahead"); it is cheap but "causes delays when trains aren't well synchronised" ([wiki, Two Tracks](https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Manual/Tutorial/Two%20Tracks)).
- Overtaking sequence described on the forum: the slow train pulls into the siding, the signal ahead goes red because a train follows, the fast train passes on the other track ([tt-forums](https://www.tt-forums.net/viewtopic.php?p=812921)). Trains of equal speed can leapfrog each other and waste time (same thread, per search summary).
- Free-platform choice: a train at a path signal gets green if some platform in the station is free, otherwise it waits ([OpenTTD wiki design notes via search summary](https://github.com/OpenTTD/website/pull/229/files/b4a981ff46206daf5dab3f1cd139e02c5fc59b7a)). Platform exits need their own signal or the first train blocks the way out. Version 12 shows only path signals by default ([OpenTTD news](https://www.openttd.org/news/2021/11/06/explaining-signals-ui-change)). Pathfinder is YAPF ([wiki](https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Archive/Manual/Yet%20Another%20Pathfinder)).
- Known pitfalls: a stopping train can block the line when there is no free platform ([tt-forums](https://www.tt-forums.net/viewtopic.php?p=815685)).

## 8. Transport Fever 2 (and Train Fever)

- Stations and passing loops allow overtaking, but "there is no guarantee that an express train will not be slowed down by a slow train ahead"; the guide's fix of quad track is "both costly and unrealistic". Two methods: the fast train passes a station where the slow one is stopped, or both stop and connect (a cross-platform transfer). The Timetables mod holds early trains so the slow one does not leave before the fast one passes, and it "can only control departure times" ([TransportFever.net tutorial](https://www.transportfever.net/thread/17500-advanced-timetabling-tutorial-to-enable-real-local-express-running-using-the-tim/)).
- Base-game cause, from Train Fever: each line follows a fixed route, a train waits if the next section is occupied, "no alternative path will be used"; so a siding with signals does not work, only a station on the slow track where the slow train stops ([Train Fever forum, 2014](https://train-fever.com/forums/topic/impossible-to-build-overtaking-sections/)). TF2 players say there is no dynamic pathing, so a passing track must be built per direction ([Steam thread, snippet](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1066780/discussions/0/2569816492288466759)). I did not confirm whether TF2 itself changed this: **unconfirmed**.
- Passing loops for capacity on single track: one loop per pair of trains, one-way signals outside the tracks ([Train Fever forum](https://www.train-fever.com/forums/topic/increasing-line-capacity)).

Closest thing found to a station-level upgrade, but it is about platforms, not through tracks.

- **Subway Builder**: routes choose stations and frequency; nothing found on express or passing tracks ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subway_Builder)). **Unconfirmed** either way.
- **Train Valley 1/2**: switches set by click; nothing found on overtaking ([review](https://operationrainfall.com/2019/05/08/review-train-valley-2/)). **Railroad Tycoon 3, A-Train, Mini Metro**: nothing found; from memory Mini Metro has no overtaking (each line's trains run independently), but I have no source: **unconfirmed**.

## 10. Station-level "add passing track" upgrades

None found. A Train Fever forum member proposed upgradable stations that include passing lanes ([suggestion thread feed](https://train-fever.com/forums/topic/suggestion-upgradable-station/feed)); a proposal only. Railway Empire's gridiron and platform count (section 9) is the nearest.

## What this suggests for the design (my reading)

- Real Shinkansen: a passing option at 10 of 17 stations, five without; the Kodama loses about 4-5 minutes per wait and about 40 minutes per trip in total. Passing at stations only, not mid-line, matches both Shinkansen and Japanese commuter practice (Yutenji, Jiyugaoka), and is what the game's option (a) models. Mid-line loops (b) exist in Western games for single track and freight.
- Overtaking stations about every third stop on a busy line (Toyoko) is a reasonable density.
- In games, the sim decides who waits by what the track allows (reserved paths, free platform), never by priority. The one explicit priority/timing tool is a timetable hold (TF2 Timetables mod, NIMBY timed overtake). A game with a formula for the loss can instead say: the express keeps its time if a passing station is passed at the right place, else it follows the local.
- Platform choice at the station (Jiyugaoka, NIMBY platform assignment, Railway Empire's gridiron) is where most real delay hides, not just the track count.
