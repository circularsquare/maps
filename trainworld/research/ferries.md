# Ferries: real boats, costs and traffic

Background for the ferry types and long routes (T-3435, T-3892). Looked up 2026-11-20 unless marked. Earlier tables:
notes/T-2177.md 3.4 (city ferries, 2026-11-03), notes/T-3435.md (long routes and their real traffic, 2026-11-16).

## Boat classes

| Class | Example | Riders | Speed | New price | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| City ferry | NYC Ferry | 150 or 350 | about 25 knots top, 19 km/h door to door | US$4.0-5.8M (150), US$7.1-9.1M (350) | [1], [2] |
| Fast craft | Kitsap Transit (Seattle) Finest, Enetai, Commander | 250-350 | 35-37 knots (65-69 km/h) | a 250-seat replacement about US$17.5M (2024) | [3] |
| Fast craft | Hullo, Vancouver - Nanaimo (Damen Fast Ferry 4212) | 354 | 40 knots (74 km/h), 70-75 min for 60 km | not published | [4] |
| Big harbour ferry | Staten Island, Ollis class | 4,500 | 16.3 knots (30 km/h) | about US$105M each | [5] |
| Ro-pax | Tallink Megastar, Helsinki - Tallinn | 2,800 plus cars | 27 knots (50 km/h), 2 h for 82 km | about EUR 230M (2015) | [6] |
| Ro-pax | BC Ferries Coastal class | 1,600 plus 370 cars | about 21 knots (39 km/h) | C$542M for three (2004 contract) | [7] |

Fast craft cost about twice a city ferry of the same size. Ro-pax prices are mostly the car decks; a pure passenger
ship of 1,000-1,500 seats would be well under them (Molslinjen's 1,006-seat fast catamaran: A$109M, notes/T-3435.md).

## Running costs

- **NYC Ferry, FY2024** [2]: operating expenses US$89.2M, 86,700 revenue hours (US$1,029 an hour), 7.12M trips,
  fares US$3.23 a trip (26% of costs), subsidy US$9.09 a trip; fuel US$11.4M. At its 19 km/h average (11.8 mph,
  NYCEDC route appendix 2025), about **US$54 a boat-km** for 150-350 seat boats. The game's US$40 is about three
  quarters of that, the same discount rail has (research/running-costs.md).
- **Staten Island Ferry** [5], [8]: about US$112M a year, US$5.69 a ride (NBC New York; the year is not given in the
  piece, so about); 117 trips a weekday, 24 hours a day, so roughly 40,000 crossings of 8.4 km a year, about
  **US$330 a boat-km** for 4,500-seat boats: per seat about half NYC Ferry's.
- Fast craft burn far more fuel per seat-km than displacement ferries; no clean published figure was found.

## Real traffic on long routes

**BC Ferries, quarter ended 30 June 2025** (91 days, Operations Report, section 1A) [9]. Passengers include those in
cars; in the game a car ferry is no road, so every trip across is the player's.

| Route | Passengers in the quarter | A day | Car deck space used |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Tsawwassen - Swartz Bay (Vancouver - Victoria) | 1,748,729 | 19,200 | 88% |
| 2 Horseshoe Bay - Nanaimo | 807,708 | 8,900 | 79% |
| 30 Tsawwassen - Duke Point (Nanaimo) | 518,123 | 5,700 | 78% |
| All BC Ferries | 6,072,462 | 66,700 | 70% |

Only route 1 covers its costs of BC Ferries' 25 routes (UBCM workshop, September 2025) [10]. Almost one sailing in
five was overloaded in that quarter, over 40% on the major routes [9].

Other routes: notes/T-3435.md (Hong Kong - Macau about 19,000 a day, Helsinki - Tallinn 20,000-25,000, Hullo about
1,100, Clipper about 550).

## Fares

Real fares per km fall with the boat's size: TurboJET HK$194 (about US$25) for 64 km; Hullo from C$40 for 60 km;
Jumbo Ferry 1,990 yen for 116 km; Helsinki - Tallinn about EUR 30 for 82 km (notes/T-3435.md). Fast craft charge
about two to three times a ro-pax per km.
