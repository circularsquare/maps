# Upkeep by terrain, structure and street crossings: real figures and other games

Research note, 2026-11-07 (T-2732). Companion to research/upkeep-france.md, which has the French
per-km figures the game's T-2619 rates came from. **[SRC]** = read in the source. **[SECONDHAND]** =
seen quoted (a search summary or another article), primary page not opened. **[EST]** = my arithmetic
or judgement. Many primary PDFs would not open (403, 502, binary); those are marked secondhand.

## Short answers

- **Does terrain raise real maintenance?** Yes in kind, but no published figure puts a number on it.
  Curves wear rails faster (sharp curves under ~300 m radius much faster), and mountain lines pay for
  snow clearing, avalanche work, rockfall and slope repairs and drainage. Nothing found says "mountain
  track costs X% more a km". The best regional spread found (Network Rail, below) is driven by
  traffic and city density, not hills. Any terrain factor on upkeep would be a design choice, not a
  measured one.
- **Tunnel against open line:** the one hard figure is the Gotthard Base Tunnel, CHF 35M a year for
  57 km of twin single-track tubes: about CHF 0.6M a route-km, roughly 2-3 times an average European
  open line. Tunnel upkeep is mostly fixed systems (ventilation, power, monitoring, cleaning) and does
  not depend on the mountain above it. So the real split is mostly tunnel or not, as the game has it.
- **Viaduct, cut and cover:** no per-km upkeep figure found for either.
- **A level crossing** with lights and gates costs about US$3-4k a year to maintain in the US
  (secondhand), US$250-400k to install, and US$30-50M to replace with a bridge or underpass.

## 1. Terrain

- No terrain, gradient or curvature multiplier found in: Swedish track-maintenance cost studies (VTI,
  KTH), ORR and Network Rail benchmarking, RhB, ÖBB, Bane NOR, JR/RTRI, CP.
- Odolinski and Boysen (2019), KTH working paper CTS2018-10: curvature is one of the track
  characteristics that set maintenance cost levels; coefficient not seen. [SECONDHAND]
  https://www.cts.kth.se/polopoly_fs/1.824862.1550158947!/CTS2018-10.pdf
- Odolinski and Nilsson, CTS2016-27: "length of bridges and tunnels" as a cost variable comes out
  with an unexpected negative sign, a null result. [SRC, weak] https://www.transportportal.se/swopec/CTS2016-27.pdf
- Andersson (2006, VTI): marginal maintenance SEK 0.0029 a gross tonne-km (0.0065 with renewal),
  traffic-driven. [SECONDHAND] https://swopec.hhs.se/vtiwps/abs/vtiwps2006_006.htm
- ORR: Network Rail's regions averaged about £152k a track-km a year (maintenance plus renewals,
  2010-11 to 2023-24); Southern highest at £208k, Scotland (the hilliest) lowest at £116k.
  [SECONDHAND] https://www.orr.gov.uk/search-news/benchmarking-network-rails-costs
- Curve wear: inner rails on curves wear much faster than on straight track; friction modifiers trade
  wear for lubrication cost. [SECONDHAND]
  https://chestofbooks.com/finance/economics/Railroad/Chapter-IX-Track-Economics.html ,
  https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/82311/dot_82311_DS1.pdf
- Semmering (Austria): ÖBB's answer to parliament, about ATS 25M a year of upkeep on the Gloggnitz
  section since 1980, tunnels and viaducts left out (1997 prices); no per-km figure. [SECONDHAND]
  https://www.parlament.gv.at/dokument/XX/AB/3153/fname_131464.pdf
- Bergen line (Norway): snow sheds dear to keep, rebuilt about every 20 years; rotary snow cutters
  since 1976; no cost figure. [SECONDHAND] https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finse_Tunnel
- Albula line (RhB): 42 tunnels and galleries (16.5 km), 144 bridges (2.9 km), avalanche blasting by
  helicopter; no cost figure. [SECONDHAND] https://whc.unesco.org/document/164277
- Rogers Pass (Canada, the highway): avalanche control by the army, about C$70k a year plus over
  C$200k of shells a season (old figures); C$95M for snow shed repairs in 2019. Highway, not rail.
  [SECONDHAND] https://legionmagazine.com/snow-wars/ ,
  https://www.canada.ca/en/parks-canada/news/2019/05/2019-glacier-national-park-avalanche-mitigations-announcement-federal-infrastructure-investment-program.html
- Adif (Spain), high-speed double track, track only: about €25-30k a km a year (2014), "depending on
  layout, infrastructure and terrain". [SECONDHAND] https://globalrailwayreview.com/?p=74622
- EU PRIME 2022: network average about €120k a main track-km a year operating spend, €145k capital.
  [SECONDHAND] https://wikis.ec.europa.eu/download/attachments/44167372/PRIME_External%20Report_2022%20data_Final.pdf?api=v2
- Japan: RTRI papers rank rockfall and slope-failure risk; no per-km costs. [SECONDHAND]
  https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/rtriqr/53/2/53_107/_article/-char/en

## 2. Tunnels and viaducts

- Gotthard Base Tunnel: SBB pays CHF 35M a year for cleaning, monitoring and technical renewals
  (Blick, 2026-11-03). [SRC] https://www.blick.ch/schweiz/zentralschweiz/uri/gotthard-basistunnel-sbb-saugen-15-tonnen-dreck-raus/rvr74v6
  Earlier CHF 39M (SRF). [SECONDHAND] https://www.srf.ch/news/schweiz/schweiz-nach-dem-fest-folgt-die-pflicht-mit-dem-gotthard-basistunnel
  [EST] 57 km of two single-track tubes: CHF 0.6M a route-km, CHF 0.3M a track-km, against the EU's
  ~€120k a track-km: 2-3x open line. The game's bored rate is 3.7x its ground rate.
- Channel Tunnel: Colas Rail's routine track maintenance contract €2.5M over 3 years for 200 km of
  track (track only). [SECONDHAND] https://railway-international.com/news/53232-eurotunnel-renews-its-confidence-in-colas-rail-for-the-maintenance-of-its-rail-tunnels
- Network Rail structures spend 2024/25: underbridges £183M, overbridges £78M, tunnels £29M, major
  structures £18M (probably mostly renewals). [SECONDHAND]
  https://www.networkrail.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Annual-expenditure-2024-25.pdf
- HS1: ORR set £26.1M a year for 67 miles. [SECONDHAND]
  https://www.expressandstar.com/news/transport/high-speed-1-rail-line-upkeep-to-cost-companies-ps26-million-a-year-6140173
- PIARC: a tunnel costs about 10 times open-air infrastructure to build (capital only). [SECONDHAND]
  https://tunnelsmanual.piarc.org/en/strategy-and-general-design-strategic-issues/costs-financial-aspects
- Lötschberg, Seikan, Brenner, Chinese HSR viaducts, cut and cover against bored: nothing found.
- The US National Academies' transit track maintenance synthesis leaves bridges and tunnels out, so
  track-only figures understate structures. [SECONDHAND] https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/23033/chapter/6

## 3. Level crossings

- Michigan DOT table (from 2026-01-01; page not opened, figures inconsistent between summaries): the
  road agency's yearly share for flashing signals US$1,623 (single track) and US$1,931 (multiple);
  signals with gates about US$3,000-3,600; with cantilevers and gates US$3,454-4,641. [SECONDHAND]
  https://www.michigan.gov/mdot/travel/mobility/rail/safety-regulation/maintaining-railroad-crossings ;
  Michigan statute: lights and gates US$2,867 and US$3,394. https://zeus-milenia.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-462-315.pdf
- FRA: four-quadrant gates cost about US$2,500 a year more to maintain than two-quadrant (about 2005).
  [SECONDHAND] https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20081105163921/http://www.fra.dot.gov/downloads/safety/reg_eval/reg-eval_appendix_b.pdf
- Install lights and gates: US$150-300k (2015), later US$250-400k; TxDOT about US$300k. [SECONDHAND]
  https://highways.dot.gov/safety/hsip/xings/highway-railway-grade-crossing-action-plan-and-project-prioritization-7 ,
  https://www.cbsnews.com/texas/news/i-team-txdot-spends-300000-on-average-to-install-lights-a-gate-at-a-train-crossing/
- Grade separation of one crossing, US: US$5-40M planning range (2015); projects US$30-42M, newer
  US$50-70M. [SECONDHAND] (FHWA link above), https://www.freightwaves.com/news/fra-grants-570m-to-projects-eliminating-railroad-crossings
- UK: £400 a year to run and maintain an automatic half barrier, £70k to install (Hansard, 1984).
  [SECONDHAND] https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/written_answers/1984/mar/26/railway-level-crossings
  Network Rail's closure programmes: 500 closures for £109M (about £218k each, [EST] division), and a
  750-closure target in a £131M scheme. [SECONDHAND]
  https://www.transportxtra.com/publications/evolution/news/37705/network-rail-rejects-target-to-end-level-crossing-deaths-says-risk-lower-than-mps-claim ,
  https://ciltuk.org.uk/News/News-Centre/ArtMID/7018/ArticleID/3935/Network-Rail-reaches-level-crossing-closure-target
- Japan: gating an unguarded (class 4) crossing about ¥20-30M; no yearly figure. [SECONDHAND]
  https://trafficnews.jp/post/133687/2
- [EST] In game money: US$4k a year real is US$11 a real day, so about **US$1k a game day** at the
  100x economy. Next to ground track's US$35k a km, that is small unless crossings are dense.

## 4. Street spacing (for the city cases)

- Manhattan: the 1811 Commissioners' Plan put the numbered streets about 200 ft of block plus a 60 ft
  street apart, about 80 m centre to centre, and the avenues about 920 ft, about 280 m (274 m is the
  usual figure for the block between avenues). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioners%27_Plan_of_1811
- Barcelona's Eixample: blocks 113.3 m square with 20 m streets, about 133 m centre to centre.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eixample
- The game's own count (notes/T-1929.md, OpenFreeMap roads as the game reads them): Midtown 11.2
  crossings a km, Lower Manhattan 11.9, central London 12.7, central Tokyo 13.3, central Paris 7.4,
  US suburbs 6-10, rural 0.6-0.8.

## 5. Other games

- **OpenTTD** (infrastructure maintenance setting): rail pieces per tile n², bridges and tunnels 4
  pieces a tile; cost per type = pieces x factor x sqrt(all pieces), so upkeep grows as network size
  to the 1.5. No terrain term; level crossings not covered on the page. [SRC]
  https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Archive/Community/Infrastructure%20maintenance
- **Transport Fever 2**: track, station and signal upkeep billed apart from vehicles; players find
  track upkeep small next to trains'; bridges dear to build. No terrain or structure multiplier found.
  [SRC] https://steamcommunity.com/app/1066780/discussions/0/2639605437881762306 ;
  [SECONDHAND] https://steamcommunity.com/app/1066780/discussions/0/1740010344366988160
- **Railway Empire 2**: upkeep mostly trains and maintenance buildings; tunnels and bridges are a
  construction cost. [SECONDHAND] https://www.gamepressure.com/S013-amp.asp?ID=22334
- **Subway Builder**: construction = base x depth multiplier (0.3x to 4.5x, cut and cover the
  baseline) x track type (single 0.75, double 1, quad 1.5); no maintenance formula found.
  [SECONDHAND] https://wiki.subwaybuilder.com/index.php/Construction
- **NIMBY Rails**: no cost page found.
- None of these charges terrain in upkeep. Where structures cost more to keep (OpenTTD), it is by
  structure, not ground.
