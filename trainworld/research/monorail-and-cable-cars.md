# Urban monorails and cable cars: real figures

Gathered 2026-11-27 for T-4334, T-4335 and T-4336 (notes/T-4334.md decides the game's values from these). Each figure
gives the system, the year where it matters, and the source. "(snippet)" means the figure came from a search summary
and the page itself was not read. Per-km figures marked "my division" divide a published total by a published length.

## A. Straddle-beam monorails

### Speed
- Top speed 80 km/h in service: Innovia 300, Sao Paulo Line 15 (90 km/h design), https://www.monorails.org/tMspages/TPInnovia300.html;
  Colombo design 80 km/h with 3.5 km/h/s acceleration (JICA 2014), https://openjicareport.jica.go.jp/pdf/12234092_03.pdf
- Lower on older lines: Osaka 75 km/h, Tama 65, Okinawa Yui Rail about 65. Chongqing Line 3: 100 km/h design (snippet).
- Scheduled average: Tama about 27 km/h (16.0 km in 36 min), https://www.kansa.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/documents/d/kansa/22zaien332 ;
  Yui Rail 27.4 km/h, https://www.yui-rail.co.jp/yuirail/plan/
- Ranges: monorail 25-40 km/h against metro 25-60 and LRT 18-40 (Eskisehir 2022,
  https://dergipark.org.tr/tr/download/article-file/1356738); 20-30 mph, "comparable to subways" (Kennedy 2002,
  https://monorails.org/webpix%202/RyanRKennedy.pdf).

### Cars and capacity
- Chongqing Line 2 (Hitachi 2005): 4-car train 60 m; end car 151 riders, middle car 165 (Chinese standard, 6 a m2);
  platforms 120 m, built for 8 cars; 12,600 / 23,000 / 32,000 riders an hour each way with 4 / 6 / 8 cars.
  https://www.hitachi.com/content/dam/hitachi/global/en/insights/media/hitachihyoron/2005/r2005_04/r2005_04_107.pdf
- Colombo design (JICA 2014): cars 14.8 m (lead) and 13.9 m (middle), 3.0 m wide; 800 riders a 4-car train at 10 a m2.
- Innovia 300, Sao Paulo: 7 cars, 86.0 m, 3.14 m wide; 1,002 riders at 6 a m2, 122 seats.
- Capacity claims for Innovia disagree: over 40,000 an hour each way (Bombardier,
  https://rail.bombardier.com/content/bbd-transport/en/solutions-and-technologies/urban/monorail.html); 28,000 (Bangkok,
  https://www.bangkokpost.com/business/general/1994983); 45,000 (Cairo, https://www.alstom.com/monorail-story-greater-cairo).
- Small systems: Hitachi's range 2,000-25,000 an hour each way; Sentosa 3,000-4,000 (2008,
  https://www.hitachi.com/ICSFiles/afieldfile/2008/03/04/r2008_01_009.pdf); Kuala Lumpur 3,416
  (https://www.railway-technology.com/projects/kualalumpurmonorail/).

### Headway and switches
- Shortest headway: 75 s claimed for Innovia (Bombardier); Colombo design 2 min; Chongqing 2.5-12 min (snippet).
- A beam switch takes far longer than a rail point (0.6 s): Las Vegas about 15 s, "would likely result in increased
  minimum headways" where switches are used in regular service (Kennedy 2002); Osaka about 10 s
  (https://karapaia.com/archives/52236894.html); a Disney beam switch 12 s (https://www.monorails.org/tMspages/switch.html,
  unsourced); 15-25 s at the top end, pivot switches about 50 s in yards (review paper, snippet,
  https://www.researchgate.net/publication/301302321_Monorails_for_sustainable_transportation_-_a_review); new Chongqing
  sliding turnouts under 30 s, passed at up to 30 km/h (2025,
  https://www.ichongqing.info/2025/08/23/chongqing-monorail-wows-the-world-with-seamless-track-switching/).
- Turnback at a terminus: 2-4 min (Colombo design).
- A switch set: JPY 191-229M, about US$1.9-2.2M (Colombo 2014).

### Curves and gradient
- Smallest curve: main line 100 m preferred, 60 m least, depot 50 m (Colombo design standard); Chongqing main line
  100 m, depot 50 m; Hitachi small type 40 m; Sentosa 35 m. Monorail 40-60 m against 250-300 m for LRT and metro
  (Eskisehir 2022, the one direct comparison found).
- Steepest grade: 6% on the main line, stations level (Colombo); Chongqing 5% on the route, 6% design (the two
  conflict); Sentosa 5.79%; Hitachi small type 6%. Rubber tyres could climb over 15% in theory, against at most 10% for
  rail, but motors and standing riders limit it (Kennedy 2002).
- Underground: service lines run elevated; at ground level only in depots.

### Construction cost
- Colombo (JICA 2014 prices, no land or taxes): JPY 131.1 bn for 23.2 km, about US$55.5M a km with stations (my
  division at 101.79 JPY/US$); stations JPY 932-982M each (about US$9.2-9.6M); a 4-car train JPY 920M, about US$2.3M a
  car; elevated beam and piers about JPY 1.43 bn a km; depot beam on the ground JPY 190M a km (about a seventh). A line
  at ground level still needs fencing all along, since a monorail cannot cross a road at grade
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombo_Monorail).
- Same city: Mumbai monorail Line 1 Rs 3,000 crore for 19.54 km (about US$69M a km at PPP) against Metro Line 1
  Rs 3,800 crore for 11.07 km (about US$154M a km), https://pedestrianobservations.com/2013/08/24/monorail-construction-costs/ ;
  about Rs 150 crore a km monorail, 500 elevated metro, 2,000 underground,
  https://www.moneylife.in/article/mumbai-monorail-tall-claims-and-the-reality/36208.html
- Colombo study: elevated LRT and monorail about the same, about US$50M a km; at-grade LRT about US$30M a km.
- Eskisehir 2022: monorail dearer than tram, about LRT's, about 30% under metro a km (Las Vegas US$50M a km, Kuala Lumpur
  61, Mumbai 37; Bangkok Skytrain 90, Izmir LRT 74, Manila LRT 3 64).
- Bangkok Yellow Line about THB 1.8 bn a km and Pink Line THB 1.6 bn a km (estimates before construction); both were
  switched to monorail to cut cost, https://www.railwaypro.com/wp/bangkok-approves-two-monorail-lines/ . Daegu Line 3
  (23.95 km) was switched from subway to monorail in 2008 partly for cost,
  https://www.railway-technology.com/projects/daegu-urban-railway-line-3/
- Other a km at PPP (Pedestrian Observations 2013): Tama US$151M, Okinawa 85, Chongqing Line 3 85-88, Moscow 109.

### Running cost
- No clean per car-km comparison. Rubber tyres use about 25-30% more energy than steel wheels (Eskisehir; Tennessee DOT,
  snippet); tyres last about 160,000 km. Tennessee DOT and the Texas Transportation Institute put operating cost "about
  equal to LRT".

### Sharing track
- Never with ordinary rail, and makers' beams are not interchangeable either
  (https://www.monorails.org/tMspages/wmraabi.html, https://humantransit.org/2009/08/briefly-on-monorails.html).

## B. Urban cable cars (gondolas)

### Speed and station times
- Line speed of urban monocable gondolas (World Bank 2020 data sheets,
  https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/140251611326011996/pdf/Urban-Aerial-Cable-Cars-as-Mass-Transit-Systems-Case-Studies-Technical-Specifications-and-Business-Models.pdf):
  Mi Teleferico 5 m/s (Azure and Purple lines 6), Medellin 5 (Line L 5.8), Bogota, Mexicable and Ankara 6, Caracas 5.
- Catalogue tops: monocable 6 m/s (21.6 km/h), tricable 8.5 m/s, aerial tram 12 m/s (Alshalalfah et al. 2012,
  https://transitchoices.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/Urban-Gondola-Report.pdf); monocable 6.1 m/s, bicable and tricable
  8 m/s (Miami-Dade 2016, https://miamidadetpo.org/library/studies/aerial-cable-transit-feasbility-study-final-report-2016-02.pdf).
- In a station cabins creep at about 0.25 m/s along the platform (Miami); boarding 12 s a 10-rider cabin at peak
  (Miami, snippet); 42 s a station in a Doha study (snippet, https://journals.qu.edu.qa/index.php/CIC/article/download/3954/2626/11875),
  1 min a stop in the New Zealand study. An in-line station is 98-122 m long, 18-24 m of it platform (Miami).

### Cabins, intervals and capacity
- Cabins: monocable 8-15 riders, bicable 15-20, tricable 20-35, aerial tram 50-200; intervals: monocable 10-30 s,
  tricable 15-45 s (Miami).
- Mi Teleferico: 10-seat cabins every 12 s, 3,000 riders an hour each way (Global Infrastructure Hub,
  https://content.gihub.org/live/media/1555/mi-telef%C3%A9rico-cable-car.pdf). Real lines 2,400-3,600 an hour each way
  (World Bank: Bogota 3,600, Medellin J and Caracas 3,000, London 2,500, Ankara 2,400); up to 3,600 at 12 s
  (Alshalalfah).
- Tricable up to 5,500-6,000 an hour each way; Koblenz 3,700; Toulouse Teleo 1,500-2,000
  (https://www.doppelmayr.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/DM_WIR195_ENG.pdf). Aerial tram: Roosevelt Island 1,200.

### Geometry
- Spans are straight between stations. A tower deflects the rope only a little: about 2 degrees
  (https://www.gondolaproject.com/2010/01/03/grip-module-lesson-4-corners/), up to 7 (Utah DOT 2020,
  https://littlecottonwoodeis.udot.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/LCC-EIS-Alternative-Screening-Report-2020-05-21_AppendixE.pdf),
  over 5 needs an angle mechanism (Miami).
- Turns are made at angle stations: cabins detach, go round and reattach, turns of 90 degrees or more possible, no
  riders board (Miami, Utah); slowing there is not strictly required (Gondola Project). An angle station costs about 60%
  of a terminal, about US$10.2M (Utah 2020); about US$7M extra in a Georgetown-Rosslyn study.
- Towers every 120-180 m along city streets (Miami), 150-250 m (Abley and Doppelmayr 2024,
  https://www.doppelmayr.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/IntroductiontoCableCars-PartCFinalID30000.pdf); Cablebus Line 1
  62 towers on 9.2 km. Tricable spans to 3 km (Peak 2 Peak 3.03 km); a monocable example, Gaia, 396 m (snippet).
- No authoritative steepest slope for urban gondolas was found.

### Line lengths
- Cablebus Line 2 10.55 km with 7 stations (https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Transportation_Planning_Casebook/Mexico_City_Cableb%C3%BAs_Network),
  Line 1 9.2 km with 6; Mi Teleferico 10 lines, about 30 km, 2-5 stations a line; Medellin 1.05-4.6 km with 2-4.
  Stations typically 0.7-1.2 km apart.

### Operating cost and hours
- A year: Bogota about US$5.4M, Cali US$3.37M, Santo Domingo Line 1 US$4.65M, London US$2.75M; Mi Teleferico's first
  three lines US$13.7M (2014). Miami's model: about US$440-1,260 an operating hour.
- No urban system runs all night: Mi Teleferico 17 h a day, Medellin 20 h, Mexicable 5:00-23:00, Teleo 5:00-00:30.

### Wind
- Service stops in winds over about 64 km/h (Miami); London closed 520 hours in 354 closures over 2.5 years, not all for
  wind (https://www.london.gov.uk/press-releases/assembly/len-duvall/cable-car-no-option).
