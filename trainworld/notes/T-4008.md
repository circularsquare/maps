# T-4008: do income groups even out express and local (measured, 2026-11-21, agent timegroups)

## For Anita

**Short answer: no.** Giving riders different values of time doesn't move anyone between the express and the
local. Express and local charge the same fare, so a richer rider counts both the wait and the ride more by the same
factor, and the choice between them comes out the same. Groups only split them where the express costs more.

**New York today** (commutes and everyday trips, T-3246's fares). On the stretches where an express and a local both
run, the express carries 47% of riders' km. A narrower look: on station pairs both serve directly (about 193,000
commute trips a day, 5% of commute rail), 56% take the express and 44% the local, and 37% of those trips are on
pairs where at least 99% take the same line. Crowding already evens things out: before it, the split was 65/35.

**With time-value groups**, each group choosing its own route:

| | km on the express (all riders) | express share (pairs) | trips on one-line pairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| today | 46.8% | 55.7% | 37.0% |
| 3 groups (poorest fifth 0.57x, middle 1x, richest fifth 1.72x) | 46.8% | 55.6% | 36.8% |
| 2 groups (poorer half 0.69x, richer half 1.23x) | 46.8% | 55.6% | 37.4% |
| how much riders mind waiting, spread (0.6x, 1x, 1.6x) | 46.9% | 56.1% | 25.8% |
| soft rule (below; worked out from today's costs, pairs only) | | 47.9% | 4.9% |

**The passing-track map** (the 10/10 and 30/10 lines): every A, D or G trip takes the express, and B or C to G
riders all change to it at D. With locals every 10 and expresses every 30, 25% take the express (by trains an
hour). Groups change none of it. With a 10% higher fare on the express at 10/10, the poorest fifth would all take
the local and the richest fifth half or all the express (at 30/10 the poorest still ride the express A to D).

**Cost of groups.** Natively, 2 groups other than today's add 38-42% to calculating trips and 67 MB to the peak;
one extra group (the poorest fifth alone) about 20% and 34 MB. In the browser on the real save, 3 groups took
trips from 94-111 s to 200 s, and each demand worker's peak went from 337 to 407 MB. The browser figure is this
prototype's and probably high (its cache is simple and the save has 4 cities); I didn't check why. That's route
choice alone; T-3246's 3x for each group's own train-or-car choice too was a sum, never run.

**My pick:** drop income groups for this. If you want express and local more even, the thing to try is a soft
rule: a rider on the platform takes a slower train that comes first with a chance that falls the more it costs
against waiting for the faster one (today that chance is 1 or 0). On the pairs above it gives 48% express and
almost no one-line pairs, and on the passing map A to D 68%, D to G 84%. That's from today's costs, without
crowding catching up, and it leaves alone the choice to change at D. It needs no extra search. Riders would move
but the trip costs shown would stay today's, a small lie. I'd prototype and measure it properly first (about 2-3
hours), if you want it.

## Method

Prototype in tree `tw/timegroups`, not for landing, diff in `notes/T-4008/prototype.patch`: `sim/src/demand/t4008.rs`
(groups, probe, the soft rule), `t4008_tests.rs` (the passing map), a group cache `with_group_skims` in
`kernel/search.rs`, marked branches in `api.rs` (commute assignment, `t4008_set`, `release_skims`) and
`everyday.rs` (`assign_lanes_groups`), `bin/calib.rs` (`--t4008`, `--probe`, stretches), and the worker's
`?t4008=` in `demandClient.ts` and `demand.worker.ts`. With no groups and no probe every site branches past it:
calib `--exact --cells` gives the untouched build's output to the bit apart from timings (`same.py`).

- **Groups** are route choice only. Mode choice runs once at today's costs, and each group's share of the
  station-pair trips is assigned on its own strategies. The crowding rounds see every group's riders. A strategy
  with every minute times k is the one with every fare over k, since the search compares minutes plus fare and its
  comparisons scale, so a group's graph is today's with rn_fare, the transfer fare and base over k
  (`group_graph`). The fixed 2 change minutes and walks count as minutes. The wait spread scales waits alone
  (boarding edges, `st_wait`, left-behind waits). Factors: income^0.5 from T-3246's US fifths (the poorest at 0.318
  of the middle, the richest at 2.79), halves from the same table relative to the mean.
- **Probe** (`--probe`): station pairs that two or more lines ride between with no change, at different numbers of
  stops. The line with fewest stops is the express. For each pair it records the first-boarding share on each
  line that rides straight there, weighted by the pair's commute trips, for the free-flow pass and each period's
  last pass. A one-line pair has 99% or more of its direct riders on one class. "Other" (19%, riders changing or
  walking first) is left out of the split. Stretches: rider-km on express hops against the local's hops between
  the same two stations, every layer.
- **Soft rule** (analysis only, `pair_split`): over the direct lines, c* is the cheapest of (half headway + cost
  from boarding). Each line's riders are trains an hour × exp(-theta × max(0, cost from boarding - c*)), with theta
  T-710's 20 / (25 + 3 per km, at most 60). For two lines this is exactly today's rule with a tail. It is evaluated
  on the last pass's crowded costs with no rounds of its own, so with crowding it would land somewhat nearer
  today.
- **Runs**: `runs.py` (calib on `nyc_real.json`, 3 rounds, calib's $1.50 + $0.10 a km with T-3246's curve
  `0,1.5,20,3.5,1000,248.5`, `fare_w` 4.4, one thread each). Timings were taken one at a time. Solve s: base 57.7 and 51.0, prototype off 60.8 and 50.5, 3 groups 81.7
  and 70.4, halves 72.2, poorest fifth alone 66.7. Group stages for 2 extra groups: search 17.5-20.2 s,
  assignment 5-5.7 s. Heap peak: 172 MB today, 239 MB with 2 extra groups, 206 MB with one. Between groups, 1-5%
  of pairs' splits differ by over 1%, all of it from fare-km differences (an express hop's straight line is
  shorter than the local's hops). Browser: `play.mjs` on `T-007-real-nyc.save`, 3 cores (2 solving workers), 4
  cities and 9 market packs. Rounds published at 37/55/73/94 s and 55/75/94/111 s off, 65/115/164/200 s with 3
  groups (one groups run, against two off runs 17 s apart). Not checked why it is 2x against native +40%: each
  city has its own graph, so each city's groups search apart, and the group cache keeps 2 tables, oldest out,
  without the main cache's rule that spares the costliest, so 4 cities may search their groups again; likely an
  upper bound.
- **Passing map** (`cargo test --profile agent --features demand --lib t4008 -- --nocapture`): T-2228's line built
  in the track model (`shinkansen`, passing tracks at B to F), its timetabled run times (local 56.0 min, express
  38.8 at 10/10), the US fare at the high-speed multiplier on both, and strategies for every pair. Route choice
  only, no riders or crowding. The calib market layer is off, as are the game's longer trips on that map.
