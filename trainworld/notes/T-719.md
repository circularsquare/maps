# T-719: whole regions instead of lone metros (planning note, 2026-10-19)

Anita's question, 2026-10-19: if we do Madrid and Barcelona, are we set up to fill in the rest of
Spain later; are we recording the data we use; should data work shift to whole regions as in Japan;
how long do cities and regions take now; and what would speed it up. Nothing was built for this
note. The scripts behind its numbers were throwaways in the session scratchpad (GHSL people per
NUTS-3 unit, token wake-ups per agent); the figures are below.

## In plain words

**Yes, Spain can be filled in later without redoing Madrid or Barcelona.** The pipeline gives each
metro whole statistical units: Madrid's pack is the whole Comunidad de Madrid (one NUTS-3 unit),
Barcelona's the whole province of Barcelona. Everything around them is still free, in whole units,
so zones for the rest of Spain can be laid next to them with no slivers or overlaps. The packs never
need rebuilding for that.

**What is recorded.** Every pack's header lists its sources by name and version (GHSL population
2023 release, the World Bank jobs ratio and year, OSM, the borrowed commute curve and so on), the
world plan row records which units, which commuting area, which OSM extracts and how long each step
took, and every build log is kept in the artifacts folder. Madrid and Barcelona use no Spanish
national data at all: they are global-only (archetype D, like Lagos). Two gaps: the data register has
no row for Spain, or for any of the 20 or so countries the runner has added (it only covers the
countries we tailored by hand); and the OSM downloads are deleted after each region (the disk rule),
with the date of the OSM copy not written down, so a rebuild uses newer OSM and comes out slightly
different. Neither stops anything; the first is worth fixing.

**Whole regions.** Japan was covered by 7 metro packs plus 38 prefecture zones. The method carries
over to any country: take every unit no metro owns, join the very small ones to a neighbour, and build
each as a zone pack. What was special about Japan is its data: a census population grid, jobs by
grid square and a table of commuters between every pair of municipalities, which gave each zone its
own commute curve. Spain has open equivalents: a 1 km census population grid from INE, and the
transport ministry's mobile-phone trip tables between about 3,800 districts covering the whole
country, with home-to-work trips marked. Without them a Spanish zone is still buildable from global
sources, as Madrid was.

What does not exist yet: the planning step that picks a country's non-city zones (today only
Japan's script does it, by hand-coded prefecture lists), the runner treating zone rows like metro
rows, a commute curve for countryside zones when no flow table exists (the borrowed curve is a big
metro's), and solving the joint commute gravity once per country rather than again after every
region. Before Europe-wide cover, two runtime limits also need work: the registry (every zone adds
about 3 KB to a file the app loads at startup) and keeping market packs light.

**Spain in numbers.** Spain has 59 NUTS-3 units. 5 are metro rows (Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia,
Seville, Malaga: 19.7M people by GHSL). The other 54 hold 27.0M people, 8 of them under 100,000
(small islands, Soria, Ceuta and Melilla). Under today's rule only 4 of the 54 would become zones (a
unit must touch two metros). Whole-country cover would be about 46-48 zones of about 0.55M people
each, half Japan's average zone size.

**How long things take now.** On a clean run the runner adds a metro in about 8 minutes of machine
time: about a third reading the OSM download, a third building, a fifth checking (the gate), the
rest downloading and joining commutes. Counting everything so far (12 batches, 11 hours, 42 metros
shipped), it is about 16 minutes a metro, because of one 3-hour hang (Stockholm, now fixed) and
reruns. 21 of the 52 metros tried parked at least once: 9 for tooling mix-ups unrelated to the city,
6 for code bugs new data exposed, 6 for real data or threshold questions. Agent tokens per metro were
0.22M on the UK batch and 0.36M on the first Europe batch, where 80% of the 8.4M went on the agent
waking every 10 minutes to check on the run.

Rough totals at today's pace: the rest of Spain (3 metros and about 47 zones) about 4 hours of
machine time; the rest of Europe's metros 4-7 hours; Europe's countryside 400-1,000 zones depending
on zone size, 1-3 days of machine time; the rest of the world's metros (429 rows) 3-5 days; the
whole world's countryside (perhaps 5,000-6,000 zones) weeks, and it needs the runtime work first.

**Speedups, biggest first.** (1) Agents wait once for the run to end instead of checking every 10
minutes: about three quarters of a batch's tokens, minutes of work. (2) A short pre-flight before a
batch that catches tooling mismatches: 9 of 21 parks. (3) A tighter time limit on a step: the
Stockholm hang cost 3 hours, a sensible limit 20 minutes. (4) Keep a parked region's download until
its rows ship: about 45 minutes of repeat downloading and reading so far. (5) Read the next region
while this one is checked (T-592): about a fifth of a clean batch. (6) Join commutes once per
country: small now, essential with zones. (7) Two builds at a time for zones. (8) A lighter gate for
zones.

The decisions this needs from Anita are at the end.

| Part | General? |

| Name from the most populous unit, origin at the densest square | yes (densest 1 km of GHSL instead of the 500 m mesh) |

So the method generalises; the data does not. Without a flow table a zone takes the borrowed curve,

Borrowing a metro's curve for countryside would send too many rural commuters long distances.
T-314's leave-one-out table already includes the 31 fitted Japanese zones, so a "zone curve" could be
borrowed from them by the same rule (a demand rule change: Anita's call).

### Steps that do not exist yet for a country-wide cover

1. **Zone rows in the world plan** (`world_plan.py zones --countries ES`): free units of the
   country after its metro rows, the 100k merge, an island rule, names, origins, extracts, a
   `kind: "zone"` row. Japan's `plan` is the model.
2. **The runner and `plan_config.py` take zone rows** (`kind` and `name` into `CITIES`). The gate
   already skips `pop_vs_fua` for a zone; `pop_vs_plan` and the rest apply.
3. **A countryside curve** for zones without a flow table (above).
4. **Joint gravity once per country**, after all its regions, instead of after each region (section 4).
5. **Runtime scale**: registry size and market pack size (section 2).
6. **A register row per country**, filled automatically from the plan (archetype, modules, "global only").

## 2. A general "whole region" method

**Zones.** Free units of the plan's unit level, in the country, after its metro rows; each zone one
unit or a group of touching units in one first-level region; a group grown by adjacency (the plan's
40M split already does this) until it reaches a target size. Unit sizes vary a lot:

| Country | Free NUTS-3 units | Under 100k | People free |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germany | 312 (Kreise) | 83 | 50.9M |
| Italy | 98 | 1 | 41.1M |
| France | 87 | 1 | 44.3M |
| Poland | 59 | 0 | 29.7M |
| Spain | 54 | 8 | 27.0M |
| All NUTS countries but Turkey | 1,075 | 175 | 326.8M |

(Free means no world plan row and not mostly inside a hand-made pack. The UK's ITL3, Russia, Ukraine
and Belarus are outside NUTS and not counted.) Free units' median is 240k people (quartiles 130k and
430k). Outside Europe the plan's units are the finest geoBoundaries level with a median unit of
20,000 people or more, far too small for zones: there zones must be grown from units to a size.

Options for the target: (a) units as they are, under 100k merged (Spain 47 zones, Germany about 230,
Europe about 900-1,000); (b) grown to about 0.3-1.5M inside each first-level region (Spain about 30,
Germany about 60, Europe about 350-450); (c) Japan's size, 1M or more. Fewer, bigger zones mean fewer
packs, registry rows and builds; smaller ones follow lines people know (provinces) and keep the
countryside's commutes inside one pack.

**Empty land.** A pack holds cells only where people or jobs are, so a huge empty zone is cheap in
cells but not in water mask, outline and OSM reading (Lapland, Siberia, the Sahara, the outback).
Japan had none. A rule is needed: say, a zone needs 20 people a km² overall or it stays dead space;
or an area cap (the gate's 40,000 km²) with emptier units left out.

**Gravity and market.** Each zone's own gravity is solved at build; it then joins its country's joint
commute group (packs within 50 km chain together, so a covered country is one group, and a covered
continent one group joined across borders). Today a group is re-solved after every region a batch
adds: every member back to res-9, `joint_gravity`, merged again, re-gated. Japan's 45 packs take
about 5 minutes a solve on 8 threads; a covered Europe would be roughly ten Japans, so perhaps 30-60
minutes a solve if it scales with pairs (pairs are cut at 150 km), and it must not run per region.
Market rows are a few seconds a zone (`market_zones.py`).

**Pack size and runtime cost.** Japan's 38 zones: `.bin` 0.4-2.3 MB raw (0.2-1.1 MB gzipped), 50.7
MB raw for 46.6M people; each zone also has a market pack (Nagano 0.44 MB) and a place list (0.2
MB), so about 2 MB a zone. In the browser a zone opened in 0.4-0.6 s; zones open only when viewed or
reached and close after 60 s (T-301), so play cost grows with the network's reach, not the zone count.
What does grow with the count:
- **The registry**: 352 KB for 109 entries today, about 3.2 KB each, bundled into the app. Spain adds
  about 0.15 MB, Europe at option (a) about 3 MB, the world's countryside 15-20 MB. It needs splitting
  into a small index (id, name, box, centre) and per-zone details fetched on demand before Europe-wide
  cover; zones.md 8 already says startup must not grow with the zone count.
- **`data/packs/zones.json`** (far-zoom bubbles): one row a zone, fine to a few thousand.
- **Market packs** at 0.44 MB a zone: 2-3 GB for the world's countryside. Hosting is T-060 (R2).
- **A long line across a covered country** reaches many zones, each opened in every solving worker
  (a worker with 67 packs open held 466 MiB). A national network across Spain reaching 40 zones is in
  that range; worth measuring on Japan's packs now.

**Open questions for Anita** are in the decisions list at the end.

## 3. How long it takes

### Wall time per metro, by phase

The cleanest large batch, 20261017-193804 (21 rows in 14 regions, 164 minutes, `--jobs 3`, phases
one after another):

| Phase | Minutes | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Download extracts | 15.0 | 9% |
| Read extracts into the caches | 59.1 | 36% |
| Build, places, routes, merge, nocar, everyday | 49.6 | 30% |
| Gate (register, snapshot, own Vite, browser check, market) | 28.6 | 18% |
| Joint gravity and market rows | 10.7 | 7% |

7.8 minutes a row, 10.9 a shipped row (15 shipped). Per row over all 42 shipped: build median 66 s
(max 242 s, Voronezh), routes median 26 s (max 80 s), places 3 s, the other three steps about a second
each: 1.7 minutes median for a row's own steps. Reading is per region: 1.4 minutes (Madrid) to 11.8
(Russia's Central district); the UK batch's Ireland read was 5.8 of its region's 8.0 minutes.

### Over every runner batch so far

12 batches, about 11.3 hours, 42 rows shipped: **about 16 minutes of machine time a shipped metro**,
twice the clean rate. The difference: Stockholm's two 90-minute hangs (184 minutes, 27% of all runner
time) and regions downloaded and read again after parks (Hamburg's region, Upper Bavaria and Silesia
three times each, Wales and Sweden two or three times: about 45 minutes).

## 4. Speedups, ranked by time saved for effort

| # | Change | Saves | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Batch agents wait once**: start `world_run.py run` in the background and wait on the process ending, no timed foreground waits or progress checks (CLAUDE.md already says so; put it in the batch brief and adding-a-city.md "Running the pipeline" with the exact call) | about 75% of a batch's tokens: 6.7M of the first Europe agent's 8.4M | minutes |
| 2 | **Pre-flight** before a batch: the runner makes its own sim copy from HEAD with its junctions (T-616), and runs one tiny row through every step into a scratch root, failing fast on any step (a missing merge rule, a stale `pack_walk`) | 9 of 21 parks; each cost a rerun with a fresh download and read (2-10 minutes a region) and agent time | small |
| 3 | **A step limit from the row's size**: about 10 times the expected step time from people and area, at least 10 minutes, instead of a flat 90; `TW_FAULT_S` stack dumps on | Stockholm's 184 minutes would have been about 20 | small |
| 4 | **Keep a region's download and caches until its rows ship** (delete on ship, not on park) | about 45 minutes so far of repeat download and read | small |
| 5 | **T-592**: download and read the next region while this one gates and joins | about a fifth of a clean batch (gate and join 39 minutes could hide most of 59 minutes' reading) | moderate (a second process inside the core budget) |
| 6 | **Join once per country**, after all its regions (or at the batch's end) | small for lone metros (7%); for a covered Spain about 18 re-solves of a growing group (5-10 minutes each plus re-gating every member) become one | small to moderate |
| 7 | **Two builds at a time for zones** (`--jobs 6`, 12 overnight) | builds were 30% of wall; with many small zone packs, about half of that | already supported; check memory |

A note from the UK retrospective still holds: more `--jobs` alone buys little for metros, since the

## Decisions for Anita

1. **Whole countries from now on?** Replace T-300's "a zone touches two metros" with whole-country
   cover, country by country. I'd pilot it on Spain (about 47 zones), then do each European country
   after its metros, and keep the world's metro batches going beside it.
2. **Zone size.** (a) Units as they are, under 100k merged (Spain 47, Germany about 230, Europe about
   1,000); (b) grown to about 0.3-1.5M within each region (Spain about 30, Germany about 60, Europe
   about 400); (c) Japan's 1M and up. I'd take (a) where units are provinces or departements (Spain,
   France, Italy, Poland) and (b) where they are small (Germany's Kreise, Belgium, Greece, and every
   country outside Europe).
3. **Islands and enclaves.** Small islands join their own province's largest island (Menorca to
   Mallorca, La Gomera to Tenerife). Ceuta and Melilla: their own small zones, or left out? I'd keep
   them as zones.
4. **Empty land.** Leave very empty units as dead space (say under 20 people a km²), or cover
   everything? I'd leave them out for now.
5. **A countryside commute curve.** Zones without a flow table borrow a curve from Japan's 31 fitted
   zones rather than a metro's (d^-1.4 exp(-d/16 km) against Madrid's d^-1.8 exp(-d/80 km)). It is a
   demand rule change, so it is yours. I'd do it.
6. **A Spain module** from the transport ministry's trip tables and INE's 1 km grid: now, with the
   Spain pilot, or later as tailoring? I'd do the tier 0 cover first and the module after, since it
   would also rebuild Madrid and Barcelona.
7. **Runtime first.** Split the registry before Europe-wide cover (Spain alone is fine without it).
8. **Machine time.** Whole-country runs overnight at 12 cores, two to four builds at once.
