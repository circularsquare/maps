# T-1431: plane fares and long-distance rail fares (2026-10-26)

Analysis only, no game code changed. Scripts in `notes/T-1431/` (`fares.py` every variant,
`grid.py` the fit, `finding2.py` Shanghai - Beijing and the plane's base), outputs in
`../artifacts/trainworld/T-1431/`. They apply the market rule as T-1411's scripts do, with
T-1430's waits built (15 minutes at an hourly train, the plane's flat 25). Rail is a direct line
at the real best train time, its fare on 1.05 x the great circle (the game charges the straight
line between stops) at the starting curve. A corridor's share here is the mean of "centre" (a
10 minute walk at each end) and "suburb" (a 15 minute drive at each end).

## The causes

**Both findings have one root: since T-1277 a fare dollar weighs what it weighs on a local
trip.** T-712 tuned the plane and rail against published shares at 3 minutes a dollar. T-1277
then set the weight by national income for local reasons (the US 4.4, the UK 5.3, Japan 5.6,
China 7.7; rail's curve at India's 12.2), and the market took it too. Every long fare got 1.5 to
2.6 times heavier.

**Finding 1, the plane.** Two things stack.
- The plane's $100 + 5 cents a km is a real airfare (T-712's sources: about $108 in Japan at
  766 km, $204-216 in the US under 1,000 km). Rail's starting curve is a metro curve, and on
  long trips it is half or less of real fares: Shanghai - Beijing $65 against ¥591 (about $82),
  Tokyo - Fukuoka $73 against about $150, New York - Washington $37 against $80-200 on Amtrak.
  So at the start the plane costs 2-3 times rail's ticket, where in life it costs 0.8-1.4 times
  (Beijing - Shanghai's average airfare is ¥818 against ¥591 by rail).
- That dollar gap times the new weight is 340-700 perceived minutes (Shanghai - Beijing: plane
  fare 1,189, rail 499). A long trip's logit moves about one share step per 30-45 minutes, so
  the plane gets nothing. With fares left out the two are close: the plane's door to door is 306
  minutes on Shanghai - Beijing, rail's 315 from the centre.

**Finding 2, long rail against the car.** A direct line is not hopeless today. Shanghai -
Beijing at the real 4 h 30 costs rail 814 (fare 499) from the centre and 914 from the suburbs,
against the car's 1,102: rail takes 99-100%. Even a 160 km/h line keeps 69% from the suburbs.
T-1500's 790 minutes came from its route: changing at Wuhan, the fare is charged on about 1,800
km, not 1,070, and the ride is longer, so rail costs 1,284 and gets 1%. Still, the fare is 60% of
rail's whole cost on long trips, which is the same root: the value of time behind the weight is
a local, average-income one. Long-distance travellers value time far more (US DOT's guidance:
an hour on a local trip $14, on an intercity drive $20, by air or high-speed rail $47), and in
China the people on the trains earn well above the national average.

The per-km rate itself is not the problem. In dollars, the starting curve is at or under real
long fares everywhere (a km costs about 6 cents in China against ¥0.45, about 8 in Japan against
¥27, 10 in the US against 20-40 on Amtrak).

## What the candidates do

Rail of rail + air on the ten pairs, mean error against the published figure (in log-odds,
`grid.py`): today 4.65.

| Candidate | Error | What goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Plane base lower, nothing else ($30 + 5c) | 0.79 | A cliff: each $10 moves a long pair about 25 points. US flights at $45. |
| Rail's per-km halves past 200 km | as today | Helps rail against the car, the plane stays at 0%. |
| Fare minutes capped at the trip's time | not fitted | The plane's fare caps at its short time, so it takes nearly everything from the suburbs (London - Edinburgh 1% rail of rail + air there). |
| Long-trip fares weigh less, plane unchanged | 2.8-4.0 | Rail beats the car again, but the plane's dollar gap still keeps it out. |
| **Long-trip fares weigh less, plane $40 + 5c** | **0.68** | Closest overall, half as steep as the plane alone. |

A flat half weight for every inter-city fare fits about as well (0.54), but it jumps at the
market's 25 km edge, so I prefer the taper.

## Proposal

Two changes, inter-city trips in the market only (visits, business and leisure; commutes and
outings across zone lines keep today's fares, as T-1430 did with waits):

1. **A long trip's fare counts less.** Past 50 km a fare dollar counts the country's minutes
   times (50 / km)^0.3, km being the zone pair's distance: 0.81 at 100 km, 0.62 at 250, half
   at 500, 0.41 at 1,000, a third at 2,000. The same for rail and the plane, so they stay
   balanced. In rich countries a 500 km trip lands near T-712's 3 minutes a dollar (the US 2.2,
   the UK 2.6, Japan 2.8), and the spread by income stays (China 3.9).
2. **The plane's fare is $40 + 5 cents a km** (was $100). It then sits as far under real
   airfares as rail's starting curve sits under real rail fares, so the two compare as they do
   in life: on Shanghai - Beijing $94 against rail's $65, 1.45 times (real 1.38). A player who
   raises long fares to real levels loses riders to the plane, as before.

Prices stay a base plus per-km. The taper is one formula, applied to the weight, not to any
price the player sees.

## Round 2: travellers by income group (2026-10-26)

Script `notes/T-1431/income.py`, output `../artifacts/trainworld/T-1431/income.txt`, the same
pairs and harness as round 1. Market inter-city trips only.

**The rule tested.** Each country's travellers are split into five equal groups by income (the
World Bank's income shares by quintile, SI.DST.FRST.20 to SI.DST.05TH.20, latest year,
`wb_quintiles.json`). A group's fare weight is the country's minutes a dollar times (group
income / mean)^-0.5, the exponent T-1277 already uses between countries. Each group makes long
trips in proportion to (group income / mean)^0.7, the market rule's own trip rate by income
(`eps`, `s_inc = inc^eps` in `intercity.rs`). The `u^tho` term is not income: it is how dense
the traveller's home cell is, so it does not carry over; `eps` does. Equal trips per group gives
nearly the same answer (error 0.56 against 0.51). The shares are the trip-weighted mean over the
groups.

The groups' income is 0.26-0.39 of the mean in the poorest fifth and 2.0-2.4 in the richest (the
US widest). So the richest fifth weighs fares at only 0.65-0.71 of the average, the poorest at
1.6-2.0.

**The answers.**
- **Does the income mix alone fix the plane at 0%?** No. At $100 the plane still gets 0-1%
  everywhere (error 4.62 against 4.65). Even the richest fifth weighs fares at two thirds of
  the average, which leaves a 200-450 minute gap.
- **With the plane's base retuned, the mix fits best of everything.** At $30 + 5c the error is
  0.51, against the taper's 0.68. It also keeps the car where the published figures put it, and
  the taper does not: New York - Washington 30/8/62 against 20/5/64 published, New York -
  Boston 7/4/89 against 5/4/87, Madrid 64% of all against 64%, Tokyo - Osaka 62% against 64%.
  It reads low on London - Edinburgh (23% against 45%) and Paris - Marseille (55% against
  73-82%), and high on Tokyo - Fukuoka (18% against 11%).
- **Is the taper still needed on top?** Not for the fit: the mix with the taper and a $40 plane
  scores 0.58, no better, and the car drops again (Tokyo - Osaka 5%, Madrid 1%). What the taper does
  that the mix does not is soften the cliff. With the mix each $10 on the plane's base still
  moves a long pair 10-25 points (Tokyo - Fukuoka 18 / 28 / 39 / 61% at $30 / $35 / $40 / $50);
  with the taper it is about half that.
- **Finding 2** is unchanged by the mix: a direct Shanghai - Beijing line keeps 70-88% of all
  trips. T-1500's trip still loses, for its route.
- **Why the car gains**: the poorest fifth weighs rail's fare at 1.7-2 times, while the car's
  money stays at 3 minutes a dollar for everyone (T-1277 left it), so those trips drive. That
  matches the published car shares here. If the car's money were later weighed by income too,
  this would move.

The mix also takes one structural choice, how many groups. The World Bank's quintile table has
gaps (some countries have only old surveys, a few none), so it needs a fallback, the region's
median say; I have not counted the gaps.

**Groups: 3 do as well as 5.** Equal thirds from the same Lorenz curve: error 0.53 against 0.51
at $30, shares within 1-2 points of five groups on every pair. Ten groups match five.

**Compute.** In the market's solve (`intercity.rs`, the pair loop around line 2594) each zone
pair calls `rail_logsum_x` once: up to 8 x 8 station pairs, one exponential each, then one
logit against the pair's car and plane. Each station pair's fare is already inside the route
cost, and `FareX` (T-1286) can already weigh it again by a factor from its expected fare km. So
groups need no new route search: each group is one more `rail_logsum_x` call with its own factor,
so 3 groups mean 3 logsums a pair (up to 192 exponentials instead of 64) and 3 logits. The car
and plane side (`corridor_modes`, by place pair) is computed 3 times, which is cheap. The
expected fare km table (`trip_km_on`, stations x stations) would be built every period instead
of only where a player set a fare scale. The loads loop over the station pairs runs once per
group. I have not timed how much of the market solve the pair loop is, so I cannot give the
whole solve's cost, only that this loop goes about 3x. A cheaper, inexact version: one logsum at
the mean, then each group's fare difference added on the pair's expected fare (one logsum and 3
logits). The trip explorer (`market_trip.rs`, line 269) does the same, 3 logsums for one trip on
a click, which is nothing.

**The real save**: inter-city trips only, so local fares do not move. The mix with a $30 plane
moves New York - Washington's rail by +2 points and Boston's by +4; my estimate is a few hundred
rail trips a day either way out of 12.19M. Not run.
