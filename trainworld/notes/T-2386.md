# T-2386 Demand falling behind the clock (measure and design, 2026-11-04)

Measurement and design only; no app or sim code changed. Anita decides from this note.

## The answer

- **Recomputes never pile up.** Only the newest network is ever solved: an edit (or a zone changing
  level of detail) replaces the solve in hand between populations. The time of day starts no solve,
  since one solve covers all five periods and every kind of day at once.
- **But a whole solve can never finish**, and the game can run a long stretch on figures that leave
  out an edit. Each new edit throws away the whole solve in hand, so whoever edits more often than a
  whole solve takes never gets one: on all of China that is about a minute and a half, on all of Japan
  21 minutes (2 workers). Five new lines a minute apart on China: 4 minutes with nothing landing, the clock held
  at 4x all along (16 game hours), up to five new lines showing "constructing..." and earning no fares.
- **What guards it today** is the 4x cap (T-1665), which holds only until the zone in view's figures
  are final. After a train-count or stop edit that is the local result (9-62 s on New York and
  China, 3.2 minutes on Japan); the whole solve then runs on with the clock free: to its end 20-28
  game hours pass at 16x on New York and China, 12.6 game days on Japan.
- **How wrong are the figures meanwhile?** Usually little: once the local result is in, lines are
  within about 1% of the whole solve (T-1733). Two cases are far off: a line still reading
  "constructing..." (no riders and no fares, though its trains run and cost), and a new or changed
  long line, whose longer trips stay as last solved (T-1760: a new express showed 26% of them).
- So yes, it happens at the sizes the country saves reach, and at 16x and up on a city game. The fix
  is small (below), plus landing T-1734, which is the real answer to "never finishing".

## How it works today

Docs: demand-runtime.md "Clusters", "The local solve after an edit"; architecture.md "Only final
results replace final results"; ui-panels.md "The clock", "Status".

- An edit sends the new network; the workers drop the solve in hand at their next population. With a
  local solve possible, the touched zones are solved against everything else frozen and shown at once
  (clearing "constructing..."), then 8 s later the whole solve of every touched cluster runs and
  replaces all of it. A new line went straight to the whole solve on both saves measured (the status
  step read 0/1, no local step), so its riders and the cap wait for the whole solve.
- "Demand final" for the clock: the cap lifts once the zone in view is final (`viewRecomputing` false,
  `lineFigures.ts`), even while other zones, the longer trips and the network totals are still solving.
- Money: fares are paid from whatever is shown (`incomeByDay`, local results included); running costs
  follow the timetable at once. So a line reading "constructing..." costs from the moment it runs and
  earns nothing until its riders land.
- The player sees "Trips here being recomputed…" (or "Trips in 171 cities…") while capped; once the
  zone in view is final the tag goes, though a whole solve may run on for minutes.

## Measurements

`measure.mjs` (scratch, kept beside the outputs in `../artifacts/trainworld/t2386/`): import a save
into `?new=1&paused=1`, wait until nothing solves, set 16x, make one edit at a time through the game's
edit calls and sample the page's own signals (`clock.capped`, `demandUpdating`, the view's version)
every 0.4 s. `?perfOff=focus` (the load's second pool) to keep the page inside its core claim.

**The machine was saturated throughout** (CPU 99-100%; 11-14 of the 15-core cap claimed, a pipeline
join at 8 jobs running beside the Japan run). New York's load took 118 s against 38-69 s on 3 workers
in earlier notes (T-756, T-786), so read every time below as about 2-3 times an idle machine's.

Game time from 1x = 1 game minute a wall second (`SPEEDS`), and the cap at 4x while it holds.

**Edits faster than the whole solve** (China, five new 3-station lines 60 s apart): from the first
edit at 442 s to 681 s nothing landed (the solve's version climbed 7 to 12, the shown one stayed at
6), "constructing..." on 4-5 lines, the clock at 4x throughout: 16 game hours. A whole solve landed
only after a gap between edits outlasted it.

**All of Japan** (2 workers, claim 4, beside a pipeline join at 8 jobs): load 22.5 minutes; the
edit's local result 3.2 minutes after it (6 lines "constructing..." until then), then 18 minutes of
whole solve with the clock free. No level-of-detail change replaced that solve in its 18 minutes (the
view did not move). Earlier figures on 3 workers (T-1733, 2026-10-28, a busy machine): load
17 minutes, a new Tokyo line's local result 118-155 s after it was built, the whole solve 1,020-1,199 s.
T-1734 (built in tree tw/t1734, not landed): building a line a minute on all of Japan, a whole solve
lands every 11-14 minutes only with it; on master none lands at that pace.

**Cores.** The page used `navigator.hardwareConcurrency` 16, pools of 3 and 2 as noted. A player's
default is 3 solving workers on 12+ threads, 2 on 6-11, 1 on phones (architecture.md "The demand
pool"); "Use more cores" gives up to 5. Five periods over 3 workers put two big periods on one worker
(T-1733 "What bounds Japan's time"), so 5 workers solve a whole solve up to about twice as fast, 2
workers about 1.5 times slower than 3, and 1 worker 2.5-5 times. A phone on all of China is the
worst case: a whole solve of several minutes, every edit inside that throwing it away.

## Elsewhere

- **OpenTTD's cargodist** is the closest match: link graph jobs run on a thread and must finish
  within a set number of game days (`recalc_time`); if one has not, the game blocks until it does.
  The JGRPP patch pack pauses the game instead, so the UI stays live
  ([OpenTTD-patches readme](https://github.com/stormcone/OpenTTD-patches),
  [linkgraph docs](https://code.theleruby.com/cpp/openttd-patchpack/source/annotate/85c710859649115ac1340d21643792c3a3b381aa/docs/linkgraph.txt)).
  Its settings trade lag for staleness, and a job that is behind is always finished, never restarted.
- **Factorio** slows the whole game when a tick takes longer than 1/60 s; the UPS counter shows it
  ([wiki: game tick](https://wiki.factorio.com/Game-tick)).
- **Cities: Skylines II** shows simulation speed as a percent of the speed chosen; players traced
  drops to pending pathfinding queries
  ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/949230/discussions/0/4364625713977633758)).
- Paradox games (Victoria 3) just run their days slower when the CPU falls behind
  ([forum](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/victoria-3-significant-slowdown-after-several-decades.1553214)).

The common shape: finish the job in hand, and tie game time to it (block, pause or slow) only once it
is late by a set amount of game time. Nobody restarts a late job, which is what trainworld does now.

## Proposed design

**1. Land T-1734 first** (the whole solve runs to its end; edits made meanwhile wait and go locally on
top). It is what makes "never finishing" impossible; nothing below can promise a landing without it.

**2. How wrong, cheaply**, all on the page from figures it already holds:
- **Behind**: game hours since the oldest edit no whole solve covers yet (set at the first edit after
  a whole final, cleared when a whole final covering it lands).
- **Missing**: lines reading "constructing..." (riders and fares not counted at all).
- **Long**: lines edited since the last whole solve whose longer trips matter: at least 10% of their
  riders on longer trips in that solve (demand-views "loads by purpose"), or, for a new line, a
  one-way run of 20 minutes or more (T-1735's threshold).
- Any other edit is within about 1% after its local result (T-1733), and counts for nothing.

**3. What to do**, by those:
- Any line **missing**, anywhere: clock at most 4x (today's rule, widened from the zone in view to every
  zone, so panning away no longer lifts it while a new line earns nothing).
- A **long** line edited and **behind** over 6 game hours: at most 4x until the whole solve lands.
- **Missing** or **long**, and **behind** over a game day: at most 1x, and the status tag says so.
- Nothing else caps or warns, however far behind (a city edit's figures are already right).

With T-1734 and these, China at 16x after a train-count edit on a long line runs 9 s capped, then
16x until 6 game hours (about 20 s), then 4x to the whole solve: about 9.5 game hours behind at the
end instead of 20 (if line 23 counts as long). A city line's edit is not held at all after its local
result. Japan's measured edit, if line 386 counts as long: 4x for its 3.2 minutes, 4x for 2.8 more,
then 1x for the last 15 minutes of the whole solve: about 39 game hours behind at the end instead of
12.6 days, but a player at 16x sits at normal speed for 15 minutes. **The open call:** that
1x step, or stop at 4x and only show the tag (Japan then ends about 3.5 days behind).

**Strings** (status tag; the speed button reuses its hover with the cap's number):
- new: "Trips 1 day behind" / "Trips 2 days behind" (the tag's text while capped at 1x)
- new hover: "Clock capped at 1x while updating demand" (today's "Clock capped at 4x while updating
  demand" with the number)
- the speed button: "16x (capped at 1x while updating demand)" from today's pattern, no new text

**Cost.** Parts 2-3: `editCap.ts` and `lineFigures.ts` (the behind clock, missing and long sets),
`clock.ts` (`DEMAND_CAP` becomes a cap value, 4 or 1), `StatusTag.tsx` and `TopBar.tsx` (read it), and
a check that drives China with `?demandWorkers=1` and edits a minute apart. About half a day, a
Sonnet task once the thresholds are settled. T-1734: its tree is six days behind master (T-1764,
T-2188, T-2180 since), so a rebase and re-measure, about a day, Opus.

**Also found.** A new line skipped the local solve on both saves (status step 0/1; New York 70 s and
China 99 s capped through to the whole solve), though T-1733 showed a local result for a new line on
Japan. Possibly the cluster's market part is new after the stations are added, so nothing is held to
freeze (`demand.worker.ts` `editOf`). Worth its own task: with it, new lines would clear in seconds.
