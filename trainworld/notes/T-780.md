# T-780: the market's zone pairs dealt in chunks (with T-767), 2026-10-20

Anita (2026-10-20), on the market's cost on all of Japan (about 85 s a period, 3.4 GB of a 4 GB
worker): "if we have 100 stations in one city and 100 in the other, we don't necessarily need to
track every pair. Perhaps we do some random sampling. Like if a station in Philly has 50 people
that want to go to New York, we can maybe split it into chunks of ~20 people and assign them to
stations in New York, rather than splitting it into 50 trips. Do we already do this?"

## For Anita

**What it does now.** The market never splits trips into single riders. It works on 6 km zones:
every pair of zones that track joins gets its trips a day as a number (0.01 or 500), and rail's
share of them is a fraction. Each zone has up to 8 stations to start from and 8 to end at, so a
zone pair spreads its rail riders as fractions over up to 64 station pairs. A pair with 0.01 riders
costs exactly as much work as one with 500. The routes then pool: one search per destination
station routes everybody bound for it from everywhere at once, so the routing scales with stations,
not pairs.

On all of Japan that is 9,114 zones, 12.4 million zone pairs and 494 million station pairs, with
8,502 destination stations. Most pairs are tiny: 82% carry under 0.1 trips in the midday period and
together hold 6% of the trips. On the New York save it is 713 zones, 378,000 zone pairs and 7.4
million station pairs; half the pairs carry under 0.1 trips. So the waste you guessed is real, but
it sits in the zone pairs rather than in riders.

**What I built.** Your chunks, one level up: for each zone, its trips with each other place are
dealt in chunks of about 1 trip a day to that place's zones, in proportion to each zone's trips,
from a starting point fixed by the two zones' ids. A pair of at least 1 trip a day always keeps its
own pair; smaller ones get a chunk or nothing. Each zone's trips with each place stay exact, and
the same network always gives the same figures. Mode choice and station choice inside each pair
stay as they were. A station that newly serves a zone only adds that zone's pairs; the rest are
not dealt again.

**What it moves.** Whole game, the real demand worker, every figure the network shows: on all of
Japan the network's rail trips move under 0.002% a period, the market's own rail 0.06-0.17%, the
day's boardings 0.001%, the worst line 0.25% (56 riders). The seven cities: the worst line 0.008%.
New York: line boardings identical, the worst total 0.006%. Looking at the market alone, the
biggest move is at small stations on long lines: on all of Japan the worst is a station with 7
long-distance riders a period that gets 5.

**What it saves** (all of Japan, 2 workers as in your browser):

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| zone pairs | 12.4 million | 3.1 million |
| the market's pass, a daytime period | 83-93 s | 62-70 s |
| the whole first solve | 1,256-1,288 s | 965 s |
| a worker's memory | 3.1-3.6 GB | 2.2-2.4 GB |
| in the browser, demand's final | 1,150 s | 1,034 s |

The seven cities gain less, 327-353 s to 278 s (their market is mostly commuting between
neighbouring zones, which is not sampled); New York nothing to speak of.

I also tried your literal version, each pair's riders dealt to a few station pairs. It saves about
another eighth of the market's time, but moves stations' figures several times as much (on New
York 6% of all station entries, the worst station 78%), because a busy pair then sends everyone
through its best few station pairs. So it is not in.

What is still slow: the routing, one search per destination station, twice a pass (about half the
market's time now). Keeping more of those searches between the two rounds halves the second one
natively, but in the browser's WASM growing memory for them costs minutes, so that stays as it was.

## How the market holds trips now

Counted natively on the networks `checks/t756-reach.mjs capture` recorded (`t780_census` in
`sim/src/demand/intercity/tests.rs`, `checks/t761-plain-net.mjs`), midday (period 1, 35% of the
longer trips), before T-780:

| | New York save | Japan, seven cities | all of Japan |
|---|---|---|---|
| served 6 km zones | 713 | 3,724 | 9,114 |
| stations (merged) / destinations searched | 873 / 647 | 5,273 / 4,561 | 9,253 / 8,502 |
| directed zone pairs | 377,860 | 1,614,808 | 12,412,720 |
| station pairs over them | 7.4M | 57.8M | 494M |
| commute pairs | 5,645 | 57,156 | 97,307 |
| pairs under 0.1 trips a period | 51%, 0.7% of trips | 61%, 1.3% | 82%, 6% of trips, 11% of rail |

- A **zone pair** has its trips a day each way from the rule (a number, never whole riders) and its
  rail share by the logit against car and plane on its logsum over its **station pairs**: up to 8
  access stations at each end (walk or drive), so up to 64.
- Its rail riders are then shared as **fractions** over all its station pairs (`demand`): no pair
  is too small to spread over all 64, so the work is per station pair whatever the riders.
- **Routing already pools**: one strategy search per destination station (`DestSearch`) gives every
  origin's cost to it, and its column of riders from every station is assigned along it at once
  (`assigned`). On all of Japan a sweep of 8,502 searches is 17-23 s natively, done twice a pass
  (T-761 keeps a quarter of the strategies between them).
- Where the time went natively, all of Japan, one pass at free flow: a search sweep 22.6 s, the
  logsums alone 8.8 s, `demand` 27.2 s (so spreading riders over station pairs about 18 s), the
  pass 61-86 s (another batch was running).
- Far places (250 km and more) already pool their trip counts: a place pair's trips are spread over
  its zone pairs by shares (T-377). That pools the rule, not the work.

## Built

- `solved_pairs` (intercity.rs): which served zone pairs are solved and their trips, counted
  before `set_served` makes the table (T-761's counting, moved). With `chunk`, for each served zone
  a and each run of one place's zones after a in zone order: its candidates' trips (every zone, served
  or not, with at least `MIN_TRIPS` and a car or plane), `draws = ceil(total / chunk)`; with fewer
  candidates than draws all are kept, else systematic sampling at `(j + u) x total / draws`, `u`
  from `unit_hash(a's gid, the run's first gid)`, each candidate getting its draws times the step;
  kept when served and joined. A place with no served zone, or with neither car nor plane from a's
  place, is skipped whole (neither depends on which pairs are dealt).
- `Market::eachway_with` and `curves`: `eachway` with the curves worked out once a pair (a far run
  once): the same to the bit (test `eachway_with_its_curves_is_eachway`); set-up on all of Japan
  4.7-8.0 s with every pair against 2.3-2.5 s sampled.
- `IntercityApi::set_sampled` (wasm), `chunk`; the worker calls it before each market part's
  network, `!job.exactMarket`; `?perfOff=chunks` sends `exactMarket`.
- `selection`: with chunks, a selected zone's rail with a place is the sum over its sampled pairs,
  spread over the place's zones it could pair with (`solvable`) by their trips, so no far zone
  shows more rail than trips. `station_flows` is the sampled pairs' as they are.
- Tests `sampled_pairs_keep_each_zones_trips_with_each_place` (Tokyo and Gunma packs, every zone
  served: deterministic, every sampled pair a pair, none of 1 trip or more left out, each zone's
  trips with each later place equal to every pair's), `sampled_selection_rail_stays_within_trips`.
- Benches (ignored tests): `t780_census`, `t780_compare` (`TW_T780_CHUNKS`), `t780_keep`.
- `checks/t756-reach.mjs compare <label> chunks` (`exact` against the default), `ROWS=` a copy of
  the market rows; `TW_REGISTRY` / `TW_ROWS` for the native benches (the France batch was writing
  the registry: one bench run picked up new places mid-run).
