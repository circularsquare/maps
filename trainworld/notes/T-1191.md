# T-1191: zone rules for the next countries; Korea's plan ready to build (2026-10-24)

T-1140's dry runs (notes/T-1140.md section 8) found what Korea and Canada needed before their plans.
The rules are in `pipeline/world_plan.py` (tree t1191); the dry runs went through the planner's own
`plan_zones` into scratch plans, never into `pipeline/world_plan.json`.

## 1. The rules

**Per country** (`ZONE_RULES`; both take the US's 0.5M floor, 1.5M cap, no density floor and no
exclaves, `contiguous`):

- **Korea: zones of whole si, gun and gu** (`group_level: 1`). The plan's Korean units are eup,
  myeon and dong (geoBoundaries ADM3, 3,450), the grain the four metro rows were planned on. Zoned
  one by one they made dong-named zones ("Seonjuwonnamdong") whose lines cut through cities.
  `group_units` now takes the free units by their ADM2 parent (si, gun or gu, 228 in geoBoundaries,
  large si whole: Cheongju, Changwon), split into pieces that touch or lie within 10 km across water;
  the pieces are the units the merge rule sees. A metro row or Seoul's pack holding part of a gun
  leaves the rest as its own piece (Gijang, Dalseong). 21 dongs with no ADM2 parent (all of
  Yeonggwang-gun, missing from the ADM2 file) stay single units.
- **Korea's names**: a grouped zone is named after the group holding its densest square km (its
  origin), "-si" and "-gun" dropped; a gu takes its metropolitan city's name (Ulsan's zone, whose
  densest km is in Nam-gu). The most populous group would misname Jeju: GHSL people fall badly into
  geoBoundaries' dong polygons there (Namwon-eup, a town of about 19,000, gets 74,000 and Jeju city's
  dongs too few), so the Seogwipo group outweighed Jeju-si's. Two zones of the same name would carry
  the province (none in Korea's run).
- **Korea's islets**: `contiguous` gives them T-897's handling: an island zone joins the nearest zone
  within 10 km across water, else an island zone of its region within 50 km, else it stays, and under
  10,000 people it is dead space. Dead space: Ongjin's islands (Baengnyeong, Daecheong, Yeonpyeong,
  Deokjeok, Yeongheung), Ulleung and Wido, 18,600 people. Gadeokdo joins Geoje's zone with Busan's
  other Gangseo-gu remnant. Ancheon-myeon (inland, cut off by dead space under T-1140's 20 a km²
  test) is gone with the density floor.
- **Canada: cities and the corridors between** (`reach: (150, 100000)`): a census subdivision whose
  own centre (its polygon's centroid) has fewer than 100,000 people within 150 km is dead space.
  People are GHS-POP on 5 km cells, every country's (Windsor counts Detroit). That is a mean density
  of 1.4 a km² over a 150 km disc: it keeps the land between cities within reach of each other and
  drops the north; the territories need no rule of their own. The centroid, not the people centre,
  so a giant unorganized unit beside a city (Thunder Bay, Unorganized: 112,000 km² reaching 51° N)
  is judged by its own middle and goes. First set at 100 km (1,668 units and 2.21M people dead,
  Fort McMurray, Gander and Yarmouth among them); the orchestrator asked for 150 km so those towns
  count (2026-10-24). By centroid (scratch `can150b.py`), 150 km leaves 907 units and 0.97M people
  dead. Corner Brook stays out even so: 70,000 people within 150 km. A floor of 70,000 would keep it, with
  Stephenville, Swift Current, Gaspé, Terrace, Hinton, Smithers and Kitimat; 50,000 adds Sept-Îles,
  Dryden and Chibougamau (0.57M people dead in all). The urban-centre
  distance tried first fails in Canada: GHSL has no urban centre at Sudbury, Saguenay, Thunder Bay,
  Moncton, Saint John or Fredericton (their cores are under 1,500 a km²).
- **Canada: no join between people centres over 150 km apart** (`join_km`, great-circle distance).
  Across dead space a zone's few neighbours are all far, and gravity between their facing edges
  (both zones reach out far and meet halfway) makes any two partners: with only the
  reach rule, Regina joined Saskatoon as partner 0.62, Rimouski joined Fredericton across 300 km,
  Abitibi joined Sudbury, and Sudbury joined Barrie. Mollweide's own distances run short at 45° N
  (Moncton to Halifax, 186 km, measured under 150), hence `ground_km`.
- **Canada's units**: the plan state's (`plan_units`), the ones its metro rows were planned on, not
  geoBoundaries read afresh: `fill_gaps` takes Natural Earth's land as the country, which holds the
  Great Lakes, and handed Lake Huron's 36,500 km² and Erie's 13,400 km² to shore units.
- **Canada's names**: geoBoundaries' Canadian names are UTF-8 read as Latin-1 ("QuÃ©bec") and cut at
  30 characters with a "*"; `zone_name` repairs both.

**General** (every country planned from now on):

- **Two cities never join by shape alone** (orchestrator, 2026-10-24): when both zones hold the
  centroid of a GHSL urban centre (UCDB R2024A) of 100,000 or more (`CITY_POP`), only the partner test
  can join them, so a city's zone just under the floor stays a zone.
- **A unit under 100,000 joins its biggest partner whose union wraps nothing** (the ring test),
  the biggest only when every join wraps (follow-up, 2026-10-24). Before, these small joins skipped
  the test, so remnant pieces built rings round inland metros that left their neighbours with no
  join: Gongju (0.18M) could join nothing without wrapping Daejeon.
- **Dead space counts as one area in the partner test** (`travel`): before, a zone ringed by dead
  space had its one far neighbour as its biggest partner by default (Regina joined Saskatoon and
  Sudbury Barrie, each "partner 1.00" across 250 km). Travel into dead space now competes. On its
  own it was not enough in Canada (Regina still passed at 0.62), hence `join_km` there; it changes
  nothing in Korea.
- 100,000 was chosen over GHSL's 50,000 floor so small centres (Grande Prairie 57,000, Lethbridge
  63,000) do not count as cities. In Korea the rule changes nothing (a run without it gives the same
  zones).
- **Unbuilt metro rows take their remnants** (orchestrator, 2026-10-24, under Anita's "built cities
  are not fixed"; `metro_remnants`, where no commuting table gives belts). Two steps before zoning:
  1. *The rest of a city the row mostly holds*: with `group_level`, a si, gun or gu of which the row
     has at least half the people (Gwangsan-gu's rest with Gwangju, 98%; Naju 61%, Okcheon 56%).
     Repeated, so a piece beyond a piece that joined follows.
  2. *Remnants under 100,000 whose biggest gravity partner is the row* as it stands after step 1
     (every unit, metro, pack and dead space as one area counted), judged once and touching the row
     as it stood then.
  Each join needs a land border with the row (an island: within 10 km across water) and must leave
  the row wrapping nothing it did not wrap before; units under 100,000 the join would enclose come
  along (Alberta's towns inside their counties). Built rows and packs never grow; the log notes each
  unit whose biggest partner is one.
  Forms tried and dropped: **any piece of a si or gun the row touches** (`kor6.log`) took all of
  Changwon (0.87M) into Busan, since Busan's row holds a few of its dongs; **partners judged as the
  row grows, and 10 km over land** (`kor7.log`) let rows run on from gun to gun with exclaves
  (Daejeon reached Mungyeong and Buyeo, Gwangju took Jindo and Wando across Mokpo's zone); **any wrap
  refused** (`can8.log`, stopped) blocked every join round Montreal, whose row already wraps
  Chambly; **enclosed towns not taken along** (`can9.log`) refused Alberta's counties for wrapping
  their own towns (Wheatland County round Strathmore and Hussar).
