## The fitted rule

A resident of site *i* makes two kinds of departures a weekday, each production constrained on its
own over the world field around *i* (`world_field.around`, never the zone list):

- **Ground**, unchanged from T-276: `0.107 x s_i`, weight `a_j b_ij on(d) max(d,25)^-2.37`.
- **Tail**: `Rt x s_i x u_i^0.50 / 1.241`, weight `a_j u_j^0.75 min(y_j/y_i, 1)^1.0 b_ij ont(d) d^-0.70`.

`s_i = min(y_i/y_US, 1)^0.7`; `a_j = pop_j min(y_j/y_US, 1)^0.7`; `u = r5 cell people / 100,000`
(1.241 is its people-weighted mean over the US to the power 0.5, so the US average stays Rt);
`on` 0 at 25 km to 1 at 50; `ont` 0 at 300 km to 1 at 700; `b_ij` 1 within a country, else
`0.26 x min(y_i/y_US, 1)^1.50` (the origin's income). **Rt 0.00473** a weekday.

## Why the rule differs from T-362's design

Each change was forced by a check failing badly, not by fine tuning.

1. **The tail has its own rate and normaliser** (T-362 had one normaliser over ground plus k x tail).
   With one normaliser a sparse place's ground term is small, so the tail takes its share: US-wide
   residents made 3x the Northeast's long trips, while NHTS has the two within 10%. On the same
   targets (an earlier target set) the split form scored 6.5 against 8.9 for one normaliser. Stage 3 keeps two numbers per
   zone (`Σ O/N` for each segment) instead of one.
2. **The tail starts at 300 km** and the ground curve keeps everything below unchanged, so near
   pairs (under 250 km) and the T-276 fit are untouched.
3. **Income works relative to the origin for the tail.** With destination income^0.7 alone the
   world's poor, populous places pulled hard: the trial gave US residents 1.9 trips abroad a year
   (0.3 real) with Asia 30x too high, and in a fit with a stronger absolute income term India's
   residents went abroad 22x too often, since every domestic destination was "poor". Now a poorer
   destination is damped by `(y_j/y_i)^1.0` and a rich one is not boosted.
4. **The border factor falls with the origin's income** (`eps_b` 1.5): India's residents abroad
   0.02 trips a year (real 0.02), the UK's 1.16 (1.39) and the US's 0.57 (0.30) with one rule.
5. **Origin density** (THo 0.5): city residents make more tail trips than rural ones; it lifts the
   big-metro pairs without raising the national rate.

GDP elasticity 0.7 stays (the brief: only if the data clearly reject it). A sea crossing factor
(abroad across a landmass boundary) was tried and fitted to 1.0, so it is not in the rule.

## Targets and weights

Least squares on log ratios, soft L1 (scale 0.5), judgement weights:

- NHTS 2017 all-mode non-commute, 300-5,000 km bands (under 300 is the ground's): Northeast weight
  3 x sqrt(n/100) capped at 3, US half that. NHTS weighs 3x the first try because it is a national
  survey and the flights side rests on the judged 0.6.
- US residents abroad by region (NTTO 2019, 99.7M trips: Canada and Mexico 54.5M, Europe 18.4M,
  Asia and Oceania 8.2M, the rest 18.6M) weight 1 each; over 5,000 km about 0.1 a head a year
  (judgement) weight 0.7. Japan 0.16 (JNTO 2019 20.1M departures), the UK 1.39 (ONS Travel Trends
  2019 93.1M visits), India 0.02 (26.9M departures), weight 1 each.
- 538 flights metro pairs over 1,000 km with direct service, target 0.6 x segment passengers; three
  distance classes of equal total weight (about 3 bands each), pairs within a class by sqrt(size).
- NEC FUTURE: New York - Boston, New York - Washington, Boston - Washington; New York -
  Philadelphia reported only (a definition gap, T-299).

## Checks

- **Outbound share (Risks)**: departures over 5,000 km are 1.4% of US residents' (6.8% in the
  trial), 1.3% of the world sample's (7.7%); trips abroad 4.7% of the world's departures (23%).
  China's residents 0.6% abroad, India 0.4%, Bangladesh 7.7%.
- **US outbound per head**: 0.57 trips a year against 0.30 (trial 1.92), Asia carrying most of
  the excess.
- **MLIT 2015 (Japan, domestic, trips of 100 km and more)**: model 73% at 100-300 km, 19% at
  300-700, 5% at 700-1,000, 2.5% beyond; MLIT 71% / 21% / 3.8% / 5% (its bands are route km, ours
  straight line). Domestic trips over 300 km 0.0061 a person a weekday against MLIT's about 0.0076
  (4.02M weekday prefecture-crossing trips, 24% over 300 route km). A no-mode model can say no more:
  the 300-700 band (rail's in MLIT) and beyond 700 (air's) both carry about the right shares.

## What is still off, and why it was left

- **Japan's residents go abroad 6x too often** (0.97 trips a year against 0.16), and East Asian
  neighbour pairs run 2-4x high (Seoul - Tokyo 3.6x, Hong Kong - Seoul, Hong Kong - Tokyo). One
  border rule serves the US, the UK and India; Japan's home bias is stronger than its income says.
  A per-country factor is the natural fix and belongs with country tailoring.
- **NHTS US bands at 300-1,000 km are 2-2.7x** and the Northeast's 300-500 km 2.4-3.1x: that is
  the ground curve (the trial and T-276 are the same there), which was fitted to the Northeast's
  50-1,000 km ground trips as a whole. Not this task's to refit.
- **Big domestic pairs go both ways**: US and Chinese and Indian trunk pairs 1.5-2.2x high, New
  York - Miami and New York - Boston low, long intercontinental pairs (New York - London) 0.4x,
  and the medians 0.5-0.7 of the 0.6 x segments target. The 0.6 is a guess, hubs (Dubai,
  Istanbul, Singapore) carry far more connecting traffic than it allows, and the May sample is
  peak season, so a median under 1 is not alarming. NHTS and the flights cannot both be met: the
  flights pairs alone ask for about twice NHTS's long-trip rate.
- **New York - Philadelphia** 3.1x: the T-299 seam gap, stage 5.

## For stage 3 (T-377)

- Two normalisers per site, so two numbers per zone; the tail needs the origin's income per pair
  (`min(y_J/y_I, 1)` and the border's `y_I` factor) as well as `a_J`.
- The tail's destination weight sums `pop x inc^0.7 x (pop_r5/1e5)^0.75` over r5 cells; for r4 and
  r3 cells it must be that r5 sum (one more column in the world field or computed at load), not
  the coarse cell's own population to the power.
- `pipeline/longhaul.json` has every constant; `u_mean_us` is the origin density's normaliser.
