## For Anita, in plain words

**Your model works.** A double in the middle and a one-way single track on each side is exactly how this game can
already hold four tracks paired by direction: every piece it needs exists (double track, one-way single track,
single track joined to one track of a double, flyover legs). Subway Builder builds its quad the same way: one line
on the inner tracks, the other on single-track outer tracks, tracks 1 and 2 one way, 3 and 4 the other.

**What a player would do**
1. Pick the new quad-by-direction icon in the track bar and draw from a double. The stroke lays the middle pair plus
   a one-way track each side, 4 m apart like quad. Where it starts on double track, each outer track peels off on its
   own side, so nothing crosses.
2. Carry on from the end of a quad (either kind) with the pointer: on the middle, the middle pair carries on; on a
   side, that side's tracks carry on. With double track picked and the pointer on a side, both outer tracks become a
   new double heading off that way, the far one going over the middle pair on a bridge: a flying split, no flat
   crossing. With single track picked, only that one outer track carries on. With quad picked on the middle, all four
   carry on. This is the same rule as carrying a single on from a double's end today (left track, right track, or
   both merged when you point at the middle).
3. Or pick a stretch of double with Select and turn it into quad by direction, the way doubling up makes quad.
4. Stations: the station bar keeps the 1 and 2 track icons and gets a 4. The icon says how many tracks get a
   platform; the pointer says which, as with track. On quad by direction, 2 on the middle gives the middle pair, 2 on
   a side gives both outer tracks, 4 gives all four. On ordinary quad, 2 gives the pair under the pointer and 4 both
   pairs (that is T-3363).
5. The station panel shows a small picture of the station's tracks, which way each runs, its platforms as grey
   blocks and the lines that stop on each track. Click a platform to take it away, a dashed gap to add one. No
   "inner / outer / both" words anywhere.
6. Lines: a line stops on the tracks of the platform you clicked when adding the stop. A stop at a station with
   platforms on more than one set of tracks gets a little track picture in the stop list; click it to move the line
   to the other tracks there. So: express on the middle pair, local on the outer tracks, and at a station with all
   four, a rider changes from local to express across the island.

**Riders**: a change at one station already costs the same small amount whatever the layout (2 perceived minutes, the
wait, and a walk of a few metres). So cross-platform changes are short already, but no shorter than any other change
within a station. Making them cheaper would be a new demand factor, so it's your call (I'd leave it).

**What's hard**
- The big one: a local's two directions stop at different station pieces (one outer track each way). Today a line's
  way back stops at the same station pieces as its way out, so a local on the outer tracks has no way back. The fix
  is a general rule: a line stops at a station, and each direction uses the platform on its own track. That same
  rule also covers one-track stations in pairs and one-way street pairs for trams.
- The flying split reuses the automatic flyover, but that code starts its legs at a junction on the main line, not
  at the end of an outer track, and it needs about half a kilometre of room.
- Knowing that three loose ends side by side are "one quad end" for the pointer: the game has no such idea yet, so
  the map works it out from where the ends lie, the way T-3356 finds the station beside.
- Small rules to adjust: the Left/Right running switch doesn't turn round one-way tracks with two loose ends; prices.

**How big**: about **27-39 hours** for the whole package as one deliverable, in five internal stages, probably
toward the low end (T-3332 and T-3333 were each estimated at 11-16 h and each built within a day; T-3137's double
track rework, estimated in days, took about 8 h). Two trees can run at once (stations and stops in one, track drawing
and extending in the other).

## How things stand (what this builds on)

- **Double track** is one piece with two lanes (T-3137); a path entry `edge << 1 | dir` names the lane, so "one track
  of a double" and "one direction" are the same thing. `lanes.rs` holds at most two lanes a piece.
- **One-way single track** (T-3094) runs as a directed track with double's per-direction capacity; its way is set or
  inferred from its joins (`derive_ways`). **Joins** (T-3137 C2) attach a single end to one track of a double at a
  node of three or more ends, or at a double's loose end (T-3190).
- **Flyovers** (T-3092, T-3170): a double route making a junction on double track lays a plain leg and a crossing
  leg, both one-way single, the crossing leg 12 m off the main, over it on a ramp; least room 234.5 m per ramp.
- **Quad** (T-255, T-708): two double pieces 8 m apart, express pair to the left; `express_verts` lays a pair
  concentric at any spacing (`side` scales `QUAD_SPACING`; drawing beside track uses it at 12 m). A quad end in open
  ground leaves the pairs' ends apart; drawing on from them carries both pairs (`quad_beside`).
- **Stations** are nodes splitting the piece; one per click, on the pair clicked (T-1687), twins named alike
  (`twin_at`). T-3332 (landed in tree tw/onetrack): a platform on one lane of a double (`StationData::one`), a
  two-way line through it stops one way and passes the other (`Run::pass`). T-3333/T-3356: a line on both pairs
  through a hidden twin line; the station beside a stop is the nearest station of its complex on a track beside
  (`Network::complex_of`, `station_beside`).
- **Lines** store stops as nodes and one forward path; the return run is the path backwards, or, past one-way track,
  `back_path_of` routes the **same stop nodes** reversed (`net.rs`); `build_run` matches the run's nodes to the stop
  nodes (`match_stops`). So today a local whose stops sit on an eastbound-only outer track has no way back: the line
  is broken.
- **Pointer picking at a double's end** (`tools.ts` `joinPoint`, T-3137 C3, T-3204): with single track picked, the
  pointer's side picks a track; within `DEAD_PX` of the centre line zoomed out, or between the tracks at a loose end,
  it merges both. Her smart extension builds on this.
- **Demand**: stations within 100 m are one demand station; a change costs a fixed 2 perceived minutes, the wait
  (half the combined headway) and the walk between the two platforms' centres (T-717). Outer and middle stations sit
  6 m apart, so a local-express change is about 2 minutes plus the wait, the same as a change between quad pairs.

## Options

### (a) Her model, built from existing pieces (recommended)

The middle pair is an ordinary double; each outer track an ordinary one-way single, 6 m from the centre line (4 m
from the nearer middle track, the same footprint as quad), its way stored. New pieces:
1. **Per-direction stops**: a line stops at a station; each run stops at the platform on the track it runs on. A
   stop stays a node (the platform the player picked, which also says which tracks the line prefers); a run that
   reaches that node's place going a way its track does not carry uses the station of the same complex on a track
   beside, running its way (`station_beside`, extended to want a way). `back_path_of` routes through those nodes,
   `build_run` matches them, each run keeps its own stop nodes (`Run::stop_nodes`), and the readers that pair a
   run's stop index with `line_stops` read the run's own. Generalises T-3332's "stop one way, pass the other": stop
   at the other-way platform beside where there is one, else pass.
2. **Drawing preset**: a track bar icon; the route op lays the middle double and two `express_verts` strokes at
   `side` +-0.75 (6 m) as one-way singles, joined to the double's tracks where an end meets double track, ends apart
   in open ground (as quad's do). Doubling up by direction in the Select bar for a picked double stretch.
3. **End groups and the pointer**: the map treats loose ends lying side by side (within quad spacing, parallel) as one
   end; `joinPoint`'s screen-distance rule decides middle or side.
4. **Flying split**: a double drawn from an end group's side: the near outer track is the plain leg, the far one a
   crossing leg over the middle pair to J3 (`flyover.rs`, legs starting at the outer ends instead of J). Ramp switch
   off: it crosses flat.

What a player sees: four tracks with arrows, outer ones drawn as one-way single track; the middle pair a double.
Save impact: none new for track (one-way and joins exist); per-direction stops are derived, not stored; platform
slots (below) a few bits per station.

### (b) A first-class four-lane piece

One piece with four lanes, two per direction. Stations one node with platforms per lane. Cleaner stop model (one
node serves both ways), but a path entry would need a lane as well as a direction (`edge << 2 | lane << 1 | dir`),
which every reader of paths, runs, capacity, joins, flyovers, rebuilds and the save changes with: a second T-3137,
**about 40-60 h** with a long tail and a parity check rewrite. It also points the wrong way for her station editor:
there, tracks should be separate pieces routed into a station, not lanes hidden inside one piece. Not recommended.

### (c) Simpler: ordinary quad plus platform choice

Two pairs (T-3333) + quad station in one click (T-3363) + the stop list's track chip. Locals on one pair, expresses on
the other, changes as cheap for riders as under (a) (the demand model sees no difference), and a pair branching to
its own side needs no flyover. About **9-12 h**. Misses the directional layout and her flying split, and she asked
for one package, so it is only a fallback. It is also contained in (a): stage 4 below is (c).

## Stations

**One model for both kinds of quad, and a step toward her station editor.** Anita 2026-11-12: she wants, later, a
station editor with platforms placed and shaped by hand and tracks routed into an arbitrarily shaped station. So the
platform choice is not an enum on the station (outer / inner / both); it is **which tracks have a platform beside
them**:

- **Stored now**: each track with a platform is a station node on that track, as today (one per pair of quad, one per
  outer track, T-3332's lane bit for one track of a double). The station is the complex they form (`complex_of`).
  Plus a few **platform slot** bits on the station for the look: for N parallel tracks, N+1 places a platform can
  be (outside each edge, between each two tracks). A track is served when a platform touches it. Slots drive the
  drawing (islands between same-direction tracks on AA/BB, side or island on quad) and the panel picture.
- **Placing** (station bar): 1, 2, 4 icons, the pointer picks which, as in the mock. The sim adds one to three
  station nodes in one edit, named as one (T-3363's plan: `add_station` / `preview_station` take a count), priced
  per node (a one-way single station at the single-track station price).
- **Changing** (station panel "Platforms"): the picture of the station's tracks; click a platform to remove it, a
  dashed slot to add one. Adding a platform beside a bare track adds its station node; removing the last platform
  touching a track removes that node. A line stopping only on that track moves to another platform of the station
  its way, or the edit is refused naming the line (as T-3332 refuses).
- **Lines**: a stop is a station; the node stored says which tracks the line prefers there; each run uses the
  platform on its own track (per-direction stops). The stop list's chip moves the preference to the other tracks
  there (`station_beside`): that is the "assign trains to stop at specific platforms" of her TODO, per line and
  stop. Per train belongs to Exact timetables (T-3346), later.
- **Riders**: unchanged rules. All members are one demand station; a local-express change at an all-four station is
  2 perceived minutes, the wait, and a few seconds' walk. A timed connection (the local waiting for the express) is
  T-3343 / T-3346 territory.

**How this grows into the editor**: the editor replaces slots with free platform objects (shape, length, position);
"served tracks" stays derived from which tracks a platform touches; station nodes stay the points where trains stop
on each track; lines keep stopping at the station with each run using the platform on its track. What it would
undo: the slot bits (each becomes a rectangle platform, a small load step) and the Platforms row (replaced by the
editor). Not undone: per-direction stops, the station as the complex, outer tracks as separate pieces. Had we built
an outer/inner/both enum or option (b)'s four-lane piece, the editor would have to replace both.

## Outside

- NIMBY Rails: every track is its own piece; a line's stop is a pair of platform tracks picked per stop (basic or
  advanced mode, a stop anywhere on a platform track); since 1.9 trains may share a platform; stop selection signals
  let a train pick among a stop's platforms.
- Transport Fever 2: the line manager picks, per stop, the platform (terminal) and optional alternatives used when
  the first is taken.
- Subway Builder: its quad is directional (tracks 1-2 one way, 3-4 the other); joining two double lines into quad
  means one onto the inner tracks and the other, through single-track pieces, onto the outer ones.
- Real railways: paired by direction gives cross-platform changes (NYC subway, many Dutch lines, where two doubles
  meeting at a flying junction become directional quad); paired by use lets a pair branch without crossing the other
  (UK fast and slow lines); the West Coast Main Line switches from one to the other at flyovers near Rugby.

Sources: wiki.nimbyrails.com/Stop; steamdb.info NIMBY Rails patch notes; Steam community discussions for Transport
Fever 2 (app 1066780); wiki.subwaybuilder.com/index.php/Construction; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_junction;
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-platform_interchange; railforums.co.uk "preferences on track pairing".
