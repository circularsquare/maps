## Where the time went

T-3473's "0.2-0.3 s of worker time" was the wrong clock: after an edit a period's `ms` times only `figures()`
(demand.worker.ts, the results sent after every cluster is solved), not the solve. The wait was demand compute,
done up to twice. The real NYC save, 2 workers, master, one edit each:

| | network to the workers | local solve shown | whole solve's final |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 train every level | 0.6 s | 54 s | 132 s |
| +1 low only (7 to 8) | 0.7 s | 52 s | 125 s |
| +1 high only (26 to 27, under 5%) | 0.6 s | none | 55 s |

Per worker: the local solve was 2-3 periods of New York and its county at 17-23 s each, then 8 s
(`EDIT_SETTLE_MS`), then the whole solve 8-12 units (a period's round) of 5-8 s. Three things made it slow:

1. **The local solve threw away period keeping.** `localSolve` forgot every period of the touched populations
   and solved them all, and set their `heldQ` to nothing, so the whole solve after it solved all five periods
   again. A one-level edit cost all five periods twice.
2. **The first edit after a load (or after any network that kept nothing) kept no period anyway.**
   `EverydayApi::set_fare_weight` (sim/src/demand/everyday.rs) is called before `set_network` and dropped every
   period when `engine.same == 0`, the previous network's mask: after a load, a stop edit or an all-levels change,
   every everyday population lost all its periods though its weight had not changed. Seen in the worker: each
   everyday population's mask 10101 went to 0 at `set_fare_weight`, the commute populations kept theirs.
3. **On a one-city game the local solve is most of the work.** New York and its county are 53-82% of the
   cluster's passes; the local solve showed the line's figures at 54 s and put the whole solve's final 62 s later.

## What changed

- `localSolve` keeps a period every population of the cluster holds from its last whole solve on the same inputs
  (`keptOf`, setUp's test), its figures and trail as they are; the whole solve after keeps it too.
- `EverydayApi::set_fare_weight` drops periods only when the weight changes (test `same_fare_weight_keeps_periods`).
- A worker whose touched populations took 35% or more of its changed periods' passes in the cluster's last
  whole solve (`passMs`, `LOCAL_SHARE_MAX` 0.35; 0.5 in round 1, see round 2) skips the local solve. Two
  workers can disagree; then the page sends `whole` at once (as for any worker planning no local solve) and a
  worker still solving locally stops at its next pass and goes to the whole solve, rather than finishing a
  local result nobody can show. Dev `?localShare=a,b` sets each solving worker's share, to force that case.
- `setUp` and `localSolve` put back a kept period's `trail`, which `disturbed` dropped, so the next edit's local
  solve of that period crowds on the trail and not the ended loads.
- `?perfOff=localKeep` and `?perfOff=localSkip` give the old behaviour (not the Rust fix).

### The run that never reached its final: an undo that took back something else

Not a demand hang. The checker's never-final run, and three of mine (default, forced stop, and master's code
alike), started their first edit within a second of the load's final. T-3165 (game/capLoad.ts) lowers hand-set
counts over the panel's cap once a game has loaded, as ordinary undoable edits, one line at a time behind a
clock query each; on the NYC save it lowers line 8 at the load's final and lines 20 and 23 a moment later, after
an edit made at once. The player's undo then takes back line 23's lowering, not their own edit (the probe:
line 0 stays 27-21-8, line 23 goes 19 back to 23). Counts over the cap are trains the track cannot run, so demand
reads the same network, sends no solve (the client stays on the last version with nothing waiting, and
`finalWorld` equals the world's version: the game itself shows settled), and a check waiting for a newer
version waits for ever. The checker's run did two undos after low and all (two lowerings taken back); its
repeat that finished ended at 12,680,226 rail trips, not the load's, one undo having gone to a lowering. The
round-1 traces waited 5 s after the load, after the lowerings. `hang.mjs` now starts its ops only once the
world's version has held for 5 s after the load, checks after each op that the busiest line's trains are what
the op implies (an undo: as before the op it takes back), and fails at once if no newer solve starts within
20 s. The game side is a small bug of its own (an edit right after a load, then undo, undoes the cap lowering):
for the orchestrator to file.
