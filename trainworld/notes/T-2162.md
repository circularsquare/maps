# T-2162: names on the network diagram

Rules in docs/ui-diagram.md ("Names"); figures and reasoning here. Code: `app/src/diagram/names.ts`,
placed in the layout worker (`workers/diagram.worker.ts`) after the drawing, which now also runs there
(`draw.ts` posts each line's path as commands, the page makes the Path2D). The page picks and measures
the names (`diagram/view.ts` `namesOf`). Check: `checks/t2162-diagram-names.mjs` (`--svg` writes each
view to `artifacts/trainworld/t2162/<save>-<px>px.svg`).

## How it works

Per zoom level (a pitch of 7 px x 2^(k/4), k -3 to 12), a greedy pass in priority order (interchanges,
line ends, the rest) over candidate places round each station's pills: eight directions, east, west,
north and south also slid flush either way, at the pill or 1.5 pitches out (interchanges also 3 and 4.5,
at most 32 px), on one line or two. A candidate is cheapest off the pill's ends and on its run's
preferred side; the last level's place is favoured. Kept off lines (half a pitch plus a px from the
middle), pills, passages, names (`BoxGrid`) and nearer no other station's pill than its own.

**Why levels and not every frame**: the text is a fixed size in px while the drawing scales. The box
tested runs from the pill's outline (or the lifted point) to the far side of the name, so past its level a
name only shrinks into it: anything it was clear of at the level stays clear at every zoom up to the next
level. The check measures each level both at its pitch and 1.18 times it.

## What leaves interchanges unnamed

Every unnamed interchange in the check is blocked by lines on all sixteen-plus places (scratch `why.mjs`
listed the blockers): the layout packs bundles round the busiest stations (Times Sq, Penn, Grand Central,
Shinjuku, Shibuya in Node's layout) so tightly that no 2-pitch-tall gap is free near the pill at 7 px,
and the 47-50 Sts / 5 Av / 7 Av knot (T-2242's close pairs) at any zoom. Tried, in order, with the
New York interchanges named at 7 px: the eight places at two distances, 50 of 77; two-line names, 54;
slid places, 57; interchanges out to 4.5 pitches, 59. What is left is room in the layout, the way
Nöllenburg and Wolff reserve label space as part of the drawing: a follow-up.

## Tried and dropped

- Two-line names at a cost of 0.7: short names like "Anderson Street" went over two lines with room for
  one. Now 2 (above a side switch, below a lift).
- Every lift capped at 32 px: from 23.5 px a pitch the plain 1.5-pitch lift is over 32 px and about 110
  names that hop a neighbouring line at every zoom disappeared (847 to 737). Only the interchanges' longer
  lifts are capped.

## References

- Nöllenburg and Wolff, "Drawing and labeling high-quality metro maps by mixed-integer programming"
  (IEEE TVCG 2011), https://i11www.iti.kit.edu/extra/publications/nw-dlhqm-10.pdf : labels are part of
  the layout, space reserved for every name, labels along a run kept on one side; hours a city.
- TU Wien, smw19, https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/files/pub/smw19-paper-5.pdf : names placed progressively
  from the crowded centre outward, ordered by distance in the network.
- KIT labeling projects, https://algo.iti.kit.edu/en/projects/labeling/index : point-feature label
  placement with fixed candidate positions (the four- and eight-position models) and sliders, which this
  greedy pass follows.
- Wolff's survey of drawing subway maps, https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00450-007-0036-y.pdf .
