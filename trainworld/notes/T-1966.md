# T-1966 Trains wait for each other at flat crossings on screen

Anita, 2026-11-02: "ideally we have trains wait for each other and not collide at flat junctions";
2026-11-03: "in any case we only need to do it where we're looking closely". Agent t1966, own tree.

## Sim or screen

The sim already models crossing conflicts, as an average: a flat crossing and each crossing pair of
junction moves is a capacity resource (90 s a train), its Kingman queueing delay is baked into the
run profiles as a hold just short of the conflict point (track-and-service.md 6.2). So the
timetables already carry the waits; what was missing is drawing one. This is an on-screen rule
only, like T-1965's: no run time, boarding or demand changes, and nothing to put to Anita.

## Why T-1965's version piled up

It judged occupancy from where trains were drawn, each train's body swept over the whole frame
step (16 game s at 960x, 300 m and more) plus a margin, so almost every crossing looked held, and
held trains queued behind each other's bodies frame after frame (100-150 held, lags to the 20-minute
cap).

## The rule now (`map/trainGuard.ts`, `holdAtConflicts`)

- **Where**: the track model's own conflicts, `TrackApi.guard_conflicts` (sim
  `capacity::move_conflicts` for junction pairs, the same test `build_nodes` uses), carried to the
  renderer as `NetworkBuffers.conflicts`. The real save has 340 flat crossings and no crossing
  junction moves (no two lines share a node's moves there).
- **When**: from zoom 14 (`CONFLICT_ZOOM` = `STATION_BOX_ZOOM`, where trains are drawn as cars at
  about their true length), for trains in or within 150 px of the view. Further out nothing waits.
- **Time over a conflict** from each train's timetable and lag: from its front 20 m short of the
  point (the sim's hold margin) to its back 10 m past it; a train still over it at its run's end
  holds it to the end of its stand there (`ends`), and one over it at its run's start from the
  start. A timetabled hold at the hold point is not time over it (1 m tolerance).
- **Turns**: a train over a point last frame keeps it. The rest go in the order they reach it
  (trains already over another conflict first), each once every train on a crossing route ahead of
  it has cleared; meanwhile it stands at the hold point (its lag grows). A train that will have to
  wait at a conflict before its back clears the one it is at stands short of that one too and
  takes no turn there, so it does not stop on a crossing. Passes repeat (up to 3) while one holds
  someone, so a hold ahead is seen behind.
- **Bound**: no wait longer than 240 s (`CONFLICT_WAIT_MAX`); past it the train is let over, drawn
  through whatever is there. What a player sees then: two trains drawn through each other at that
  crossing, the case before T-1966. The cases seen were trains standing at a terminal over a
  crossing through their layover.

## Dead ends on the way

- Overlaps every frame at 1x: a train at its run's end stood over a crossing past the profile's
  last offset, so its time over it ended at arrival. Its stand now runs to the trip's `ends`.
- Waits to the cap behind a timetabled hold: the sim's conflict hold stands exactly at the hold
  point, so float32 put it a hair inside; 1 m tolerance.
- Lags to the 20-minute cap: a train waiting at two crossings close together restarted its wait
  clock at each (fixed: the wait runs from when it first stood), and trains of crossing-only lines
  queued behind a train standing out its turn at a terminal (that train now holds no one).
- Box blocking: a train let onto one crossing then stopped on it for the next; now it stands short
  of both, and trains already over a conflict take their turn first at the next.

## What is still a lie

- Zoomed out (below 14), trains are drawn through each other at crossings, as before.
- A wait is drawn, not run: the train's riders board on the timetable (T-1965's lies apply).
- A wait longer than 240 s ends with the train drawn through the other.
- Trains outside the view and its 150 px margin do not take part; one coming in late can find the
  point taken and wait, or be let over at the cap.
