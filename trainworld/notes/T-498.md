# T-498: gravity weighted partly by real-world travel time (design stage)

2026-10-16. Design and prototype only: no pack, registry, market or sim file was changed.
Anita (2026-10-16): yes to weighting commutes and errands by real-world travel time, "slightly".
The time is the real world's (real roads, real rail), computed once in the pipeline, never the
player's network: real rail already shaped where people live and work, so the game starts from the
real pattern and the player competes for it.

Scripts in `notes/t498/`: `car.py` (car minutes from a pack's own drive tables), `rail.py` (rail
minutes from OSM track and stations), `fit.py` (the refit against census flows), `lon_prep.py`
(London's 2011 census flows by district), `sanity.py` (minutes between named places),
`get_pbfs.py`. Tokyo's census side is `notes/t465_prep.py` (script removed 2026-10-24, in git history). Fit logs:
`../artifacts/trainworld/T-498-fits.log`.

## Short answer

- **The gain is real but small.** Refitting the curve on a travel-time-weighted distance takes
  Tokyo's misplaced share between municipalities from 16.5% to 15.9% at w = 0.5 (15.5% at w = 1),
  and London's between districts from 14.0% to 13.9%. Own-municipality shares move towards the
  census. A fast rail corridor gains: Musashino to the five central wards 23.8% becomes 26.4% at
  w = 0.5 and 29.1% at w = 1 (census 36.4%).
- **It does not fix west Tokyo to the centre as a whole.** The eight west municipalities to
  Chiyoda, Chuo and Minato go from 14.6% to 14.1% (census 17.1%), and London's outer districts to
  the City and Westminster from 6.8% to 6.3% (census 7.4%). Both get slightly worse. The centre
  still fills too much from within 5 km (Tokyo 55% against 45%). That is a margins problem (who
  lives where, occupations), as T-465 said, not a cost problem.
- **Parking decides it.** With bare minutes (the game's car model with no parking), rail beats the
  car on only 4.5% of Tokyo's commute-weighted zone pairs, and the weighting changes nothing
  (16.5% at every w). Priced the way the game prices a trip (congested driving x 1.33, parking at
  the destination), rail wins 31% of pairs and the gain above appears.
- **The Japan pairs in T-497 are not travel-time problems.** Shiga to Chukyo (3.4x the census) and
  Kagawa to Okayama (6x) are about as quick as their distance would suggest (Kusatsu to Nagoya
  about 65 min by Shinkansen via Kyoto; Takamatsu to Okayama 55 min by the Marine Liner). That is
  at or below the typical time for 55-85 km, so the weighting would, if anything, send more. They
  need a labour-market term, not a cost term (not solved here, estimated from timetables).
- **Recommended: w = 0.5**, built in four stages; whether the gain is worth stages 2-4 is Anita's
  call (end of this note).

## 1. The cost

One rule for commutes and everyday purposes:

    d_eff = d x (t / t_typ(d)) ^ w
    f = d_eff^-a exp(-d_eff / L)        (the pack's curve, unchanged in form)

- `d`: the zone-centre distance the gravity uses now (2 km grid, 1.04 km within a zone).
- `t`: the real-world generalised minutes between the two zones, the smallest of car, rail and
  walk (2).
- `t_typ(d)`: the pack's typical `t` at that distance: the commute-weighted median over its zone
  pairs, in 40 log-distance bins, interpolated.
- `w`: the weight of time. w = 0 is today's model exactly.

Why this form and not a linear blend `(1-w) d + w v t`:

- **It only reshapes.** Because `t` is divided by the pack's own typical time, a city that is
  slow everywhere (Lagos) or fast everywhere is not shifted as a whole; only pairs faster or slower
  than usual for their distance move. That matters because most packs have no flow table to refit
  on and borrow a curve; a borrowed curve keeps its overall trip lengths.
- **It is a factor per zone pair**, so it stores in the same byte layout as the drive tables and
  the runtime applies it with a table lookup (3).
- The linear blend is the same to first order (r near 1: d(1 - w + w r) against d r^w); no reason
  to carry two.

Everyday purposes (errands 3 km, social 6 km) use the same `d_eff` from the same table, with their
own exponential: `exp(-d_eff / scale)`. Same `t` (rail included): in dense cities rail does carry
errands to big centres, and within 3-6 km rail rarely beats walking or the car anyway, so the
table mostly brings in water and missing-bridge detours there. Measured only for commutes (no
everyday flow data).

## 2. The real times

**Car**: the game's own drive model (`kernel.rs` `drive_min_f`, `place_min_per_km`: density speeds
are the congestion), on the pack's routed factors decoded from `drive_near` and `drive_far`. No
routing and no new data. Generalised: driving x 1.33, plus 3 min to park and, for the share who pay,
`15 ln(jobs density / 3,000)` minutes capped at 50 (`park_parts`). Leaving parking out makes the
car beat rail almost everywhere in Tokyo (the density speeds give Kichijoji to Tokyo station 51 min
by car), which no Tokyo commuter would recognise.

**Rail**: OSM only, no timetable, so it works anywhere OSM has the track:

- Track: `railway` = rail, subway, light_rail, monorail, narrow_gauge, tram; without
  `service` = yard, siding, spur, crossover and `usage` = freight, industrial, military, tourism.
- Speed (average including stops): subway 33 km/h, monorail 30, light rail 25, tram 15; high speed
  (`highspeed=yes` or maxspeed 200+) 0.55 x maxspeed; other rail 0.6 x maxspeed (mph converted)
  clamped to 30-110, else 50 on `usage=main`, 35 on branch, 45 untagged.
- Stations: `railway` = station or halt nodes; each joins the nearest point of every track within
  300 m at 2 min (so a change costs 4 min, also between lines with no track in common).
- Door to door: access and egress from the zone's commuter-weighted centre to stations within
  3 km, by walking (6 km/h on 1.3x the line) or a feeder (bus or bicycle: 5 min then 15 km/h),
  whichever is quicker; 5 min wait; ride. Generalised: wait x 1.37, the platform links x 1.39.
- Graph: track points that are junctions, way ends or station joins; Dijkstra from every station,
  then zone to station to station to zone by the 3 km access lists.

Checks (minutes door to door, prototype): Kichijoji to Tokyo station 35 (real about 35-40),
Hachioji 40, Yokohama 51, Chiba 65; Bank to Woking 62, Stevenage 53, Chelmsford 50, Croydon 40,
Canary Wharf 18. Short central trips run a little slow (Bank to Westminster 27, a fixed 5 min wait
where the Tube runs every 2-3); a little fast where OSM's track has rare service. GTFS where open
would fix both but is not worldwide; at w = 0.5 it is not worth a source per city.

Sources considered: OSM through Overpass failed for both bboxes (502 and 500 from three servers
on anything larger than a few km); Geofabrik extracts worked and are what `routes.py` already
downloads.

**Cost per pack** (prototype, numpy, 4 threads): Tokyo (4,792 zones, 2,168 stations, 20k rail
ways): reading rail from the Kanto pbf 74 s, stations 14 s, zone matrix 15 s. London (3,094 zones,
8 county extracts) 90 s, 7 s, 7 s. A 25k-zone pack: the zone step grows with zones² x access
stations, about 5-10 minutes, done in row blocks (a full 25k x 25k float matrix is 2.5 GB).

## 3. Storage and the chain

The runtime rebuilds zone trips from `row x col x f(d)` with `f` a table by grid offset (cheap,
because the decay depends on the offset only). With a per-pair cost it needs the ratio per pair:

- **Pack arrays** `time_near` (per zone, its 13 x 13 window, one byte each, same order as
  `drive_near`) and `time_far` (supercell x supercell, same as `drive_far`); byte q = round(64 ln
  r) + 128 (r from 0.14 to 7; the commute-weighted middle 80% of Tokyo's pairs is 0.75-1.03). Same size as the drive tables: Tokyo 0.8 MB raw. Header block
  `gravity.time` with `w`, the source and `zones_check`.
- **Sim**: `f(d r^w) = f(d) x r^(-a w) x exp(-d (r^w - 1) / L)`: the offset table as now, a
  256-entry table for the middle term, one `exp` per pair. In `pack_gravity`, `joint_gravity`,
  `gravity_from_pack` and the everyday destination weights.
- **Fit**: the curve is refitted on `d_eff` where a flow table exists (Tokyo moves from
  d^-2.2 exp(-d/80) to d^-2.0 exp(-d/60) at w = 0.5); borrowed curves stay as they are.

The stored layout costs part of the corridor effect: with the ratio averaged over 8 km supercells
beyond 12 km, Tokyo at w = 0.5 is still 15.9% misplaced, but Musashino to the five central wards
gives 24.8% instead of 26.4%. A finer far table (4 km supercells, Tokyo 2.2 MB raw) would keep more;
measure at build time.

Across packs (the joint gravity, Japan's group, the US Northeast) nothing is stored: start with
r = 1 there (today's model) and add cross-pack times in a later stage.

for its distance (r about 0.7-1). Same for Kagawa to Okayama. A cost term cannot do it.

## 5. Recommendation and the rule

**w = 0.5.** It takes most of w = 1's gain in misplaced share where the curve is refitted (Tokyo
0.6 of 1.0 points; London all of the 0.1), moves Musashino half way, and keeps distance the larger
part of the cost, which is what "slightly" asks. w = 1 is a little better in Tokyo's misplaced
share but makes the centre's aggregate pull worse in both cities and leans fully on a rail model
with no timetable.

For `docs/demand-worldwide.md` (Commute flows), once built:

> **Travel time (T-498).** Gravity distance is `d x (t / t_typ(d))^0.5`, with `t` the real
> world's generalised minutes between two zones (the smaller of the game's car model with parking
> and an OSM rail model: track speeds by class, 5 min wait, walk or feeder access within 3 km) and
> `t_typ(d)` the pack's commute-weighted median at that distance; the same for everyday
> destinations. In Tokyo it lowers trips misplaced between municipalities from 16.5% to 15.9% and
> sends more along fast rail corridors (Musashino to the central wards 24% to 26%, census 36%); in
> London 14.0% to 13.9%. Why it may not carry over: OSM rail with no timetable rates a line with
> two trains a day like the Tube (Lagos, Mumbai's outer lines, rural lines anywhere), parking
> prices come from New York's fit, and jobs density stands in for parking scarcity. Check: where
> GTFS is open for a new country, compare its door-to-door times on 20 commuter pairs with the
> OSM model; where a flow table exists, refit the curve on `d_eff`.

## 6. Build plan

1. **Pipeline, `pipeline/real_times.py <city>`** (P2, one agent, ~3-4 h): rail from the pack's
   Geofabrik extracts (the same `EXTRACTS`; add the railway lines and station points to
   `routes.read_ways`' cached read so later rebuilds need no second download), car from the drive
   tables, `t_typ`, the ratio bytes into `time_near` and `time_far`. Machine time: re-downloading
   the extracts `routes.py` deleted (Japan's eight regions about 2 GB, England 1.3 GB, the US
   states about 3 GB) and 2-10 min a pack, about 2-3 hours for the ~60 packs, in the background.
2. **Sim** (P2, one agent, ~3 h): pack format, the `f(d r^w)` evaluation in the four places, a
   `time_weight` parameter (0 = today), tests; measure the runtime gravity rebuild (one more lookup
   and `exp` per pair; Tokyo 23M pairs).
3. **Refit and rebuild** (P2, ~3-4 h): refit the curve on `d_eff` for the packs with flow tables
   (the nine US packs on LODES, Toronto, London, the seven Japanese metros, and the 38 Japan zones
   by the zone grid, minutes a zone); then `pack_gravity` for every pack, the Japan joint solve
   (~5 min), `merge_cells.py`, `read_pack.py`, `register.py --market --trips` for all packs.
   Every pack rebuilds (all of them carry gravity factors).
4. **Across packs** (P3, later): cross-pack times for the joint gravity groups (Japan, US
   Northeast), from `routes.drive_factors` and a national rail graph; r = 1 until then.
