# T-4225: New York's map 6.8 s on rc046, 9.7 s on 0.4.6 (agent mapslow, 2026-11-24)

Outcome: **no step grew**. Measured side by side, 0.4.6 shows the map as fast as rc046 or a little faster. The
ledger's 6.8 against 9.7 is two single runs; the 9.7 run was slow in every part of the tab during its first 15 s, not
in a map step. Nothing to fix; nothing built.

## Why the 0.4.6 ledger run read 9.7 s

Its record (perfbench/runs/v0.4.6-nyc-1.json) has the load's timeline: the NYC pack opened in the workers at 8.8 s
and the markets at 15.2 s, against 4.2-5.6 s and 6.1-11.4 s in the five other NYC runs that record it (rq-c2-base,
rq-c2-head, t4199-headc, and the 0.4.6 probes above at 4.2-5.3 s). The demand workers were as late as the map: the
tab as a whole had less CPU in that run's first 15 s (its setupMs, also wall time inside the workers, doubled too:
5,093 to 10,043 ms, flagged), which no single step explains. Its CPU, memory and bytes are the trustworthy part.

## T-4204's +2.3-2.7 s

Both of T-4204's compares were one run an arm, base first, head second. Over the 50 two-run (ABBA) sets on file, the
run right after the first is on average 8.6% slower than its set's median (positions: 0.99, 1.09, 1.01, 1.02), and
one-run pairs move from -12% to +183%. The change itself (`memCheck` keeping a worker's slack) runs only once the
pool is quiet, never in a load's first seconds.

## If the map is to get faster (not built)

The first state is two thirds of the map's wait and moves with contention. Next step: its `networkSteps` timing by part
on a quiet clock worker (`?debug=1` timing) against the same with the demand workers opening, to tell the work from
the waiting; then either a cheaper first build or opening the packs a moment later (the trips wait would grow by about
that much). About 2 hours to measure and propose.
