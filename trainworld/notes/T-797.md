# T-797 lines that backtrack, and T-798 loop lines: design

Anita, 2026-10-21: "lines that can backtrack (just limit throughput a lot)" and "loop lines". Design
only; build stages at the end. Related: T-657 (turnback capacity, tail tracks and loops at
terminals). Code read: `track/service.rs`, `net.rs` (`check_line`, `find_path`, `route`),
`capacity.rs` (`build_sections`, `build_nodes`), `profile.rs` (`run`), `world.rs`
(`settle_counts`, `auto_trains`), `wasm.rs` (`best_insert`, `stop_tiers`, `passed_stations`),
`demand/kernel.rs` (`build_graph`), `demand/api.rs` (`period_net`), `boarding.rs` (`simulate`).

## What both build on

A line today is stops plus a path of `(edge, dir)` entries that never turns back
(`check_line`: "arrive on one side, leave on the other"). It runs as two runs, forward and
reversed, with a turnaround at each end: a circuit with exactly two reversals, both at termini.
Both tasks generalise that circuit without a new route model: a backtrack adds reversals inside a
run; a loop removes the turnarounds. Neither needs a stored "reversal" field: a reversal is two
consecutive path entries arriving and leaving a node on the same side, and a loop is a line
whose last stop is its first. Everything below derives those from the path and stops.

A station may now be a stop twice on one line, never twice in a row (`check_line` already allows
it; `best_insert` and `passed_stations` refuse it): needed for a balloon's stem and for a
backtrack that passes a junction station twice. Demand handles a repeat already: each visit is its
own route node, and a station passed twice really does see twice the trains.

## T-797: lines that backtrack

**Making one.** No new tool. In stop editing, a station reachable only by turning back is today
"unreachable" (ui-building.md). It becomes a fourth marker tier, "Click to add a stop. Trains
reverse at <station>" (grey like "change the route"). The route search may reverse:
- at a **stop** (the hop's start in `find_path`: the arrival side is allowed, at a cost);
- at a **bare track end** (a node with one port that is not a station) met mid-hop (stage B3).
Never at a station the line passes without stopping, nor at a junction with track beyond it (a
train needs a stub to reverse into). `find_path` charges a reversal 600 s of search time and
`best_insert` 5,000 m of length, so a route without one wins unless it is much longer. A reroute
after a deletion (`Network::route` from `derive`) may not add reversals: if the new path has more
than the old, the line is broken as now.

**Route.** Unchanged storage. `check_line` accepts a same-side pair at node k when k is a matched
stop or a one-port non-station node. The stop list shows a reverse icon on the stop where trains
reverse (hover "Trains reverse here: 6 an hour at most"; panel rules: icon, number in the hover).

**Run times and the hold.** A reversing train stands for the line's turnaround (default 180 s,
the same setting as at its ends), not its dwell. To keep a train from being drawn folded over
the turn, its middle stands at `s_a = node_s - d` on arrival and leaves from `s_d = node_s + d`
(the same place on the ground, in the run's unfolded offsets): d is half the train at a through
station, half the platform at a dead-end station, half the train plus 20 m at a bare track end.
`profile::run` gains a per-stop departure offset (the hop starts at `stops[i] + jump[i]`, the
standing phase is pushed there, so the drawn train jumps once as it stops). A bare track end is a
stop passed to `profile::run` but masked out of `arr`/`dep` (new `Run::turns`, kept parallel).
`time_reaching` and `segment_at` stay valid: offsets only move forward. The visible cost: at a
through station a reversing train stands half a train short of the platform's middle.

**Capacity.** A reversal at a through station uses what a short turn there uses today
(`build_nodes`' last-node branch): its arrival platform for turnaround + 60 s, and the turnback
crossover against opposing trains (90 s, `Tag::Turnback`). At a dead end, the pooled terminus
(`(turnaround + 60) / tracks`). It is not a junction move and not a through train. On top, the
stopgap: a new resource per (node, arrival side), `Tag::Reversal`, `ResKind::Terminus`,
`wire_kind` 9, every reversing train holding it for `REVERSAL_LIMIT_S = 600 s`: **6 reversals an
hour at most per station side**. Both directions of a branch served by reversing usually arrive
from the same side, so one such line runs at most 3 trains an hour each way (20 minutes); a
second line reversing there halves it. `Capacity::fill` already cuts the fleet and holds the rest
at the ends, and the line's alert names it ("Reversing at Nerul Village is full"). T-657 later
replaces the constant with real reversing design.

**Schedules and fleet.** No change: the reversal stand is inside each run's duration, so the
round trip and `auto_trains` see it. Trains enter and leave only at the two termini.

**Demand.** No change. The line is still ordered stops; `stop_to_stop` includes the dwell at the
stop left, so a rider through the reversal pays the stand. A backtrack via a station twice
(A, Y, J, Y, B) gives Y two route nodes in each direction; common lines may combine them, which is
right (two trains an interval pass Y heading for B).

**Map.** Nothing new beyond the capacity marker of kind 9. `rebuild_edge_lines` dedups, so an
edge used both ways draws once.

**Saves.** No format change.

**Needs care.** `build_sections` must never join keys across a reversal (at a two-port reversal
node with equal signatures it would merge both directions into one section). `build_nodes`' moves
loop must skip same-side pairs (`track_pos` pairs them meaninglessly). `through` must not get the
reversing visit. `passed_stations` and `stop_tiers` (`reachable_from` treats turning back as
unreachable). Station inspector (`ui/inspector/others.tsx` 336, 356) and `game/complexLive.ts` 96
use `stops.indexOf`, so a twice-visited station shows its first visit only: sum both.

## T-798: loop lines

**As built (L1 + L2, 2026-10-21).** As designed below, with these settled on the way:
- `LoopKind` in net.rs (`loop_kind`, `line_loop`), `LineSvc::kind`; `turn_count` counts a balloon's
  closing turn, so a reroute never turns a circle into a balloon. `route`/`route_memo` ask the
  closing hop for the carry-on side (`find_path_side`, `carry_on_side`), else any side.
- Closing is offered (tier 5) only when the closed path adds no reversal at the old last stop:
  a two-stop line on plain track would otherwise "close" by going back the way it came.
- The line's middle for T-835 is half a lap from A: `Lattice::lead`'s formula already gives it.
- Demand learns a circle from its times, no new API: a line whose first and last stops are the same
  station with a positive run-0 time at its last stop (the snapshot's `wrapS`, the dwell) wraps
  (`period_net`). A T-756 reach view keeps the wrap only when it spans the whole line.
- The boarding account runs a circle twice: the first pass collects who stays aboard at A, the
  second queues them at stop 0 when their train arrives. The alighting shares start a lap with the
  least load that keeps every stop non-negative (`alight_shares`); demand's wrap load is not passed.
  Left-behind estimates in the kernel (`left_behind`) still empty trains at the last stop.
- A circle's boardings at A show on stop 0 (run 0) and the closing row (run 1) of the stop list.
- Real save: 109 lines, none whose last stop is its first (`t798_real_save_has_no_loops`).
- Left for L3: one-way loops (`one_way`, `LOW1`, panel toggle, balloon default one way),
  `passed_stations` offering a balloon stem's return leg.

**Making one.** In stop editing, the first stop's marker, while the line has two or more stops
and track leads from the last stop back to it, says "Click to close the loop", and the click
appends the first stop. On a closed line it says "Click to open the loop" and removes the closing
stop. Removing the first stop otherwise goes through the stop list's cross. The closing hop is
searched to arrive on the side that lets the train carry on (`find_path` gets a wanted arrival
side); only if none exists does it arrive on the same side, a closure by reversal.

**Route.** stops `[A, B, C, D, A]`, path from A round to A. Two kinds, derived in `build_line`:
- **circle** (closes by a through move at A): no turnaround anywhere;
- **balloon** (closes by reversal at A, e.g. A to D, round a loop, back down the other track):
  turns back at A only. Stem stations repeat on the way back (added with "Add all N stations the
  line passes", which must then offer stations stopped at on another visit).
`check_line` needs no new rule. A line in an old save whose last stop is its first becomes a loop
(the real save has none to check before building).

**Directions.** A circle runs both ways by default (run 0 one way round, run 1 the other, each
with its own trains); a balloon runs one way by default. Both can be switched in the line panel
(icon toggle, stage L3; until then every loop runs both ways). One way means run 1 carries no
departures: `Lattice`, capacity, the account and demand (infinite headway, as for a period with
no trains) skip it.

**Run times, round trip, fleet.** A lap is one run's duration plus the closing stand: the dwell
for a circle, the turnaround for a balloon (`trip_len`). The round trip is
`2 x max(lap0, lap1)` both ways, `lap0` one way (a normal line stays `lap0 + lap1`, the
turnaround as closing stand: one formula, `LineSvc::round_trip`). Each direction needs
`ceil(lap x tph / 3600)` trains, so a two-way loop's count is even: the stepper moves by 2,
`auto_trains` doubles the per-direction count, an odd count from a save rounds up. Trains are
evenly spaced and stand at A until their next departure: hold = n x headway - lap, under one
headway. Example: a 34 km circle of 30 stops, lap 60 min, every 4 minutes each way: 15 trains a
direction, 30 in all.

**Schedules.** `Service::lattice`: `off` = 0 for loops. `hand_over`: a train finishing run r takes
run r's next departure from A, not the other run's. That stand is the timing hold, so trains enter
and leave service at A only, as at a terminal now. Eras and shifts are per run and unchanged.

**Capacity.** For a circle, A's arrival is a stop (dwell + 60 s), not a short turn; add the
junction move between the last and first entries; `build_sections` joins across A. The timing
hold does not occupy the platform (under one headway, it never blocks the line's own trains; it
can slow another line sharing that platform: accepted). A balloon's A is today's short turn or
dead-end terminus, unchanged. The loop track itself is a plain section, about 34 an hour at
80 km/h: a balloon is a terminal with no turnback limit (T-657's loop, done this way).

**Demand.** A circle's rider must be able to ride through A. `demand::Network` gets
`line_wrap: Vec<bool>` (sent in `DemandNetwork` from `clock.worker.ts`, kept in
`demandClusters.ts`), and `build_graph` for a wrapped line gives the last route node of each
direction a ride edge to the first (ride = the dwell, `adj_seg` = that node, so its load is the
riders staying aboard) and no boarding edge at the last route node (it would double A's trains).
Route nodes stay 2n a line, so every `base + dir x n + k` index holds. Shortest paths take the
short way round; on a one-way loop the long way is the only way, and walking or the car compete.
A balloon is not wrapped: everyone gets off at A, the train turns back.

**Boarding account.** `simulate` empties trains at the last stop; for a circle run, only demand's
alighting share leaves there and the rest join stop 0's queue at that moment, ahead of new
arrivals, so they board the next departure (normally the same train after its hold). Fares come
from demand's riders (`game/money.ts`), so this changes only timing.

**Map.** Nothing new; a loop is its edges. Trains stand at A through the hold like at a terminal.

**Saves.** Two-way loops need nothing (stops and path). One-way: `LineData::one_way`, a trailing
`LOW1` block of line indices, like `LAU1`; missing means both ways.

**Needs care.** Anything that maps a ride to (stop k, stop k + 1): the wrap segment is k = n - 1
to k = 0, zero km (`hopKm` 0) and length; check the trip explorer's drawn ride, line widths by
riders and `Boarding::pulses` (same-platform transfer, nothing to walk). Line panel: "lap" in place
of round trip, no "turning at the ends" for a circle. `pendingTrains` in `game/world.ts` steps by
2. The stop list shows the closing A with the lap time.

## Recommended design, in short

- **T-797**: reversals derived from the path, only at stops and bare track ends, chosen by the
  route search with a penalty; the train stands the line's turnaround; each reversal uses today's
  short-turn resources plus a limiter of 600 s per station side (6 an hour; one line every 20
  minutes each way). No demand or save change.
- **T-798**: a loop is a line whose last stop is its first, closed by clicking the first stop;
  circles run both ways with their own trains and an even count, balloons one way; trains hold at
  A to the headway and enter and leave there; demand wraps a circle's route nodes.

## For Anita

- Balloon loops (and loops at both ends) remove the turnback limit at a terminal at once (the
  loop is plain track, ~34 an hour against 15 for reversing on a through platform). That is
  T-657's loop half, realistic, but it lifts the terminal ceiling before tail tracks exist. Fine?
- The stopgap's size: 6 reversals an hour per station side, so a branch served by reversing runs
  every 20 minutes at best. Decided; 12 an hour (every 10) is the other sensible value.
