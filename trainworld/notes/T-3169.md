## T-3170: high-speed flyovers

Two gates kept them flat: `with_flyovers` returned early unless the route's kind was Regular, and `plan_flyover`
refused a route edge that was not Regular. Past those, the plain leg (the route's own first stretch, made single
and one-way) keeps the route's kind, and `Network::set_edge` refused any single high-speed track, so the edit would
fail and fall back flat.

Fix: both gates take any rail kind; the crossing leg takes the route's kind; `set_edge` allows high-speed track as
one-way single (two-way single high-speed is still refused, and nothing else lays it). The leg's least curve is the
kind's smallest plus 5 m (55 m on high-speed track), and the preview's leg speeds use the kind's cap (320), so a
high-speed flyover's legs are high-speed track with high-speed upkeep and price. The other choice was regular
legs (no rule change, but a high-speed line's junction made of 160 km/h track); not taken, since a fast flyover
target would then be capped at 160 on a high-speed line.

## T-3169: why the tight side was always 54 km/h, and the flyover target

The 54 km/h was the S where the crossing leg joins the route at J3 (Anita's lead: "the taper" in the speed view).
It ran 20 m along the route (`SPLIT_LEAD`) and parted 12 m over 60 m (`SPLIT_LEN`): its corner nearest J3 had 20 m
of tangent for an 11.3 degree turn, a 202 m radius, 54 km/h, whatever the room. The jog at J (12 m out at 12
degrees) was a fixed 275 m (61 km/h) too. The added "no kissing" gap was right; its shape was fixed.

Now each pass aims for a radius `r` (the flyover target's, then 20 km/h less in turn, then the leg's least):

- the jog and the S at J3 are each two arcs of `r` stepping 12 m sideways, the turn the largest that fits
  (`s_turn`: 2% under `acos(1 - 12 / 2r)`, at most the old 12 and 11.3 degrees), so a small `r` keeps the old
  shapes exactly and a big one stretches them (at 80 km/h the S and its lead are about 110 m long, at 160 about 220 m);
- every curve of the leg must be at least `r` (less 1%) or the try fails (`set_radii` floor);
- case A no longer pins the crossing where J3's tangent meets the main: it starts there and moves the crossing
  further ahead (steeper) while the crossing curve wants more room, or back while the ramp down wants more;
  case B moves its crossing out while a curve is short of `r`;
- J3 steps out by 10 m or `r / 60`, whichever is more, so a 160 km/h target is not 10x the work.

A target with no room falls back 20 km/h at a time, then to the old tightest shape, so the setting never makes a
flyover flat; the tag shows what was laid.

Measured (`cargo test --release --lib t3169 -- --nocapture`), a branch drawn as one curve at 160 km/h from the
click, metres of main ahead of the click / the leg's slowest curve:

| flyover target | 30 degree branch | 60 degree branch |
|---|---|---|
| 20 (as tight as it goes) | 453 m / 45 km/h | 447 m / 46 km/h |
| 60 | 491 m / 61 | 466 m / 61 |
| 80 (default) | 519 m / 80 | 842 m / 80 |
| 100 | 1,448 m / 100 | 842 m / 80 (no room for 100) |
| 120 | 2,397 m / 120 | 842 m / 80 |

Before this, both were about 500 and 475 m with the slowest curve 54 km/h. The jump from 80 to 100 at 30 degrees
is the geometry: the branch is itself a curve, so a leg that must end up running beside it at 12 m, after
turning across the main, needs a long run when its curves are wide. 80 km/h costs almost nothing over the
tightest shape and is the default (DECISIONS.md).

The bar: an Advanced expander at the right end of the second row (chevron, "Advanced"), which adds a third row,
right-aligned under it, with Target max speed (moved there) and Flyover target max speed. Open state and the
flyover target are remembered per browser (`build.advanced`, `build.fly`). On a phone the speed button opens a pop
with both steppers.

## T-3168: both speeds on the map

The map's curve labels came from the route's own vertices only (the plain leg, 106-108 km/h in her picture); the
tag's speed was the lowest over everything previewed, the crossing leg included (36-39). Now the preview also
returns each crossing leg's slowest curve (`preview_leg_curves`, x, y at the curve's middle, radius, km/h), and the
track tool labels it beside that leg. The leg labels are listed first, so where the two overlap (zoomed out) the
slower shows. One label per leg, not one per leg curve: at the default target the leg has five curves all near 80
km/h, which would be clutter. Picked "show both".

Found on the way: `clockClient.ts` copied the preview answer field by field and left out `noFlyover`, so "No room
for a flyover here" (T-3092) never reached the tag; it and `legs` are passed now.

## T-3188: shallow splits

What set the limit: J3 had to be where the route left the main at 4 degrees or more (`SHALLOWEST`), and case A
only crossed where J3's tangent line met the main, which for a gently curving branch is far behind or ahead of
anything that fits; case B needs the leg to come in steeper than the route at J3 from a crossing 234 m ahead,
which a shallow route rarely allows. On a route drawn as one curve from the click, that put the first workable J3
past about 15 degrees of route heading, as she saw.

Now J3 may be anywhere from 80 degrees down to parallel (-4 degrees allowed), at least 24 m off the main when
under 4 degrees, and case A picks its crossing freely (above), so the leg crosses steeper than the route and turns
back along it before the S. Measured at the 80 km/h default: branches drawn at 3, 5, 8 and 12 degrees take 950,
950, 500 and 560 m of the main; a branch that runs beside the main 30 or 50 m off gets one too. At a 20 km/h
target (the old shapes) a 5 degree branch can still fall back flat (the 8 ramp tries run out on tight legs); not
chased, as 40 km/h and up all fit.
