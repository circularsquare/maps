# T-4504 Overtake plans worked out faster after an edit (agent planbest, 2026-11-27)

After T-4444 the two capacity updates of a click on Asia's trunk (weekdays, then the weekend copy) were about 34-36 ms, most of it
`Plan::best`: 24 sweeps of a cycle of trains through every point of a shared stretch, one a relative phase.

## What the sweep cost, and why

Each sweep, at every point, sorted all its trains with `sort_by` on a comparator that read three fields of 72-byte structs
(and, through `Ordering::then`, worked out all three every time), and at every passing point went over every faster train for
every slower one, skipping from the start of the list each time. Every slower train had its own `Vec` of waits, cloned again
for `best`'s longest wait.

On Asia's plans (172, 860 level outcomes; a count, no timing) a sweep is about 950 train-points (work-weighted: 61 trains, 18.5
points, 16.5 of them passing points): almost every point is a passing point, so the per-passing-point passes were as big as the
sort. No few huge sweeps carry the cost (hs/hf under 4 for all of it). The longest stretches take 10,254 s from end to end, so
about 87% of a sweep's trains are warm-up.

## What changed (sim/src/track/overtake.rs)

- `Trains`: one sweep's trains in flat lists (start, arrival, departure, measured, waits as rows of points), reused over the 24
  phases of a `best`.
- The order at each point: the first point as before (`sort_by`, which merges the two lists as built); after that an insertion
  sort of the last point's order, a plain `<`/`>` first and the full comparator only on a tie. A stable sort with the same
  comparator gives the same order as any other stable sort, and a train moves only past the few it waits for or outstands.
- At a passing point the faster trains' departures and the slower trains are collected in the order the forward pass runs them
  (each train keeps at least the spacing behind the one ahead, so both come out in departure order; a check sorts the faster ones
  if not). Each slower train's first faster train it could wait for comes from a cursor that only moves on (back to the start if
  a slower train ever left earlier than the one before), so the waits go over only the faster trains that matter.
- `best` takes the longest wait from the rows; `trains_at` builds its per-train lists from them.

## Exactness

Every float is worked out by the same expression in the same order as before; the sorted lists are the same (a stable sort, and
`total_cmp` is a total order on the values); the measured sums run in the order trains were built.

- Rust: both suites pass (835 + 1072).
- `best_is_the_same_as_before` (not kept; `T-4504/exactness-tests.patch` adds it back): 2000 random stretches (2-41 points,
  rates 0.2-30 an hour, capped headways, a rare slower line behind a frequent faster one and the other way), 1438 of them with a
  slower train waiting: every outcome (loss, waits, behind, offset, the 24 costs and longest waits) the same as a frozen copy of
  the old `best` to the bit, and `trains_at`'s per-train waits too.
- `best_on_a_real_save` (same patch; `T4504_TRACK=<track bytes>`): NYC (T-007-real-nyc) has no plans (no passing tracks), so
  nothing on it reads a plan and no NYC figure can move (total rail trips and every line unchanged). Asia (asianita-day568): all
  172 plans at every level, 860 outcomes, 748 with waits, the same to the bit.

## Asia, T-4441's driver (edge 1482, 4 clicks; 1 run, machine quiet: no core claims)

The instrumented build (T-4441's patch with `git apply --reject`, plus a timer around `best` in `outcome`; reverted) was of the
version before the last step (collecting the faster trains and slower ones in the forward pass, and the cursor). That step is
exact and only takes work away: a pass over every train at each passing point and a binary search per slower train.

| ms | T-4441 | T-4444 | now (clicks 3, 4, final settle) |
|---|---|---|---|
| both capacity updates (`us.cap_update`, 2 calls) | 37-44 | about 34-36 | 21.3, 26.3, 21.3 |
| both `update_scoped` (`sa.update_scoped` + `sa.mirror_weekend`) | 57-66 | about 55 | 39.9, 45.8, 39.6 |
| `Plan::best` in all the click's work | | about 0.8 a call | 26.6, 32.9, 26.9 over 66 calls, about 0.41 a call |
| the click (`add_station`) | 166, 174 | | 88.9, 99.6 |

(`us.shared_changed` reads about 14 ms now because the `us.profiles` lap no longer applied; the profiles part of it is as before.
The whole click is also faster from T-4449's retime work, landed since T-4441.)

So the two capacity updates are under 25 ms at two of the three later readings and 26.3 at the third; both `update_scoped` calls
are about 40-46, not under 25: they also hold the line profiles (14-16) and line rebuilds (about 4), which `best` doesn't touch.
