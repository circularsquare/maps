## Cause

Single track takes its way from its **run** (`lanes.rs` `derive_ways`): the pieces through nodes where just two
single pieces meet, voted on by the run's two ends. An end at a junction with no join counting says "both ways".
#193, where the bypass leaves the north leg, is three single pieces and nothing else, so each of the north side's
three runs (leg west of #193, Parque Central's track, the bypass) had one join vote from the double and one "both":
all three ran both ways, while the south leg kept its way. Then:

- Parque Central was no one-way station (both its pieces must run one way, T-3565), so it was an ordinary stop
  both runs must make;
- Avenida Sur 17 was a one-way station no run could take without turning back (the way back had to pass Parque
  Central westward on the north leg); `best_slot` put it at the line's end, which gave the zigzag above.

Proof the routing (T-3565, T-3651) is right once the ways are: pinning the north side's eight pieces eastward with
the panel's switch made the same line [Sur 9, Sur 13, Parque Central, Sur 17, Artesanos, Bolivar], each run on its
own leg, bypass unused, no turn back, 1,059 / 1,064 m.

## Fix (the general rule: legs that split and join again, any number of tracks)

`lanes.rs`: runs that meet at a node where only single track meets (`single_junction`: three or more single,
non-crossover pieces and nothing else) are taken together (`run_group`, transitively). Trains pass such a junction
from the pieces on one side to those on the other, so each run's way fixes the others' (a two-colouring over the
junctions' sides, `group_ways`). The group runs one way where every end it has elsewhere is a join that counts and
all of them agree. An end the player left unjoined at the double track (drawn zoomed out, where the pointer picks
neither track) says nothing when another of the group's ends joins that double at the same node: the pointer-drawn
scene below gave the bypass no join there, so without this the group fell back. Anything else (a bare end, a station at an end, an end with no join, joins that disagree, a
junction with nothing on one side, a contradiction round a loop) keeps today's rule: each run by its own ends, so a
run ending at such a junction runs both ways. A lone run is untouched. `derive_ways` re-derives the whole group when
any run in it is touched, so adding or taking out a bypass sets the legs right in the same edit.

Docs: track-and-service.md "The way single track runs" (one paragraph added); wiki line-capacity (one sentence).

## For Anita (design calls, not built)

1. **A bypass joined to the far track.** Drawing the bypass into the double track junction, the pointer picks which
   of the double's two tracks it joins (T-3137). Joined to the track that runs against its leg, the group's joins
   disagree and the whole north side runs both ways again: her bug comes back. Options: (a) leave it; the map shows
   the bypass crossing to the far track and the track panel's way switch puts it right; (b) when a single track is
   drawn into a junction where another single from the same split already joins, it takes that one's track whatever
   the pointer. I'd take (b), as a small drawing task.
2. **A terminus spur off a one-way leg.** A branch to a station leaving one leg of a split must run both ways (trains
   go in and come back out), and today that makes the whole leg two-way, so the platform on it is no longer one-way
   and a line through both side platforms zigzags as before. Options: (a) leave it (the leg is two-way, players set
   the way with the switch); (b) keep the leg one way past the spur's junction and two-way between the split and
   the spur (trains to the spur come back the way they went); (c) refuse nothing but badge the leg. I'd take (a)
   for now; it is rare and (b) is a bigger rule.
3. **Two-way platform beside a one-way one.** Whenever ways do contradict (1 and 2 above), a line listing both side
   platforms still ends at one and turns back in the middle (T-3651's fallback, `best_slot`). Option: serve the
   two-way one once too, by whichever run passes it, so no line ever zigzags through a split station. That changes
   T-3605's "the stop list says where trains stop" for two-way stations, so it is hers to decide.
