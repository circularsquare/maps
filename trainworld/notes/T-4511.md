## Interlined lines and the waits (her 11-27, T-4514)

- **The spacing today** (`spacing::place`, track-and-service.md 6.3 "Spaced from other lines"): on every edit the
  edited lines get the phase (24 tried a headway) that keeps their trains furthest from half the smaller headway apart
  from each other line's at the first place they meet, a merge counting 25 times anything else. Two lines at one
  headway come out even; a Headway line's spare time is placed with it, so both directions take even turns (T-2972).
  Count lines' reverse direction is a compromise where their branches take different times.
- **Rates with no simple ratio drift**: every 11 and every 14 min, each train of the 11 lands 3 min further round the
  14's cycle, so within 14 trains one pair is at most 30 s apart, whatever the phase. Belgrade's morning (T-4514's
  log): 247 of 1,764 gaps under 1 min, trams 7L, 12 and 9L 0 s apart at closest. Only single trains can fix that,
  which is why she wants single moves, the gap and auto-space together.
- **The waits (T-4514, built, held off master)**: where lines leave one platform for one platform at the next stop
  (rides within 30 s), riders who take whichever comes wait by the real gaps: the sum of the squared gaps over twice
  their sum, a factor on the evenly spaced wait. Belgrade: 453,190 -> 431,687 rail trips (-4.7%), nearly all from lines
  at different rates; NYC's real save unchanged. An Offset step or re-spacing calculates trips once a wait moves more
  than 5 s. `gaps.rs` lays each line out as an even pattern from its departure nearest the period's middle; with moves
  it lays out the real departures (stage 9).
- **Readout** (T-4515, P3, separate): on the departures board, "Average wait with the 5: 3.1 min", hover "2.5 min if
  the trains were evenly spaced".

## What the player gets

In the schedule window (a rail or tram line's, or a group's), a click on a train's column in the timetable, or on its
line in the chart, selects it: the column is tinted, its chart line drawn lit as on a hover, and a stepper stands above
its column ("< +2 min >"; her 11-12 call for Exact: "nudge arrows be drawn directly above the timetable column of the
train we're looking at"). A click elsewhere in the table or chart, or Esc, clears it. Each step moves that one
departure, the whole way along the line (its times at every stop), earlier or later than the game runs it; the board,
the chart, the table and the drawn train follow at once, with nothing to settle and no trips calculated. Back at 0 the
train runs where its row puts it. A moved train's column has a small dot over its bullet; the bullet's hover says
"Moved 2 min later". While the line has any moved train, the table's tools have "Reset 3 moved trains" (one undoable
edit; it counts and clears moves waiting for their train too, below).

Any rail train in the table can be moved: the window's line, a related line's columns (T-3818) and, in a group's
window, every line's. Bus and ferry lines have no table and no moves. A line on a circle can't move one train alone
(its trains never stand at an end).

Trains the game moved itself (the 90 s gap, auto-space) show in the same places with no dot; the bullet's hover says
why: "Moved 40 s later to stay 90 s from the 7:38 on Red", "Moved 1 min later to even out gaps with other lines".

## How far it moves, and what refuses

A move of `d` seconds of one departure (one direction) is allowed while all of these hold, for it and for every
train moved with it (timed partners, below). The range is worked out on the slot's own timetable (variant 0);
retime then fits each train's own waits into what is left (rule 1's last point).

1. **Its train makes its next departure.** A train may stand at the end of its journey down to
   `TURN_MIN_S.min(close_stand)` (60 s; `LineSvc::trip_len_var`'s rule for stage 2's per-train waits). Later: the
   train reaches the far end `d` later, so `d` is at most its stand there minus that. Earlier: the train that runs
   it reached the first stop on its last journey, so `-d` is at most its stand there minus that. On a line set by
   train counts and not paced each end stands exactly the turnaround (`spare_paced` is 0), 180 s for rail: about
   2 min either way. A Headway or paced line adds its spare time at that end (`spare` times `line_split`'s share).
   Three parts of the sim must agree with this, and don't today:
   - `hand_over` (Gemini 1): a train is ready at its `end` (journey plus the whole turnaround) and waits at most
     `min(1 h, headway)` for its next departure, so today a departure moved earlier finds no train and a new one
     enters service, and a train whose stand at the other end grew past the window leaves service. `Trip` gains the
     seconds its departure was moved, and `hand_over` measures each train's wait from its unmoved times: a departure
     moved earlier takes a train standing down to the 60 s, a departure moved later may keep its train waiting that
     much longer, and a train whose journey was moved earlier may wait that much longer at the far end. Nothing
     changes for a trip with no move (no global change to `ready`).
   - The moved trip's own end: `max(departure + the mean journey and stand, moved departure + journey + 60 s)`, as
     `trip_len_var`.
   - retime's room (Gemini 2): `lead_in` and `following` give every train of a slot the same room to lose time, its
     unmoved stand at the far end minus 60 s. Each train's room loses its own later move. And `floor` checks only the
     slot's average stand (`stand_at_end`), so it can't see one moved train: a per-train check is added, under which a
     moved train whose own waits would leave it short of the 60 s runs its slot's timetable (variant 0), which rule 1
     already fits. The rest of the run keeps its variants.
2. **It stays clear of the line's trains before and after it** in that direction, as they run (moved or not): at
   least the longest time one of its trains holds any place it uses that way (the capacity users' `occ`: a platform's
   dwell and clearance, a terminal's, a junction's crossing time), and at least 1 min. Not `Limit.capS` (Gemini 3):
   that is the line's share of its bottleneck with the other lines' trains taken out, so a line running near its limit
   would refuse every move. So moves never change the order of a line's trains, and every departure keeps its place
   in the pattern (stage 2's `place` key, the table's columns). A one-platform terminus still fits (its `occ` is the
   turnaround and clearance). And at least 90 s from another line's train at a stop they share, after that train
   has moved out of its way where it can ("The 90 s minimum gap").
3. **Its line runs a timetable**: a rail or tram line, running, not a bus or ferry line, not a circle (its stand at an
   end is the dwell, so rule 1 leaves nothing).

At a limit the arrow knocks and shakes (as the Offset stepper), and its hover names the reason: "Its train has to
leave Gum again at 8:14", "Its train gets to Ash at 7:58", "Too close to the 7:38", "Too close to the 7:38 on Red". There is no separate half a
headway cap: rule 2 is the one that keeps it the same train.

Not chosen (her 11-27, "sure" to the rule above): up to half a headway, as the Offset column. A train later than its stand allows
would then miss its next departure; `hand_over` would draw another train entering service for it and the late one
leaving service: for those minutes the map shows one more train than the line has, and nothing pays for it. Exact
mode's fleet from chaining (T-3346 6a: "a departure no arriving train can make after its turnaround brings a train
in, so a move or add can buy a train") is the honest version, and lifts rule 1.

## How a moved train lasts through later edits

Only the player's moves and their partners' are keyed and saved; the gap's and auto-space's are worked out again on
every edit near them, so they need no key.

**Its key.** A move is stored per line as (direction, time, seconds moved, own or moved with another train). The time
is the departure from the line's first stop that way as its pattern runs it, **plus the line's `lead`** (every
departure leaves `lead` before its pattern's point, and a reroute can change `lead` by minutes; Gemini 5). Only an
edit that moves the row's phase on purpose rewrites keys, inside its own op so undo is exact: `Op::Nudge` (the row
Offset, T-3742 and T-4510) shifts by its seconds the keys of every line whose phase it sets (the anchor and its timed
partners) and of their quad twins, which `pin_twin_phases` moves with them. Subtracting `line_offset` at read time
would not do (Gemini 5): a partner's phase moves with no offset of its own, and so does a twin's.

**Its match.** Whenever the line's departures are laid out, each move goes to the departure of that direction whose
time (plus `lead`) is nearest its key, within half that departure's headway; if two moves want one departure, the
nearer wins. A move nothing matches waits: not shown, not run, kept and saved (so it comes back when its train does,
after an undo or a reload alike), counted and cleared by "Reset N moved trains", removed with the line. The seconds are
clamped to the range as it stands now (shown and run clamped; the stored value comes back if the room does). So:

- **Rates change** (a count or headway step, more periods, a row's hours): the train leaving nearest the moved one's
  old time takes its move ("the 7:32" stays "the 7:32", or becomes the 7:30 at a new headway, still 2 min later).
- **Stops added, track edits, a reroute**: run times change, so `lead`, the reverse departures and a count line's
  headway drift; the key leaves `lead` out and the reverse departures move continuously (the modulo in `off` only
  renumbers them), so the nearest match follows; the range is worked out again. An edit that swaps a line's first and
  last stops swaps its moves' directions in the same op.
- **The row's own Offset moving**: rewrites the keys with the phases, above; the moved train keeps its own minutes.
- **The ends of a period**: a row's Offset or a rate change can push a period's last train past its end or bring a
  new first one in (`stretch_points`, `MIN_GAP_SHARE`); a move on a train that stops existing waits, as above.
- **The spacing**: a row whose slots carry any move (own or a partner's), or whose quad twin's do, is held as an
  Offset holds it (`Network::line_offset`): `spacing::place` doesn't re-place its phase or spare time split,
  weekdays and, where moves run then, weekends (`place_weekend` swaps the weekend fields in; `held(l, s)` is read
  there too). "Held at 0" can't be said by `line_offset` (under 0.5 s is released, `ZERO_S`): holding becomes "an
  Offset or a move" (`held(l, s)`), derived, nothing new stored. Resetting the row's last move releases it; it keeps
  its times until its line's next edit places it again.
- **The line removed**: its moves go into the removal's undo (as `nudge::release` does for offsets). Lines made by
  an edit from another start with none; copying a schedule doesn't carry them.
- **Eras** (T-206): a move is a service edit with the edit lead (T-1071): an era at the edit, trains running carry
  on, a train that has left today moves from tomorrow. `encode_eras` doesn't save moves: after a load, an era's
  trains (its last minutes) run unmoved.

## What the sim does with an uneven pattern

- **The lattice** stays a pure function of the pattern and the line's moves, so every window agrees. Per stretch it
  makes its points `ps + k hw` and drops those too close after the stretch before (`MIN_GAP_SHARE`) as now, on the
  unmoved times; then each matched move adds its seconds. So moves never change which departures exist or their
  order. `Lattice` stays `Copy`; the moves (an immutable `Arc<[Move]>` per line in `Service`, an era keeping the
  one it ran) are passed beside it into `lattice_deps` and its callers (service.rs, timing.rs, span.rs, boarding.rs
  and tests), not looked up from an id the lattice holds (Gemini 7). The keys that hash a pattern name their fields,
  so each takes the moves explicitly: `Lattice::timing_key` (variants), `timing::cluster_key`, `span::span_key`,
  `Boarding::key` (whatever the weekend rates), and the eras' part of it. A game with no moves is byte for byte and
  bit for bit as now.
- **Terminals and the fleet**: `hand_over` as in rule 1. Within the range no train enters or leaves service; the
  same trains run the same distance, so the fleet, running cost and fares are unchanged.
- **Spacing** (`spacing::place`) scores a line's even pattern over one headway (`crowding`). Rows held by the player's moves aren't
  placed (gap and auto-space moves hold nothing); other lines are placed against a moved line's even pattern (**a lie**: a line placed later can meet a moved
  train; the player sees it on the chart).
- **Capacity** (Kingman delays from rates): unchanged (**a lie**: two trains moved close together queue no more on
  shared track than evenly spaced ones; the map's guard holds a train that would run into another, T-3264).
- **Each train's own waits** (`timing::retime`): lays out the other lines' trains with `lattice_deps`, so it sees
  the moved times, and its key sees the moves. A moved train keeps its place in the pattern, so its variant slot.
  Its room and the per-train floor are in rule 1.
- **Boarding** (the per-train account): runs the drawn departures to the bit, so it is honest: a train moved later,
  after a longer gap, takes more riders and the one after it fewer.
- **Demand**: with the waits on, a move at a shared stop changes that stretch's factor (from the moved departures,
  stage 9) and calculates trips past 5 s. Elsewhere waits are by headway, so riders don't see a move. **Lies**: an uneven pattern's mean wait is longer:
  about 0.3 s for one train moved 2 min on a 10 min, 14 h row, up to about half a minute where every other train of a
  30 min row is moved 6 min (gaps 24 and 36: a mean wait of 15.6 min, not 15) (Gemini 8); a connection timed by moving
  a train shortens no rider's wait in the trip figures; a stop's first or last train moved reaches demand at the line's
  next edit (as T-3742, DECISIONS 2026-11-27). Elsewhere no trip calculation per click.
- **Weekends** (her yes, 11-27: weekdays and weekends apart, with a switch): a move belongs to one kind of day. The
  table, chart and board get a Weekdays / Weekends switch (today's kind first) showing a reference day of that kind
  (`run_deps_as` already takes a kind of day; `line_trips`, `station_departures`, the clock worker, table, chart
  and board learn it). Where the weekend has its own Offset (T-4510) a weekend move is keyed without it, as weekdays.
  Moves never turn `rest_on` on: `retime` skips timing a pair train by train where one line has it and the other
  not (`flat.rest_on != slat.rest_on`, timing.rs `lead_in`, `following`, `passing_waits`; Gemini 4), so a
  weekday move would quietly untime its pair. Instead the lattice carries the line's calendar (`cal`) whenever the
  line has moves of one kind of day only, and applies each move only on days of its kind; retime works on weekdays
  (`DAY_REF`), so weekend moves never reach it, and a train moved on weekends only runs its slot's timetable then
  (variant 0). `Boarding::key` takes both kinds' moves. (T-4510's weekend offsets meet the same `rest_on` check if
  they set it on a line with the weekday rates; for T-4510's builder.)
- **Twins** (T-3333): a twin's trains show with its parent's bullet and move like any (the move is on the twin line);
  they hold the parent's row and follow its Offset (above). Gone with twins at rework stage 6c.

## Timed pairs

T-3742's rule, per train, kept to one step (Gemini 6). In a slot where lines are timed together, a train's partners
are, in that direction, the trains retime timed directly with it: the faster train timed behind it with nowhere to
pass (`following`: one faster train behind each slower one), and at passing stations the faster trains it waits for,
or for a faster train the slower trains that wait for it (`passing_waits`). Never through them: a faster train that
passes two locals moves with the one moved, not the other, so one click can't spread through the day. retime keeps
these pairs per run and slot as it works them out (derived, not saved; `Capacity::follow_pairs` is line-level and
can't say which train). Moving a train moves its partners by the same seconds, each stored as a move of its own line
marked "moved with"; the range is the tightest of the group's; resetting it resets theirs in the same edit; their rows
are held too, so the spacing doesn't re-place them away from their keys. The arrows act on the train clicked, faster
or slower, and the value's hover names the others: "Express is timed with this train and moves with it". Trains a
move reaches only through a partner (the local the express also passes) are retimed, within rule 1's room and the
per-train floor. A later edit that changes the pairing leaves each partner move on its nearest train, placed and
retimed as today.

## The 90 s minimum gap (her 11-27)

**Rule.** At every stop where trains of two or more rail or tram lines leave one platform onto the same track (every
stop of a shared stretch in T-4514's sense, and a platform just before lines split), departures of different lines
are at least 90 s apart, wherever a train can move that far. Weekdays and weekends; trains and trams alike. A line's
own trains: unchanged (rule 2, `MIN_GAP_SHARE`). Not covered: lines merging between stations with no shared stop (the
spacing's merge weight and capacity's queueing, as now) and trains passing a platform (retime's following and
passing waits, as now).

**Where it's enforced: the timetable.** The gap pass makes moves of its own, kind "gap", in the same per-line list as
the player's ("own", "with"), which the lattice applies to every departure it lays out (`lattice_deps` with the
moves, stage 2). So the board, the timetable, the chart, the drawn trains, retime, boarding and T-4514's factor all
read the same times. The map's display guard stays as it is, for trains meeting on track.

**What shifts.**
- Gap moves are derived, never saved: worked out again for every cluster of lines linked by shared stops that an
  edit touches (its lines, any line whose phase, moves or option changed), from no gap moves, after the spacing places
  phases. A pure function of the network, so a load, an undo and every window agree. Eras keep the ones they ran.
- Order: the player's moves and their partners' stay put; then the auto-space lines (below); then, walking each
  stop's departures in time, a pair closer than 90 s moves one train: the one whose line runs less often (moving a
  train d seconds raises its own line's mean wait by about d² / (trains x headway): half a second for 90 s on a 10 min
  line over 4 h, less on a rarer line); where it can't move, the other. Ties: the lower line id.
- How far: the smallest shift, earlier or later, that clears 90 s at every shared stop of that train, within rules 1
  and 2 of "How far it moves" (its next departure with its 60 s stand, at least 1 min from its own line's trains), counting moves on
  its other journeys. Its timed partners move with it ("Timed pairs"). So no train enters or leaves service, and the
  fleet, running cost and fares don't change.
- Where nothing fits (both trains at the end of their range, a circle line, more than 40 trains an hour at one
  platform) the two stay as far apart as moves allow, and a count goes into diagnostics. Belgrade's morning
  (estimate below): 371 of 1,834 trains move (20%), 6 pairs left.
- Gap moves don't hold a row (only the player's do), so the spacing keeps placing phases on every edit.
- Worked out on the slot's timetable (variant 0), as the range is. **A lie**: retime's passing holds run after it, so
  a hold can bring a train back under 90 s further down a stretch; rare, since a shared stretch is lines with the
  same stops and passing pairs are expresses and locals.

**What refuses.** Only a player's step: one that would leave its train under 90 s from another line's train at a
shared stop, after that train has moved out of the way where it can, knocks with "Too close to the 7:38 on Red". Rate,
count, Offset, stop and track edits are never refused for it (lines at different rates would refuse most of them);
the gap pass clears again after each.

**Cost.** Per edit, estimated 2-5 ms natively for a whole Belgrade pass and 8-20 ms on the Asia save (from
`groups`' measured 1.8 and 6.1 ms and 20% of trains moving), about 2x that in the game; an edit runs only its
clusters. Memory: about 20 bytes a moved train, about 30 KB on Belgrade and 120 KB on Asia. Figures: Belgrade's
morning factor 1.41 -> 1.34, so about -3.9% rail trips with the waits instead of -4.7% (estimates, "Performance").

## Auto-space (her 11-27)

Her words: "perhaps also just an auto space mode to auto shift a line's trains to maximize gaps with other trains on
shared sections ... in some cases you might not actually want to do this, for instance, if you want headways to remain
uniform on the separate sections and its not worth shifting to have consistent gaps on a short tiny shared section."

- **The option**: per line, off by default, a toggle in the schedule window's tools, "Space trains evenly with other
  lines". `Op::AutoSpace { id, on }` with undo, saved in `LMV1`. Not shown for a bus or ferry line, a circle, or a
  line on no shared stop.
- **What it does**: moves that line's single trains, within rules 1 and 2 and the 90 s gap, to make its waits as
  short as it can, counting each of its stops once per period, periods by their hours: at a stop it shares, the
  combined wait of every line there (T-4514's sum of squared gaps over twice their sum); at a stop it runs alone, its
  own wait from its own gaps. A whole-train move leaves its own gaps the same at each stop, so the own part is one
  wait times the stops it runs alone. That is her caveat inside the rule: 2 shared stops of 20 and it moves little; a
  line sharing most of its route evens the shared part. Its rate, fleet and the row's phase don't change (the spacing
  places the phase first).
- **Search**: per auto line in id order, each of its trains in time order, shifts in 15 s steps across its range,
  the best kept; two sweeps, from no auto moves, so it is a pure function of the network as the gap pass is. Stops of
  one stretch with the same lines and relative times are scored once. The player's moved trains stay put; another
  auto line's are scored as they stand.
- **When it runs**: with every edit that touches its cluster, as the gap pass; derived, never saved. Not on demand: a
  one-off "Space now" would store player moves, which the next rate or run time edit leaves uneven, and the option is
  the player saying what they want kept. Turning it off drops its moves in the same edit.
- **A line's own headway elsewhere**: no train moves past its range (about 2 min on a count line, 4-6 min on a
  Headway line) or closer than 1 min to its own trains, and its unshared stops are in the score.
- **Shows**: an auto-moved train's bullet hover, "Moved 1 min later to even out gaps with other lines"; no dot (the
  dot is the player's own); "Reset N moved trains" counts only the player's.
- **Estimate** (Belgrade's 60 logged morning stretches, each scored on its own, so an upper bound): every line on
  takes the mean factor 1.41 -> 1.19 (1.15 with its own gaps not counted, 1.24 counted three times); one line per
  stretch on, 1.27. It moves about 95% of an auto line's trains, 90 s on average. So about -2.2% rail trips with

## Ruled out

- **Moving a train's whole day** (the vehicle: all its departures, both ways, as the track-drawing rail game's shifts
  do): no turnaround limit, but the game has no stable vehicles across rows (`hand_over` chains them as it draws), and
  it moves 1 in n of the line's trains all day to fix one meeting.
- **A move pushing the train's later departures** when it eats past its stand (as a delay works through real
  terminals): moves departures the player didn't pick, in the other direction too. Exact's chaining is the honest
  version.
- **Keying moves by place in the pattern** (stretch and index): any headway change lands every move on another train.
- **Absolute times** (the 7:34, whatever the row does): the row's Offset would leave moved trains behind, and a count
  step would put two trains close together.
- **Partners through partners**: closes over the whole day where a faster train passes two slower ones.
- **Weekday moves by turning `rest_on` on**: untimes the line's pairs (`flat.rest_on != slat.rest_on`).
- **A conflict warning**: the meetings the spacing reads could flag "within 40 s of a Red train at Elm" cheaply; left
  for Exact's conflict marks, as T-3742 left it.
- **Enforcing 90 s by refusing edits**: lines at different rates meet whatever the phase, so most rate edits on
  Belgrade would be refused.
- **More phases per row** (96 tried a headway, not 24): drifting rates aren't a phase problem.
- **The gap in the drawing only** (the display guard): the board, the chart and the waits would still show trains
  seconds apart.
- **Saving gap and auto-space moves**: they follow from saved phases, moves and flags, so a load gets them back;
  saved, they could go stale after an edit that didn't redo them.
- **Auto-space as a "Space now" button**: its moves would be the player's, keyed and saved, and the next rate or run
  time edit leaves them uneven.
- **Auto-space warm-started from the last edit's moves**: quicker, but the result would depend on the edits before
  it, so a reload could space trains differently.

## Review (Gemini, 2026-11-27; checked against the code)

1. **`hand_over` in both directions: stands.** The design named the earlier case but not how; `ready` is the whole
   turnaround and `can` allows a wait of `min(HANDOVER_MAX_S, hw)` (service.rs 2229-2234), so a later move on a
   line with its spare time at the first stop, or an earlier move's longer stand at the far end, would drop and spawn
   trains too. Fixed in rule 1: `Trip` carries its move, waits measured from unmoved times, nothing else changes.
2. **Slack spent twice by retime; `floor` blind to one train: stands** (the 60 s vs `TURN_MIN_S.min(close_stand)`
   point is the same number for every rail line, adopted anyway). `lead_in` (timing.rs 726-730) and `following`
   (901-905) size room on the unmoved stand; `stand_at_end` averages over the slot (398). Fixed: per-train room less
   the move, a per-train floor to the slot's timetable, the range worked out on variant 0.
3. **`Limit.capS` blocks moves near capacity: stands.** It is `3600 / most` with the other lines' load out
   (limits.rs `line_limits`). Rule 2 now uses the line's own longest per-train occupancy (`User.occ`), at least 1 min.
4. **`rest_on` for weekday-only moves untimes pairs: stands** (timing.rs 594, 704, 859). Moves never set `rest_on`;
   with her yes to separate weekends (11-27) the lattice carries the calendar for per-kind moves instead (stage 9).
   Flagged for T-4510.
5. **Keys after Offset steps, twins, reroutes: stands, mostly.** Partners and twins: `line_offset` is None while
   their phase moves (nudge.rs `nudge_op`, `pin_twin_phases`); keys are now rewritten inside `Op::Nudge` instead of
   subtracting at read time, and twins hold the parent's row. Reroutes: `lead` moves every departure, now left out of
   the key; swapped ends swap directions. Waiting moves are now saved and cleared by Reset (the old note kept them only
   in memory). Partly: the wrap at a period's ends drops or adds a train at the ends whatever the key; a move on such a
   train waits, which the note now says.
6. **Per-train partners ambiguous: stands.** `follow_pairs` is line-level; passes can be many to many and a closure
   can spread through the day; unheld partner rows drift. Now one step only, by direction, pairs kept by retime,
   partner rows held, no anchor redirection per train.
7. **`Copy` lattice with an id; keys hashed by hand: stands (medium).** Moves are passed beside the lattice; each key
   is named in stage 1; `Boarding::key` takes them whatever the weekend rates; eras don't save them (said).
8. **Demand lie understated; save gaps: partly.** One moved train is the 0.3 s; the note now gives the worst case
   (about 30 s on a 30 min row). `SAVE_LEVEL` and `LMV1`'s checks added; the block order after T-4510's was already
   there.
