## For Anita

- **What you get.** Click a train in the schedule window's timetable (its column, or its line on the chart) and arrows
  appear above its column: 1 min a click, 15 s with Shift, 10 min with Ctrl. Only that train moves; one undo a click.
- **How far.** Until its train can't make its next departure from the far end (it may stand 1 min there instead of 3)
  or comes too close to the trains before and after it: about 2 min either way on a line set by train counts, often
  4-6 min on a Headway line. The arrow then knocks and says why ("Its train has to leave Gum again at 8:14").
- **It lasts.** The game stops re-spacing a row with a moved train, as with an Offset. Through later edits the move
  stays with the train leaving nearest its old time; the row's Offset carries it; an express timed behind moves too.
- **Not done.** Riders, money and delay figures barely move; a train moved into another line's train is held back by
  the map, with no warning (Exact mode's job). About 9-11 h in 9 stages of about an hour.
- **Calls for you:** (1) how far: only as far as the train still makes its next departure, or up to half a headway
  with the map borrowing a train you never bought for those minutes? I'd keep the first; Exact mode lifts it later by
  buying the train. (2) Weekends: moves apart per kind of day, with a Weekdays / Weekends switch over the timetable, or
  every day? I'd keep them apart, like your weekend offsets (T-4510). (3) Build now, or wait for Exact mode (24-34 h)?
  I'd build now: the selection and arrows are Exact's first part.

## What the player gets

In the schedule window (a rail or tram line's, or a group's), a click on a train's column in the timetable, or on its
line in the chart, selects it: the column is tinted, its chart line drawn lit as on a hover, and a stepper stands above
its column ("< +2 min >"; her 11-12 call for Exact: "nudge arrows be drawn directly above the timetable column of the
train we're looking at"). A click elsewhere in the table or chart, or Esc, clears it. Each step moves that one
departure, the whole way along the line (its times at every stop), earlier or later than the game runs it; the board,
the chart, the table and the drawn train follow at once, with nothing to settle and no trips calculated. Back at 0 the
train runs where its row puts it. A moved train's column has a small dot over its bullet; the bullet's hover says
"Moved 2 min later". While any train of the line is moved on the kind of day shown, the table's tools have "Reset 3
moved trains" (one undoable edit).

Any rail train in the table can be moved: the window's line, a related line's columns (T-3818) and, in a group's
window, every line's. Bus and ferry lines have no table and no moves. A line on a circle can't move one train alone
(its trains never stand at an end).

**Recommended: the column and the chart line, one shared selection, arrows above the column only.** She left the chart
arrows "up to you"; one place for the control is clearer, and the chart line lights up under it. The map gets an
entry, not a control: the train view (TrainInspector) gets the schedule icon, which opens the window with that train
selected (the map's selection is already `line, profile, dep`, the table's key). The board stays read-only; the
selected train's minute could be tinted there later (not in these stages).

A click in the table today starts a drag (it scrolls the table); a click is a press and release within 4 px.

## How far it moves, and what refuses

A move of `d` seconds of one departure (one direction) is allowed while all of these hold, for it and for every
train moved with it (timed partners, below):

1. **Its train makes its next departure.** Later: the train reaches the far end `d` later and may stand there down
   to `timing::TURN_MIN_S` (60 s), the rule stage 2's per-train waits already use (`LineSvc::trip_len_var`). So
   `d` is at most its stand at the far end minus 60 s. Earlier: the train that runs it reached the first stop on its
   last journey and may stand there down to 60 s. On a line set by train counts and not paced, each end stands exactly
   the turnaround (`spare_paced` is 0), 180 s for rail: about 2 min either way. A Headway or paced line adds its
   spare time at that end (`spare` times the split, `line_split`), on average half a headway shared by the two
   ends. A train already running its own timetable (a stage 2 variant that waits longer) has less.
2. **It stays clear of the line's trains before and after it** in that direction, as they run (moved or not): at
   least the row's shortest headway the track allows (`Limit.capS`, the T-679 figure), and at least 1 min. So
   moves never change the order of a line's trains, and every departure keeps its place in the pattern (stage 2's
   `place` key, the table's columns).
3. **Its line runs a timetable**: a rail or tram line, running, not a bus or ferry line, not a circle (its stand at
   an end is the dwell, under 60 s, so rule 1 leaves nothing).

At a limit the arrow knocks and shakes (as the Offset stepper), and its hover names the reason: "Its train has to
leave Gum again at 8:14", "Its train gets to Ash at 7:58", "Too close to the 7:38". There is no separate half a
headway cap: rule 2 is the one that keeps it the same train.

The other choice for her (call 1): up to half a headway, as the Offset column. A train later than its stand allows
would then miss its next departure, and `hand_over` would draw another train entering service there for it, and the
late one leaving service or standing a headway: for those minutes the map shows one more train than the line has, and
nothing pays for it. Exact mode's fleet from chaining (T-3346 6a: "a departure no arriving train can make after its
turnaround brings a train in, so a move or add can buy a train") is the honest version of that, and lifts rule 1.

## How a moved train lasts through later edits

**Its key.** A move is stored per line as (kind of day, direction, time, seconds moved). The time is the departure
from the line's first stop that way as its row runs it, in the line's local day, **without the row's Offset**. It is
written once and never rewritten by another edit, so every undo is exact.

**Its match.** Whenever the line's departures are laid out, each move goes to the departure of that direction and
kind of day whose own time (again without the row's Offset) is nearest its key, within half that departure's
headway; if two moves want one departure, the nearer one wins. A move nothing matches is dormant: not shown, not run,
kept while the game runs (so undoing the edit that hid it brings it back), not saved. The seconds are clamped to the
range above as it stands now (the clamped value is what is shown and run; the stored value comes back if the room
does). So:

- **Rates change** (a count or headway step, more periods, a row's hours): the train leaving nearest the moved one's
  old time takes its move ("the 7:32" stays "the 7:32", or becomes the 7:30 at a new headway, still 2 min later).
  Usually that keeps what the player was after; where it doesn't, the move is one click to reset.
- **Stops added, track edits, a reroute**: run times change, so the reverse departures and a count line's headway
  drift by seconds to minutes; the nearest match follows them, and the range is worked out again.
- **The row's own Offset moving** (T-3742, T-4510): the keys leave the Offset out, so a moved train moves with its
  row and keeps its own minutes on top.
- **The spacing**: a row (its slots) with a moved train of its own is held, as an Offset holds it
  (`Network::line_offset`); the game stops re-placing its phase and spare time split. A held row with no Offset is
  "held at 0", which `line_offset` can't say today (under 0.5 s is released, `ZERO_S`): holding becomes
  "an Offset or a moved train" (`held(l, s)`), derived, nothing new stored. Resetting the row's last moved train
  releases it; it keeps its times until its line's next edit places it again (as for an Offset).
- **The line removed**: its moves go into the removal's undo (as `nudge::release` does for offsets). Lines made by
  an edit from another start with none; copying a schedule doesn't carry them.
- **Eras** (T-206): a move is a service edit with the edit lead (T-1071): an era at the edit, trains running carry
  on, a train that has left today moves from tomorrow.

## What the sim does with an uneven pattern

- **The lattice** stays a pure function of the pattern, so every window agrees. Per stretch it makes its points
  `ps + k hw` and drops those too close after the stretch before (`MIN_GAP_SHARE`) as now, on the unmoved times;
  then each matched move adds its seconds. So moves never change which departures exist or their order. The table of
  moves is immutable and shared (`Arc`); `Lattice` (which is `Copy` and compared everywhere) gains only its id per
  kind of day, so eras keep theirs and every key that hashes a lattice (`timing_key`, `span_key`, boarding's key,
  retime's `cluster_key`) sees a move. A save or a game with no moves is byte for byte and bit for bit as now.
- **Drawn trains and terminals** (`run_deps`, `hand_over`): a moved train's end is `max(departure + the mean
  journey and stand, moved departure + journey + 60 s)`, as `trip_len_var`, so a later train stands less at the far
  end and takes its usual next departure. `hand_over` lets a train stand down to 60 s at the first stop for a
  departure moved earlier. Within the range no train enters or leaves service for a move.
- **The fleet and money**: unchanged. The same trains run the same distance; the range keeps every train making its
  next departure.
- **Spacing** (`spacing::place`) scores a line's even pattern over one headway (`crowding`). Held rows aren't
  placed. Other lines are placed against a moved line's even pattern, not its moved trains (**a lie**: a line placed
  later can meet a moved train; the player sees it on the chart).
- **Capacity** (Kingman delays from rates): unchanged (**a lie**: two trains moved close together queue no more on
  shared track than evenly spaced ones; the map's guard holds a train that would run into another, T-3264).
- **Each train's own waits** (`timing::retime`): reads the moved times, since it lays out the other lines' trains
  with `lattice_deps`, and its key now sees the move. A moved train keeps its place in the pattern, so its variant
  slot. Where a move leaves a timed train unable to make its next departure, `timing::floor` puts that run back on the
  averaged waits, as today for any pair the spacing can't suit; the partner rule below keeps that from happening on a
  move itself.
- **Boarding** (the per-train account): runs the drawn departures to the bit, so it is honest: a train moved later,
  after a longer gap, takes more riders and the one after it fewer. Its key gains the moves.
- **Demand**: waits are by headway, so riders don't see a move. **Lies**: an uneven pattern's mean wait is a little
  longer (moving one train of n by `d` at headway `h` adds `d^2 / (n h)` to the mean: about 0.3 s for 2 min on a
  10 min, 14 h row); a connection timed by moving a train shortens no rider's wait in the trip figures; a stop's first
  or last train moved reaches demand at the line's next edit (as T-3742, DECISIONS 2026-11-27). No trip calculation per
  click.
- **Weekends**: a move belongs to one kind of day (call 2). A weekday move doesn't run on weekends, so a line whose
  weekends run its weekday pattern now lays its weekend out on its own (`rest_on`) where the moves differ; T-4510 has
  to do the same for weekend offsets on such lines, so the builder takes its path. Weekend departures carry no stage 2
  variants (`NO_PLACE`); moves don't need them. If she picks every day: moves run on weekends only where the weekend
  runs the weekday pattern (T-3742's rule), never on a line's own weekend rates.
- **Twins** (T-3333): a twin's trains show with its parent's bullet and move like any (the move is on the twin line);
  gone with twins at rework stage 6c.

## Timed pairs

T-3742's anchor rule, per train. In a slot where lines are timed together (`Capacity::follow_pairs`), a train's
partners are the faster trains the game times behind it (`follow_one`: each faster train behind the slower one ahead
of it) or that pass it at passing stations (`overtake::train_waits`), and so on through them. Moving a train moves
its partners by the same seconds, each stored as a move on its own line marked as moved with it, so passes and waits
stay as sized and retime's variants stay put. The arrows on a faster train act on its anchor's train (the slower one
it is timed behind, up the chain), and the value's hover names the others: "Express is timed with this train and
moves with it". The range is the tightest of the group's. Partner moves don't hold their rows: re-placed against the
anchor's even pattern, their moves still mirror the anchor's (spacing reads the unmoved times of both). Resetting the
anchor's move resets theirs in the same edit. A later edit that changes the pairing leaves a partner move on the
nearest train, placed and retimed as today.

## Saves

**A new block, not LOF1.** LOF1 (T-3742, level 43, unpublished) is per row and T-4510 is about to change it for
weekend offsets; moves are per departure, a different shape, and most games will have one without the other.
Trailing `LMV1` after LOF1 (or after whatever T-4510 adds): count, then per move the line index, the kind of day,
the direction, a flag (own, or moved with another train), the key (f32 bits, local seconds) and the seconds (f32
bits); dormant moves left out; written only when there is one. Holding needs nothing saved (derived). Level: the next
free one when it lands (44 unless T-4510 takes it; if both land before a publish, one level for both is the
orchestrator's call). The shonei-server save check (T-4435's parser) learns `LMV1` and the level: commit there,
deploy on her word before the publish.

## Ruled out

- **Moving a train's whole day** (the vehicle: all its departures, both ways, as the track-drawing rail game's shifts
  do): no turnaround limit, but the game has no stable vehicles across rows (`hand_over` chains them as it draws), and
  it moves 1 in n of the line's trains all day to fix one meeting.
- **A move pushing the train's later departures** when it eats past its stand (as a delay works through real
  terminals): moves departures the player didn't pick, in the other direction too. Exact's chaining is the honest
  version.
- **Keying moves by place in the pattern** (stretch and index): any headway change lands every move on another train.
- **Absolute times** (the 7:34, whatever the row does): the row's Offset would then leave moved trains behind, and a
  count step would put two trains close together.
- **A conflict warning**: the meetings the spacing reads could flag "within 40 s of a Red train at Elm" cheaply; left
  for Exact's conflict marks, as T-3742 left it.
