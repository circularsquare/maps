# T-2122: a few lines in a giant city on a phone

Anita, 2026-11-03: "t-2071 yes this is quite improtant to get. working. we should definitely try to make it so that
jakart a is at least somewhat playable on a typical smartphone. we dont have to support big networks, but i think

memory caps i guess"

**Result**: a new Jakarta game on an emulated phone (2 GB tab cap, one solving worker) peaked at 1.82-1.94 GB
before (one to five lines, then loading the save) and 1.10-1.29 GB after; desktop figures to the bit.

Agent t2122, main tree, Vite 6160. Scripts in `notes/T-2122/` (`phone.mjs`, `ab.mjs`, `summ.mjs`,
`series.mjs`, `lines.json` with five lines each in Jakarta, Manila and Tokyo; run from the maps root with
`BASE`, `ZONE`, `TAG`, `DM`, `HC`, `UA`, `SCR` set as each script's head says); JSON and
logs in `artifacts/trainworld/t2122/`.

## What a phone gives a tab

- **iPhone (Safari, and every iOS browser, which is WebKit)**: the tab's WebContent process holds the page and all
  its workers, and iOS kills it at a per-process limit: reported "ActiveHard 2048 MB (fatal)" on an iPhone 17 Pro
  with 12 GB on iOS 26.3-26.4.1 ([Apple forums 823061](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/823061), an Apple
  engineer confirming iPhones' jetsam limits are stricter than iPads'); "ActiveSoft 1536 MB" in an older webkit-help
  log; kills at about 1.4 GB on an iPhone 13 Pro ([WebKit bug 221530](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=221530)).
  So about 1.5-2 GB for the whole tab, whatever the phone's memory. Safari reports no `deviceMemory`, and clamps
  `hardwareConcurrency` (2 on iOS by caniuse's note, 4 by others): the pool's thread rule gives 1 solving worker.
- **Android Chrome**: Chrome is 64-bit only on Android 10+ with 8 GB or more (Chrome 89's rule,
  [Android Police 2021](https://www.androidpolice.com/2021/03/15/these-android-devices-are-eligible-for-64-bit-chrome/));
  below that the renderer is 32-bit, a 4 GB address space shared by the page and every worker, with a 2 GB
  ArrayBuffer cap ([WebAssembly spec issue 1116](https://github.com/WebAssembly/spec/issues/1116)) and fragmentation
  making large WASM memories fail well before that. Past that, Android's low-memory killer takes the renderer under
  pressure; no fixed per-tab figure is published.
- **A typical phone**: the world's average phone had 8.4 GB in December 2025 ([Counterpoint](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/Global-Smartphone-Average-DRAM-Hits-Record-8.4GB-in-2025)),
  mid-range Androids 6-8 GB (reporting `deviceMemory` 4 or 8: a power of two, rounded down), 8 cores.

**The cap used here**: a phone's tab of 2 GB in all (iPhone's hard limit; about what a 32-bit Chrome renderer can
hold with room left for the map and GPU), emulated as a 412 x 915 touch viewport, `deviceMemory` 4,
`hardwareConcurrency` 8 (so one solving worker, as such a phone gets), with the renderer's private bytes measured
against 2 GB. Chrome has no switch that caps WASM memory, and `--max-old-space-size` only caps each JS heap, which
is not where the memory goes (below).

## Where a phone's tab went (before)

`phone.mjs`: a new sandbox game (`?new=1&mode=sandbox&opened=all&paused=1`), jump to the zone, then lines
1, 3 and 5 of `lines.json` built (10/10/5 trains, each line at its own level), the final and 15 s after each;
then the game exported and loaded in a fresh page ("load"). Every 1.5 s: the Chrome processes' private
bytes, each worker's WASM (`twDemand.client.memory()`), each target's JS heap and ArrayBuffer backing
stores (CDP `Runtime.getHeapUsage`). Peaks are the renderer's, which holds the page and all its workers.

Jakarta on the emulated phone, before (`jak-phone4-before.json`): the renderer peaked at **1.82 GB** with one
line, 1.67 and 1.66 GB with three and five, **1.94 GB** loading the five-line save (the focus pool's two more
workers). It loads and every line solves, but at the iPhone's 2 GB that is a crash or near it. The line count
hardly matters; what did:
- **The world's corridor table, about 0.5 GB**: each solving worker took four whole-world arrays from its
  composition to make the main thread's table (`corridor_pairs`, `corridor_trips`, `commute_base`,
  `corridor_modes`: 12.7M place pairs, about 0.1 GB each, of which 3.9-4.1M have trips) and kept the table
  (0.12 GB), the modes (0.1 GB) and a row index (0.05 GB): 0.28 GB of ArrayBuffers held, about 0.4 GB more
  while making it. The page held the table and the modes again (0.22 GB), the modes only to split one place's
  bubbles by plane and car. Making the four arrays also took the worker's WASM from 0.2 to 0.44 GB (each
  is built in WASM memory first, and WASM memory never shrinks).
- The rest: about 0.4 GB the renderer holds before any demand (map, V8 and Blink), 0.2 GB the page's JS heap,
  0.1 GB the other workers' heaps, the aux worker's 65 MB WASM.

## What changed

1. **The corridor table from its rows alone** (sim `corridor_rows`, `corridor_rows_pairs`, `_trips`,
   `_commutes`, `_modes`; `demand.worker.ts` `tableColumns`): the composition gives the table's columns
   for the 4.1M corridors with trips or commutes, not 12.7M-long arrays to be filtered in JS. The worker
   keeps only the rows' corridor numbers (16 MB, binary-searched to place a period's rail) and transfers
   the columns at the first send, making them again on `corridorsAgain`. The page no longer receives or
   keeps the 0.1 GB modes array: the zone in view's split by plane and car (`ownModes`) comes from the
   table's rows (a pair with no row has no trips to split, so every bubble figure is the same).
2. **The corridor trips kept sparse** (sim `Corridors.trips_at`, `trips`: the corridors with any and their
   trips, sized once for every pair of places with zones): it was a dense f64 per corridor of the world,
   0.1 GB in every solving worker's WASM and growing with the square of the places. The row functions'
   arrays are sized exactly too: once the rows passed 2^22 (4.19M, India's places arriving during this
   task) every array grown by doubling took twice its size in WASM, and WASM memory never shrinks (a run
   at 4.28M rows had the worker at 315 MB). `corridor_trips` still gives the dense array (tests, checks).
3. **Small devices** (`workers/deviceMemory.ts`): a phone or tablet by its user agent, or a device reporting
   4 GB or less, gets one solving worker (before: only 4 GB or less; an 8 GB Android phone got 2) and no
   focus pool; a phone reporting no memory (iOS) counts as 4 GB for the levels-of-detail and pool budgets
   (before: 8 and 6 GB). The focus pool on a phone was a second solving worker and aux for the zone the
   main pool was already solving.

No demand rule changed; nothing was made coarser.

**To the bit** (`ab.mjs`, desktop, one worker, Jakarta's first line; the table's key and a hash of each column,
the final view and every zone's city view hashed, six bubble selections' longer trips hashed): the old code
and change 1 the same in every part (same data, 08:53's registry; `ab-old.json`, `ab-new.json`); change 2
against change 1 the same in every part (09:11's registry; `ab-mid.json`, `ab-sparse.json`). Rust: a new test
(`corridor_rows_are_the_corridors_with_trips`, the rows' columns equal to the bit to the whole-world arrays at
those corridors) and all 48 intercity tests pass. Smoke on 6160 after everything: passed, 23 checks, the real save
11,297,375 rail trips a day (the machine at 100% CPU, so 414 s). Typecheck passes.

**What happens on such a phone.** Before: a new Jakarta game loaded and every line solved, but the tab sat at
1.7-1.9 GB and loading the save took it to 1.94 GB: past the iPhone's 1.5 GB soft limit and at its 2 GB hard
one, so on an iPhone very likely a reload ("A problem repeatedly occurred") by the first line or the load; on
a 32-bit Android Chrome close to what its address space holds with the map. After: 1.1-1.3 GB at every step,
0.7 GB under the 2 GB cap. The peak is the first line (the corridor table being made and sent) and a load;
lines past the first cost nearly nothing (the zone's packs are the same, the network is tiny).

What is left in the 1.1-1.3 GB: about 0.4 GB the renderer holds before demand starts (map, V8, Blink), the
page's JS heap 0.13-0.2 GB, its corridor table 0.14 GB (T-2136), the solving worker's WASM 0.19 GB and the
aux worker's 65 MB, the other workers' heaps about 0.1 GB.
