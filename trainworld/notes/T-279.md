# T-279: would routed drives between cities change much? (measured 2026-10-24, no build)

The market's car (docs/regional.md 5.1, `intercity.rs` `drive_min`): 15 minutes plus 1.25x the
great circle between place centres at 85 km/h. Against OSRM's public demo server (car profile,
free-flow times; 50 requests, 2 s apart, a plain User-Agent), centre to centre of 50 pairs of
market places. Script `notes/T-1147/osrm_pairs.py`, summary `notes/T-1147/sum_osrm.py`, answers
cached in `artifacts/trainworld/t1147/osrm_cache.json`, table in `osrm_pairs.json` beside it.

"Rail odds" is how rail's odds against the car would change if the market priced the car on the
routed drive (its minutes and its road km at $0.15 shared by two) instead of the rule, at the
market's scale 0.10 x sqrt(60 / car): under 1 rail loses trips, over 1 it gains. OSRM's
free-flow minutes are optimistic, typically by 10-20% against driving in traffic, so the true
gap on flat pairs is smaller than the "model / OSRM" column says.

Notable pairs:
- Flat motorway corridors drive at 85-100 km/h centre to centre and their roads are 1.09-1.19x
  the line, so the rule is 20-38% slow: Warsaw - Poznan 263 against 190 minutes, Munich - Berlin
  455 against 349, Dallas - Houston 343 against 273, Berlin - Hamburg 242 against 189.
- Mountains split two ways. Expressway countries (Chengdu - Chongqing, Xi'an - Chengdu, Kunming -
  Guiyang, Tokyo - Nagoya, Madrid - Bilbao) are like flat pairs or right. Old passes and coasts are
  where the rule is too fast: Taipei - Hualien 113 against 222 minutes (road 2.35x the line),
  Athens - Thessaloniki 282 against 344 (1.65x), Zurich - Milan 197 against 217 (1.36x), Lyon -
  Turin and Sofia - Thessaloniki about 5% fast. Munich - Innsbruck came back at 187 minutes for
  149 km, likely a closure or an odd snap; not to be trusted.
- Water: Copenhagen - Aarhus (1.68x, round the Great Belt) and Copenhagen - Gothenburg (1.54x)
  are 13-17% too fast; Osaka - Fukuoka 13% fast; Hiroshima - Matsuyama 78 against 159 minutes
  (Shikoku is joined to Honshu as one landmass, but the drive goes round by the Shimanami bridges
  or a ferry). New York - Boston, Seattle - Portland and Hong Kong - Guangzhou are right.
- Not comparable: London - Paris and Tokyo - Sapporo (the market adds its own Channel and Tsugaru
  crossings; OSRM's route took a ferry), and Helsinki - Tallinn, London - Dublin, Athens -
  Heraklion and Barcelona - Palma, where the market has no car at all (separate landmasses).

## What rail shares would move

The market's scale makes 30-60 minutes matter a lot at 200-500 km (0.04 a perceived minute at
four hours by car). Routed drives would cut rail's odds against the car to a tenth on the flat
motorway corridors (Berlin - Hamburg, Paris - Lille, Shanghai - Nanjing, Madrid - Valencia),
before any allowance for traffic, and raise them several times on mountain and inlet pairs
(Taipei - Hualien, Athens - Thessaloniki, the Danish pairs, Shikoku). Rail against the plane does
not move: the plane's access drives use the same rule at both ends. The rule's 1.25 is about the
median detour; its 85 km/h is the weak part on motorways.

## Is it worth building

Yes, if the market's rail shares on single corridors matter: the error is not noise around the
rule but sorted by geography, the size of a whole mode's share on some corridors, and one speed
cannot fix both kinds (a faster rule makes the mountain and water pairs worse). A routed drive
time between place centres per pair (OSRM-style durations on the packs' or a global road graph,
with a traffic factor) would replace both the 1.25 and the 85. Not built here (brief: measure
only).
