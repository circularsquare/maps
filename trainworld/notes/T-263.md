# T-263, T-264, T-262: places that pull from farther, holidays with their own effects, and telling the player

A design for discussion with Anita (2026-10-24). No code yet. The "For Anita" part is for her; the
rest is for the agent who builds it. Scripts cited are in `notes/T-263/`; their outputs go to
`../artifacts/trainworld/`.

## For Anita

Each item is a choice, with my pick first.

1. **Which far places, and in what order.** Pick: **sights first** (museums, zoos, aquariums, theme
   parks, big temples, shrines and cathedrals, castles and palaces, observation towers), then
   **stadiums and arenas as events**. Later or never: big malls, beaches, ski areas, convention
   centres (malls overlap with T-1092's trips into town; beaches already pull as parks; ski areas
   lie mostly outside the packs).
2. **How sights count.** Pick: **their own trip purpose, "Sightseeing", built like airport trips**:
   each place has a number of visits a day, drawn half from homes and half from hotels and leisure
   jobs (where visitors staying in town come from) across the whole city, the chance halving about
   every 10 km. The other option is a share of social trips aimed at sights (like T-1092), which is
   cheaper to build but leaves remote homes sending trips to a sight 60 km away and cannot rise on
   holidays apart from social trips.
3. **How big a sight is.** Pick: **Wikidata's yearly visitor figure where it has one, otherwise
   10,000 visitors a year for each Wikipedia language edition that writes about the place**, and
   only places with at least 10 editions or 100,000 visitors. Visitor figures are common in the US
   and Western Europe and rare elsewhere (China 26 places, Japan 32, India 27, Nigeria none), while
   language editions exist everywhere but under-count Asia's big draws by up to 20 times (Shanghai
   Disneyland 22 editions, so 220,000 a year, against 5.3M). Together they give the big draws their
   real size where it is known. Pure editions everywhere would be consistent but small. This is a
   fitted constant (10,000 a year per edition, from 2,102 places that have both), so it needs your
   yes.
4. **How big the pull is in all.** At that size New York gets about 25,000 to 60,000 sightseeing
   visits a day (0.1% of its trips), Kyoto about 26,000, London 45,000 or more. These are small
   numbers, but long trips to single spots, so a station at the Met or at Disneyland gets a few
   thousand riders it would not have. My view is that this is right; if you want sights to matter
   more, there is one multiplier to turn up.
5. **Stadium events.** Pick: **venues from Wikidata with a capacity of 10,000 or more**; home games
   a year from the sport of the venue's teams where Wikidata names them (baseball 81, basketball and
   ice hockey 41, football (soccer) about 25, American football 9, rugby 12, cricket 7), otherwise
   12 events a year for a stadium and 40 for an arena; dates generated per venue, never random from
   day to day; the crowd 80% of capacity, arriving over two hours before the start and leaving within
   45 minutes of the end, from across the region. Alerts only where you have built (a built station
   in the venue's city).
6. **Holidays: four kinds, decided by the calendar the game already uses.** Pick:
   - **Day off** (most holidays): runs as a Sunday. This is today's rule.
   - **Long holiday**, three or more days off in a row with a holiday among them (Golden Week,
     Easter, May Day week in China, long weekends): the day before, more long trips out of the big
     cities; the last day, more back; the days between run as Sundays with more sightseeing.
   - **Going home** (Spring Festival and Tet, Seollal, Chuseok, Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha, Diwali,
     Thanksgiving, Christmas, Obon and Japan's New Year): the same, stronger, and the cities quieter
     on the days between because people are away.
   - **Working holiday** (holidays most people work through, such as Columbus Day and Veterans Day in
     the US): runs as a weekday.
   The new numbers, all to propose to you: long trips twice a normal day on the going and coming
   days (1.5 times for a 3 or 4 day run), up to 70% of a pair's trips in the outward direction,
   local trips at 0.7 of a Sunday on going-home days, sightseeing 1.5 times a Sunday on long
   holidays. Rough anchors (from memory, to check): China's rail carries about 12M a day over the
   40 days of Spring Festival travel against about 10M normally, peaking near 2 times; US air on the
   Sunday after Thanksgiving is about 1.25 times a normal day.
7. **Saturdays apart from Sundays, and each country's own weekend (T-262).** Pick: **yes, with the
   first holiday stage**. The holiday package already knows each country's weekend (Friday and
   Saturday in 18 countries such as Saudi Arabia, Egypt and Israel; Sunday only in 7; Friday only in
   Iran), so this comes almost free. The same stage switches the calendar to include the substitute
   days off, which is what makes China's Spring Festival and National Day weeks a week long (today
   they show as 4 and 3 days).
8. **Telling the player.** Pick, smallest first:
   - the holiday's hover says what the day does ("Runs as a Sunday", "More long trips out of
     cities on Wed 24, back on Sun 28");
   - a calendar icon by the date with the next three weeks' holidays and games in places you have
     built, each with that hover;
   - holiday days marked under the Money charts, so a dip or a spike has its reason;
   - stadium games as small marks in the sky at their start time, with the expected crowd in the
     hover, and afterwards how many came by train.
   Maybe later: a "next holiday" choice in "Demand shown for", to see a rush's loads before it comes.
9. **Order.** Pick: (1) holiday calendar fixes with Saturday and Sunday apart (small), (2) sights
   (medium), (3) stadium events (large), (4) long holidays and going home (medium to large). Sights
   come before long holidays because the holidays lean on them. If you would rather have stadiums
   first, they can go before sights: they share only the places file.

## 1. Far-pulling places

### What exists now

- Everyday outings (docs/demand-sources.md 4.1) go to jobs, shops, leisure jobs, parks and people
  at a 1.0 km scale, with T-1092's 20% to dense centres at 6 km. Parks (T-196) include beach resorts
  and named beaches already. Nothing pulls from across a whole city to one spot except airports
  (20 km, sized by passengers).
- The airport purpose is the pattern to copy: sites with a budget of outings a day, the other end
  half residents, a quarter jobs, a quarter leisure jobs, drawn with a 20 km decay and normalised
  per site, two destination samples per home cell.

### How a sight is sized and drawn

- `visits a year V` = P1174 (the latest figure) if given, else 10,000 x sitelinks. Kept if V is at
  least 100,000 (10 sitelinks). Probably no cap needed: the builder lists the largest figures and
  drops any that count passers-by rather than visitors (a square or a whole district that slipped
  through the class list).
- Visits a day = V / 365 on an average day, set on weekdays so the weekend's factor brings it back:
  weekday visits = V / 365 / (5 + 2 x f_weekend) x 7, with f_weekend social's 1.60. Each visit is two
  legs (there and back), like an airport outing.
- The other end: half residents, half leisure jobs (the existing `leisure` destination arrays,
  food and recreation jobs or OSM leisure places: where hotels and restaurants are), each
  normalised, drawn with exp(-d / 14.4 km) (the chance halves every 10 km), none within 1 km of the
  sight, normalised per sight so each keeps its visits, before the 40-80 km taper. Two destination
  samples per home cell, as for airports.
- Visits come on top of social outings, not out of them: at 0.1-0.5% of a city's trips the double
  count is below anything a player can see, and taking them out would mean a per-pack correction.
- Weekend and holiday factors and timing: social's (T-260), so no new numbers until stage 4.
- Plausibility: the Met at 2.0M (its Wikidata figure) is 5,500 visits a weekday, 11,000 legs; if a
  third go by train (a guess for Manhattan), some 3,500 riders a day at its station. Tokyo Disneyland at 4.2M
  is 11,500 visits, 23,000 legs (Maihama station's real count is several times that: Wikidata's
  figure is the old single-park one and the resort draws from all Japan, which the market carries).

### Stadium events

- Venues: Wikidata stadiums and arenas (subclasses included) with P1083 >= 10,000 and coordinates.
  Tenants: P466 on the venue or P115 (home venue) on teams; a team's sport from P641.
- Dates: a small table by sport, home games a year, the usual start times and days (football
  Saturday and Sunday afternoons; baseball, basketball and hockey weekday evenings and weekend
  afternoons; American football Sunday afternoons), a season of about 9 months with the break by
  sport. Dates come from a hash of the venue id and the game year, so a game is on the same day
  every time the save opens. Two tenants (MetLife: Giants and Jets) add their dates. No tenant:
  12 events a year for a stadium, 40 for an arena, evenings, spread over the year. One table of six
  sports is the only new table; it is general (leagues play about the same number of home games
  everywhere).
- Crowd: 0.8 x capacity (one number; real leagues range from half full to sold out).
- Where from: residents of the venue's pack and its neighbours by exp(-d / 20 km) (the airport scale:
  fans come from across the region), before the 40-80 km taper. Away fans are left out.
- Mode and route: one destination column of mode choice per venue (the `flows` query's shape,
  10-20 ms, T-170), solved when a game is within the next two game days and its city has a built
  station, kept until the network changes. Event riders route against that day's ordinary
  crowding and are added on top of that day's loads, fares and boardings.
- Timing: its own minute profile, not the five periods: arrivals spread over the two hours before
  the start, departures within 45 minutes of the end (game length by sport: 3 h baseball, 2.5 h
  basketball and hockey, 3.5 h American football, 2 h football). It feeds the boarding account
  directly; this is the one part that needs new plumbing in the track model (riders by minute for a
  station pair), and the builder should look at the boarding account first.
- Plausibility: Yankee Stadium, 46,537 seats, 37,000 fans, half by train, 18,500 riders each way in
  the hour after the game: real 161 St on a game night is in that range.

### What the player sees

- Sights: a small mark on the map from about zoom 11 (one icon for every kind; the kind and name in
  the hover). Hover: the name, "About 5,500 visits a weekday", the share by train today. A click
  selects it like a bubble: where its visitors come from.
- Venues: a different mark. Hover: the name, the capacity, the next game ("Yankees home game, Sat
  Apr 12, 13:05, about 37,000 expected"), and after a game "Last game: 52% came by train".
- Alerts, only where a built station is in the venue's city (its pack, or within 5 km of it): a mark
  in the sky at the start time, the same hover. The sky already shows about the next day and a
  half, which is the right warning for a game.

### What it costs

- **Packs: no rebuild.** One world file, `app/src/game/places.json` or by country, from a new
  `pipeline/sights_json.py` (a few QLever or WDQS queries, minutes): id, name (English label, the
  local one as a fallback), lng, lat, kind, V or capacity, tenants' sports. About 15,000-30,000 rows,
  2-3 MB raw; split by country or by place so the app fetches only what its open zones need, as
  holidays.json is fetched after startup. The production build's list of copied files
  (`vite.config.ts`, T-377) must include it.
- **Runtime**: at a pack's opening the app maps its sights to cells (the pack's grid, as station
  nodes are), and gives the sim a site array per purpose. That is a new WASM function
  (`set_sights` or a general `set_sites(purpose, cells, weights)`), so the builder works in its own
  tree (CLAUDE.md, T-792) and builds the Rust side first.
- **Solve**: two samples per home cell for one more purpose, like airports. Expect a few percent on
  the everyday part; measure on the real New York save (final 10.4 s, T-260's figure; check today's).
- **Views**: a new purpose touches every per-purpose list (kind totals, loads by purpose, the day
  factors, inspectors, the purpose colours). That is most of stage 2's work. Shown as its own purpose
  "Sightseeing" with its own colour.
- **Events**: one column per venue with a game soon, so a handful at a time, 10-20 ms each, in an
  idle worker. The boarding account's minute profile is the unknown.

## 2. Holidays with their own effects

### What exists now

`pipeline/holidays_json.py` writes each country's public holidays from the `holidays` package
(v0.83, 250 countries, 994 names) with `observed=False`. A holiday runs as a weekend day in its
country (T-872, `kindOfIn`); the sky shows a pink ribbon and the hover names it (T-862, T-1098). The
weekend is Saturday and Sunday everywhere, one kind of day (T-260).

### Stage 1: the calendar itself (T-262)

- **Each country's weekend** from the package's `weekend` attribute, into holidays.json: Saturday and
  Sunday in 223 countries; Friday and Saturday in 18 (AF BD BH DJ DZ EG IL IQ JO KW MR MV OM QA SA
  SD SY YE); Sunday only in 7 (AI CD HK KN NE PY TK); Friday and Sunday in Brunei; Friday only in
  Iran. (The package's AE says Saturday and Sunday, which is right since 2022.)
- **Substitute days on** (`observed=True`): China's Spring Festival goes from 4 days to 6 and with
  the weekend to 8; National Day from 3 to 5, so 7 with the weekend; Labor Day from 2 to 4; Korea's
  alternative holidays come in. Also Japan's `bank` category (Dec 31, Jan 2, Jan 3), which is Japan's
  New Year break. The builder prints each country's added days and drops the ones that are not days
  off (US "observed" Friday for a Saturday holiday is one, which is right).
  China's make-up working Saturdays and Sundays are not in the package; leave them out.
- **Saturday apart from Sunday**: a third kind of day. NHTS has both (research/weekend/nhts_results.json,
  T-170: work 0.33 and 0.22 of a weekday, shopping 1.42 and 1.01, social 1.57 and 1.59). Where the
  weekend is Friday and Saturday, Friday is the Sunday-like day (the day of rest) and Saturday the
  Saturday-like one; a one-day weekend is a Sunday. A day off holiday runs as a Sunday.
- **Working holidays**: a short list of names that run as weekdays: Columbus Day, Veterans Day,
  Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Washington's Birthday, Juneteenth (US federal days most private
  employers work). The list is by name, so it applies wherever those names occur; the builder looks
  through the 994 names once for others like them.
- Cost: the boarding account keeps one account per kind of day (two now, 77-86 ms after a result on
  the real save; three would be about 120 ms, still in steps of at most 6 ms). Weekend views are built
  from the weekday result by factors (7 ms a publish each). The Settings choice "Demand shown for"
  gets Saturdays and Sundays in place of Weekends. No solve added.

### Stage 4: long holidays and going home (T-264)

**How a holiday's kind is decided** (`notes/T-263/hol_kinds.py` tries it on 2027's calendars):

1. Days off are the country's weekend days and its holidays, less working holidays.
2. A single working day between two days off with a holiday next to it is a bridge (Thanksgiving
   Friday, Japan's days inside Golden Week). It counts as part of the run, and as a going-out day for
   trips.
3. A run of three or more days off with a holiday in it is a **long holiday**.
4. A run holding a going-home holiday is a **going-home holiday** whatever its length. The names
   (regex, `HOMECOMING` in the script): Lunar New Year, Spring Festival, Chinese New Year, Korean New
   Year, Tet, Chuseok, Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha (and Turkey's bayram names), Diwali or Deepavali,
   Thanksgiving (not Japan's Labor Thanksgiving Day), Christmas Day, Nowruz, Songkran, Hari Raya.
   Plus a small dated table for what the package lacks: Obon in Japan (Aug 13-16).
5. Everything else is a **day off**.

What it gives on 2027's calendars (observed days off, so China's weeks are short in this run):
China 4 long holidays (Spring Festival going home), Japan 10 (every Monday holiday weekend; Golden
Week a 7 day run), Korea 6 (Seollal and a 9 day Chuseok going home), the US 5 (Thanksgiving a 4 day
going-home run with its Friday bridged; Christmas), the UK 5, Germany 5, France 7, India 7 (Diwali
and both Eids going home), Indonesia 10, Saudi Arabia 4 (both Eids, 5 days each), Turkey 7 (a 9 day
Ramadan Feast run), Brazil 7, Vietnam 5 (Tet 5 days), Nigeria 6.

**What each kind does**, as factors on the existing purposes and the market, no new model:

| Day | Local trips | Sightseeing | Longer trips (the market) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day off | Sunday | Sunday | Sunday (1.58 of a weekday, as the weekend now) |
| Working holiday | weekday | weekday | weekday |
| Long holiday, going-out day (the day before the run; with bridges, those too) | as the day would be | as the day would be | x 1.5 for 3-4 days off, x 2 for 5 or more; outward weighted |
| Long holiday, days between | Sunday | 1.5 x Sunday | Sunday |
| Long holiday, coming-back day (the run's last day) | as the day would be (Sunday) | as the day would be | as going out, the other way |
| Going home, going-out days (two before a run of 5 or more, else one) | as the day would be | as the day would be | x 2, outward weighted |
| Going home, days between | 0.7 x Sunday | Sunday | Sunday |
| Going home, coming-back days | as the day would be | as the day would be | x 2, back weighted |

**Direction**: on a going-out day, a place pair's trips from i to j are weighted
`1 + 0.4 x (P_i - P_j) / (P_i + P_j)` and j to i by the opposite, P a place's people (the market's
places, so a big city against a small town is close to 70/30, two big cities 50/50); the pair's total
is unchanged by the weight. A coming-back day takes the minus sign. It applies to the market's
longer trips and its commutes between zones are left alone (they are a weekday's anyway).

Why these and not more: the local side reuses the Sunday of stage 1; the market side is one factor
and one direction rule. Local trips at 0.7 on going-home days stand for people away from home; their
trips where they visit are not modelled (the home towns would be busier, which a player would only
see in small places). The anchors are rough: China's Spring Festival travel season averages about 1.2
times a normal day's rail over 40 days, with peaks near 2; JR's Obon and New Year peaks are 1.3-1.6
times normal on the Shinkansen, the outward and return peaks on separate days; US air on the Sunday
after Thanksgiving is about 1.25 times normal (air only; road travel rises more). From memory: the
builder checks two or three of these against published figures before settling.

**Cost and the one hard part.** The local factors and sightseeing are kinds of day like the weekend:
worked out from the weekday solve, no new solve. The market's direction is not: its rail trips are
kept per directed zone pair and period, but its loads on the lines are summed, so a direction weight
needs the market's loading done again with the weights, on the routes and shares the weekday solve
chose (no new mode choice; the weekend keeps the weekday's crowded choices too, T-260). One such pass
for "out" and one for "back", in an idle worker, asked when a long holiday is within the next week
and kept like the weekend's bubbles. Measure it first; if it is slow, compute it only for the
country's long holidays the player has built in. The market trips scale up without their mode choice
seeing the extra crowding: trains on the rush days fill and leave riders, which is the gameplay
(T-170: the challenge is capacity for the rush).

The holidays data grows by the observed days, the weekend per country and a kind per holiday run,
all computed in `holidays_json.py` (the kind is a per-country list of runs a game year, so the client
does no regex work). Still about 250 kB.

## 3. Telling the player

Rules that apply (CLAUDE.md "Panels show what can change"; Anita's minimal UI text): no explanatory
text in panels; facts go in hovers; icons with hovers over word labels.

- **The holiday hover says what the day does** (stage 1, small). The sky's hover and the date's dot
  today give "Tomorrow: Thanksgiving Day, United States". Add one fact line per holiday:
  "Runs as a Sunday", "Runs as a weekday", for a long holiday "More long trips out of cities on Wed
  24, back on Sun 28", for the days between of going home "Cities quieter". Wording to settle with the
  player-text rules.
- **The sky marks going-out and coming-back days** (stage 4): the pink ribbon over the holiday, and a
  paler pink over the going and coming days with a small arrow (out or back) at their start. The sky
  already reaches about 30 hours ahead (`holidaysNear`), so a player sees the rush a day before.
- **A calendar icon by the date** (stage 4, small to medium): hover or click for the next 21 days in
  places the player has built: date, name, country and a kind icon (day off, long holiday, going home,
