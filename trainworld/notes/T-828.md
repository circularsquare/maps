# T-828: does crowding near the city ends drive long-distance riders off a London - Paris line?

Anita, 2026-10-21: "i think eurostar is maybe hard to get working cuz it has a segment near the city
thats very crowded and this dissuades long distance people". The worry: locals fill the trains on a
short stretch near a terminal, through riders boarding there are left behind with them, the search
prices that wait into the whole trip, and they drop the line.

**Answer: no.** Through riders London - Paris do not move with crowding at all (17,471 to 17,490 a
day whatever the player does to capacity). Demand charges them almost nothing for it. What the player
sees as crowding on a new line is mostly the boarding account's opening day, which runs about a third
of the morning's trains short. What holds London - Paris rail down is how much of each city can reach
a station on the line. No demand rule change is proposed.

## Method

New sandbox game in London on my own Vite (6173, `demandWorkers=2`), headless Chrome. High-speed track
(T-817) at level -1 through the tunnel, 400 m platforms, high-speed trains, trains by headway: 4/3/1
an hour (high/medium/low) unless said. Weekday after the final crowding round. Through riders are the
market's London - Paris corridor by rail (both ways, `twDemand.intercity()`); locals are the network's
rail trips less the market's. Demand's left-behind wait per stop is a JS copy of
`kernel::left_behind` run on the final boardings, alightings and loads (the kernel does not export
`rn_left`); crowding factors from the same loads (`1 + 2x(1+x)/2` from 70% of crush). The account is
`linePeriods` (riders left behind, rider-minutes waited after full trains per stop). Scripts: scratchpad
`t828/run.mjs` (build, scenarios, capture) and `t828/analyse.mjs`; not kept.

## 1. The line as briefed: St Pancras - Ashford - Lille - Paris Nord

91 minutes end to end. 30,177 riders a day, all but a few from the market: London - Paris 13,460,
Paris - Lille 10,879, London - Lille 2,811, London - Kent zone 1,924.

| | London - Paris rail | of the corridor | air | line riders |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4/3/1 an hour, 10 cars | 13,460 | 45.0% of 29,889 | 16,104 | 30,177 |
| (a) 8/6/2, 10 cars | 13,466 | 45.1% | 16,098 | 30,803 |
| (b) 4/3/1, 20 cars | 13,460 | 45.0% | 16,104 | 30,177 |

Busiest hour on any stretch: 8-17% of crush. Nobody is left behind, in demand or in the account.
There is no crowded stretch, and the through riders still stop at 45%.

The only stop where demand's estimate leaves anyone behind is St Pancras towards Paddington in the
evening peak (1.5 minutes, 1,102 riders), a local stretch. With (a) or (b) every factor on a London -
Paris ride is 1 and nothing is left behind. So the search adds 0-6 perceived minutes to a trip of about
2 hours plus access, and on this pair even that changes nothing (next section).

## 3. Why through riders do not respond

- **Inside the pairs that rail reaches, rail already wins outright.** The rail fare is $1.50 + 450 km x
  $0.10 = $46.50 (140 perceived minutes); the plane's is $117 (351) plus 90 minutes at airports. At the
  market's damped scale for this pair (0.10 x sqrt(60/380) = 0.04 a minute) a 6 minute penalty is 0.24
  against a lead of about 8. The share in those pairs is close to 100% either way.
- **The 41-55% that do not go by rail are zone pairs no station reaches** (docs/regional.md 5.1: pairs
  are solved only between 6 km zones some station serves, by a walk or a ride to a station within
  15 km). They stay with the plane whatever the trains do. (Inferred from the rule and from the share
  moving under 0.1% with frequency, train length and crowding; not counted zone pair by zone pair.) Two terminals alone reach 45% of the
  corridor; adding Paddington, Stratford, Chatelet and Gare de Lyon reaches 58.5%. Reach into the two
  cities is the player's lever: more city stations, and local lines feeding the terminals.
- **The left-behind rule is not unfair to long riders.** It gives every boarder at a stop the same
  average extra wait, which is how a platform queue works, and the market weighs a minute at 0.04
  against a local trip's 0.10, so a long rider minds the same wait less than a local does.
- Locals do fill trains on the city stretches, but in the model the extra service the player adds goes
  to them, and through riders never needed it.

## 4. What the player sees: the account's opening day

On the day a line opens the account runs the morning short. Same network, account by period:

| | opening day | one day later (and two) |
|---|---:|---:|
| morning trains (both ways) | 21, all full | 31, 12 full |
| morning left behind | 27,820 | 2,733 |
| morning rider-minutes after full trains | 1.31M (Stratford 635k, Paris Nord 533k) | 40k |
| midday left behind | 5,668 | 0 |
| evening peak left behind | 9,438 | 9,438 |
| night trains | 4 | 19 |

The riders are the same both days (78,165 in the morning); only the trains differ. Demand assumes the
full timetable, so on the opening day the account shows waits that demand does not price (Stratford
and Paris Nord: the morning's first trains from the far end). This is what made T-818's line read red
"at Gare du Nord and Stratford", and probably what Anita saw. The steady day's real crowding is the
evening peak at Chatelet and St Pancras, on local stretches.

Cause not found: it lives in the account's day of departures (`boarding::simulate`,
`Service::run_deps` and its eras at the line's opening), which is Rust; not changed here. Raising the
night service to the peak's (4/3/4) left the opening-day morning exactly as it was, so it is not the
period edge or the night pattern. T-680's rule (nobody waits for a new line's first train) did not
take riders off either: the morning's riders are the same on both days.

## Other things found

- An express line added after the track and the all-stops line were constructed got no trains: its
  `setHeadway` took, but the line stayed at 0 trains an hour, and `constructLine` and `constructAll`
  answered `ok: false` with no issues. Added before `constructAll`, the same line ran and carried
  13,590. Seen through the edit API only, not checked in the UI.

## Options

1. **Nothing in demand** (recommended). The worry does not hold: demand charges through riders 0-6
   minutes on this line, and it moves none of them. Getting Eurostar to work is a game-play matter:
   reach into London and Paris (city stations, feeders), not capacity.
2. Fix the account's opening day, so a new line does not read crowded on its first day while demand
   says otherwise. Rust, in the account's departures; a task.
3. Look at the express-after-construction case.
4. If London - Paris at 58% of the corridor (published rail about 70-80% of rail and air) should be
   higher with a sensible network, the lever is the market's reach rule (the 15 km ride to a station),
   which is a demand rule and would go to Anita first. Not proposed here: a player with a local network
   at both ends gets more of the corridor already.
