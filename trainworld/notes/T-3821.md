# T-3821: games store growth on the server (agent gamestore, 2026-11-20 UTC)

Anita, 11-20: "is a little concerning. do we think theres potential for additional like compression / efficiency
there? or is there a better cheap way to store?"

Short answer: yes. More than half the store is backup copies, and they are about 90% the same bytes as the game's
current save. Storing an idle game's backups as differences from its current save frees about 1.25 GB today (a quarter
of the store) and roughly halves daily growth. Nothing changes for players. Plain codec swaps (zstd, xz, brotli) save
much less. Storage elsewhere is cheap if it's ever needed, but it isn't needed yet.

Everything here was measured read-only: one-off probes over ssh, everything held in memory, nothing written on the
box, only counts and sizes printed. No player save left the box. Probe scripts are in
`C:\Users\anita\AppData\Local\Temp\claude\dce1fefcc1d43aef\a6a6b4a3-3cb7-4e31-ac63-f1cd25a92ff1\scratchpad\gamestore\`.

## 2. Smaller saves

**Today's encoding:** the game gzips the whole file with pako at level 6 (`workers/gzipCore.ts`). Inside is "TWG1",
a JSON header, and the track network in a packed binary form (varints, millimetre deltas; `sim/src/track/save.rs`).

**What's inside a player's save** (600 random current saves, 47 MB stored): unpacked, the header is 88% of the bytes
and the network 12%. Compressed, they're about half each, because the JSON compresses well and the network doesn't.
- In the header, `regions` (each region's money history by day and by hour) is 68% of the unpacked save. The
  network-wide day ledger is 13%, `cities` 5%, everything else small. Per-region day entries outnumber hour entries
  about 7 to 1; the median save has 2 regions, p90 13, max 107.
- In the network, the core (nodes, edges, stations, lines) is 78%, bus routes (`LBS2`) 10%, build days (`LBD1`)
  3.5%, ferries 2.6%. The saved settle rates (`LRT1`, `LPC1`, the only derivable parts) are 2.3%, and they're there
  so a game loads without settling again. Nothing big in the network is derivable.
- The local real-network saves (`data/packs/real/*.save`, the NYC save) have 152-byte headers. They aren't like
  players' saves, so these figures come from the box sample.

**Folding the history (game-side).** T-3757 (built 11-19) folds days older than 120 into months once a ledger spans
90 weeks. Measured on about 300 saves at the game's own gzip level. The second column is a different sample of 300,
because the store changed between runs:

| Change | Sample 1 | Sample 2 |
|---|---|---|
| T-3757's month fold, as built | 0.83 | 0.88 |
| Fold days older than 120 into weeks before 90 weeks (the chart shows weeks there anyway) | 0.79 | 0.83 |
| T-3757, plus the history written as one list per figure instead of one object per day | | 0.80 |
| The same, with money rounded to US$1 | | 0.80 |
| The same, with money rounded to US$1,000 | 0.72 | |

T-3757 does most of the work. Writing the history as lists takes about 9% more off every save, and rounding to $1
adds nothing. Rounding to $1,000 adds a few percent more, but small hourly figures would then show as 0. The week
fold adds only 3-5% and needs care at month ends, so it isn't worth doing. All of these shrink a game only when a
new build saves it again; idle games keep their old bytes.

**Codecs** (same 600 saves, whole file, vs what's stored; box CPU speed on the unpacked bytes):

| Codec | Size | Speed |
|---|---|---|
| gzip -9 | 0.99 | 14 MB/s |
| zstd -3 | 1.01 | 320 MB/s |
| zstd -9 | 0.92 | 67 MB/s |
| zstd -19 (and -22) | 0.82 | 2 MB/s |
| zstd -19 with a dictionary trained on other saves (112 KB) | 0.72 | |
| xz -9 | 0.76 | 4 MB/s |
| brotli -11 (local real saves only; no brotli on the box) | about 0.87 | |

zstd -19 and xz are far too slow to run per save on a 2-core box taking 65 GB a day, so they would have to run in
the browser. That means a WASM codec shipped with the game and a new format for the server's save check (T-3395) to
read. The trained dictionary helps small saves the most (saves under 60 KB unpacked: 0.30 of gzip), but those are
few of the bytes. After the history change, zstd -19 still takes about 14% off.

## 3. Cheaper backups

**Backups as differences from the current save.** For 1,040 backups of the sampled games, compressed with zstd
using the current save as the reference: recent ones come to 6.4% of their stored size, day ones 9.8%, week ones
9.2% (level 19, 0.17 s each). At level 9 they're 16% (0.005 s each); at level 3, 33%. All 329 level-9 round trips
in the second probe came back byte for byte. That's a real game changing between real autosaves, which is better
evidence than edits I'd make locally, so I didn't build local revs with play.mjs.

The catch: the current save changes every autosave, and a difference is useless without the exact save it was made
against. A design that works:
- An hourly sweep (like the deleted-games sweep) packs the backups of games not saved for a day. An idle game's
  current save stays put, so its backups are stored as differences against it at level 19.
- The next write to that game unpacks them first. Restores and the admin restore read them through the same unpack
  and get the usual gzip back.
- Only a current save that passed the full check is used as the reference, and the pinned copy is never packed.
- It needs zstd in Go: `github.com/klauspost/compress` (pure Go, widely used, has raw-content dictionaries). Go's
  built-in flate only looks back 32 KB, which is far too short. Its strongest setting is roughly zstd level 11, so
  expect differences of about 15% (between my level 9 and level 19 figures). Getting the 10% of level 19 would mean
  running the box's `zstd` program from the sweep.
- Things the build has to handle: the store deletes files the index doesn't name (packed files must be named), the
  T-3395 check and scan read gzip, and each backup's byte count feeds the account and store caps.

Figures: games idle 1+ day hold 1,498 MB of backups. At about 16% that frees about 1.26 GB now (1.35 GB at level
19). Since nearly every game goes idle eventually, the backups part of growth (55%) shrinks by about 84%: about
0.3 GB a day less.

**Fewer copies, simpler code** (no new dependency):
- Drop the week tier: 296 MB now, about 0.05 GB a day.
- Drop all backups of games idle 7+ days: 0.2 GB now. A week later it removes nearly all of the backups part of
  growth (about 0.3 GB a day), like packing, but a player who comes back after a week has no old copies to restore.
  Packing keeps them for about a sixth of the space. This is a call about what a returning player keeps, so it's
  yours.

## 4. Storing it elsewhere (prices checked 11-20)

- **This box, cap raised:** free. The disk is 38 GB with 26 GB free. About 4.4 GB is used by other things now, and
  the other stores' caps (community, quarantine, feedback, ride saves) add up to about 4 GB more, so about 24 GB is
  a safe games cap.
- **Hetzner Volume:** about €0.052-0.057 per GB-month, 10 GB minimum (Hetzner's page lists no price; €0.0572 is a
  third-party figure after a 2026 rise, €0.052 is older). 16 GB about €0.9 a month, 50 GB €2.9, 100 GB €5.7.
  Hetzner's daily server backups don't include volumes, I believe, so the off-site copy would be the only other copy.
- **Bigger box** (Hetzner's page, US prices in EUR, https://www.hetzner.com/cloud/regular-performance/): CPX11
  €17.99, CPX21 €32.49 (3 vCPU, 4 GB, 80 GB), CPX31 €62.99 (160 GB). CPX21 is +€14.50 a month and covers up to about
  70 GB of games. The page shows the US plans as "currently unavailable", which may block a resize.
- **R2** (https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/): $0.015 per GB-month. The free 10 GB is per account and
  anitamaps already uses it. 16 GB $0.24 a month, 50 GB $0.75, 100 GB $1.50. Writes (Class A) are $4.50 a million
  and the account already makes about 0.8 million a month. Moving about 5,000 newly idle games a day with 3-4 files
  each is about 0.5 million a month, roughly $2. Reads (Class B, $0.36 a million) would only happen on restores and
  when a cold game is opened, so they cost nothing that matters. Infrequent Access ($0.01 per GB, but $0.01 per GB
  to read, a 30-day minimum and $9 a million writes) isn't worth it at this size. Cost: a Go S3 client, a cold-tier
  rule, a fetch when a cold game is opened (slower, and it depends on R2 being up), about 5-6 agent hours.
- **The off-site copy costs the most.** The nightly backup (T-2918) puts a full tarball of `/opt/shonei` in R2 and
  keeps 7 (`BACKUP_KEEP=7` on the box). Today that's 22 GB, the newest copy 4.8 GB. That's about 112 GB ($1.70 a
  month) at a 16 GB store, 350 GB ($5.25) at 50 GB and 700 GB ($10.50) at 100 GB. That's more than any of the
  storage options above, and anything that shrinks the store shrinks this 7 times over.

## 5. When the disk becomes the wall

At 0.55-0.75 GB a day: the 8 GB alert in about 4-5 days (about Nov 24), 80% of the 16 GB cap (12.8 GB) in about
11-14 days (Dec 1-4), and the cap itself in 15-20 days (Dec 5-10). At the cap the disk would be about 20.5 of 38 GB,
so **the cap fills first; the disk only matters if the cap is raised.** With no cap, the disk would fill in 35-47
days (Dec 25 to Jan 6). Growth rose from 0.50 to 0.64-0.68 GB a day between 11-12 and 11-20 while sign-ups fell from
about 800 to 460 a day, because older games keep filling their backup slots. So treat these dates as a range.

| 4 | Raise the games cap to ~24 GB | room, not bytes | | 0 | minutes + a deploy | Nothing | Then the disk is the limit, around Dec 25 at today's growth, much later with 1 |
| 5 | Drop the week backup tier | 0.3 GB | ~0.05 GB | 0 | ~1 h | One fewer old copy in the restore list | Small |
| 6 | Move cold games to R2 | as much as is moved | | ~$2-3 a month | 5-6 h | Opening a long-idle game is a little slower | Opening one depends on R2 |

1 and 3 overlap: they go after the same bytes. 1 keeps the copies at about a sixth of the space; 3 throws them
away. 2a and 2b also shrink backups, so they shrink what 1 saves a little.

Together, 1, 2a and 2b bring growth from about 0.64 to about 0.3 GB a day, and the store from 5.05 to about 3.8 GB
once the sweep has run. That puts the 16 GB cap around New Year without raising it, and a 24 GB cap around the end of
January. Pictures (490 MB, 9.5%) weren't measured further; smaller or WebP thumbnails could come later.

**Recommendation:** do 1 (the biggest gain, server-only, nothing for players to notice), and publish T-3757 as
planned. Add 2b as a small follow-up when the save format is next touched. Keep 4 for when the 8 GB alert fires
(about Nov 24) if 1 isn't deployed by then. Pick 3 over 1 only if you're fine with idle games losing their old
copies. Leave 6 until the store passes about 20 GB; at today's prices R2 ($0.015 per GB-month) or a Hetzner volume
(about €0.055) is cheap enough not to worry about.
