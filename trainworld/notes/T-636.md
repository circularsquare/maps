# T-636: two "maximum ridership" tests for a simpler, more game-like demand model (2026-10-17)

Round 1 (the first sections) was measurements only. Round 2 (the last section) measured Anita's
chosen set and made it the game's defaults. Anita wants building a rail line to have real impact. Her targets, as rail's share of ALL trips (walk included):
**A** a straight train between every pair of points at 140 km/h, free, a 3-minute walk at each end:
at least 80%. **B** 100 km/h, a 3-minute walk at each end, fare $1.50 + $0.05/km: at least 40%.
**C** is the real network as built (New York: the real save's network; London: T-551's).

## Reading

- **B is met by every setting**, including today's: 45% in New York, 43% in London; the simple set
  gives 60% and 55%.
- **A is met by none.** The best of the planned settings is simple with beta 0.10: New York 77.5%,
  London 73.1%. The variants that steepen only short trips (3a, 3b) reach 71.0 / 72.5% in New York
  and 66.6 / 68.3% in London, a little under the same beta applied everywhere. Plain beta 0.075
  gives 72.7 and 68.0. Today's constants give 51 and 48.
- **What holds A down is short trips.** The 0-2 km band (17% of New York's trips, 24% of London's)
  gets rail 10% today and 21% on the simple set (38% at beta 0.10), because the walking constant
  `asc_walk` 2.80 is worth 56 perceived minutes at beta 0.05 (37 at 0.075, 28 at 0.10) in favour of
  walking, and the train costs 11 minutes before it moves. From 5 km up the simple set already
  gives 76-96% at A. Everyday trips lag commutes: New York at A, simple, commutes 79.8%, everyday
  60.3%. An extra run, not asked for, with the walking constant 1.5 instead of 2.80 (still one
  constant for both layers): beta 0.10 reaches 79.2% in New York and 75.6% in London; at beta 0.05
  68.4 and 64.6. So A's 80% needs both a steeper scale and a much weaker walking constant, or
  the shortest trips counted differently.
- **The real networks hardly respond.** Rail on C goes from 13.6% to 17.0% in New York and from 18.7%
  to 23.4% in London on the simple set, then stays 17.0-17.4% and 22.7-23.4% at every steepness:
  what limits it there is coverage (the pairs that have no station within a walk), which the scale
  does not touch. The walkers that a steeper scale takes off their feet go to the car on C (New
  York walk 16.1% -> 9.3%, car 56.6% -> 62.8% at beta 0.10), since no train is there for them.
- **Station counts move the wrong way for the cities.** The model already carries too many
  riders at the stations against the counts: New York x2.32 today, x2.95 on the simple set, x3.0-3.1
  with the steeper scales; London x1.70, x2.21, then x2.0-2.1 (steeper scale slightly better there,
  because London's commute walking falls from 19% toward the census 9%). The simple set
  cuts the commutes' rail bonus (`asc_rail` 0.4 -> 0) but raises everyday rail (its -1.1 goes), and
  the ownership cost adds rail on top, so New York's everyday rail goes from 9.7% to 14.9% of
  trips. New York's commute rail by home area stays near its census (23.5% against 20.3%).
  The simple set is not separated here into its parts (ownership against the constants).
- The two damped variants (3a, 3b) leave the real cities as the plain steeper scales do (stations
  x3.03 / x3.08 in New York, x2.09 / x2.10 in London), and keep long trips exactly as in the simple
  set at 20 km and longer (New York A 91.0 against 90.7 at 20-50 km, 95.0 against 95.0 at 50+).

## Method

The model's own Rust code in a private copy of the crate (`notes/t636_copy.py` (script removed 2026-10-24, in git history) copies `sim/` and
adds the test `notes/t636_ceiling.rs`; the shared crate is untouched). The method is T-551's
(a train between every pair of cells) with these changes for the new model:

- Walking is a mode in the choice (T-558), so all four shares come from one logit: drivers (parking
  free, or paying for a space) choose among rail, walking the whole way and the car; the people
  without a car among rail, walking and other. The expressions are the model's lanes (`car_drive`,
  `park_parts`, `mode_beta`, `nocar_choice`'s) and the walk's cost is the model's (`walk_cost_at`
  for commutes, `walk_trip_cost` for the everyday samples, both with the walk factor round water).
  Commutes are by zone pair (2 km zones, the gravity's zone-centre distance, 0.52 x 2 km inside one
  zone), everyday trips by the layer's own cell samples, both directions.
- The made-up train: straight-line km at its speed, a 3-minute walk at each end weighed
  `w_walk` 1.5 (4.5 perceived each), a **5-minute headway, 2.5 minutes of wait in plain minutes**
  (T-585 made waits plain half headways; T-551's 3.4 perceived is no longer how the model weighs
  a wait, so I used Anita's 2.5), fare at 3 perceived minutes a dollar on the straight-line km.
  Both tests use the same wait.
- C is `calib`'s run of `nyc_real.json` / `lon_real.json` (the network of the real save; starting
  fares $1.50 + $0.10/km; three crowding rounds), with rail by band read out of the copy's pair
  loop (the hook of T-551) and the day's walk and other from `summary`. The rail by pair sums to the
  model's own totals (New York commutes 4,375,731 against 4,375,723, everyday 5,085,118 against
  5,085,118). Car is the rest.
- A check that the ceiling machinery agrees with the model: with no rail at all it gives the commutes'
  walking at 6.96% (New York today, the model's own 6.65%), 7.21% (6.98%) on the simple set, and
  London 21.07% (20.45%), 21.89% (21.40%). The 0.3-0.6 point difference is the ceiling taking one
  walk cost per zone pair where the model averages its cell pairs. Walk shares of A and B are
  correct to about that.
- Station level and scores: `pipeline/calib.py run <city> --set ...` on a `calib` binary built from
  the copy (identical code), the "Stations x counts" being the geometric mean of model over
  measured entries of the scored stations (hubs apart). Score is calib's total (lower is better).
- The settings, all through `--set` (nothing was added to `Params`; the logit scale is `beta`, its
  damping `damp_min`):
  - Today: the shipped constants, ownership off.
  - Simple: `car_own_usd_trip=1.5, car_own_usd_km=0.1, asc_rail=0, everyday_asc_rail=0,
    country_asc_rail=0, everyday_asc_walk=0` (so `asc_walk` 2.80 for both layers; `other_asc` 10).
  - beta 0.075 / 0.10: simple plus `beta`.
  - 3a: simple plus `beta=0.075, damp_min=26.67`; 3b: `beta=0.10, damp_min=15`. Both keep
    beta x sqrt(damp_min) at 0.387, so a trip whose car costs 60 perceived minutes or more is
    exactly as today's.
  - Extra: simple plus `asc_walk=1.5` alone, with beta 0.10, and with 3a.
- Shares are of all trips, commutes and everyday together, as the model counts them (New York a weekday: 17.3 million
  commute trips and 52.2 million everyday; London 12.6 and 30.7). "Commutes" and "Everyday" columns
  are rail shares within each layer.

Outputs: `../artifacts/trainworld/T-636/` (`<city>_<setting>.json` from the test, `run_t636_*` and
`score_t636_*` from calib, `*.calib.txt`). Rerun: `notes/t636_copy.py`, build the test and `calib`
in the copy, `notes/t636_run_all.py` and `notes/t636_run_extra.py` (set the paths at their top),
`notes/t636_md.py` for the tables. A whole sweep (12 test runs, 12 calib runs) takes about 10 minutes
on 5 cores.

## Round 2: the new model (Anita, 2026-10-17)

Targets are now rail as a share of rail + car (walking and other left out), **A at least 85%, B at
least 50%**. Anita removed the walking constant (2.80 is worth 56 perceived minutes at beta 0.05) and
chose the set below. The tables show rail of rail + car, with rail of all trips in brackets.

### The new model's values

Ownership $1.50 a trip + $0.10/km; `asc_rail` 0 and no country constant; no commute-against-everyday
rail gap (the `everyday_asc_rail` parameter stays, at 0); one walking constant worth 10 perceived
minutes for commutes and everyday trips alike (`walk_bonus_min` 10; the logit's constant is that
times `beta`, so it follows the scale); the whole-trip walk flat at 1.5 a minute; parking's base 5
minutes (was 3); `beta` 0.10 with `damp_min` 60. Paid parking by density is unchanged. Measured
through `--set` on the earlier binary as `beta=0.1, asc_walk=1.0, walk_trip_w_far=1.5, park_min=5`
plus round 1's simple set; "parking base 3" is the same without `park_min`.

### Reading

- **A is 86.3% in New York and 85.5% in London, B 79.3% and 78.9%: both targets are met** (of all
  trips: 79% and 75% at A, 71% and 67% at B). Plain beta 0.10 with the simple set was 84.5% and
  83.8% at A: the 5-minute parking base and the walking change added about two points. Everyday
  trips lag commutes at A (New York 83.8% against 93.3% of rail + car).
- **Walking is still believable.** With the real network (C) New York walks 55% of trips under
  2 km (NHTS 2017: 58%; the old constants 80%), 10% at 2-5 km (NHTS 4.5%), and under 1% beyond.
  Under the ceilings A and B, where a good train is everywhere, it walks 36% and 42% within 2 km,
  so a train that serves a trip does take its walkers, as intended. By purpose, New York's
  errands walk 17% (NHTS 26%), social trips 16% (35%), medical 10% (12%); commutes 5.1% (ACS 6.2%).
  London's commutes walk 16.6% (census 9.4%), errands 27% (LTDS 42%), social 18% (41%), education
  18% (44%). Everyday walking is below the surveys, by a third in New York's errands and by more
  than half in London's social trips; the game has few short trips of its own (T-558: 17% under
  2 km against NHTS's 43%), which the survey's walking is made of.
- **The real networks respond a little.** Rail on C goes from 13.6% to 17.1% of all trips in New York

- **Against NHTS, rail of rail + car by distance** (New York, C): 18.6, 18.0, 19.5, 27.2, 18.0 and

- **The 5-minute parking base** barely moves the ceilings (A 84.2 to 86.3%) and puts 3 points on C's
  walking within 2 km (52.4 to 55.3%, because the car is dearer).
- **Every pack on the test cross** (80 packs, T-509 stage 7's cross; before the constants shipped
