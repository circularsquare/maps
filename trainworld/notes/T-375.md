# T-375: the world field (T-362 stage 1)

2026-10-15. Design: notes/T-362.md "How it is computed and stored".

## What it is

`pipeline/world_field_build.py` (one command, about 6-8 minutes from a clean `data/work`; downloads
cached in `data/raw/world_field/`, 32 MB) writes, in `data/work/`:

- `world_field.npz`, 4.2 MB: 393,791 populated H3 r5 cells (Kontur's r6 summed to r5), each with
  h3 id, people-weighted centre, population, country index, landmass id and island id.
- `world_field.countries.json`: 252 rows, one per ISO code: iso2, iso3, name, Natural Earth
  subregion, GDP per head at PPP and its source, the World Bank's population.
- `world_field.links.json`: the fixed-link joins (Wikidata items, the islands each joins) and each
  landmass's population and main country.
- `world_field.r6.npz`, 65 MB: a cache of the Kontur r6 points so a rebuild skips unpacking.

`pipeline/world_field.py` reads it: `load().around(lon, lat)` gives the field as one site sees it,
r5 within 300 km, r4 to 1,000 km, r3 beyond, every person once (about 13,000-14,000 cells, 1.7 ms;
12,600 of them r3); `locate(lon, lat)` gives a place's country and landmass. r4 (71,283 cells) and
r3 (12,761) are derived at load (0.7 s): population sums, people-weighted centre and GDP, the most
populous country and landmass.

## Choices against the design

- **Levels kept as designed.** 300 km stays the r5 radius although the near-pair cutoff is now 250
  km: the radius is about the normaliser's accuracy, not which pairs are near, and 300 covers 250.
  The field is 4.2 MB, not 6. A site's normaliser costs about 14,000 terms instead of 394,000.
- **Countries from Natural Earth admin 0** (10m, public domain) rather than geoBoundaries: one
  file, ISO codes on every feature (`ISO_A3_EH`, else `ADM0_A3`), de facto borders (Northern
  Cyprus, Somaliland and Kosovo are their own rows). Each r6 point takes the polygon it falls in or
  the nearest within 0.5 degrees; 128,000 people (0.002%) fall in none.
- **GDP per head**: World Bank NY.GDP.PCAP.PP.KD (constant 2021 international $), the latest year
  per country, 2025 for most. Where it has none (Taiwan, Venezuela, North Korea, Cuba, Yemen,
  Eritrea, South Sudan and small places), the Maddison Project 2023 figure (2011 $) times 1.39,
  the median ratio of the two over 160 countries in 2022. What neither has takes the median of its
  Natural Earth subregion. IMF's datamapper API refused scripted requests (403).
- **Landmasses**: Natural Earth 10m land plus minor islands, one landmass per polygon (polygons
  meeting across the antimeridian joined). The 1:10M coast closes straits under about 1 km
  (Kanmon, the Bosporus, the Little Belt), which is harmless here as all are bridged. 3,253
  populated landmasses after joins; 0 is Afro-Eurasia with Great Britain, 1 the Americas, 3 Japan's
  four main islands.

## Fixed links

No precompiled global dataset of crossings exists (two web searches: national bridge registers,
GRIP and gROADS road lines without rail or tunnels, KeepRight and OSRM's connectivity checks with
nothing published). A global OSM road and rail extract is too big. So links come from **Wikidata**
(CC0), through QLever's public endpoint (`qlever.dev/api/wikidata`; the Wikidata Query Service was
rate-limiting to one request a minute). The query: every item that is a bridge, tunnel,
bridge-tunnel or causeway (subclasses included) with a coordinate, its length normalised to metres
where given; not a proposed structure, not in any state but "in use", no dissolved, closure or
retirement date. 75,949 items.

Geometry decides what each joins: a link counts only if some point 2 km from its coordinate is
sea (inland bridges and mountain tunnels cross no strait; 6,513 remain); it touches every landmass
within half its length plus 3 km of its coordinate (Natural Earth's coast sits up to ~2 km off);
links whose discs overlap chain, so a crossing named in parts joins as a whole (Øresund's bridge
and Drogden tunnel, the Great Seto bridges, Shimanami's chain). 347 chains join two or more
islands.

Checked joined (`checks/world_field_check.py`): Seikan, Kanmon, all three Honshu-Shikoku routes
(Akashi-Onaruto, Great Seto, Shimanami), the Channel Tunnel, Øresund, the Great Belt,
Hong Kong-Zhuhai-Macau, Hong Kong's harbour crossings, the Confederation Bridge, King Fahd
Causeway, Johor-Singapore. Checked apart: Ireland, Korea-Japan, Sicily (Messina), Taiwan,
Mallorca, Sakhalin (from Hokkaido and from the mainland), Sumatra (from Malaysia and Java), Sri
Lanka, Tasmania, Hawaii. The two Sakhalin and Malacca failures of a first run were proposed links
(the "Sakhalin Tunnel", the "Malacca Strait Bridge", the Qatar-Bahrain causeway), now excluded by
the query. The 90 largest joins were read by eye and were all real.

Known limits: a link with no length on Wikidata reaches only 3 km, so a long crossing missing its
length would be missed (none among the checks); a short-lived Wikidata error could join two
islands, so the check's list is the guard. Ferries are not links (T-362).

## Check (`checks/world_field_check.py`)

Kontur 8.032 bn people against the World Bank's 8.190 bn summed over countries. Of people in
countries of 1M and over, 79.5% are in countries within 5% of the World Bank and 96.0% within 10%.
Off by over 10% among large countries: DR Congo 0.90, Yemen 0.83, Somalia 0.72 (Somaliland is its
own row), Poland 1.12, Côte d'Ivoire 0.88, Chad 0.88, the UAE 0.82 (Kontur undercounts migrant
workers' housing). Plausible for the bar. All link checks pass, and `around()` counts the world
total exactly at New York, Tokyo and Fiji.
