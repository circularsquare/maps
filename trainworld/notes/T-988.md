# T-988 Games on the account (cloud saves)

Design, 2026-10-25; Anita approved it that day with all three defaults ("yeah, that sounds good").
Stage 1 (the server) is built in shonei-server, not yet deployed: see "Stage 1 as built" at the
end. Stage 2 (the page) is built too: "Stage 2 as built". Builds on T-972 (notes/T-972.md, the account and leaderboards, live on shonei-server) and the
save rules in docs/architecture.md 11.

## For Anita

**What you get.** While signed in, every game in the browser is also kept on your account and
follows you to any device. Your saves stop depending on one browser's storage (the 2026-10-24
loss). The browser stays the working copy; the account gets a copy at a save by hand at once, after
autosaves at most every 2 minutes, and when you leave the page. A new device lists your account's
games on the start menu with their pictures; Open downloads one.

**Two devices on one game** work like two tabs do today (T-1251): the device that opened the game
last saves it, the other stops autosaving with "Autosave is off here because this game was opened on
another device." and its Save takes the game back. The copy it replaced is kept on the account as a
backup. Only a real fork (changes on both devices that never reached the account, say offline)
keeps both, the second as "Tokyo at 9:41 PM", the way a restored backup is named.

**Signing in with games in the browser** asks "Add the 3 games in this browser to your account?"
Add uploads them; Don't add asks "Are you sure? Games kept only in this browser are lost if its
storage is cleared." and then leaves them in this browser only.

**Where they live**: on the shonei-server box (market.anita.garden) beside the leaderboard figures,
one folder per account. Not R2: saves are small (New York's real network is 75 KB), the box already
does this for noritetsu, and R2 would need a new secret on the box.

**Questions** (my default in brackets; the build can start on the defaults):
1. **Backups of the box.** It has none, and its README says to revisit that before account saves
   ship. Hetzner's nightly backups are about 20% of the server's price, roughly a dollar a month.
   [Turn them on; one click in Hetzner's console.]
2. **Save by hand on the device that lost the game** writes over the other device's newer copy
   (that copy kept as an account backup), as a tab does today. The alternative keeps both every time.
   [Write over, as tabs do.]
3. **Which games sync**: every game of a signed-in player [yes], with the merge question deciding
   only for games made while signed out. Picking game by game is left out.

**What needs you**: a shonei-server deploy (`.\deploy\deploy.ps1`, a minute) after stage 1, the box
backups (question 1), and a read of the changed privacy page, which today says games are "never
sent anywhere". No other cost.

## 1. What syncs

- **Every slot of a signed-in player** that this browser writes, normal and sandbox alike (a
  sandbox's score is still never sent). Not synced: `?new=1`, `?synth=1` and `?demandTest=1` pages
  (they own no slot), games declined at sign-in (section 3), and slots tied to another account.
- **What is sent per game**: the save bytes exactly as Save to a file writes them (TWG1, gzipped),
  the slot's name, and its card facts (day, mode, cities, lines, stations, cash, the clock time) with
  its picture (the slot summary's PNG, tens of KB), so another device can draw the card without
  opening the game. Not sent: the demand cache (rebuilt on load, megabytes), local backups, the
  T-1214 folder handle.
- **Sizes** (gzipped save bytes, measured 2026-10-25): the real save `T-007-real-nyc.save` 74,638 B;
  the all-China save `t1156-all-china.save` 22,410 B (123 lines, 2,363 stations, straight lines that
  compress well); all of Japan's real network (`jpn_real.save`, 709 lines, 10,171 stations) 568,307
  B, the biggest known. A typical game is 50-100 KB.
- **Backups**: the browser's two backups per slot (T-744) stay local. The account keeps its own two
  per game: a PUT at least 30 minutes after the game's newest account backup also becomes a backup
  (over the older one), and a copy written over by a save by hand from another device (section 2)
  always becomes one. Listing and restoring them is stage 3.
- **When**:
  - a **save by hand** (Save, Ctrl+S): upload at once;
  - an **autosave**: upload if the last upload of this game was at least 2 wall minutes ago (the
    score's gap); the New York save costs about 2 MB an hour of play, Japan's about 17 MB;
  - **page hidden**: upload at once if anything is unsent. This is best effort (a closing page can
    cut the request), so
  - **on load**, before the start menu's games are shown, every slot changed since its last upload
    is uploaded, and every slot whose account copy is newer (and which has not changed here) takes
    the account copy.
  - a **rename** or **delete** goes at once (delete is asked first, section 5).

## 2. Conflicts

Each account game has a **rev** (a counter the server raises on every write) and a **writer**: the
device that last opened or saved it. A device is one browser profile: a random id in localStorage,
nothing about the machine. Each browser slot remembers its game id, the account, the rev it last
uploaded or downloaded, and the slot's save time at that moment, so "changed here since" is a
comparison of two times.

- **Within one browser** nothing changes: the Web Lock (one writer per slot) picks the tab, and only
  the tab holding the slot's lock uploads it.
- **Across devices, the same lease idea**: opening a game claims it on the account (the device becomes
  its writer). Any upload from another device then answers 409 "elsewhere"; that device stops
  autosaving the game, locally too, and says "Autosave is off here because this game was opened on
  another device." in the saves panel and the top bar's Autosave off notice, exactly like a tab that
  lost its lock. Its slot is marked as having given way, so its next load takes the account copy
  without a fork (only the minutes between the other device's open and this notice are dropped,
  as a tab loses them today). Its **Save** claims the game back: it uploads with `force`, the
  account's copy becomes an account backup, and the other device gets the notice in turn.
- **No expiry on the claim**: opening is a deliberate act, so the last opener wins. A card whose game
  another device saved in the last 10 minutes says "Open on another device" (the time on hover), so
  the player knows before opening.
- **A real fork**: this browser's slot changed since its last upload, and the account copy moved on
  from that same rev (offline play, or a page closed before its last upload and the game then played
  elsewhere). Nothing is written over (T-743's rule): the slot takes the account copy, and this
  browser's version goes into a new slot named "Tokyo at 9:41 PM" (its own save time, the
  restored-backup naming) with a new game id, uploaded as a new account game. The player sees two
  cards and keeps or deletes either.
- **Last-writer-wins alone** was rejected: a tab left running on one device (the clock moves, so it
  saves every minute, T-826) would silently write over play on another. **Keeping both every time**
  was rejected for the same case: every device switch with a tab left open would make a copy.
- **Deletes**: deleting a game on one device leaves a tombstone on the account (its id, kept a year,
  at most 1,000). Another device whose slot for it has not changed since its last sync deletes it
  too on its next load; one with changes keeps it as a new account game.

## 3. Signing in with games in the browser

- On sign-in (and on a load where the session is already there and this question was never
  answered), the slots not tied to any account are counted. None: nothing is asked. Otherwise the
  account panel asks "Add the 3 games in this browser to your account?" ("Add Tokyo to your
  account?" for one) with **Add** (lit) and **Don't add**.
- **Add** uploads them as new account games; the start menu and saves panel then list the account's
  games from other devices too.
- **Don't add** asks "Are you sure? Games kept only in this browser are lost if its storage is
  cleared." with **Keep them here only** and **Add them**. Kept here only: those slots are marked
  local, never asked about again, and their cards carry a small crossed-cloud icon (hover "Only in
  this browser") that is also a button: a click adds that game to the account (stage 3).
- **Games made while signed out** later are slots tied to no account, so the next sign-in asks about
  them alone.
- **Games another account synced here** (a shared computer) are not offered and not synced; their
  cards carry the same crossed-cloud icon.
- **Signing out** (stage 3) asks "Remove your games from this browser?" with Keep and Remove. Remove
  clears only slots whose latest version is on the account; a slot with unsent changes uploads first,
  and offline it stays. noritetsu asks the same about rides.

## 4. The server

shonei-server's `trainworld.go` grows a games store beside the scores, with the same habits as
noritetsu's saves: one folder per account, files read per request, nothing in memory but byte
counts, atomic writes, never overwriting what cannot be read.

**Storage: the box's disk.** Layout under `TRAINWORLD_DIR`:

    games/<account id>/index.json        every game's meta: name, rev, writer, savedAt, facts, sizes, backups; tombstones
    games/<account id>/<game>.r<rev>.save the current save bytes, a new file per rev
    games/<account id>/<game>.r<rev>.png  its picture
    games/<account id>/<game>.b0.save, .b1.save   the account backups

A PUT writes the new rev's files, then the index (temp and rename), then removes the old rev's
files, so a crash never leaves the index pointing at nothing. An unreadable index refuses that
account's writes (503), as an unreadable noritetsu save does.

**R2 was considered and set aside.** The game's data already comes from R2 (`anitamaps`, public), so
saves would need a second, private bucket, an R2 key on the box, S3 signing in a stdlib-only Go
server, and signed URLs that do not carry the rev check. It wins only on durability and on saves of
many megabytes; the box backup (question 1) covers the first, and the sizes above rule out the second.
If saves ever outgrow the box, the routes stay and only the store moves.

**Limits** (vars, so tests shrink them):
- 4 MB a save (7 times Japan's real network); 512 KB a picture; 8 KB of meta.
- 50 games an account; 64 MB an account, backups counted; past either, 507 with "Your account is
  full." and the game stays in the browser. Never a silent drop (unlike scores' 20-game cap).
- 4 GB for every account together (the box has about 40 GB), 507 past it; a save that shrinks still
  goes. One alert through `SHONEI_ALERT_WEBHOOK` at 2 GB.
- The account cap stays noritetsu's (2,000, alert at 1,000).

**Routes** (all take noritetsu's token through `nori.authed`, behind `noriCORS`, each in `twRoutes`
so the preflight test walks it; `/noritetsu/config` adds `trainworldGames: true` so the page never
calls them on an older server):

| method | path | what |
|---|---|---|
| GET | `/trainworld/games` | every game's meta (no bytes, no picture), the tombstones, the account's bytes used and limit |
| GET | `/trainworld/games/<id>` | the save bytes, `rev` in a header |
| GET | `/trainworld/games/<id>/picture` | the PNG, cached by rev |
| PUT | `/trainworld/games/<id>` | multipart: `meta` (JSON: name, facts, `baseRev`, `device`, `force`), `save`, `picture` (optional, kept from before when absent). 200 `{rev, savedAt}`; 409 `{code: "moved", rev}` when `baseRev` is not the current rev; 409 `{code: "elsewhere", at}` when another device is the writer and `force` is not set; 413 too big; 507 full |
| POST | `/trainworld/games/<id>/claim` | `{device, baseRev}`: makes this device the writer. 200 `{rev}`; 409 "moved" when this device's copy is behind (it downloads first) |
| DELETE | `/trainworld/games/<id>` | the game, its backups and its score figures go; a tombstone stays. 204 |
| GET | `/trainworld/games/<id>/backups/<0\|1>` | an account backup's bytes (stage 3) |

`force` skips both 409s and is sent only by a save by hand. A game id is the 8-24 character id the
page already makes for scores, so one id names a game's save and its figures.

**Rate limits** per IP, as the leaderboard routes: reads 60/min (list, bytes, pictures: a start menu
with 20 account games stays inside the burst of 60); writes 30/min, 20 back to back (PUT, claim,
delete). Per account, uploaded bytes at most 1 GB a day as a backstop against a page stuck in a loop.

**Deleting the account** already calls `tw.deleteAccount`; it also removes `games/<id>/` and its
bytes from the budget. The account panel's question becomes "Delete your account? Its games, its
leaderboard entries and its noritetsu rides go too. Games in this browser stay." T-993 (noritetsu's
own delete question) should name anitabuilder's games as well as its leaderboard entries.

**Costs**: disk inside the box's 40 GB (a typical account of 5 games with backups is about 1 MB);
traffic of a few MB an hour per player, well inside what the box includes; the box backups about a
dollar a month (question 1). No new secret, no Caddy change.

## 5. The client

**Where it hooks in.** A new `workers/cloud.ts` in the clock worker, which already holds the bytes:
`autosave()` in `clock.worker.ts` calls `void syncOut(key, bytes, summary)` right after `writeSlot`,
beside T-1214's `copyOut`, and `saveByHand` passes `force`. The worker cannot read localStorage, so
`game/account.ts` posts `{kind: "account", base, token, acct, device}` on start and on every session
change; a 401 in the worker posts back and the main thread drops the token as it does now. Each
slot's sync record (`sync:<slot>` in the same IndexedDB store, deleted with the slot) holds the game
id, account, rev, synced save time, given-way and local-only marks. The game id moves there from
`trainworld.score-games.v1`, folded in once, and the slot list the worker publishes carries it for
the score sender.

**On load** (start menu or `?continue=1`) the worker lists the account's games and settles every slot
it can lock: uploads what changed here, takes newer account copies into unchanged slots (writing the
slot record from the server's meta and picture, so nothing is decoded), applies tombstones, makes
fork copies (section 2). The account games not in this browser are published with the slots.

**Start menu and Saved games**: the account's games not in this browser are cards like any other,
with the server's picture and facts and a cloud icon (hover "On your account"); Open downloads it into
a new slot and opens it through T-1196's reload; Delete asks "Delete Tokyo from your account?".
Deleting a synced slot asks "Delete Tokyo from this browser and your account?".

**The indicator**, under the existing "Autosaved 2 min ago" / "Saved 9:41 PM" line, one of:
- "Saved to your account 9:41 PM" (the full date on hover; "Oct 24" form after a day, as the cards'
  times are written);
- "Not on your account yet", the reason on hover: "Offline", "The server could not be reached",
  "Your account is full. Delete a game to make room.";
- "Autosave is off here because this game was opened on another device." with Save (section 2);
- the crossed-cloud icon for a local-only game (section 3);
- nothing while signed out, or where accounts are off.

**Offline**: a failed upload keeps the slot marked unsent and tries again at the next autosave, on
the worker's `online` event, and on the next load. Local autosaves never wait for the network. A game
opened offline cannot claim; it plays with "Not on your account yet" and claims when back online
(a fork if the account moved on meanwhile).

**localhost**: as for scores, a localhost page syncs only with `?acct=live` (or `?acct=local`) given
once, so agents' pages never touch the account. Anita's 8800 is its own browser storage, apart from
anita.garden's; signing in on both (8800 with `?acct=live` once) puts both sets of games on the
account. A game saved by a newer build and opened on an older one loses only what the older build
does not know (zones missing from its registry, architecture.md 11 "Removed zones"), as Load a file
does today.

**Text** follows the user-facing rules: sentence case, no dashes between phrases, no exclamation
marks, the player as subject. Every line is quoted above.

## 6. Privacy and safety

- **Stored**: the save (the player's network, money and clock, the game's free-text name), its card
  facts and picture, the times it was saved, and a random device id per browser. No IP, no user
  agent, no device name. The game name is private, so it needs no name filter.
- **Who can read it**: only the signed-in owner, through the token; no route lists another
  account's games. Anita, as the box's admin, can read the files on the box; the privacy page says
  so. Not public until T-989 (sharing a game by link) makes one game readable by link.
- **In transit** over HTTPS (Caddy); at rest unencrypted on the box, as noritetsu's rides are.
- **Deleting**: a deleted game goes from the box at once (its backups too); deleting the account
  takes every game. Box backups (question 1) keep a deleted file for up to their retention (Hetzner
  keeps 7 days); the privacy page says that too.
- **Privacy page** (`app/public/privacy.html`): "Your games are saved in your own browser and are
  never sent anywhere." changes to say signed-in players' games are kept on the server, with the
  points above. Anita reads it before stage 2 ships.
- **Safety**: every route bounded and rate limited; a malformed save is stored as bytes and never
  parsed by the server (the page parses it on load, as it does a file); the server only checks the
  TWG1 magic and the size.

## Stage 1 as built (2026-10-25)

shonei-server commits `cc9f692` (code and tests) and `9c37d27` (docs), not pushed or deployed.
Code in `trainworld_games.go` (beside `trainworld.go`, which got the wiring, as noritetsu keeps its
password accounts in their own file); tests in `trainworld_games_test.go`; the full API is
`docs/trainworld-api.md` there, which stage 2 should read rather than this section. Every route
was also exercised against a locally run server (dev sign-in, real HTTP, an anita.garden Origin).

Calls the design did not make, decided here:

- **File names.** Every save file is `<game>.r<rev>.save`, current and backups alike, and the index
  says which revs are the backups (no `.b0`/`.b1` files). Keeping a backup is then free: the
  replaced rev's file is simply not removed, with no copy or rename that a crash could split. The
  picture is `<game>.r<rev>.png` named by the rev it came with (`pictureRev` in the meta), so a PUT
  without a picture keeps it under its old name. Backups keep no picture or facts, only rev,
  savedAt, bytes and writer.
- **"elsewhere" is checked before "moved"**, and a claim never raises the rev. Otherwise a device
  that lost the game, autosaving on its old rev after the other device saved, would hear "moved"
  and fork instead of giving way. "moved" comes only to the writer itself (say, a response it
  never received), and from a claim whose baseRev is behind.
- **A deleted game id stays deleted**: PUT and claim on a tombstoned id answer 410
  `{code: "deleted", at}`, even with force. Stage 2 uploads a kept copy under a new id, as the
  design's delete rule already implies. GET of it is 404 with the same code. Deleting a game the
  account never had is 204, removes its score figures and makes no tombstone.
- **The 30-minute backup** is measured from the PUT's time to the newest backup's savedAt, and the
  copy kept is the one being replaced. So a game idle for a week still keeps its old copy at the
  next PUT.
- **At a cap, the routine backup gives way first**: a PUT that only goes over 64 MB (or the 4 GB
  store) because of a due 30-minute backup goes without making it. A copy written over by force is
  never dropped; that PUT gets 507 instead.
- **Rev and save time travel in headers the page can read.** `noriCORS` sends no
  `Access-Control-Expose-Headers`, so a cross-origin page could not have read the rev header; the
  games routes add `Access-Control-Expose-Headers: X-Rev, X-Saved-At, ETag`. The headers are
  `X-Rev` and `X-Saved-At`. Times everywhere are unix seconds, as noritetsu's.
- **The picture** is served with its rev as ETag and `Cache-Control: private, no-cache`, so the
  browser revalidates and gets a 304 while it is the same.
- **The save check**: the page gzips the whole file, magic included (saveFile.ts), so the server
  checks a gzip header and that the first four decompressed bytes are `TWG1`, and nothing more.
  The picture must start with the PNG signature. The meta's name is at most 200 bytes, `facts` any
  JSON object (stored compacted, never read), the device id 8-64 of `A-Z a-z 0-9 _ -`.
- **Writes are flushed to disk** (fsync) before the index rename, since a new file renamed
  without one can come back empty after a power cut. Nothing is held in memory but the store's
  byte total (recomputed from the indexes at start) and each account's uploads today.
- **Separate rate buckets** for games (reads 60/min, writes 30/min burst 20), not shared with the
  score sender's, so settling 20 games on load cannot starve the scores. The 1 GB a day backstop
  answers 429 `{code: "daily"}` and resets on a restart.
- **Games do not depend on players.json**: a broken leaderboard store leaves games working, and
  `/noritetsu/config` reports `trainworldGames` on its own.
- **One global lock for game writes**, as the scores have; reads take it briefly too (Windows
  cannot rename over a file open for reading). Lock order: `gamesMu` before the scores' `ioMu`.

For stage 2: check a save's size before uploading it. Past 4.6 MB the server stops reading and
closes the connection after its 413, and a browser may see that as a network error rather than
the 413.

## Stage 2 as built (2026-10-25)

The page side, in the t988 agent tree; not deployed (it needs stage 1 deployed to do anything on
anita.garden: until `/noritetsu/config` says `trainworldGames` the page makes no games request).
Current rules are in docs/architecture.md 11 "Games on the account" and docs/ui-panels.md (Saved
games, Account and leaderboards, Start menu); this section keeps the calls made on the way.

Code: `workers/cloud.ts` (plain functions of a `CloudContext`, used by both threads), the clock
worker's T-988 block (`cloudAfterWrite` after `writeSlot`, `openOnAccount`, `cloudKick`, give way),
`game/accountGames.ts` (main thread: start-menu settling, the account's list and pictures,
downloads, deletes, the sign-in question), `saveFile.ts` (`SyncRecord` under `sync:<slot>`,
`cloud-deletes`, `listSlots` carries each slot's record, `writeSlot` says whether the slot is new),
`account.ts` (`cloudContext`, device id, the score sender taking the slot's account id),
`SavesPanel.tsx`, `StartMenu.tsx`, `AccountPanel.tsx`, `public/privacy.html`.

Calls the design did not make:

- **Settling runs on both threads.** The start menu has no clock worker yet, so the main thread
  settles there (`settleOnStart`; a card click waits for it, 10 s at most; once a game is picked it
  stops before the next slot, so the worker can lock the picked one). The worker settles again on
  start (that covers `?continue=1` and reloads into a slot) and on `online`.
- **Which slots are tied.** A slot first written while a session exists is tied to it (the record
  is made at that write); any other slot without a record is untied and waits for the sign-in
  question. The account is posted to the worker as soon as a session is in storage, before the
  config answers, so a game started at once is still tied.
- **Upload in the worker reads the slot back** from IndexedDB rather than keeping the bytes, so the
  2-minute timer, the page-hide flush, a rename and a retry all send what is stored. A non-forced
  upload of a slot not changed since its last sync is skipped.
- **"moved" to the writer** (its own write whose answer was lost) is sent again on the account's
  rev; a 410 gives the slot a new id and sends it as a new game.
- **The game in hand, behind the account and unchanged**, is replaced in place (`loadGame` through
  the game's message queue, the check for changes repeated there); changed, the game carries on in
  the fork's slot and the old slot takes the account copy, so the player never loses the game they
  are looking at.
- **A game opened unchanged after another device deleted it** stays in this browser only (crossed
  cloud) rather than being deleted under the player or uploaded again; changed, it is kept as a new
  account game, as the design says for other slots.
- **"elsewhere" always gives way**, even when the page's unsent changes came from playing while
  the server was out of reach after it had claimed the game: those changes are dropped at the next
  load (the slot's local backups keep older copies). A fork needs the page to see the account move
  on before its own claim, as for a game opened offline. Forking on "elsewhere" whenever the rev
  had moved would fork on most ordinary device switches with a tab left open.
- **Leaving a game that gave way with unsaved changes** is refused as for a tab without the lock
  ("Save this game to a new slot before leaving it."); the other device has it.
- **A page opened while the server is down** remembers whether the server keeps games
  (`trainworld.games-on.v1`), so it plays with "Not on your account yet" and catches up at its next
  save once the server answers. Its account icons stay hidden for that page (the config did not
  answer), as before; a reload brings them back.
- **The indicator** stays on "Saved to your account <time>" while a change waits out the 2-minute
  gap (the time is when the account's copy was saved); "Not on your account yet" only after a
  failure or before the first upload.
- **"Open on another device"** shows on the saves panel's other cards, from the list the panel
  fetches when it opens; not on the start menu's (it would need the list before the cards show).
- **Picture fetches** are not cached across pages; with 20 account games that is 20 reads inside
  the 60/min bucket. Fine for now.
- **Device id** from `getRandomValues`, not `randomUUID`, so a plain-http page (a phone on the LAN)
  still starts.

Checked with `checks/t988-cloud.mjs` (builds and runs shonei-server locally, two profiles); smoke
passes in the tree. Screenshots: artifacts/trainworld/t988-question-{light,dark}.png,
t988-saves-{light,dark}.png.

Left for stage 3 or later: the crossed cloud as an "Add to your account" button, the account's
backups listed and restored, the sign-out question, the account panel's games and bytes, retrying
the config on a page opened while the server was down.
