## Stage A: money as fares are paid (built)

Before: the clock worker settled money once a wall second, but only whole game hours: each hour's
fare (the main thread's income for its period and kind of day, `game/money.ts`) and running cost
went into the cash and the day ledger at once, and the top bar showed the cash to $10M
(`money()` gives "$6.13B"). So the cash moved once a game hour, and visibly only every several
hours on a small network.

Now (`workers/clock.worker.ts` `settle`, `shapeHour`, `liveMoney`):

- Money is settled to the game second (`settledT`), four times a wall second while the clock runs
  (`LIVE_MONEY_MS` 250: 15 game seconds at normal speed, 4 game minutes at the fastest).
- An hour's fare is paid by a share curve over its 3,600 seconds (`hourShape`), built from the
  boarding account's slice for that hour as it is sent to the main thread (`sendTrips`): each train
  leaving a platform takes the riders waiting (left by the last train plus arrivals at the
  platform's rate since) less those it leaves, and the hour's fare lands in proportion. So cash
  rises in steps as trains pull out, in lumps on a small network and almost continuously on a
  big one. An hour without a slice (no riders yet, or an hour the renderer never asked for) pays
  evenly.
- Running cost drains evenly over the hour.
- `partFare` / `partCost` are the shares of the hour already paid; each step pays the rise in share
  (never negative) and the hour's end pays the rest, so each hour still pays exactly its fare and
  cost and a day's ledger is what whole-hour settling gave. Per-train credit was cheap enough
  (one pass over the hour's platform events, the same events the platforms are drawn from), so
  the per-second fallback is only for hours without an account.
- A load starts the loaded hour as paid evenly up to the clock.
- The worker sends a small `cash` message (cash and today's ledger) when either moved, and the
  full `money` state when an hour ends (as before, so `income` is not recomputed four times a
  second). `game/money.ts` `liveMoney` holds it; only `TopBar`'s `Cash`, the Money tab's
  `LiveCash` and its Today column's `TodayCell`s read it, so nothing else re-renders.
- The top bar and the Money tab show the cash to the dollar (`moneyExact`, "$8,135,164,375"),
  so the movement is visible; the top bar's hover gives today's fares and running cost.

**The bigger design question (Anita's review, per "Task size").** The panels show forecasts of a
whole day: riders a day, boardings a day, fares a day, the account's trains-today summary. In a live
simulation none of these would change much; what would move is a running count of today so far
(riders carried, fares taken, trains that left full, riders left behind), reset at midnight. Adding
such counters, or replacing some forecast figures with them, changes what the line and station
panels are for, so it was T-293 for Anita to decide. She chose (2026-10-14) counts so far today only, no forecasts; built in T-293 (ARCHIVE).

## Check

Real New York save, day 2 (a Tuesday), whole day at 960x (output in the scratchpad's
`agent-live/day-out.json`):

- Ledger for the day: fares US$2,582.6836621802577M, running US$590.4042714822494M. The day's
  fares and running cost from `income` (what whole-hour settling pays): 2,582.683662180259 and
  590.4042714822494. Difference 1.4e-12 and 0: the same to rounding.
- At normal speed the top bar's cash changed in all 80 samples taken four times a wall second
  (23:50 to 00:11): about +$200k a sample late at night, +$400-1,100k a sample at 08:00, with a
  few small dips at night when running costs outpace fares. One step of +$1.5M at midnight: the
  remainder of the 23:00 hour, paid at its end (not looked into; most likely the check's own jump
  of the clock to 23:50 left that hour's share behind its curve).
