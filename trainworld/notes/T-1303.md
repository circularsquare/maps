# T-1303: real networks on real track

Anita (2026-10-25): "the real networks look quite shabby, cuz they are just straight lines between
stations. how hard would it be to improve these?"

## How a real network was made

Not in the game: `pipeline/realnet.py net` (stations, lines, frequencies) and `geom` (per hop the
line's GTFS shape between its stops, Douglas-Peucker 15 m, else a straight line), then the sim's
`realsave` bin builds the track model's network (each line its own double track, one level per line
from a palette of six so few line pairs cross on one level), `gamesave` wraps it and `ship` copies it
to `data/packs/real/<id>.save`. The game only loads that file (`loadGame(..., "new")`).

Straight hops before (from the geom files): New York 34% of hops (17% of km), Paris 26%, Toronto
71%, London, Tokyo, Seoul and Sendai 100%. Shaped hops were raw 15 m polylines with auto radii.

## Estimate

So the work belongs where the network is built, not in the clock worker: done once per city when
the saves are built, the game loads the result at no extra cost.

| Step (2 cores) | New York | All seven |
|---|---|---|
| Graph load (`realgraph.read`, the city's box) | 1.5-2.5 s | 0.3-2.4 s each |
| Route every hop (Python A*, `realroute.py`), slots and levels (shapely) | 1,655 hops, 12 s | 9,335 hops, 86 s (London 37 s) |
| Trace every routed hop (`trace::trace`, native) | 0.16 s | under 0.3 s each |
| Build + allocate trains (`realsave`) | 2.5 s | London 13 s, rest under 4 s |

Hops on real track: New York 95% (1,567 of 1,655), London 98%, Sendai 99%, Seoul 90%, Tokyo 88%,
Paris 83%, Toronto 28%. The rest have no graph track within 150 m of a station (Paris's trams,
Tokyo's Toden, monorails and AGTs, Toronto's streetcars: the graph holds rail and subway only), a
route over 1.5 x straight + 400 m, or one more than 120 m off the hop's GTFS shape; they keep the
shape or straight line.

Save size (gzipped): New York 76 KB to 116 KB, London 64 to 224 KB, all seven 270 to 700 KB. Memory
in the game: a few thousand more corners per city, nothing to speak of.

Load time in the browser (headless, my Vite, import of the save): New York 11-12 s either way,
London 28 s either way, Tokyo 4 s. A sandbox New York started through the sheet with the new save:
lines after 15 s, demand final after 71 s.

### What made it slow at first, and the fix

Traced at the track tool's 25 m, lines on the same real track wound over and under each other (each
its own track): London went from 700 flat crossings to 1,789, and `allocate_unallocated` (the
capacity settling) from 11 s to 42 s natively, 72 s to load in the browser. Two changes fixed it:
- **Side by side** (`realnet.py spread`): each line gets one of seven sideways offsets (0, ±6, ±12,
  ±18 m), the lines sharing most track first, each taking the slot it shares least with lines
  already placed; to the right of the line's way from first stop to last, flipped for lines heading
  west, so lines run either way along a track keep apart.
- **Fit at 3 m** (not 25), well under the 6 m between slots, so two lines do not cross over and
  back along a shared track. Corners triple, hence the save sizes above.
- Levels are chosen again (`assign_levels`, the same greedy palette) on the new geometry.
London then: 791 flat crossings, 13 s native, 28 s in the browser.

### Shared track (not built)

Lines still each have their own track: a trunk of eight lines is eight double tracks side by side,
which reads well as a bundle on the map (Line view) but is not a railway. Sharing pieces, one double
track for lines on the same real track, is a different build:
1. Keep each hop's route as chain intervals (chain, from, to), not a polyline.
2. Real trunks are often several OSM ways (one per track, or per pair): cluster parallel chains
   within ~15 m into corridors first, or two lines on neighbouring tracks never share.
3. Cut the corridors at every station and every point where the set of lines changes; each piece is
   one edge (double, or quad where its lines' trains exceed double track), traced once.
4. One station node per station and corridor, where every line on it stops or passes; express lines
   pass nodes they do not stop at (needs checking that a line may run through a station node it
   does not stop at); junction nodes where corridors split, with leads along the real track so the
   turnout is tangent.
5. Levels per piece from the graph's bridge and tunnel flags rather than per line, and bridges over
   water where a piece is at ground level (the track tool's `bridge_long`).
Size: a few days of agent work and its own design pass with Anita, since it changes what the real
networks are (capacity on shared track, crowding, which lines are "the same track"). Worth filing
as its own task if she wants it.

## Built (stage 1)

`pipeline/realroute.py` (the router), `realnet.py route` (+ `spread`, `assign_levels`, `REALNET_OUT`
staging), `realsave` reading `<city>_real_route.json` (node positions and headings where the routes
reach the track, a lead each side of every station along the track's heading so the platform is
straight, the real line between the leads traced at 3 m, falling back to the shape or straight line
where the trace fails), `gamesave` writing `<city>_real.save` for every city (New York's no longer
overwrites `T-007-real-nyc.save`, the smoke test's own copy), `ship` reading those.

The seven saves are staged in `artifacts/trainworld/t1303-realnet/` (the China join owned `data/`
on 2026-10-25). New York's `nyc_real_geom.json` there is a fresh `geom` run: the one in
`data/work/realnet/` (109 lines, 2026-10-09) no longer matched `nyc_real.json` (111 lines).

To ship, from `trainworld/` once nothing else is writing `data/`:

    set REALNET_OUT=C:\Users\anita\projects\maps\artifacts\trainworld\t1303-realnet
    ..\..\venv\Scripts\python.exe pipeline\realnet.py ship

To rebuild from scratch for a city: `realnet.py geom <city>` (New York only, or after `net`), `route
<city>`, `realsave --city <city> [--out DIR]`, `gamesave <city>`.

**The shipped New York real network does not open today**: `data/packs/real/nyc.save` holds a TWT2
network, retired by T-1146 (2026-10-24), so a sandbox New York with the box ticked waits for ever.
The other six are TWT4 and open. Shipping the staged saves fixes it.
