# T-3264: one way to stop trains being drawn through each other (proposal)

Agent trainsdraw, 2026-11-12. Anita's TODO: "try to come up with more general framework to fix trains being drawn in
ways that dont make sense. mostly trains passing through each other." Proposal only; no code changed. Measured on
master (1f89d798 plus the tree's uncommitted work) on my Vite 6147.

## How it works now

The sim runs trains as a timetable (keyframe profiles per line, run and level), never as positioned objects. The map
draws each trip from its profile on the GPU; the only knobs the drawing has are:

- **where** it is drawn across the track: its lane (T-658 own track close in, T-3212 split stations), its band (T-1799
  lines side by side zoomed out), a passing station's outer track (T-2233), its place at a line end (T-2980), its return
  run's own row (T-3250);
- **when**: one number per trip, `lag`, game seconds it is drawn behind its timetable, set each frame on the main thread
  by `TrainGuard` (map/trainGuard.ts) and made up at 0.3 s a second.

The guard is four separate rules, each added for one case, each with its own idea of occupancy:

| rule | task | occupancy judged by | who takes part | zooms |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| follow: 30 m behind the train ahead on its track | T-1965 | drawn position this frame | trains in view + 150 px, lines sharing track or with places | all |
| flat crossings, crossing junction moves | T-1966 | time over the point, from timetable + lag | in view + 150 px | 14 up |
| two-way single track in turn | T-3202 | time on the stretch, from timetable + lag | every train of the line | all |
| places at a line's end | T-2980, T-2986 | standing / leaving this frame | in view + 150 px | all |

plus release valves: 20 min lag cap (`LAG_MAX`), 4 min crossing wait (`CONFLICT_WAIT_MAX`), a clock jump of 15 min
resets everyone. Past a valve the train is drawn through whatever is there.

## The cases so far, and what each was

| case | what a player saw | kind | check |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-1965 | express through the local on shared double track | timetable not drawable (express faster with nowhere to pass) | t1965-follow, t1965-jz, t2018-trail |
| T-1966 | trains through each other at flat crossings | two timetables, one point | t1966-cross, t1966-pics, t2185-merge |
| T-1967 / T-3202 | opposite directions through each other on single track | own line's two runs, no rule kept them apart | t3202-single-meet |
| T-3212 | trains meeting at a single-track station stood half off the one drawn track | geometry: drawn lane differed from where trains stand | t3212-station-split |
| T-2980, T-2986 | two trains drawn on one platform at a line's end | place occupancy | t2980-terminus, t2986-real-ends, t3053-terminal-stop |
| T-3231 | two lines on one one-place dead end, drawn on top of each other | follow rule and place rule each think the other holds the train | t1965-follow (fails), t2980 |
| T-3250 | return trains on the outbound one-way track | geometry: run 1 drawn along run 0's row | t3250-return-trains |
| T-3202 side bug | a late train drawn twice at its end | lateness carried to the wrong next trip | t3202-single-meet |
| T-2233, T-2248 | (made allowed) express passing a local at a passing station | geometry exception to the follow rule | t2233-passing-map |

Four kinds, then:

1. **Geometry**: two trains the sim allows side by side drawn in one place (T-3212, T-3250), or the guard's idea of a
   track not matching the drawn one (`outerAt` still skips T-3250 return runs).
2. **Rules not seeing each other**: follow vs places (T-3231), single-track wait vs places (a deadlock patched in
   T-3202 by "a train standing for single track holds no place").
3. **Per-frame and per-view judgement**: decisions from where trains were drawn last frame (T-3231 hole 1 at 960x: a
   frame is 17 game s), only trains near the view take part (one coming in can jump, a crossing found taken), waits at
   crossings only from zoom 14, so zooming in moves trains.
4. **Timetables nothing can draw**: the sim's averages (following loss, Kingman holds, one-place ends at rho 1.00) give
   timetables where trains must overtake with nowhere to pass or queue past what a platform turns. The drawing can
   only add lag until a valve lets the train through.

### A general probe (new, scratch)

The checks each count one kind, by the guard's own idea of a track. To count everything a player would call "through
each other", `artifacts/trainworld/t3264/overlap-probe.mjs` (run from the maps root: `node <it> <url> <save> "lng,lat,zoom,speed
index,game min;..." <tag>`; to become a standing check in stage 0) places every train
in view as drawn (`tripAt` with the renderer's lane, band and passing shifts, its lag and places; each car by
`trainCarAt`) and projects its cars to the screen: two cars of different trains whose centre lines come within half a
drawn train width are through each other. Each pair is sorted by angle (same way, opposite ways, crossing), by whether
the two are on one track piece (edge) or on different ones, and whether either stands. An event is one pair of trips,
however many frames. Morning peak, from 7:30.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| real New York, Midtown z14.5, 60x | 20 | 1,074 of 1,102 | 634 | 0 | 207 | 423 | 494 | 0.55 / 1.1 |
| real New York, Midtown z16.5, 60x | 10 | 475 of 554 | 90 | 0 | 31 | 45 | 100 | 0.45 / 1.1 |
| real New York, Midtown z17.5, 60x | 10 | 351 of 552 | 53 | 0 | 1 | 5 | 96 | 0.42 / 0.9 |

| shared London, City z14.5, 60x | 20 | 220 of 1,069 | 51 | 0 | 15 | 12 | 61 | 0.31 / 0.9 |

(Counts by kind add up to more than events: one event can change kind as the trains move. London at z12.5, 960x
placed no train in view, as the t1965 run on that save did; not chased.)

What this says:

- **On real networks almost every overlap is geometry, not timing.** No pair on one track in one direction at all.
  - *One track, opposite ways*: double track is drawn as one stroke until its tracks part (about zoom 16.7-17,
    `laneState`), and a train moves to its own track only then, so below that two trains passing on double track are
    drawn on the centre line through each other. Gone at 17.5 (1 left), as the rule says.
  - *Other track, alongside*: trains on different tracks drawn closer than a train is wide: quad track, two lines'
    tracks side by side, platforms of a big station. A train is 8-9 px wide from zoom 14 while 4 m between tracks is
    1.5-3 px until zoom 16.
  - *Crossing*: lines crossing on different levels (the real save has no flat crossing): one train drawn over the
    other at a bridge or tunnel. Not wrong, but nothing says which is on top.
- **Timing overlaps live on player-made layouts**: shared track with expresses (t1965), single track, one-place ends
  (t2980), which the real saves barely have. Those are the cases tasks have been patching.
- **The guard costs more than it did**: T-2986 guards every line with a one-place end, which is every line of the real
  save, so it now places ~57 trains a frame at z14.5 and ~210 at z12.5 (0 before), 0.4-1.2 ms a frame on average, p95 up
  to 4 ms (loaded machine; T-1965 measured 0.01-0.07 ms on this save).

`artifacts/trainworld/t3264/nyc-z14.5-s0-ev0.png` and siblings.

## Options

Both halves of the problem need an answer: where trains are drawn across the track (geometry), and when (timing). The
options below are for timing except D.

### A. One per-frame occupancy table (rework the guard)

Keep the guard on the main thread, each frame, but replace its four rules with one: every drawn track piece (an edge
in one direction on its drawn row, a single-track stretch, a conflict point, a place at a line end, a passing station's
outer track) is a resource; each candidate train asks for the resources ahead of it to its next stop as time
intervals from its timetable and lag (as T-1966 and T-3202 already do), first come first served, waiting at its stop
or the hold point. The follow and place rules become reservations in time, not positions this frame.

- Fixes: T-3231 (place and follow are one table), the T-3202 deadlock patch, `outerAt` skipping return runs, the 960x
  frame-step hole. t1965 then stays drawable up to the lag cap.
- Doesn't fix: the view (out-of-view trains still don't take part, unless every train of a line in view is a
  candidate, as singles already do), zooming in changing where trains are (crossings only from 14), different viewers
  seeing different things, timetables nothing can draw (lag to the cap, then through).
- Cost: 5-7 hours (T-1966's time rule, T-3202's stretch rule and T-3231's tried fix are most of the parts).
- Frame time: whole-line candidates would make it ~800 trains a frame on real New York (~210 at z12.5 cost 1.2 ms now):
  likely 3-5 ms, so it would need the view cull back, and with it the view lie.
- Risk: the tuned special cases (passing stations, trams yielding, stand-at-end exceptions) all get rewritten at once.

### B. A drawn timetable planned in the clock worker (recommended for timing)

Do the guard's job once per hour slice in the clock worker, for every train of the network, instead of every frame for
the trains in view. This is blocking time theory with an OpenTTD-style path rule, done on the timetable:

- **Resources**: blocks, each a stretch of drawn track between stops, cut where lines join or part (a merge or
  diverge), in one direction; two-way single-track stretches (`guard_singles`); conflict points (`guard_conflicts`); a
  line end's places (`assignBays` already picks them in the worker); a passing station's outer track. A block's rule
  is "in order, a gap apart" (no overtaking, 30 m); the others are one train at a time.
- **The rule**: a train leaves a stop only when it can have every resource to its next stop for the times its
  timetable needs them, the platform or place at the far end included (OpenTTD's "reserve to the next safe waiting
  place"). Otherwise it stands longer at the stop. Checks go in the order trains want to leave (first come first
  served), as a discrete-event run over the slice and the hour before it (as `assignBays` does, so every window draws
  the same). A train's whole stretch is reserved before it goes, so it never has to stop between stations, two can't
  each hold what the other needs (the T-3202 deadlock), and a train due at a taken place waits at the stop before, not
  on top of it (T-3231).
- **Lateness and recovery**: a late train makes time back by standing less where its timetable has slack (the
  capacity's holds at stops, its turnaround), never by running faster; what is left carries to its next trip.
- **Valve**: past 20 minutes late (a timetable nothing can draw), the train's next trip starts on its timetable and the
  late one fades out before its last stop, instead of being drawn through another train. The plan also adds up each
  line's drawn lateness: a number checks and the line panel's diagnostics can read, and the list of where the sim's
  timetable is not drawable (kind 4), to fix in the sim one case at a time.
- **Drawing**: each trip gets a short list of (stop, extra seconds) sent with the trips, like the bays; the train
  shader and `tripAt` work out its drawn time from it (`tripLag(i)` becomes a function of time). No per-frame guard.
- Fixes: kinds 2 and 3 entirely (one model, no view, no zoom, no frame steps, the same for every viewer and every
  reload), T-3231 and t1965's place half, T-1966's waits at every zoom, out-of-view trains jumping in. Kind 4 becomes
  visible and bounded (a fade and a number) instead of a pass-through.
- Doesn't fix: geometry (D). Boarding still happens at the timetable's time while the train is drawn late (T-1965's
  lie, unchanged).
- Cost: 10-14 hours over the stages below.
- Frame time: the main thread loses the guard (0.4-1.2 ms a frame now on real New York, p95 to 4 ms) for a few shader
  operations per train. The worker: real New York is 1,627 rail trips and 35,947 track pieces crossed per two hours,
  London 2,155 and 83,457; blocks are fewer than pieces, so about 0.1-0.3 M interval checks per hour slice with its
  lookback: an estimated 10-50 ms per slice in the worker, once a game hour (every ~4 s of wall time at 960x). To be
  measured in stage 2a; Japan N02 (5,170 trips) is the size to try.
- Risks: a slice and the next disagreeing about a train that spans the boundary (a jump at the hour: use the same
  lookback trick, check with the probe at 960x); the CPU and GPU hold lists must match exactly (the existing GPU probe
  check covers it); on undrawable timetables like t1965's express the valve fires every trip, which is honest but
  visible; edits re-plan everything (trips are rebuilt on edits already).

### C. Make the sim's timetable drawable

Have the sim produce timetables with no conflict at all: meets scheduled at passing points (T-3219), followers locked
behind the slower line where there is nowhere to pass (what T-1965 did before T-2419), arrivals at one-place ends
spaced to what the platform turns, all from one blocking-time model in the capacity solve.

- Fixes kind 4 at the source, and riders, fares and capacity become honest about it.
- Moves rider and money figures (T-3219 alone: 2 trains on 2 km from 5.1 to ~6.4 an hour), so it waits for Anita, and
  it is the largest: 15-30 hours, with the sim's average-loss model (T-1924, T-2419) to rethink.
- Doesn't remove the need for a drawing layer: places, passing stations and crossings are still drawn choices.
- Best taken one case at a time, from the list B's plan produces.

### D. Geometry: different tracks never drawn in one place

The rule: two trains the sim lets be at the same place at once (opposite directions, parallel tracks, a station's
platforms, a passing track, different levels) are drawn apart at every zoom trains are drawn at; only trains the
timing layer keeps apart may share a drawn place. Three parts:

1. Opposite directions on double track: from zoom 14 (cars), each train drawn on its side of the centre line, as close
   in, narrowing to fit within the stroke and its casing until the tracks part. The biggest count on real saves (207 of
   634 events at Midtown z14.5).
2. Parallel tracks closer on screen than a train: trains narrowed to the room between tracks (a minimum of a few px),
   or the tracks drawn apart as bands do for shared lines. 423 of 634 events.
3. Crossings on different levels: trains drawn in level order, the upper one with its casing cutting the lower, so it
   reads as over and under, as the line strokes at a bridge.

- Cost: 3-6 hours, plus a mock for Anita first: it changes how trains look between zoom 14 and 17 (T-3212 did the same).
- Frame time: shader only, nothing measurable.
- Risk: narrow trains read worse at 14-16; the fill gauge (how full) needs width. Bands and passing tracks already
  shift trains; the new shift must add to them, not fight them.

## Recommendation

**D and B together, D first**: geometry is what players see on real networks today, and it is cheap; the planned
timetable then replaces the four per-frame rules with one model that has no view, zoom or frame-rate in it. A is a
smaller step toward B that keeps its main weaknesses; C is for later, case by case, fed by B's lateness list.

Stages, each a task of its own:

0. **The probe as a standing check** (about 1 hour): `checks/t3264-overlaps.mjs` from the scratch probe, the table
   above as its baseline, plus the test layouts (t1965's track, t3202's single track, t2980's Y) built in one game so
   timing kinds show too. Every later stage reports its before and after with it.
1. **Geometry, opposite directions** (2-3 hours, mock to Anita first): D1.
2. **Geometry, alongside and levels** (2-3 hours): D2 and D3.
3. **The plan, follow and places** (3-4 hours): the worker's discrete-event plan for blocks and places, holds sent
   with the trips, the guard's follow and place rules off when a plan is present (`?perfOff=plan` back to the guard).
   Done when t1965-follow 1+1 and 4+2 pass (or every remaining frame is the valve) and t2980's one-platform test keeps
   0 pairs, with the worker's ms per slice on real New York, London and Japan N02.
4. **The plan, single track and crossings** (2-3 hours): stretches and conflict points into the plan, at every zoom;
   t3202 and t1966-cross (on its `test` layout, the real save has no flat crossings) pass; the per-frame guard
   deleted.
5. **Drawing from holds** (2-3 hours): the shader and `tripAt` take the hold list; picking, hover tags, the train panel
   and following use it; the GPU-vs-CPU probe checks.
6. **The valve and the lateness list** (about 2 hours): the fade, a per-line drawn-lateness figure for checks, and
   the list of undrawable timetables filed as sim tasks (stage C, Anita's word where figures move).

What a player still sees after all of it: trains drawn late against the timetable, boarding on the timetable's time;
a train fading out where its timetable can't be drawn; zoomed out (below 14) pills longer than the trains, touching;
and trains crossing over and under each other at different levels (drawn as such).

## Sources

- OpenTTD path signals: a train goes only once it can reserve a path to the next safe waiting place (in front of a
  signal, a depot or a track end); two trains share a block when their reserved paths don't overlap
  ([OpenTTD manual, Signals](https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Manual/Signals),
  [tt-forums, how path signals work](https://www.tt-forums.net/viewtopic.php?p=1225744)).
- Blocking time theory, the standard way railways find conflicts: each train occupies each block section for an
  interval, conflicts are overlapping intervals; first-come-first-served is the plain dispatching rule that resolves
  them, compared against optimisers in the literature
  ([Vansteenwegen, Van Thielen, Corman, LiU proceedings](https://ep.liu.se/ecp/069/071/ecp19069071.pdf),
  [ETH Zurich research collection, FCFS vs branch and bound](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/bitstream/20.500.11850/184815/1/293121.pdf)).
- NIMBY Rails simulates every train against path signals; trains following stop in time on their own
  ([NIMBY Rails wiki, Signal](https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/Signal), [Path signal setup](https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/Path_signal_setup));
  more in research/train-flow.md and research/nimby-rails.md.
- Live maps drawn from timetables or predictions (Matthew Somerville's live Tube map, Mini Tokyo 3D) place each train
  on its own from its times, assuming it runs on time; nothing keeps two apart, and they say so ("vaguely live")
  ([Wired via wirednews, live Tube map](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/live-tfl-tube-map),
  [TfL tech forum](https://techforum.tfl.gov.uk/t/realtime-vehicle-position-data/368),
  [Mini Tokyo 3D overview](https://minitokyo3d.com/docs/master/user-guide/overview.html); v3.6.0 fixed picking
  overlapped trains, [release notes](https://newreleases.io/project/github/nagix/mini-tokyo-3d/release/v3.6.0)).
- Mini Metro: nothing found on how it handles trains meeting (searched again 2026-11-12, as T-2974 did).
