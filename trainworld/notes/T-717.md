# T-717 and T-718: a cost for changing trains, and the regional layer's route choice

2026-10-19. Built and measured. T-717 reverses T-557 (no change penalty); T-718 gives the regional
layer T-710's route choice. The change's fixed cost was built at 3 minutes; after the
measurements Anita set it to 2 (both are in the tables).

## For Anita

**What changed.** Changing trains now costs a fixed 2 perceived minutes, plus the walk from the
platform you arrive at to the one you leave from (centre to centre, at the walking-transfer pace,
each minute counted 1.39), plus the wait as before. Before, platforms built within 100 m of each
other counted as one station, and a change between them walked nothing; now each keeps its own
platform, so platforms 80 m apart add 2 minutes and a change across one platform adds nothing.
The trips between cities now choose their stations the same way as trips inside a city (T-710):
before, every pair of stations near the two ends added to rail's appeal, so a city with many
stations got a lot of rail between cities just for having them. Riders take a straight train much
more often: on W 98 St to Houston St, 56% now ride the 1 straight through (none did before) and
37% still take the 2 or 3 and change. Richmond to Waterloo goes from 24% on the direct train to
97%. New York's and London's rail trips fall 4%, and both fit their station counts better. At 2
minutes London's Enfield branch into Seven Sisters is back to 1.00 of crush (1.41 at 3 minutes).
Between cities rail falls by a half to four fifths on the corridors measured, almost all of it
from T-718: Tokyo to Osaka keeps 42% of its trips on rail (56% before), New York to Philadelphia on
the test line 0.4% (2.8%). The old figures were inflated by the number of stations; the new levels
are not yet checked against anything real (T-721). Your tests A and B do not move.

## The rules as built

- `Params::transfer_min` 2 (was 0; 3 in the first build): every boarding after the first pays
  it; the skims subtract it once for the first boarding, as before.
- Platforms: `Network::stop_x/stop_y` (each stop's snapshot station, set by `set_network` in both
  APIs); `Graph::rn_dx/rn_dy` (the platform less its demand station's position, zero under 1 m) and
  `st_spread` (a station with a platform off its position). Walking transfers to other stations
  start at the platform left (`build_graph`).
- The search (`settle_station`, `settle_change`): an arrival at a spread station pays the
  frequency-weighted walk from where it stands (its platform after alighting, the station's
  position after a walk) to the platforms of the departures pooled there (`platform_walk`). The
  walk is added to the arrival, not to each line in the pool, so the pool does not depend on the
  platform arrived at (one strategy per station and change node, as before). The first boarding
  pays no platform walk (the access walk ends at the station's position).
- Trip explorer (`trip::walk`): each route's own platform walk, as its own walk leg or added to
  the walk into the station; a ride's slot 11 is its change's fixed cost; `routeTerms` adds a
  "change 2" or "changes 2 × 2" term.
- T-718 (`intercity::rail_logsum`): a pair's station pairs share its riders at the band scale of
  the zones' distance (`Params::band`, 0.33 from 12 km), rail is priced at their logsum and then
  damped in the mode choice (`mode_beta`) as before. Access lists keep their weights at 0.10; a
  pair takes them to the band scale by the power scale / 0.10. The walk-against-ride nesting
  (`add_access`) uses the 12 km band's 0.33 (was `station_theta` 0.2). With one station pair the
  choice is exactly as before, so T-378/T-495's one-line tests stand.
- Market packs: the rule lives in the sim only (packs carry zones, sites, commutes and the other
  modes, nothing of rail's choice). **No pack needs rebuilding.**
- Tests: `a_change_costs_fixed_minutes_and_the_walk_between_platforms` (kernel.rs),
  `regional_station_pairs_take_the_local_scale` (intercity/tests.rs).

- Where the drop comes from: before, a pair's rail weight was the sum over up to 64 station pairs,
  each at the damped mode scale (about 0.05 a minute on a 3-hour drive), so many similar pairs
  counted as if rail were 40-80 minutes cheaper; the access weights were also raised to that power
  one station at a time and summed. Now the pairs share at 0.33 and rail is their logsum, so a
  second similar station adds a little, not a whole second trip's worth.
- Riders concentrate: the three busiest stations carry 34% of the New York case's regional
  entries (25% before), 24% of London's (11%).
- Solve time about the same (1.6-5.3 s against 1.3-4.0 s).

## Softer station-pair choice on long trips (`rel_soft`, on)

Anita, 2026-10-19: "for long trips we want people to be a bit less time sensitive". Past 60
perceived minutes the station-pair scale falls further, times sqrt(60 / T) (the mode choice's
damping shape): `Params::rel_soft` 1, on by default since her go-ahead (0 turns it off). T is the car's
perceived minutes in the regional layer (the T-495 measure, per pair) and the band's rough rail
length (25 + 3 km) in the local layers, whose tables are per band: only the 12 km and over band
moves there, 0.333 to 0.291. Measured on a copy of the tree as it now is (with T-720's work in
progress, so the city figures differ a little from the 2-minute column above); s0 off, s1 and s2
on. The first regional runs with it on (s1) softened twice (the band's 79 minutes, then the car's);
the built rule softens the regional layer by the car's minutes alone (`band_unsoftened`), s2.
