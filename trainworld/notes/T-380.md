# T-380: parking cost by job density, checked against real prices

## The rule as it stands

`sim/src/demand/kernel.rs` (the `park` vector in the local solve; `Params` defaults):

    parking (perceived min) = 3 + min(jobs per km² of the destination's 2 km zone / 2,500, 40)

- Density is the zone's jobs (the pack's `jobs` array summed over the zone's cells) over the full
  4 km² square, water included.
- It is added once to the car's one-way perceived cost (`drive x 1.33 + park`), which is compared
  with the one-way rail cost. The commuter's choice stands for the round trip, so one trip's
  parking minutes should equal **half the day's parking price**.
- No money appears in the rule: it is minutes directly. Turned into money with the game's value of
  time (`FARE_MIN_PER_USD = 3`, $20 an hour, the same for every city), the density part is at most
  $13.33 a one-way trip, so about $27 a day, reached at 100,000 jobs/km².
- The regional layer uses the same per-site figure (`site_park`, job-weighted per zone); longer trips
  split it over the car's occupants.
- Densities in the table are per km² of the whole square, as the game reads them; the proposal's
  land densities would come out higher at waterfront centres.

fair for commutes.

## Verdict

**Wrong in shape, too flat in the middle; about right at both ends.**

- At the top (Midtown) the cap gives 40 minutes against a real 48-54: close enough. The City of
  London is far above the cap, but at 40 minutes the car is already a poor choice there.
- At the bottom (office parks, suburban centres) it is near zero, matching free parking.
- Between them it is badly low. Centres of 10,000-45,000 jobs/km² (Downtown Brooklyn, Croydon,
  Canary Wharf, the Financial District, downtown Toronto) charge $15-25 a day, which is 20-50
  minutes; the game charges 4-17, a third to a seventh of that. Real prices jump from free to
  $10-20 a day once a place passes roughly 3,000-10,000 jobs/km², then climb slowly; a straight
  line through Midtown's price cannot fit both.
- Two smaller faults. The zone's area includes water, so waterfront centres read thin (the
  Financial District zone takes in the harbour; Canary Wharf the river). And the 2 km squares cut
  clusters at arbitrary lines: Jersey City's Exchange Place reads 824 jobs/km² (3,300 jobs in its
  zone), so it parks free.
- About 13% of New York's jobs and 13% of London's (9% of Toronto's) sit in zones of
  7,500-50,000 jobs/km², charged 3-20 minutes today, so the change would mostly raise the car's
  cost to secondary centres.

## Proposal (for Anita; not built)

A rule in minutes, so it scales with each country's value of time without a price table:

1. **Paid parking**, per one-way trip: `15 x ln(d / 3,000)` perceived minutes for `d` over 3,000
   jobs per km² of **land** in the destination's zone (0 below), capped at 50. It gives 0 at 3,000,
   18 at 10,000, 40 at 42,000 and 50 from 85,000. Midtown 50 (real 48-54), Downtown Brooklyn 24
   (19-35), Croydon 18 (30), Canary Wharf and Toronto's core 40 (21-33, 15-34). Within about half
   of every price found, which is the bar.
2. **A free share** `f = 1 / (1 + d / 8,000)` of commuters to that zone park free and pay only
   the 3 minutes: 80% at 2,000, 50% at 8,000, 16% at 43,000 (Toronto: 78% at Mississauga's
   3,600, 20% downtown; US 95%, Puget Sound 82%). The car's share is the mix of two logit results,
   one with parking and one without, not one result at the average cost.
3. **Cordon charges** (London, Manhattan, Stockholm, Singapore, Milan: a short known list) as a
   per-city figure in the city's registry entry, added to paid and free commuters alike.

The flat 3 minutes to park stays. Why it may not carry over: prices relative to wages differ by
country (Tokyo and Hong Kong higher, much of the US Sun Belt and Africa lower), and free parking
at work is commoner in North America than in Europe or East Asia. Both are the natural first
country-tailoring knobs: the 15 minutes and the 8,000. Raising mid-density parking will cut
driving into secondary centres and move every city's mode shares, so it belongs with T-372's fit,
not ahead of it alone. The entry goes in docs/demand-worldwide.md (Mode choice) only if adopted.
