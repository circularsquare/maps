# T-3921: trams lose at least as much time at a stop as trains

Anita, 11-21: "i feel like we should add some more deceleration to the tram and maybe make their dwell times more
similar. i think their total time lost should probably be similar, or tbh i think it dprobably make more sense for
trams to have more time lost per stop." Background: notes/T-3887.md "Why the tram beats the metro".

## What changed

- **Dwell.** A new tram line starts with a train's 30 s dwell (`TrackSpec::dwell_s` of both tram kinds is
  `DEFAULT_DWELL_S`; was 15 s). Turnaround stays 120 s. The app takes it from `TrackApi.kind_defaults(3)`, so no app
  change.
- **Braking.** A tram line brakes at `TRAM_BRAKE` = 0.8 m/s², a train at `BRAKE` = 0.9 (params.rs). Keyed on the line
  (`Network::line_tram`, as `line_top`), so a tram on rail track brakes as a tram. `service::Run` carries `tram` and
  passes `Run::brake()` to `profile::hops` and `profile::run_with`; every function in profile.rs takes the rate as an
  argument (no bare `BRAKE` left there except the train-only `profile::run`), so cached hops and the held reruns
  (`Stretch`, `retime`) cannot disagree. Acceleration is unchanged (1.0 m/s² to 60 km/h, 0.5 above, both kinds).
- **Tests.** `stretch_retimes_match_whole_hops` runs at both rates; `a_tram_line_runs_onto_rail_track...` checks every
  braking phase of a tram line on rail is -0.8; the (15, 120) default asserts are now (30, 120);
  `trams_pass_and_turn_at_a_stop_as_trains_do` (T-3563 parity) now expects each tram single-track hold to be the
  rail one plus 1.157 s for each stop braked for inside it (from 60 km/h at 0.8 against 0.9); paths, resources and
  users stay identical.
- Checks (11-21): `agent_cargo.py test` all pass but the known `a_tram_carries_at_least_the_bus_it_replaces` (fails on
  this tree's base too; master's T-3918 rewrite feeds fixed 90 s hop times to the demand kernel, so this change
  cannot move its 64%); `agent_cargo.py wasm`, typecheck and smoke (Vite 6178, TW_CORES=4) pass. In the game,
  checks/t3563-tram-loops.mjs (rewritten for equal dwell: new tram and train lines both 30 s, the tram's headway
  0-10 s longer with loops, 0-20 s without) passes: 937 against 935 s and 2239 against 2230 s, no page errors.
- `timing_memo` keys on `run.free.duration`, which the braking rate changes, so a line flipping kind cannot reuse a
  profile timed at the other rate.

## Bump on load (built 11-21, the orchestrator's ask)

A tram line at exactly the old default, 15 s dwell and 120 s turnaround, loads at 30 s / 120 s
(`save::bump_tram_dwell`, at the end of decoding; no save level change). Any other dwell or turnaround stays, as do
train, bus and ferry lines. Players can't set dwell, so only lines made before T-3921 stand 15 s. Test:
`a_tram_line_at_the_old_default_dwell_loads_at_the_new_one` (15/120 loads at 30/120; 20/120 and 15/180 keep theirs).
The tram tests that built lines at 15/120 now use the new default, since a save and load no longer gives back the
same bytes for an old-default line.
- Docs: track-and-service.md "Tram track" (the defaults and braking) and the tram platform figure (dwell + 60 s: 40 an
  hour at 30 s, was 48 at 15 s); wiki train-speed.md "Trams". DECISIONS line in the main tree.

## Why these numbers

At the same top speed the time a stop costs is v/2a speeding up + v/2b braking + the dwell. With the dwell matched at
30 s and braking left at 0.9 a tram loses exactly what a train does, which meets "at least as much" but not her
"more"; 0.8 m/s² adds 1.3-1.5 s a stop. 0.7 would add about 3 s.

Real figures do not back a gentler tram: real trams brake harder than this game's trains in service (Siemens Combino
prototype about 1.2 m/s² average service braking, arxiv 2102.04034; French tramway reference 1.25-1.3 m/s² normal
service, techniques-ingenieur C4442). No source for an in-service tram stop at 0.8 turned up. So 0.8 is a game choice
on her word, standing for a tram easing into a street stop; recorded as such.

## Time a stop costs (loss.py in the scratch folder: the game's bands, flat cap, one dwell)

Hop time stop to stop including one dwell, and the time lost against running it at the line's top speed:

| | 400 m | 800 m | 1.5 km |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tram, 75 km/h, before (0.9, 15 s) | 56.2 s, lost 37.0 | 75.8 s, lost 37.4 | 109.4 s, lost 37.4 |
| Tram, 75 km/h, after (0.8, 30 s) | 72.5 s, lost 53.3 | 92.3 s, lost 53.9 | 125.9 s, lost 53.9 |
| Train capped at 75 km/h (0.9, 30 s) | 71.2 s, lost 52.0 | 90.8 s, lost 52.4 | 124.4 s, lost 52.4 |
| Tram, 60 km/h street, after | 72.8 s, lost 48.8 | 96.8 s, lost 48.8 | 138.8 s, lost 48.8 |
| Train capped at 60 km/h | 71.6 s, lost 47.6 | 95.6 s, lost 47.6 | 137.6 s, lost 47.6 |
| Metro at its own 160 km/h | 71.2 s, lost 62.2 | 89.8 s, lost 71.8 | 114.3 s, lost 80.6 |

So at the same speed a tram stop now costs 1.2-1.5 s more than a train's (it was 15 s less). Against a metro at its
own top speed the tram is now slower per hop at every spacing (at 400 m neither reaches its top: 66 and 67 km/h).

## Frontier rerun (tools/frontier.py, this tree, 11-21)

Before was rerun from this tree first, since the T-3887 charts predate T-3909 and T-3910. T-3910 (2 boarding minutes
at each end at every station, street level too) had already cut the tram's 400 m shares from T-3887's 49.5/48.6% to
43.1/44.7%, the T-3887 "tram with stairs" row to the tenth. Charts and results:
`artifacts/trainworld/T-3921/` (`share.png`, `profit.png` after; `share_before.png`, `profit_before.png`;
`result.json`, `result_before.json`). Every mode at a train every 4 minutes (the share chart's service). Only the tram
rows change.

The tram's lead over the metro is gone: it trails by 0.3-0.8 points at 400 m and 0.9-2.0 at 1.5 km (the metro's higher

day at 400 m (was 66), 63M at 800 m (73), 52M at 1.5 km (57); the metro loses as before (US$134-373M a day).
