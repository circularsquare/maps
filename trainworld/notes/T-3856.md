## What changed

| Rule | Was | Now | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bus boarding cost | 4 perceived minutes on every bus boarding | none: a bus boards as a train | `BUS_BOARD_MIN`, `Graph::rn_bus` and the trip explorer's `BUS_BOARD_FELT_MIN` removed |
| Bus moving speed | 90% of the car's, 30 km/h floor | 85%, floor kept | `BUS_SHARE` (sim/src/track/streets.rs) |
| Bus reach | homes within 300 m of a route | 600 m; the walk is still the distance to the route plus 100 m | `BUS_REACH_M` (demand/kernel/access.rs), the map's corridor outline `CORRIDOR_M` (map/busLayer.ts) |
| Route construction | US$1M a road-km | US$0.1M (a token; shows as "$100k per km of route") | `BUS_ROUTE_COST_PER_KM` (track/params.rs) |
| Default bus fare multiple | 1.00 | 0.80 | `BUS_FARE` (game/fareCurve.ts) |

A save that set the Bus fare row keeps its own value; one that never touched it has no stored value and moves to 0.80
with the default (no migration, per "saves are hers"). Bus upkeep (US$30 a road-km a day) and the bus price are
unchanged. 80% was not built (below).

Tests: the kernel test that pinned the boarding cost now pins that a bus boards as a train; the ferry test lost its
`rn_bus` line; speed tests are written in `BUS_SHARE`; the route price tests in `BUS_ROUTE_COST_PER_KM`. The tram test
"a tram carries more than the bus it replaces" relied on the bus boarding cost: on its light corridor the two now carry
the same to rounding (34,044.6 against 34,045.6), so it asserts "at least as many". A tram only beats a bus on equal
stops once the bus fills (80 at crush against 360).

- Buses about double their money and riders in the dense corridor and still earn a third to a half of the winner there
  (room: a bus every minute is full).
- Medium (Western Avenue): buses now pay a little at a bus every 15/30/60 minutes; the street tram still makes about
  ten times as much.
- Thin (DuPage): a 2 km bus is now the only thing that pays, and at 5 km with 1 km stops it breaks even. This is the
  "plain buses most profitable on short, thin trips" goal, just met.
- Round trips are identical before and after in dense and medium (the 30 km/h bus-lane floor binds there at both 90%
  and 85%) and about 1% longer in thin (66.4 to 67.3 minutes at 10 km).

Trip shares, one 28 km bus along Western Avenue at its own stops, every 8 minutes, commutes in the morning peak:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bus before | 3.7% | 21.1% | 28.1% | 30.2% | 25.8% | 21.4% | 17.7% |

Both trip ends moved off the line, bus share averaged over 1-18 km: 0 m 21% to 28%; 200 m 13% to 23%; 500 m 0% to 10%;
1 km 0% (the street tram: 38%, 32%, 19%, 8%). At 1 km walking still takes 52%: under about 1.3 km no bus is offered
at all, since "no train slower than walking" counts the walk to the stop and the wait (T-3888).

**85% or 80%: kept 85%.** Buses overshoot nowhere: they win only where nothing else pays. And on these corridors speed
is not in play: the floor binds in the city at 85% and would at 80% too, so 80% would only slow the thin and
open-country buses, the one place they should win. If city buses ever need to be weaker, the levers are the 30 km/h
floor or the reach. (Advisor called before this call; it agreed.)

## Compute cost of reach 600

The real NYC save and London's real network with twelve straight test bus routes added along arterials
(`addbuses_*.js` in the scratch folder), then a bus edit (one bus's headway toggled 8 to 7 minutes) four times; the
demand log's time from the network sent to the final round. Every edit re-solves the whole city, so an edit is a
whole calculation of the network with buses. The machine was at 87-100% load from other agents throughout, and
identical edits spread by about 15%, so two games ran side by side at the same time (2 cores each), one at 300 m and
one at 600 m, to share the load.

| Side by side, same load | Reach 300 | Reach 600 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| NYC, bus edit (mean of 4) | 106.9 s | 96.7 s | -9% |
| London, bus edit (mean of 4) | 107.4 s | 109.1 s | +2% |
| London, adding the 12 buses (one) | 103.9 s | 115.1 s | +11% |
| Demand memory per worker | 416-449 MB | 416-449 MB | none |

One after another on 3 cores (noisier): NYC bus edit median 64.7 s on master (7 edits), 74.5 s at 300 m, 69.8 s at
600 m; London 70.0 s, 58.9 s, 91.4 s (the last while the machine was full). So the cost is below what this machine
can measure, a few percent at most, well under the 15% bar: built in. A save without buses is untouched (calib below).
