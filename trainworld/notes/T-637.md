# T-637: why everyday trips come out too long, and the smallest fix (2026-10-17)

Measurements and a proposal only. No default was changed and nothing in the tree was touched except
this note.

## Reading, for Anita

- **The trips are too long in the middle, not at the far end.** In New York half of real errand and
  social trips (NHTS 2017, straight line) are under 2 km. The game has 22%. But the game's errands
  and social trips average 4.34 and 4.66 km, the same as the survey's 4.35 and 4.66, because
  T-509 set each purpose's decay scale to make the means match. A single distance decay cannot do
  both. Real trips are very short or fairly long: the survey's average is twice its median (4.35
  against 1.96 km). The game's trips cluster around the average: its median is about 3.7 km.
  Counting the three bands: under 2 km the game has 23% (survey 50%), 2 to 5 km it has 45% (21%),
  and 5 km and over it has 33% (28%). So the game does not have too many long trips. It has too
  many in the 2 to 5 km band, and too few that people would walk.
- **Other things I checked do not matter.** The 150 m cutoff (with the 80 km taper) removes 0.5% of
  New York's errand trips. The cell size (res 9) is not limiting. The game having home-based trips
  only makes no difference: the survey's home-based legs are as long as all its legs (errands 50.2%
  against 50.4% under 2 km). The destination sampling gives the intended mean (4.34 km against
  the survey's 4.35).
- **The 2 km zones do set a floor on how short the game can go.** A destination is chosen by zone
  and then by a cell inside the zone with no regard to distance. Even if every trip stayed in its own
  zone, only 12% of errands would be under 0.5 km (survey: 23%, of which 5% are under 150 m and
  would be cut anyway) and the mean would be 1.0 km. This is why shortening the decay helps
  up to about 40% under 2 km and then stops being able to match the survey's short end.
- **The same length error is in London.** London residents' trips in the survey (LTDS 2024/25, all
  purposes together) are 52% under 2 km; the game's are 24%. London's by-purpose lengths are not
  published, but the walking shares by purpose show it: the game's everyday trips walk 18 to 27%
  against the survey's 41 to 44% (T-636).
- **Simplest fix: one worldwide set of shorter decay scales** instead of each country's scale:
  errands 1.3 km, social 1.4, school 1.4, medical 2.0, university 3.5 (today New York 2.15, 2.42, 2.5,
  3.53, 6.0 km). No new mechanism, and the country table of scales goes away. I call it W1. In New
  York it moves errands from 23% to 37% under 2 km and from 33% to 13% at 5 km and over, the average
  from 4.3 to 2.9 km. Walking with no rail goes from 20% of errands to 30% (survey 26%); London's from
  30% to 33% for errands and 21% to 32% for social (survey 42 and 41%).
- **What W1 costs.** Your test A (rail of rail + car) falls from 86.3 to 83.5% in New York and from
  85.5 to 83.8% in London, under 85. B stays far above 50 (76 and 77%). The cause is that short trips
  are the ones where a good train beats the car least. A parking base of 7 minutes instead of 5
  (`park_min`, an existing number) brings A back to 85.8 and 86.1% and B to 79.0 and 79.7%.
  The real networks lose 6 to 8% of rail trips (New York 11.9M a day to 11.0M with W1, 11.2M with
  W1 and the parking base 7; London 9.7M to 8.9M and 9.1M). Rail's share of rail + car on the real
  networks hardly moves (22.1 to 22.2%, 31.0 to 30.4%), because the trips that go are the long
  ones that no station serves. Everyday rail drops 14% (7.7M to 6.6M a day in New York).
- **A heavier tail would fit better but is not worth it.** A decay that falls more slowly than an
  exponential (a shape number, a new mechanism) gets 5 to 7 points more under 2 km at the same mean,
  no more, because the 2 km zones stay. A mix of local and farther trips would fit, but is a second
  mechanism. I did not pursue them.
- **Something else I noticed, not part of the task.** New York's commutes are also longer than the
  survey's: 3% under 2 km against 16% (NHTS home-based work legs), and 33% over 20 km against 23%.

## What Anita decides

1. W1 as the one worldwide set, or something milder (all scales times 0.75: 31% under 2 km, A 84.7
   and 84.1%), or leave it.
2. If W1: whether to also move `park_min` to 7 to keep A at 85. It also changes commutes, and I did not
   rescore the station counts.
3. Whether the 2 km zone resolution is worth its own task (a decay inside the zone), which is the
   only way to get the survey's very short trips (under 0.5 km).

## Method

Everything runs on the model's own Rust code in a private copy of `sim/` (copied 2026-10-17 from the
working tree; `notes/t636_ceiling.rs` is T-636's test as it is, plus one test, `t637_lengths`, that
walks every destination sample of the everyday layer). Each sample stands for a number of outings
(budget x rate, divided over the cell's samples, times the 40 to 80 km taper); the distance is the
straight line between the home cell and the activity cell, the same as the model uses. The bands are
T-551's finer. New York and London packs at today's defaults (the pack's `everyday` block:
New York errands 2150, social 2420, medical 3530 m; London errands 1470, social 2340, medical 3530;
school 2500 and university 6000 everywhere). Setting a scale is `--set decay_<purpose>=metres`,
as T-509 stage 4 made it, before the layer is opened. The ceiling runs (A, B, C) are T-636's test
unchanged, rail of rail + car as in T-636 round 2. The base run reproduces T-636 round 2 to the
decimal (New York A 86.3, B 79.3, C 22.1; London 85.5, 78.9, 31.0).
The files are in the scratchpad of the session (`.../scratchpad/t637/`: `mkcopy.py`, `t637_lengths.rs`,
`run.py`, `sweep*.py`, `tables.py`, `out/*.json`); I can move them under `notes/` on request.
One run of both tests takes about 70 s for New York and 50 s for London on one core each; the length
test takes 2 s.

The surveys:

- **New York: NHTS 2017**, microdata `data/raw/nhts2017/csv.zip`, table `trippub.csv`, New York
  CBSA households (`HH_CBSA` 35620), weekday legs (`TRAVDAY` 2 to 6), weighted by `WTTRDFIN`. Purposes
  as in T-509 (`notes/t509_nhts.py`): the activity at the far end, `WHYTO` unless home, then `WHYFROM`;
  errands 11, 12, 14, 6; social 13, 15, 16, 17, 19; medical 18; school 8, 9, 10; university school
  trips by people 18 or older. Straight line = `TRPMILES` x 1.609 / 1.3, the game's road detour,
  as in T-551 and T-509. Without any detour factor, 45% of errand legs are under 2 km (with 1.5: 54%),
  so the finding does not depend on it. Home-based legs only give errands 50.2% under 2 km,
  social 48.8%, medical 22.3%, school 47.0%, university 14.6%; all legs, in the table below, are
  50.4, 50.3, 25.8, 48.2, 20.0.
  The survey's average is of legs under 46 km (T-509); the game's is of all its outings (80 km taper).
- **London: LTDS 2024/25, Travel in London 2025** ("The travel behaviour of London residents based
  on the LTDS", figure 16 and the text under it: straight line, residents' trips): 34% of trips are
  1 km or less, a further 16% 1 to 2 km, 71% 5 km or less, 15% over 10 km, 5% over 20 km; average
  6.0 km a trip. 2019/20: 49% under 2 km. https://content.tfl.gov.uk/travel-in-london-2025-the-travel-behaviour-of-london-residents-based-on-the-ltds-acc.pdf
  The by-purpose trip length distribution of London is not published openly (NTS0413 has the average
  miles by purpose and region, NTS0407 only long trips, NTS0308 trip length by mode, none of them
  length by purpose; the LTDS microdata need a UK Data Service account). So for London purposes
  only the walking shares of T-636 (LTDS mode shares by purpose) are available as a check.

### Why: scale against shape (New York errands)

Everyday destinations are drawn from zone mass times exp(-distance / scale), zone to zone centres at
2 km, so the number of destinations grows with distance and the trips follow r x exp(-r / scale):
a mean of about 0.7 + 1.7 x scale (T-509), and a mean close to 1.2 times the median. The survey's is
2.2 times.

Notes on these tables:

- The ceilings A and B fall with shorter trips because a short trip is where a free fast train
  beats the car least. `park_min` raises the car's cost on every trip, commutes included, so it
  lifts A and B back; it also lifts C a little.
- The real network's rail of rail + car stays at 22 and 30 to 31% because the trips that
  disappear are long ones nobody can reach a station for. The fall is in rail trips, not in share.
- New York's everyday rail share on C is 14.7% today against NHTS's 5.8% of everyday legs
  (T-551), so trip length is part of the excess, not all of it: W1 takes 1.9 points of the 8.9.
  The rest is mode choice and coverage, which T-636 already flags (stations x3.06 the counts).
- Station counts and the calibration score were not rescored for any row; that needs `calib`
  and would be part of the task that applies W1.
- The base run needed `T637_SHAPE` for the shape rows only (a one-line change in my copy of
  `DecayTable::new`); the main tree is unchanged.

## If W1 is adopted (for whoever applies it)

- The scales live in three places: `everyday::DECAY` (defaults for a pack without a block), each pack's
  `everyday` block (`decay_m`, written by `pipeline/everyday_rates.py`, whose `SURVEYS` table holds
  the country scales), and the text in docs/demand-sources.md ("each set so the game's mean outing
  length matches the survey's mean") and docs/demand-worldwide.md. The block keeps the rates; the
  decays would be dropped from it or set to the one worldwide set.
- Docs to update: docs/demand-plain.md ("how far they go comes from each country's travel survey"),
  docs/demand-sources.md and docs/demand-worldwide.md.
- The market's local overlap (25 to 80 km) shrinks: under W1 no purpose has more than 0.3% beyond
  20 km except airport (unchanged, 20 km scale) and university (3.5 km scale).
