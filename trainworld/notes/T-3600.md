## T-3601: riders on lines with no trains

Reproduced on her save (runB, before the fix): after the last local result (e+26 s) the tag went away, the view's
version stayed on the solve before the edit while the newest solve ran, and the five lines with no trains read
50,681, 27,291, 26,134, 12,610 and 9,110 riders a day for 220 s more, until the whole final gave 0. On the NYC save
(line 3 to 0 trains) it did not happen: that edit's touched share was 75-77%, so it was solved whole at once (T-3512)
and the tag said "Trips in 4 cities being recomputed…" for the whole 150 s; line 3 went from 977,392 to 0 at the
final. So it is the local-then-whole path, any big cluster.

Cause: the tag follows "the zone in view is final" (T-1851). After an edit the local result settles the lines and the
zone in view, so nothing kept the tag up through the whole solve, while those figures still held other zones' and
the market's riders as before the edit.

Fix (`ui/StatusTag.tsx`): the tag says "Calculating trips after your change…" while an edit no final covers is being
solved (`demandUpdating` and `demandBehindFrom` set), after every other case. Loads are untouched (their solves set
no `demandBehindFrom`). Its wording and hover are solveeta's.

Check (runC, with the fix, a one-off run of about 13 minutes on her save, not a standing check): one probe every 2 s
from the first edit to 299 s; the tag read "Calculating trips after your change…" in every probe until the final
(after a first "Trips in 123 cities being recomputed…"), empty only after it, and the five lines read 0 at the end;
the trains-on phase before it had no empty tag in 80 probes either.

Left as it was: the line panel. `linesSettled` still clears a line's "calculating" label at the local result, so
under the tag a line with no trains shows its stale riders (50,681 here) until the final. Smoke: see the report. No check expected the tag gone after a local
result (grep of checks/ for the tag).

## Options for T-3600 (not built)

None is small and safe enough to build here: each changes either what an edit shows or when solves run.

1. **Don't hold an edit behind a whole solve it supersedes** (T-1734's `firm`). An edit arriving during a whole
   solve stops it and starts again from the new network. runC would end at about 65 + 10 + 140 = 215 s instead of
   295 s. Cost: the stopped solve leaves the populations on the new network, so that next edit gets no local result
   (no quick figures), and someone editing every 15 s for ten minutes never sees a final. A scheduling call, about 2-3
   hours.
2. **Balance the periods across workers.** 5 periods over 3 workers gives 2, 2 and 1; runB's workers had 200, 118 and
   70 s of work. An even split is about 130 s, two thirds of now. Settings' "more cores" (5 workers on her machine, a
   period each) already gives one period's four rounds, 65-100 s, at more memory. No change to the figures; about 3-4 hours.
3. **Solve the market's pairs through Kaohsiung in the local stage too** (T-1735's window, which today opens only when
   stations change, not trains). The intercity lines' change would show within seconds, closer to the final; the
   whole solve still runs after. About 3-5 hours, and it needs a check of how close it gets.
4. **End on the local result (T-2188).** Under 5 s, but measured 7-16% off on the lines here and 9-51k riders on
   lines with no trains, and it never answers her question (whether the intercity line loses riders), which only
   the whole solve can. Not recommended.
5. **Keep far populations in the whole solve** (re-solve only those whose routes share loads that moved). The only
   option that gets near a minute, but it changes how demand converges: hers to decide, a big task.

Recommendation: the tag fix now (built, T-3601), then option 3 for the "did my edit change the intercity line"
question, and option 2 for the wait itself; option 1 only if she edits in bursts often.

end in those zones (Kaohsiung's: 29 zones of the market for line 212 alone, 77 for all five); the far pairs keep their
loads until the whole solve, as before. Also: a window's per-purpose loads are now held at 0 or more on each route node,
as its totals already were (the first run showed a switched-off line at -1,042 riders).
