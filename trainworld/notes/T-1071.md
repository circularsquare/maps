# T-1071: a train count change moves no train

Anita (2026-10-23): "when changing the number of trains on a route, the location of the trains
instantly jumps around on the line, which feels immersion breaking."

## Why they jumped

The track model was already right. T-206's eras, shifts and hand-overs keep every running train
where it was at the edit's game time; nine new Rust tests (`sim/src/track/transition_tests.rs`)
place every train on the ground every 2 s around an edit (count up and down, quick successive
edits, shared and full track, headway mode, circle, balloon, a reversal partway, another local
time, just before a period change, eras pruned as the worker prunes them) and all passed before
any change.

The jump came from the way the browser delivered the edit. The worker applied it at its own
`gameNow()`, but the renderer drew it only when the new run profiles and trips arrived. That
took 6 to 10 frames on the real save: about 100 ms of the worker's time on the edit (the
`edited` message's `ms.total` is 90 to 125 ms), plus the message and a frame. In between the
renderer kept drawing the old trips, so any train that left a terminal in those frames was drawn
by the old pattern and then redrawn by the new one. A train whose profile changed (holds on
shared track) was drawn on the old profile, then on the new profile shifted to match the edit's
earlier moment. The run profiles and the trips also came in two messages, so a frame could draw
the new profiles with the old trips. The hops grow with the clock rate: at 960x, 100 ms is 96 game
seconds. On the real save at 960x, with the F's count changed four times, the old code drew 4
trains vanishing mid-line between frames, and 61 trains differed between the old and new buffers
when they swapped. On a slower machine, or a bigger world whose edit takes longer, every train
that left in that window jumps.

Saves also dropped the eras, so a game saved just after a change loaded re-spaced.

## What changed

- **A train count or headway edit takes effect a moment ahead.** The clock worker gives it a lead
  of 0.25 s plus twice what its last such edit took, at most 2 s of wall time, at the clock's rate
  (`serviceLeadS`, `TrackApi.set_edit_lead`; 0 when paused). Rust takes the eras, shifts and
  flights at that time (`edit_start`, `edit_time`), never before an earlier edit still to take
  effect. The state carries it (`ClockState.effectiveAt`).
- **The renderer stages that state** (`renderer.stage`). It puts the state in, with the trips of
  its version, at the first frame at or past that time. The old and new buffers then draw every
  train at the same place when they swap. Any other state puts the staged ones in first.
- **Eras are no longer pruned for lying ahead of the clock** unless more than an hour ahead
  (`ERA_AHEAD_S`).
- **Saves carry the eras and shifts** after the network bytes: payload, u32 length, `ERA1`
  (`Service::encode_eras` / `decode_eras`, `TrackApi.save` / `load`). Network decoders stop before
  them, and a save with nothing changing over is byte for byte as before. Rates the load settles
  again agree to about 1e-3 s over an hour's departures.

## Which option (DECISIONS)

The brief offered two: trains phase in toward even spacing, or the change takes effect at the
termini. The model already did the second (T-206): running trains keep their trips, added trains
enter at a terminal as departures come up, and a train with no departure leaves at the end of its
turnaround. It stays that way. It keeps a train's position a pure function of the service and the
clock (SPEC 2), so fast-forward costs nothing new. Phasing in by holding or speeding trains would
need per-train state and would break that. Running costs follow the departures, which follow the
new pattern from the moment it takes effect. The boarding account reads the same departures, so
riders stay on the trains that are drawn.

## Limits, filed

- **Stop changes at a line's end move trains (T-1072).** Adding a stop past an end, or removing
  the first stop, moves the other direction's trains by the length added or taken. Flights are
  kept as offsets along the run, and the run's start moved. A Rust probe on a 5-stop corridor
  moved 3 of 6 trains by 2 km (a stop added at the end) and 4 of 6 (the first stop removed).
  Removing a middle stop moved none. The fix is to match flights by place on the ground, not
  this change.
- **Undo and redo of a count change take effect at once, without the lead (T-1073),** so at a
  fast clock they show the old small hops. The worker does not know what an undo undoes.
- A train standing at a terminal for a departure that the change gives to another train leaves
  service at the change, at that terminal.
