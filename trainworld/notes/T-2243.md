# T-2243 and T-2244: lines doubling back, and hooks

Rules in docs/ui-diagram.md ("The layout", "The drawing"); figures and reasoning here.

## The Shinjuku loop (T-2243)

The game's Tokyo graph (1,444 complexes) laid out in Node with the game's B positions reproduced it:
B had Seibu-Shinjuku north of Shinjuku, and the snap swapped them (Seibu-Shinjuku 0.25 hops south),
so the JR bundle ran past Shinjuku to Seibu-Shinjuku and came back up: a U-turn at the station. The
snap's terms only fix each hop's direction up to its sign, at weight 30 against an anchor of 1, so a
hop of no length, or one turned back, meets them; where snapped directions round a loop of the graph
disagree, it collapsed or flipped short hops. Of 49 lines drawn turning 135 degrees or more at a
station where the real line turns under 90 (the game's graph), 35 sat on an edge drawn more than 90
degrees off its real way; the snap took the count of such edges from 4 (B) to 42.

The game's layout and Node's differ for the same graph (up to 6 hops; 2 even from the same B): the
pipeline is chaotic in the last bits of float maths (V8 versions), so the check graphs and the game's
are different samples of the same mechanisms. Checked on four: the checks' New York and Tokyo, and the
game's New York (854 complexes) and Tokyo (1,444), captured from the page's worker message.

The other ways a line folded back, found stage by stage (B, snap, routing, each separation pass):
- B turned a line at a junction far past its real turn (Tokyo-Yurakucho 152 degrees for a straight
  line), and snapping rounded both pieces the wrong ways.
- The routing picked the leg order of a bent piece with no thought for lines through its ends (a 50
  degree real turn drawn 135).
- The separation shrank a station-less leg to nothing, so the piece left its end another way (Omiya),
  and turned a port-clash piece onto the one free port, which folded a line (Tsurumi).

What changed (layout.ts unless said):
- B: a line through a junction keeps its real turn there, weight 1 (`turnTerm`).
- The snap: a piece snaps the way the real line runs, chosen from B or the current round; every hop
  keeps its length along its way (weight 30, at least half its target); of two snapped pieces that
  fold a line, the one further off is left to the routing; a line B turned over 45 degrees past its
  real turn is pulled back toward it (10).
- Routing: pieces within 5 degrees are drawn straight (the separation makes them exact; with the
  hold, 0.5 left a quarter of the straight pieces bent with tiny legs), unless that folds a line; legs
  chosen against folds (4 times the length a fold), two passes.
- The separation: the port-clash turn and the piece kept avoid folds, and none is turned if every free
  port folds; a station-less leg keeps 2 pitches where shrinking it would fold a line.

Tried and dropped: weight 2 or 10 for the hold (fewer fixes); the hold keeping legs of every piece 2
pitches (cut straight pieces from 280 to 119 in New York); chooseDirs avoiding fold directions (no
change, the other end of an edge overrides); the turn term at every node (B 30% slower, Tokyo past 8 s).

## Hooks (T-2244)

The three hooks were lines folding back at those stations: a line turning 135 or 180 degrees cannot
cross any one axis, so it runs beside the pill and gets a round stop off it. With the folds gone, the
checks' New York and Tokyo have no hook at all. On the game's New York graph Atlantic Av still had one:
the 2/3 turn south there, and a corner's radius (6 stroke widths plus the bundle) cut the corner more
than 1.25 pitches from the pill. A corner at a station now has radius 3 stroke widths (draw.ts
`RADIUS_STATION_W`): Atlantic Av clear. Grand Central keeps one on that graph (the Lexington bundle
turning east), not on the checks'.
