# T-243 Loading bar and real progress

Anita (2026-10-13): "loading panel: show a bar filling, not a % number" and "we seem to freeze at
60% for like the majority of the loading time, maybe make this more real".

## Where the time goes (measured 2026-10-13)

Headless Chrome on Anita's desktop, default pool (five solving workers plus the aux worker), fresh
profile, the served production build at 8800 before the change. Times are ms since navigation.
Script: `notes/t243-load-timing.mjs` (same method as `notes/startup-timing.mjs`; it samples the
loading card every 25 ms and reads `twDemand.start.loads` and `twDemand.log`).

| Step | New game (empty network) | Real New York save (T-007, 109 lines, 1,756 stops) |
|---|---|---|
| Card up | 130-550 | 160-180 |
| Downloads (market 8.2 MB, nyc 16 MB) | 70-180 -> 250-890 | 75 -> 250-410 |
| Opening the market in every worker | to 520-1,360 (0.3 s) | to 560-590 |
| Opening New York in every worker | to 2,450-2,920 (2.0-2.1 s per worker, in parallel) | to 2,450-2,700 |
| New Haven (the save reaches it), opened after New York in each worker | | 1,375-1,550 requested -> 2,530-2,770 |
| First estimate (solve sent -> round 0) | 0.56-0.81 s (setup 25-45 ms, a period 480-770 ms) | 4.1-4.4 s (setup ~470 ms, a period 2.4-3.9 s) |
| Clock released | 3,020-3,740 | 6,700-7,030 |

So on the real save about 6% of the load is downloading, 30% opening packs and 64% the first
estimate; on an empty network 12%, 70% and 18%. The old percentage gave these 10/50/40 by count
of work, and each step's real progress arrives in lumps: every worker finishes opening New York at
about the same moment (the bar sat at 32% for 2 s), and every period's first estimate lands within
a few hundred ms of the others at the end of the solve (the bar sat at 60% for 3 s on the real
save, then jumped through 68-92% in the last half second). That is the "freeze at 60%".

## What changed

`workers/demandClient.ts` `progress()`: each step weighs as long as it is expected to take, and
inside a step the bar moves with time against that expectation, until the step's real end lands.

- Download: its own rate so far (elapsed x total / received bytes); real bytes are its progress.
- Opening: 130 ms per MB of a city's `.bin` (New York 16 MB: 2.1 s), 40 ms per MB for the market,
  summed over the items (each worker opens them one after another, the workers in parallel).
  Progress is the larger of the worker acks (by item size) and the time since the first item
  finished downloading over the expected total.
- First estimate: 600 ms + 2.2 ms per line stop of the network being solved (fits both columns
  above). The last load's real solve time and stop count are remembered per browser
  (`localStorage` `anitabuilder-load-solve`) and scale the estimate (`last.ms x model(stops) /
  model(last.stops)`), so a slower machine or another network corrects it after one load. Before
  the clock worker has sent the network, the last load's stop count stands in.
- Inside a step: in step with the expectation to 90% of the step, then slowing toward 98%, so a
  step that runs late still creeps.
- The shown value never goes back. When an expectation grows (the network turns out bigger than
  the remembered one, a download slows), the bar does not wait for the true fraction to catch up:
  the rest of the work is spread over the rest of the bar from where it stands (an anchor).
  Without that, the first real-save load after a new game stood at 59% for 2.3 s.
- Only a startup's solve is remembered, so the record describes the game a reload opens.
- A held game whose cities are already open (opening a browser slot) now gets a bar for its solve
  alone; before it showed "Calculating demand" with no progress.
- The card shows "Loading data", the bar, then the step. The top bar shows "Loading data" and a
  64 px thin bar for a city loading later. Both bars have the game's hover tag with the percentage
  and step; the bar is the card's one element taking pointer events.

## After (dev server, same machine, 2026-10-13)

Fill against time, sampled every 100 ms (`load-final.json` in the scratchpad), final code:

- New game, 3.2-4.1 s: 9-11% by the end of downloads (0.4-0.9 s), then a steady rise through
  opening to 80-85% at 2.6-3.1 s, the solve 84-99% over the last 0.6-0.9 s.
- Real New York save, reloaded after a previous load of the same save (the usual case), 6.9 s:
  3% at 0.35 s, 30% at 1.5 s (New Haven joins, 40%), 60% at 2.6 s when opening ends, then about
  1.2% per 100 ms through the solve to 98% at 6.8 s.
- Real New York save right after a new game (the worst case: the remembered solve was an empty
  network's), 7.0 s: 59% at 1.6 s, when the network arrives and the solve's expectation grows
  sixfold; the bar then slows rather than stops, 66% at 1.7 s, 76% at 2.7 s, 90% at 4.8 s, 98.7%
  at 6.9 s.

Before, the same save sat at 32% for 1.9 s and at 60% for 3.0 s. No step now holds the bar still.
