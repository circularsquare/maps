## What it would look like in play

- **New York.** Lexington Avenue becomes four tracks: the 4 and 5 on the express pair, the 6 and
  6X on the local pair, local stations on the local pair only, express stations (Grand Central, 14
  St, Brooklyn Bridge) with a platform on each pair, side by side, one complex as now. The 4 passes
  51 St on the express pair beside the 6's platform. Seventh Avenue is the same with the 2/3
  express and the 1 local, Queens Boulevard with E/F express and M/R local, Eighth Avenue with A
  express and C local. Where the 4 and 5 part in the Bronx there is a real junction. Today each of
  these is 3-5 double tracks side by side.
- **London Bridge.** South London's Southern, Thameslink and Southeastern patterns (each its own
  double track today, up to 13 abreast on the Brighton line) fall onto a few pairs. London Bridge is a
  station node per pair (through pairs for Thameslink and Southeastern, dead ends for Southern's

- **Tokyo through-running.** Ueno-Tokyo Line trains share the Tokaido pair south of Tokyo instead
  of running beside it, and the Joban local pair carries both the Joban locals and the Chiyoda

- **In the capacity tags**: shared trunks get busy, near-full or full tags at high demand where the

## Capacity on shared track: does the model give sensible results?

Yes, with quad where the real trunk is quad. A track section is one direction of a run with the
same set of lines; each train holds it 70 s + v / 0.6 (docs 6.2), so at a subway's ~70 km/h about
35 trains an hour each way, at 120 km/h about 29. Platforms: dwell + 60 s per stopping train, 40 s
per passing one. The figures are the saves' high-demand trains an hour (an average over 6-20, so
below the real peak):

| Trunk | Lines (each way) | On one double | As quad (express / local pair) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lexington Av | 4+5 21.3, 6+6X 16.5 | 108%: fleets cut ~8%, red tag | 61% / 47%, no tags |
| 7th Av | 2+3 18.0, 1 13.8 | 90%: near full, ~2 min wait a train | 51% / 39% |
| Queens Blvd | E+F 25.2, M+R 16.3 | 118%: fleets cut ~15% | 72% / 46% |

So three or four subway lines on one double track give believable results (busy, some waiting),
and the real quad trunks only fit as quad, as in life. Quad should be **two double edges side by
side** (8 m apart), not a new four-track edge: that is what the model's quad already is (T-255),
sections and platforms are per pair, and lines change pairs only at junctions or crossovers.

One trap: **Tokyo's frequencies are rough defaults** (T-509). Tokaido 18 + Ueno-Tokyo Line 18 = 36 an
hour on one pair against ~29 at 120 km/h (125%), because the Ueno-Tokyo trains are counted again as
their own line. Shared, that is red from the first minute. My call: the build trims each shared
piece's lines in proportion to at most 90% of its capacity and prints which it trimmed; real NYC
service fits without trimming on the trunks above.

## What the track model supports today (checked in code)

- **Passing a station without stopping**: supported. docs 6.3 ("A line may pass a station without
  stopping"); `capacity.rs` charges a path node that is not one of the line's stops `PASSING_S` (40 s)
  on the platform. Nothing to add.
- **Quad**: supported as two doubles side by side; stations on each pair separately (T-1687), named
  as one complex by `twin_at`. `realsave` sets edges directly, so it only needs the geometry 8 m apart.
- **Junctions with tangent leads**: supported. Every edge end at a node leaves along the node's
  heading (`NodeHeading` check); `realsave` already puts a lead PI each side of a station on the
  track's heading and does the same at a junction. Up to 8 edge ends a node (`MAX_PORTS`).
- **Limits the build must respect** (not gaps in the model):
  - a station node has at most two edge ends (`StationPorts`) and no junction in its platform, so
    a junction at a platform end moves 20 m past it (`BRANCH_CLEAR`, as the drawing tool does);
  - a dead-end node is one pair, at most 30-36 an hour; a big terminus (London Bridge's Southern
    side, Grand Central) is several dead-end nodes side by side, lines spread over them by load;
  - a throat with more than 8 ends is a chain of junction nodes;
  - ramps are 200 m per level and never under a platform.
- **Grade separation**: `realsave` sets `sep` on every edge (T-1751), so imported track never makes a
  flat crossing. Keep it. Junction nodes get `flying: true`, since OSM cannot tell a flat junction
  from a flyover; most trunk junctions in these cities are grade separated. (My call.)

What is missing is all in the build: the router keeps polylines, not which OSM chains a hop runs
on; nothing groups parallel chains; `realsave` gives every line its own nodes and edges.

## Build steps (rough size, 2 cores)

1. **Chains per hop** (`realroute.py`): keep each routed hop as (chain, from, to) intervals beside
   the polyline. Small: the A* already walks chains. An hour.
2. **Corridors**: cluster parallel chains within ~15 m, same heading, into corridors. OSM maps a
   trunk per track, per pair or as one way; a corridor's real track count is roughly its parallel
   chains (`tracks=` is not in the graph; adding it to `realgraph.py` is optional). Half a day.
3. **Cut and count pairs**: cut each corridor at stations and wherever its set of lines changes.
   Pairs per piece = 1, or 2 where some lines skip stations others stop at, or where its trains
   exceed 80% of a double; never more than the corridor's chains allow (rounded up to a pair).
   Lines to pairs: skippers on the express pair, else by load. Same piece, same pair, all along a
   run, so lines do not hop pairs at every cut. Half a day with step 2.
4. **Nodes**: one station node per real station and pair where a line on that pair stops (stops
   of different feeds within ~150 m on one pair are one node, named by the busiest line), dead ends
   split by load; junction nodes where pieces meet, moved clear of platforms; leads on the heading.
5. **Levels per piece** from the graph's tunnel and bridge flags (-1 or -2 in tunnel, +1 on a
   bridge or viaduct, 0 elsewhere on land, +1 over water), ramps placed where there is 200 m a
   level, else the piece takes its neighbour's level. Steps 4-5 in `realsave`: about a day.
6. **Trim** overloaded pieces (above), print the list; build, `gamesave`, stage.

**What can go wrong.** London's throats (London Bridge, Clapham Junction, Waterloo) are dense:
clustering at 15 m chains a ten-track approach into one corridor, and many short pieces and
junctions come out; London is likely half the work. OSM per-track ways make the router's choice
of track arbitrary (it may run a local on the express track for a block), which step 3 overrides.
Junctions right at platform ends (NYC's 96 St, 149 St) collide with the 20 m clearance where two
stations are close. Short pieces between level changes may not fit their ramps.

**How to check.** Per city: pieces, pairs, junctions, station nodes and track km against today's
(expect track km near the corridor km above); the named NYC trunks on the right pairs (a printed
table: piece, pair, lines); no red tag on the NYC subway at high demand; a screenshot of
Lexington Av at zoom 15 and of London Bridge; load time against T-1303's 11 s New York and 28 s
London in the same headless import (fewer edges and platforms, so expect it no slower); smoke.

## Stage 1 built (t1305s1, 2026-10-29)

`pipeline/realcorridors.py <city> [--out DIR]` (or `realnet.py corridors <city>`, output to

    set REALNET_OUT=C:\Users\anita\projects\maps\artifacts\trainworld\t1305-shared
    ..\..\venv\Scripts\python.exe pipeline\realnet.py geom nyc
    ..\..\venv\Scripts\python.exe pipeline\realcorridors.py nyc --out %REALNET_OUT%

How: every hop routed as `route` does (same acceptance); chains sampled every 10 m; in order of how
many lines use them, each chain is laid on an earlier one where it runs within 15 m, within 25
degrees, of the same kind (main-line rail together, else per operator), **both in tunnel or both
not** (keeps the 7 el apart from the Queens Blvd subway under it) and **joined by track within 2 km**
(so systems that never meet, IRT and IND say, stay apart where they run side by side); runs under
30 m are dropped as crossovers. Cut points within 40 m are one, stations anchor them; one station's
stops by different lines within 60 m are one node. OSM's track count per piece is the median count
of parallel chains (same kind and tunnel flag) within 15 m every 50 m. A second pair where a line
passes a station another stops at, or one pair would be over 80% loaded, and OSM shows 3 or more
tracks; a one-pair piece whose lines all run on two-pair pieces either side gets two as well. Lines
passing a station go on the express pair; pieces with none take each line's pair from its
neighbours; patterns of one route that stop alike stay together. Loads use docs 6.2 with each
line's top speed from its stop-to-stop distance under the game's traction (curves left out, so an
upper bound on speed).

## T-1774: London (t1774, 2026-10-29)

Changes to the general build (all cities):
- **Lines off other classes' track** (`realcorridors.route_hops`). Classes: main-line rail, else
  kind and operator (each tube line, each sub-surface line, the DLR). After a first routing, each
  trunk (chains parallel within 15 m, same family and tunnel flag, joined by track within 2 km:
  `track_groups`) belongs to the class with most trains on it; each hop is routed again with other
  classes' trunks at 3 times their length. The Bakerloo leaves the Northern's tunnel at Waterloo
  and the sub-surface tracks at Baker Street, the Piccadilly the District's at South Kensington and
  the Elizabeth line's at Heathrow; where OSM has only the shared track, real sharing stays (District
  and Circle, Piccadilly with the Metropolitan to Uxbridge and the District at Ealing Common, the
  Bakerloo and Overground north of Queen's Park, the Metropolitan and Chiltern). Corridor keys are
  per kind now, not per operator, so the sub-surface lines share where their tracks are one pair.
  A commuter line takes the rail family where most stops have rail in reach (the nearest track put
  the Heathrow Express on the Circle and the Piccadilly).
- **Full pieces routed around** (`build(avoid=...)`): pieces over 0.9 with more than one route
  make a second build that routes lines off them where a parallel line runs more than 30 m away
  (the Brighton line's fast trains on the Quarry line, not through Redhill and Merstham).
- **Main-line pairs filled to 0.6** (`PACK_LOAD_RAIL`; metro stays 0.9), lines then spread
  evenly over the pairs, and a route's patterns split apart when together they exceed one pair

- **Platform tracks by need** (`realshared`): a station group gets as many station nodes side by

- Junctions past a platform go at 0.4 of the room to the next node (was 0.5: two facing junctions
  met at one point and RER A turned back at Nation).
