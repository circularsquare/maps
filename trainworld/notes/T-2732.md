# T-2732: when does a tunnel or viaduct pay off against at grade? Upkeep by terrain and street crossings

Agent payback, 2026-11-07. **A proposal: no game code changed.** Real-world figures and other games:
research/upkeep-by-terrain.md.

Anita's question (2026-11-07, clarified): "tunnels or viaducts are cheaper in certain circumstances to
build, like through mountains or in dense cities with lots of crossings. in these circumstances how many
days is it to pay off". Also: "we also might wanna just jack up road crossing costs a lot. to simulate
like demolishing buildings. maybe 50% to 100% higher." And: did the higher upkeep land? **Yes**: T-2619
landed on 2026-11-06 (commit 1c9b3c72). T-2620 (open line keeps the old rates, notes/T-2620.md) is still
waiting on her.

## The game's rules today (sim/src/track/params.rs, cost.rs)

| | Build, US$M a km of double track | Upkeep, a game day a km |
|---|---|---|
| At grade (0), flat | 37.5, plus US$6M a road crossed | 35k |
| At grade, low hills / high hills / mountains | 75 / 127.5 / 262.5, plus roads | 35k |
| Viaduct (+1), flat to mountains | 75 / 112.5 / 165 / 300 | 50k |
| Cut and cover (-1), flat to mountains | 75 / 112.5 / 165 / 300 | 100k |
| Bored (-2), anywhere | 120 | 130k |

- Terrain adds to the build price at levels -1 to +3 (+0.5, +1.2, +3.0 on the multiplier), never to bored
  tunnels, and never to upkeep. Grades do not slow trains and nothing is refused for steep ground, so a
  line at grade over a pass is as fast and as long as the tunnel under it if drawn the same way.
- Street crossings: US$6M a road crossed at level 0 (a motorway counts 3), at build only. No upkeep,
  no speed penalty, no wait (`roads.rs` only prices them).
- Single track (0.6x) and high speed (1.5x) scale build and upkeep alike, so **payback days do not
  depend on the track type**.
- Time: at 1x a game day is 24 real minutes (60 game seconds a real second, `game/clock.ts`), 6 at 4x,
  1.5 at 16x. Her North America game is at day 361.

Payback below = the build saving divided by the extra upkeep a day: the day the option cheaper to build
has paid away its saving and the other comes out ahead.

## 1. Mountains

**Is the tunnel cheaper to build there today? Yes, much.** At grade in the mountains class costs 262.5M a
km, a bored tunnel 120M, a viaduct 300M. So the question flips: the tunnel saves 142.5M a km up front
and costs 95k a day more, and **at grade comes out ahead after 1,500 game days** (4.1 game years; 600
hours of play at 1x, 37 at 16x). In high hills the saving is 7.5M and at grade is ahead after **79
days**. In low hills and on flat ground at grade is cheaper both to build and to keep. A viaduct is never
cheaper than at grade on any ground (the same surcharge on a dearer base).

Real routes, classes read from the game's own terrain files (`data/terrain`, every sample covered):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Chuo line (Sasago) | Otsuki-Kofu by the valley, 35 km | 7 / 3 / 2 / 24 | 7.0B | 1.23M | Otsuki-Kofu, 34 km | 4.1B | 4.48M | **888 days** |

Even the valley floors read mostly as mountains at the game's ~115 m cells. The cheapest build of all is
mixed (at grade on flat and low hills, bored through high hills and mountains): Gotthard 6.6B, Chuo 3.3B,
Moffat 2.9B, Kicking Horse 6.5B.

**Answer: at grade is not always better in the long run.** Through mountains the tunnel is cheaper to
build and at grade only catches up after roughly 900-1,700 game days, longer than games are played, so
the tunnel is the right call for any real game. In high hills it is a near tie that at grade wins
within about 80 days. Her NA save already does this: 548 km of its bored track is under mountains, and
only 26 km of at-grade track is on mountain ground.

## 2. Cities

Crossings a km come from street spacing: Manhattan along an avenue 12.5 (streets about 80 m apart),
crosstown 3.65 (avenues about 274 m), a European centre 7-10 (blocks 100-150 m); the game's own count
(notes/T-1929.md): Midtown 11.2, central London 12.7, Tokyo 13.3, Paris 7.4, suburbs 6-10, rural 0.8.
Sources: research/upkeep-by-terrain.md section 4.

**Is the viaduct cheaper to build there today?** Where there are more than 6.25 crossings a km (37.5M of
roads), yes: so along Manhattan avenues and in London or Tokyo, about even in Paris, no crosstown or in
the country. A bored tunnel (120M) is never cheaper than at grade in a city today; cut and cover costs
what a viaduct does to build and twice as much to keep, so it never beats a viaduct.

## 3. Options

### R. Her idea: road crossings cost 50-100% more to build (US$9M or 12M)

Standing in for knocking down buildings. The point where a viaduct is cheaper to build drops from 6.25
crossings a km to 4.2 (US$9M) or 3.1 (US$12M).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Manhattan avenue (12.5) | 150.0M | 5,000 days | **316** (bored now cheaper to build) | 187.5M | 7,500 | 711 |
| Midtown (11.2) | 138.3M | 4,220 | 193 | 171.9M | 6,460 | 546 |
| European 100 m (10) | 127.5M | 3,500 | 79 | 157.5M | 5,500 | 395 |
| European 125 m (8) | 109.5M | 2,300 | (bored dearer) | 133.5M | 3,900 | 142 |
| Paris (7.4) | 104.1M | 1,940 | (bored dearer) | 126.3M | 3,420 | 66 |
| Crosstown (3.65) | 70.3M | (at grade cheaper) | | 81.3M | 420 | |
| Suburb (6-10) | 91-128M | 1,100-3,500 | | 110-158M | 2,300-5,500 | |
| Rural (0.8) | 44.7M (+2.4M) | | | 47.1M (+4.8M) | | |

- Dense centres go clearly to viaducts, and along Manhattan avenues a bored tunnel becomes cheaper to
  build than at grade. At 12M even suburbs and crosstown streets favour a viaduct.
- **On her saves: nothing changes.** It is a build price, so constructed track keeps what it paid; upkeep
  and profit stay as they are (NYC has no at-grade track at all). Only the built-total figure moves when
  road tiles load. Only new building is affected.
- One rule already shown (the quote's "Road crossings" row), one constant (`ROAD_CROSSING_COST`).

### C. Upkeep per street crossing (US$1k or 2k a game day each, at grade only)

US$1k a game day is US$3.65k a year real, about what a US crossing with lights and gates costs to keep
(research/upkeep-by-terrain.md section 3).

- Cities, at today's 6M build: at 1k a crossing at grade on a Manhattan avenue costs 47.5k a day to keep
  against the viaduct's 50k, so the viaduct stays ahead for about 15,000 days; at 2k at grade costs more
  to keep than a viaduct where there are more than 7.5 crossings a km, and the viaduct is ahead forever.
  Crosstown and rural barely move (+3.7k and +0.8k a km).
- On her saves (crossings estimated: 8 a km on at-grade track within 5 km of a station, 0.8 farther; no
  road tiles offline):

| Save | Upkeep now (T-2619) | +C at 1k | +C at 2k | Profit now | at 1k | at 2k |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NYC real save | 659.4M a day | +0 | +0 | 1,751M | 1,751 | 1,751 |
| NA day 361 | 990.5M | +45.1 | +90.2 | 5,453M | 5,407 (-0.8%) | 5,362 (-1.7%) |

  NA long lines gain 2-4M a day each at 1k (CALIFORNIA +4.2 on 254, Crescent +3.1, Chicagoo +2.6).
- It adds a second rule for the same thing, and its effect is mostly far in the long run, which players
  rarely reach. A hover would need to say it.

### T. A terrain factor on upkeep (surface track only, levels -1 to +3)

Tried at x1.1 / 1.25 / 1.5 (gentle) and x1.25 / 1.5 / 2.0 (strong) for low hills, high hills, mountains.

- Payback: at grade in mountains comes out ahead after 1,839 (gentle) or 2,375 (strong) days instead of
  1,500; routes 1,030-2,185 (gentle). It pushes the answer further the way it already goes.
- Saves: NYC +6.7 / +14.9M a day (-0.4% / -0.9% profit); NA +14.7 / +33.9M (-0.3% / -0.6%), CALIFORNIA
  +1.9 / +4.5 on 254.
- Real evidence names the causes (curve wear, snow, rockfall, drainage) but gives no number, and the
  regional spreads found follow traffic and cities, not hills. The real split is mostly tunnel or not
  (the Gotthard Base Tunnel costs 2-3x open line; the game's bored rate is 3.7x its ground).

## Recommendation

1. **Mountains: no change.** The tunnel already wins on build price and keeps its lead for 900-1,700
   days on real passes; a terrain upkeep factor would only stretch that, is not backed by a figure, and
   would make long mountain lines harder, against T-2620's aim.
2. **Cities: take R at US$9M a crossing (+50%).** Manhattan avenues, London, Tokyo and 100-125 m European
   grids then favour a viaduct for 2,300-5,000 days, and a bored tunnel along a Manhattan avenue pays
   off against at grade in about 300 days; crosstown and rural track stay cheapest at grade. Her saves
   do not move. US$12M also pushes suburbs onto viaducts, which seems too far for commuter rail, where
   real lines mostly run at grade.
3. **C (crossing upkeep): hold.** Realistic sizes cost her NA game 0.8-1.7% of profit and change mostly
   the very long run; R does the same job more visibly with no new rule. Worth it only if she wants at
   grade in a dense centre to cost more to keep as well as to build.

If R is taken: `ROAD_CROSSING_COST` 6 -> 9 in params.rs, the T-1929 doc lines and the 100-115M figure in
docs/track-and-service.md 6.4 "Road crossings" (dense centre then about US$135-155M a km), tests in
`roads::tests` and `checks/old/t1929-roads.mjs`. No player-facing string changes (the quote row shows
the price).

## Method

Offline, one core, no browser: each save opened in the track model's WASM in Node, constructed edges by
level range (an edge pays its dearest structure), track type and high speed, each sampled every 25 m and
classed from `data/terrain/*.terrain`; stations by level; lines' upkeep shares split by trains a weekday
as `line_upkeep` does. Checks: T-2619 upkeep matches `TrackApi.upkeep()` (NYC 659.4, NA 989.9 vs 990.5
summed); the old rates give 237 and 505, matching NYC's T-2620 figure and the NA save's ledger (21.04M an
hour). NA fares and running are the save's last 24 whole hours (9,625M and 3,182M); NYC's are T-2620's
measured 3,719M and 1,309M. Route cases sample straight legs between hand-placed waypoints, so a drawn
line hugging the valley floor would be somewhat cheaper at grade. Scripts (scratch, not kept):
`extract.mjs`, `routes.mjs`, `analysis.py`.
