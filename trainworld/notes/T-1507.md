# T-1507: a China test save with real high-speed service (2026-10-27)

From T-1504's cause 2 and 3 (notes/T-1504.md). The new save is `t1507-all-china-hsr.save` at the maps
root, beside `t1156-all-china.save`, which is untouched (t1373-rush-days and t1205-memory load it).
Made through the game's own edits in headless Chrome: `notes/T-1507/probe.mjs` loads the old save and
runs `notes/T-1507/edit.js` in the page, then exports. The edit log is
`../artifacts/trainworld/T-1507/edit.out.json`.

## What the old save had wrong

- **The track was high speed already** (all 137 pieces of the ten lines flagged, curves good for
  310-320 km/h). T-1504's "every stretch's limit 160" was the trains: every line ran ordinary trains,
  which top out at 160 on any track. No track was rebuilt.
- **Nine lines started one station short of their first city** (114 to 122, and 120 at its Shenzhen
  end too): the builder's track ran on to a loose end on top of another line's station, where its own
  station was refused, so the line began at the next station, 34-57 km out. Line 114's "43 km
  outside Beijing" is this. Line 116 ran through Wuhan with no stop for the same reason.
- No high-speed stop was linked to the metro, and most big-city stops were 1-2.7 km from a metro
  station.

## What was edited

1. On each of the ten loose stubs, a station (400 m platform) where the track comes nearest a metro
   station, at least 600 m back from the loose end; the rest of the stub removed; the station made
   the line's new end. Lines 114 (Beijing), 115 (Shanghai), 116 (Nanjing), 117 (Zhengzhou), 118
   (Beijing), 119 (Guangzhou), 120 (Hangzhou and Shenzhen), 121 (Jinan), 122 (Shenyang) now end
   11-531 m from a metro station. Line 114 now starts in Beijing, 13.6 km down its own track from the
   old loose end, 11 m from a metro station.
2. Line 116 got a Wuhan stop on its own track, 492 m from a metro station.
3. Thirteen stops more than 1 km from the metro moved along their line's track (2.3-11.4 km) to the
   point nearest a metro station, now 15-395 m away; the old stations removed. Tianjin, Nanjing and
   Suzhou on 113; Zhengzhou, Wuhan, Guangzhou, Dongguan and Shenzhen on 114; Hangzhou on 115; Hefei
   and Chongqing on 116; Xiamen on 120; Qingdao on 121.
4. 31 passages, each high-speed stop to its nearest metro station within 1 km (11-970 m). Every big-city
   stop is now linked except Quanzhou on 120 (11 km from the metro, nothing nearer along the track).
   Stations of two high-speed lines in one city ended up more than 1 km apart, so they are reached
   through the metro, not a passage.
5. High-speed trains on all ten lines, and headways by demand level (high / medium / low): 10 / 15 / 30
   minutes on 113 and 114, 15 / 20 / 40 on 115, 116 and 120, 20 / 30 / 60 on the rest. Cars stay at 20
   (400 m, the most the platforms hold). Trains at high demand: 113 57, 114 95, 116 45, 120 36, 118
   27, others 8-12.

Line times one way: Beijing - Shanghai (113) 4 h 40 (was 8 h); Beijing - Shenzhen (114) 7 h 53;
Nanjing - Chengdu (116) 5 h 36; Shanghai - Ningbo (115) 71 min.

Reading it:
- **Beijing - Shanghai** is now flown more than driven: air carries 9,313 of the 10,591 trips, the car
  almost none (on the old save air took 10,583; the car's 100% in T-1504 predates the plane changes
  since). Rail's perceived cost fell from 1,503 to 1,079 minutes; the car's is 1,549. Rail of rail +
  car + air is the 12.1% above.
- **Guangzhou - Shenzhen** is served now (81%) but rail stays under 1%: rail costs 670 perceived
  minutes against the car's 143. The busiest period's best route still rides metro lines 70 and 67;
  79 km with metro legs at both ends and waits cannot match a 143-minute drive. This is T-1504's
  car money question more than the save.
- **Wuhan - Changsha** rail beats the car (419 against 438 perceived), 47.9% rail.
- Longer boardings rise x29, about 60% of them on metro lines (the legs to and from the high-speed
  stations).
