# T-2364 and T-2365: the train share by km beside by trips, and the split by trip length (2026-11-04)

Design only; no app or sim code changed. Agent splitkm.

## Decision summary

- **Measured:** weighting by km lifts the train share where the player has long lines, and leaves it
  where the lines are short. Real New York network 17.8% of trips, 26.5% of km; real London 21.4%,
  37.6%; a fresh game with three short lines in Manhattan, Queens and Brooklyn 1.9%, 2.1%. Japan was not measured (below).
  Walking drops from about 30% of trips to 5-7% of km; driving rises too (New York 54% -> 68%), since
  its trips are as long as the train's. So km is not an easy path to 50%: it trades walking's
  short trips for driving's long ones.
- **Recommendation (Anita's call of 2026-11-04 taken as given):** the bar, its number, the goal ticks,
  the 10% star, the guide's 2% step, achievements and the real-network marker all stay on the share of
  trips. The share of km is a second figure: a sentence in the top bar's hover and a new row in the
  city view, "Train share of km". The star stays on trips because a km star would favour one long
  commuter line over a dense metro, and early in a game the two figures are the same anyway.
- **Alternatives weighed:** rail of rail + car (New York 24.8% of trips, 28.1% of km; London 30.5%,
  40.5%; the ceiling tests' measure, and by km Eurostat's form) and the train share of trips over
  2 km (22.2%, 26.6%). Each leaves a group of trips out, which the bar would then have to explain;
  the km share keeps every trip, is the second figure England's travel survey prints, and matches
  points, which already pay a passenger-km (T-684). Not recommended.
- **What answers "50% feels unattainable"** is T-2365's plot: per band, the short rows are walking's
  (under 2 km, 72-87% walk in every save) and the long rows show what the network reaches (New York's
  real network 39% at 10-20 km, London's 44-48% from 10 km up).
- **T-2365:** a "By trip length" section in the city view, under the Within bar: six rows of local
  bands and one "Other cities" row, each a full-width mode bar with trips/day at the right.
  Intercity joins as its own row, not inside the km figure: planes would take 58-70% of a big
  city's km (New York 70%, London 58%) and swamp everything else.
- Mockups: `artifacts/trainworld/t2364/mock-nyc-real.png` (also `mock-london-real.png`,
  `mock-nyc-new-game.png`; the .html beside each).

## How it was measured

Scripts and dumps: `artifacts/trainworld/t2364/scripts/` (`measure.mjs`, `tables.py`, `merged.py`,
`mock2.py` + `mock_page.py`, `m_*.json`). My own Vite on 6189, headless
Chrome, one demand worker (two for Japan), each save imported into a `?new=1&opened=all` game and
waited to the strict final; the average day.

- **Local trips by distance:** the page's own `flows("home", cells)` query (what the bubbles draw),
  asked for each group of home cells: every 1 km quarter of a 2 km gravity zone for real New York
  (21,194 calls, 41 minutes), each whole 2 km zone for the others (6,388 calls for New York, 13
  minutes). Each answer gives every far cell's trips by mode, all purposes; distance is from the
  group's trip-weighted centre to the far cell. The far ends sum to the zone's own split exactly
  (New York 68.24M flows against 68.23M in `cellModes`, train 17.8% both).
- **Error:** distances inside one group are blurred by up to about 1 km (2 km for whole zones):
  the under 1 km band holds 4.8M trips grouped by 1 km quarters and 6.2M by 2 km zones, while from
  1 km up the two groupings agree within 5% per band. The under 1 km band holds under 1% of the km,
  so the km shares hardly depend on it.
- **Other cities:** the average day's market corridors with the zone's place at either end, rail,
  plane and car per corridor, distance between the places' zone centres (registry `center`).
- km are straight-line for every mode, so the shares compare like with like; the train's own track
  km (what points count, T-684) run longer than this.

## Looking outward

- Transport statistics use both. Eurostat's modal split is passenger-km only, and leaves walking
  out entirely (cars, buses and trains; [glossary](https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/statistics-explained/index.php/Glossary:Modal_split_of_passenger_transport)).
  England's National Travel Survey gives both side by side: in 2025 walking was 29% of trips and 4%
  of distance, surface rail 2% of trips and 10% of distance, car 58% and 76%
  ([NTS 2025 factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/national-travel-survey-2025/nts-2025-factsheet),
  table NTS0303). The game's New York and London show the same shape: walking falls by about 5x, rail
  rises, driving rises.
- City-building games count trips. Cities: Skylines' transport view splits citizens' trips into
  public transport, walking and cars (players reverse-engineer it from ridership over population,
  [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/255710/discussions/0/1693843461185581447)). Subway
  Builder colours population bubbles by transit or car use and shows no mode-share figure in the base
  game; a community mod adds one ([mod notes](https://ejfox.com/blog/projects/subway-builder-mods)).
  Transport Fever 2 reports destinations reachable by each mode as a percentage, not a split
  ([dev comment on Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1066780/discussions/0/603018544972607669)).
  Nobody I found weights a player-facing share by distance; a second figure beside a trip share is the
  statistics offices' practice, not a game's.

## T-2365's plot, in detail

- **Where:** the city view (opened by a row of the Cities tab), straight under the Within bar and
  its goal ticks, before To other places. The Cities tab's list stays one bar per row; seven bars a
  city would not fit its 24 px rows.
- **Rows:** the six local bands above (under 1 km to over 20 km; the 20-50 and over 50 km bands
  folded, each about 3-11% of trips) and Other cities. Each row is the mode bar the panels already
  draw (train, walking, driving, and for Other cities the plane in the muted colour as the bubbles'
  mode mix has it), full width, so each row reads as its own split; the trips a day at the right say
  how much each row weighs. No key: the colours are the top bar's.
- **Hover:** one per row (4 above). Plane appears only in the Other cities row.
- **Building it** needs the trips by band from the solve. The page's per-selection queries took 13
  to 41 minutes per city here, so T-2365 needs the demand kernel to add trips into seven bands by
  mode as it already adds rail km for the fare sensitivity (`FareSense.km`): commutes per subzone
  pair, everyday trips per sample, walking by its band. That is a change to the WASM interface, so
  it goes in its own worktree. The km figure of row 2 comes from the same bands.
