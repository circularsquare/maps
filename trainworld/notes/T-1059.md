# T-1059: no train that walks further than walking there

Anita (2026-10-23, T-1056): "maybe should disallow trips where like the total walking distance if
you take the train is longer than the total walking distance if you walk directly." Measured in
T-1056 (notes/T-1055.md "T-1056"), then built here on her yes.

## The rule

A station pair (a, t) is no rail option for a trip when the walk to a, the walks between stations
and platforms on the way from a to t, and the walk from t add up to more than the trip's own
walking distance. All straight metres: an access entry's mean distance (`Subzones::acc_d`; none
for a drive to the station), the strategy's expected walks, and the trip's distance (commutes:
the subzones' centres; everyday: the pair's mean distance), times the pack's walk factor round
water. The refused pairs leave the logsum, so the train costs more for that trip; a trip with no
pair left has no train (the trip explorer says "Walking there is shorter than the walks to,
between and from the stations."). `Params::walk_rule` (1; 0 turns it off). Long trips between
cities do not apply it.

## Where it lives

- `kernel::walk_column`, called per searched destination in `skims_extend`: from the strategy (in
  settle order) each node's expected straight metres walked on the way: a walk edge to another
  station from the platform left (its cost turned back into metres), and at a change the walk
  from where the rider stands to the first train's platform, by frequency; a boarding rides a stop
  first (T-1055). Kept as `Skims::walk` (u16 metres, S x S) and `Skims::walk_max` (per station, the
  most to any destination).
- `kernel::mode_choice` (commutes' lanes): per origin `mw`, the most its trips walk before their
  last station (access walk plus `walk_max`); a lane is looked at only when its distance is under
  `mw` plus the destination's longest egress walk (1.5% of lanes in New York, 1.8% in Tokyo).
  There each station pair is passed first on the same bound and only the rest read the tables;
  the refused weight scales the lane's logsum; the lane loads as usual and the refused pairs come
  off again, or where more than half is refused, the kept pairs load alone (taking 99.99% off a
  spread lost 0.1% of rail to f32 cancellation in the first build).
- `everyday.rs` pass: the same test inside its access x egress loops, with per-subzone and
  per-entry bounds so most pairs never read the table.
- `trip::pair_choice` (`walk_kept`) and `trip::everyday_choice`: the same, for the trip explorer;
  `WALK_FURTHER` (5) is the new reason for no train (`app/src/game/trip.ts` "walkFurther",
  `TripInspector.tsx`).

Tests: `the_walking_rule_reads_the_walks_between_stations` (walk table across a 200 m walk, a 1 km
complex and a 50 m change between platforms; `walk_ok`, `walk_kept`),
`the_walking_rule_keeps_the_loads_whole` (synthetic city: rail falls but stays above 80%, and the
station matrix, both access ends, the distance bands and the work subzones add up to rail within
1e-4 with the rule on and off); `pair_choice_reproduces_mode_choice` and
`everyday_choice_reproduces_the_pass` run with the rule on.

## Effect

Native `calib` (three crowding rounds, commutes and everyday), master 135348f against the tree:

| | Rail a day | Rail of rail + car | Walking of all trips |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York (`nyc_real`) | 10.985M to 10.708M (-2.5%) | 21.16 to 20.71% | 24.98 to 25.28% |
| Tokyo (`tyo_real`) | 15.717M to 15.167M (-3.5%) | 31.65 to 30.78% | 29.32 to 29.86% |
| London (`lon_real`) | 8.793M to 8.560M (-2.6%) | 28.70 to 28.08% | 28.69 to 29.04% |

The measurement in T-1056 had New York 10.694M and Tokyo 15.149M: the build lands within 0.1%
(it applies the walk factor round water to the trip's distance, which the measurement did not).
Station entries that fall most: New York Woodhaven Blvd 43.1k to 40.5k, East 105 St 17.4k to 15.0k,
169 St 19.5k to 17.2k, Parkchester 30.1k to 27.9k; Tokyo Kasai 35.3k to 32.8k, Chigasaki 17.8k to
15.4k, Shiki 20.2k to 17.9k, Nishi-Kawaguchi 38.4k to 36.1k. Lines lose 1-5% (Tobu Tojo 5%).

In the game (my Vite on the tree, smoke): the real save 11,645,298 to 11,359,932 rail trips a day
(-2.45%); New York's train / walk / drive of all trips 16.82 / 24.87 / 58.31% to 16.41 / 25.18 /
58.42%. Smoke's far new game: its three-station Tokyo line, stations 2 km apart, 133,977 to
115,607 riders (-14%): a short line carries many hops between neighbours that walk further than
the trip. All 15 smoke checks pass.

Ceilings (T-1056's harness, `notes/T-1056/t1056_ceiling.rs`: no train where the two 3 minute walks
are longer than walking the trip; the model is otherwise unchanged since): A New York 91.9,
London 91.8, Paris 92.7, Tokyo 93.2, Houston 86.4 (target 85); B 81.5, 82.1, 83.8, 84.3, 71.4
(target 50).

## Speed

`calib` solve seconds on a shared machine (other agents running; runs of one setting vary by up to
6 s):

| | master | tree, rule off | tree, rule on |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York | 26.5-37.2 (6 runs) | 31.0-35.6 | 34.6-36.3 (4 runs) |
| Tokyo | 58.2-60.4 | 62.3-65.0 | 66.4-76.9 |

Timed by stage on one run each (rule off against on): New York's mode choice 20.4 to 22.0 s,
everyday pairs 3.0 to 3.2 s, the walk table 1.2 s beside the search's 6.3 s; Tokyo's mode choice
39.2 to 44.9 s, everyday 6.8 to 7.5 s, the walk table 1.7 s beside 9.5 s. So the rule costs about
3 s of New York's 33 and 7 s of Tokyo's 63 (about 10%), most of it in the commutes' 1-2% of lanes
that look at their pairs. The walk table is about 2 bytes per station pair beside `r`'s 4.
