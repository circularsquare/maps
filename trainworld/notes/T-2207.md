# T-2207: islands with no car access, a design

Anita, 2026-11-03, verbatim: "irl a lot of ferry demand come sforom the fact that there is no way to drive to islands
withouta bridge. perhaps we should do this too, somehow. if we can't do direct car routes everywhere cuz its expensive,
could we at least track like connected components? and recognize that some islands are unreachable by car and should
not have any car trips ro walk trips off the island. (they can appear as a new type of trip, i guess, grey demand dot
for unreachable.) then a ferry (or a train) can bridge the gap and provide the only way."

Scripts: the scratchpad's `t2207/parts.py`, `parts2.py`, `rows2.py`, `drive_check.py` (one-off, not kept). Outputs:
`artifacts/trainworld/t2207/` (`<pack>.parts.json`, `<pack>.islands.json`, `parts.log`, `islands.log`).

## The short version

- **Parts.** Every pack cell gets a *part*: the land it stands on, joined across water only where a drivable OSM way
  crosses (bridges, tunnels, causeways). Ferries do not join parts. One step in `pipeline/routes.py`, from the water
  mask the pack already has and the roads routes.py already reads.
- **The rule.** A trip between two parts has no car and no walk. The player's network (rail, bus, ferry) is its only
  way. Where the network offers it, the trip rides; where not, the trip is *unreachable* and shown grey ("No route").
  No new demand factor: a mode is unavailable, and the one bound reuses the ratio curve's own cut-off.
- **Size.** In 12 built packs, 362,000 people live on islands no road reaches (Sado, Shodoshima, Salamis, Aegina,
  Ischia, Vashon, the Lido, Cheung Chau, Lamma, Bornholm...), with about 139,000 commuters a day across. New York and
  Tokyo barely change; Hong Kong 1.4% of its commuters, Naples 1.6%, Athens 1.5%, Kagawa 1.5%, Venice 4%. The market
  has 1.45M longer trips a day (1.1% of those under 250 km) and 631,000 commutes between places on two landmasses,
  counted today but never solved, so no ferry or train can win them; stage 4 opens them.
- **Stages** T-2213 (parts in the packs, waits for the India run), T-2214 (the rule in local demand, own worktree),
  T-2215 (the grey on the map and panels), T-2216 (the market).
- **Anita decides**: car ferries ignored (my pick) or counted as slow roads; the 3-4x bound; the grey and its words.

## 1. What the game does today across water

**Local trips (cells, 2 km gravity zones).** Every trip has three modes, walk, car and rail (demand-mode.md 4.3).
- *Drives* use `routes.py`'s routed factor: Dijkstra over OSM roads (no ferries) from each zone. Where no road joins
  two zones the factor is wrong in one of three ways, none of them "no car":
  - near pairs (within 6 squares): the search stops at 50 km (`NEAR_LIMIT_M`), so an unreachable zone gets a 50 km
    road. Salamis to Perama, 5.5 km apart: a 39.7 km road (factor 7.3).
  - far pairs (supercells): an unreachable supercell pair has no finite road, `den` is 0, and `ff` falls back to
    `ROAD_DETOUR` 1.3, the straight line. Sado (Ryotsu) to Niigata: 59 km straight, a **77 km road straight across the
    sea**, factor 1.30. Salamis to Piraeus: 1.30. (`unreach` is computed in `drive_tables` and never used.)
  - small islands (road pieces under 200 junctions, `MIN_COMPONENT`): cells skip their own roads and snap to the
    nearest big component, across water if nothing nearer ("else the nearest" in `RoadGraph.snap`). Cheung Chau to
    Chi Ma Wan on Lantau, 4.6 km: a 6.8 km road over the water.
- *Walks* cross water at `WATER_COST` 3 land steps a pixel within the 2-square window (it stands for "the bus through
  the tunnel, the bridge just out of reach, the ferry, the bike"), and beyond the window go straight (factor 1). So a
  1 km strait walks as about 3 km, and Salamis or Cheung Chau keep a walking share to the mainland.
- *Access* to stations: walks over the same raster, and rides and drives to stations, never check that the station is
  on the same land.

**Longer trips (the market, regional.md 5.1).** A car only within a landmass (T-375's world field: Natural Earth 10m
land plus Wikidata fixed links, dominant at H3 r5), with T-279's routed drives, the open-water ferry inside one
landmass's path, and the Channel and Tsugaru crossings. Between places on two landmasses there is no car (T-1396), and
pairs are built for the solve only "with a car or a plane to compete with". Near places on two landmasses (under
250 km, or no airports) are *fixed*: counted in the totals, shown, never solved. Measured from market-rows.json (each
place's `near` and `commute` lists, its landmass in `road`): 1,448,000 of 135.0M longer trips a day (1.07%) and 631,000
of 161.5M commutes. The biggest: Singapore - Batam 71,900 a day, Vancouver - Saanich (Victoria) 52,900, Tenerife -
Gran Canaria 26,500, Haikou - Leizhou 19,200, Bohol - Cebu 13,800, Hawaii - Honolulu 13,200, Mallorca - Barcelona 11,300.
A player's ferry to Victoria today earns nothing from these.

A market zone's landmass is its place's, so Sado's 6 km zones count as Honshu and drive to Tokyo.

## 2. Finding the parts

**What the packs already have.**
- The 25 m water mask (`water_row`, `water_x`; OSM coastline polygons plus inland water, or TIGER in the US).
- The 50 m walk raster (`walk_row`, `walk_x`): the mask with OSM bridges a person may walk on opened, islets under
  0.1 km² closed. Its land components are walking parts, not road parts: tunnels and motorway bridges carry no
  walkers, so it splits Hong Kong Island (1.18M people), Lantau, Ma Wan, Haneda and Tokyo Bay's landfill from the
  mainland. Measured: Hong Kong 50 walk parts, 1.30M people off the main one; with the real road links, 37,000.
- `drive_near` / `drive_far`: zone to zone factors. They cannot give parts: the 50 km floor and the 1.3 fallback are
  recognisable, but a zone astride a strait (Perama's) joins both banks, and the small-island snap makes Cheung Chau
  look joined. Tried in `parts.py`: it found Sado, Shodoshima, Aegina and the Lido but missed Salamis (zones astride
  the 1 km strait) and every Hong Kong island.
- `nocar` per cell (T-511, households with no car) is no help: it is about owning a car, not reaching a road.

**The options.**
1. *Road graph components* (the drivable OSM graph routes.py builds; `connected_components` is already called there
   for `MIN_COMPONENT`). Exact for roads, but every cell must be tied to a component, and the snap is where it breaks:
   car-free islands have a few service ways or none; Venice's centre has no road at all, though its land touches
   Piazzale Roma. It needs a snap rule per cell (nearest road on the same land), so it needs the land anyway.
2. *Land plus road crossings* (my pick). The walk raster's source (the water mask at 50 m, islets closed), with every
   pixel under a drivable OSM way set to land (bridges, tunnels, causeways and places where the mask and the road
   disagree, all in one rule), then 8-connected land components. A cell's part is its pixel's component (a cell
   centred on water takes the nearest land pixel's). Robust to the snap, cheap, and the same raster machinery as the
   walk grid. What it calls joined: any land a road reaches, plus land touching it, so Venice's centre is joined (you
   can drive to Piazzale Roma and walk on), and Fire Island's car-free villages are joined to its causeway ends.
3. *World field landmasses* (Natural Earth 10m and Wikidata links, as the market has). Too coarse inside a pack:
   decided at H3 r5 (about 250 km²), so Salamis (95 km²) folds into Attica, and a fixed link within a few km joins
   whatever it touches. Kept for what it does now: the market's place-level landmass.

**Car ferries.** The choice is whether an OSM `route=ferry` way carrying cars counts as a road.
- *Ignored* (my pick, Anita's framing): the player's ferry is "the only way". Sado, Salamis, Vashon and Bornholm become
  unreachable until the player builds a link, and the player's line stands for the real island ferry. One rule
  everywhere, no ferry data to clean (OSM ferry tagging of `motor_vehicle`, `duration` and seasonal service is
  uneven; OSRM needs a `duration` tag or guesses a crawl). The lie: in Sweden and Norway free road ferries
  (vägfärjor, ferjer on national roads) are part of the road network, so Stockholm's archipelago (9,500 people on
  11 islands measured, most with road ferries) and some west-Norway islands lose their car.
- *Counted as slow roads* (option B): islands with a car ferry keep a car at the crossing's minutes and fare, so only
  car-free islands are unreachable. Measured: of the 362,000, about 47,000 stay unreachable (Hong Kong's 37,000,
  Hydra, Murano, Sant'Erasmo, a few Stockholm boat-only islands). The ferry effect Anita wants mostly disappears,
  and it needs a ferry layer of times and fares in the parts step and in the drive tables.

**Global identity** (so two cells in different packs, or two market zones, compare). A pack's part table lists each
part's people and jobs, whether it is *open* (its land reaches the raster's edge, so it may continue into a
neighbouring pack) and its world landmass (the world field's, at its most populous cell). Two cells in one pack are
joined when their part is the same. Across packs: both parts open and on the same world landmass. An interior part
(Sado in Niigata's pack, Bornholm in Copenhagen's, Mallorca's whole pack) joins nothing outside its pack.

**Cost.** Most `ways2.npz` caches have been deleted after their routes runs (data/raw/osm keeps 9), so the step
reads each pack's Geofabrik extracts again: the downloads of a routes rerun, without its drive tables (the slow part:
375 s for New York's). Reading ways is pyogrio, 1-3 minutes per 500 MB extract; the raster and labelling a few
seconds a pack. Worldwide: the downloads (tens of GB) plus a few hours on 2-3 cores. It writes `part` (u16 a cell)
and a `parts` header block into each pack in place, as routes.py does, so it waits for any run that owns data/packs
(the India run now) and backs up to `data/work/pre-T2213/`. Afterwards it rides along every routes rerun. General
sources only: OSM roads and the pack's own water mask, worldwide.

## 3. The demand rule

**Between two parts there is no car and no walk.** That covers walking the whole way, the car, and getting to and from
stations: a walk, a ride (bus) or a drive to a station only where the station's cell is in the trip end's part. A
ferry port sits on its own shore, so its part is that shore's. Buses cannot cross either (T-2178 refuses a straight
bus leg over water). Inside one part everything is as today, WATER_COST included.

**What those trips do.** The player's network is the only mode:
- *Offered* (the existing gates: access within 80 minutes at both ends, a train within the riders' wait tolerance, at
  least one train ridden): the trip rides. Not compared with walking ("no train slower than walking" has no walk to
  compare with).
- *One bound*: with nothing to compare, a pointless 5-hour detour would carry everyone. So rail's share fades by the
  ratio curve's own cut-off against a car on a bridge that is not there (the rule's drive, straight x 1.3 with the
  local speeds and parking, which is what the model drives today on a missing road): all of them up to 3 times its
  cost, none from 4 times, the curve's taper between. No new constant: the 3 and 4 are the curve's.
- *Over the trains' room*: today the overflow goes by car or on foot (T-1070, T-1255's 30-minute queue). Here it has
  neither, so it stays home.
- *Not offered*: **unreachable**, a fourth outcome beside train, car and walk. Not a mode with a cost: just the trips
  no way serves.

**Counted in the totals.** These trips exist today, counted as car and walk; they keep their place in every total,
and every train share is rail over all trips. So the grey is the player's to win, and connecting an island raises the
city's train share.

**When the player bridges the gap.** A ferry line, a rail line over a new bridge or tunnel, or a bus over a bridge
the roads already have (that pair was never apart). The rail line's own bridge does not join the parts: the parts are
the roads', and the player's track is the network. So a rail bridge to Salamis makes Salamis's trips rail-only, like a
ferry.

**Is it a new factor?** No. It is a mode being unavailable, which every logit model has (Biogeme's availability
conditions, MATSim's link modes: a car leg on a link that allows no car is not in the choice set). The bound reuses
the ratio curve. It needs a `demand-worldwide.md` entry (one rule, OSM roads and the water mask everywhere) and a
`demand-plain.md` line.

**Numbers.** Measured on the packs with no network (so every crossing trip is car or walk today, unreachable after):
commuters from each pack's own gravity (`zone_row x zone_col x decay`, split by each zone's homes and jobs in each
part); everyday outings a rough production-constrained field (errands and visits 80% halving at 0.7 km, 20% at 4 km;
medical 1.4 km; from the pack's `everyday.rate`); attraction visits those of the world file's places on each island,
a year / 365.

**Against real ferries (would a player see something wrong?).** Rough, since the gravity sends trips over water as if
it were land:
- Vashon: the model has about 19,600 trip legs a day across (commuters both ways and outings), Washington State Ferries'
  Fauntleroy - Vashon - Southworth route carried 2.26M riders in 2024, about 6,200 a day. About 3 times too many: the
  US commute curve is long (30 km, power 0.8) and does not know about the water. Not fixed here: a water penalty in
  the gravity would be a new factor. A player's Vashon ferry would run full, which reads as a busy island ferry.
- Hong Kong: about 114,000 legs a day to and from its outlying islands, the same order as the islands' ferries.
  Lamma has 28,000 estimated jobs (a tier-0 job count round its power station), giving 25,000 commuters in, which
  is too many; a jobs question for Hong Kong, not this design's.
- Sado: about 5,400 legs, the order of Sado Kisen's passengers.
- Salamis: about 46,000 legs. The Perama ferry runs every 15-20 minutes round the clock.

What changes with no network: these trips turn from red and green to grey. What changes with a ferry: they become
rail-only, so an island ferry fills far more than the same ferry beside a bridge (Anita's point). A Staten Island
ferry is unaffected (bridges exist; T-2195's ordinary competition).

## 4. What the player sees

- **The grey.** The homes, destinations and total bubbles of the focused zone and nearby zones, the zone overview's
  bubbles, a selection's far-end bubbles, and (after T-2216) the inter-city bubbles: a bubble is pulled towards a grey
  by its share of unreachable trips, under both colourings (3-color and 2-color). A Sado bubble with no network is
  plain grey; one half served by the player's ferry is half way between the ferry's colour and grey. The existing
  greys (the even three-way mix pulled to 160 or 190, planes muted grey under 3-color, unopened zones' light grey dots)

## 5. Cousins left out

- *Car-free places on joined land*: Venice's centre, Fire Island's villages, Zermatt. The car reaches the edge and the
  model's connector (straight x 1.3 at road speed) carries it on. The general fix would price the connector as a walk
  (a cell's distance to the nearest road at walking pace). Not asked for; a P3 if it shows.
- *Too many island trips* (the Vashon 3x above): the gravity's distance ignores water.
- *The phantom drives* in section 1 stay for trips inside a part (rare: the small-island snap only matters across
  water). T-2213 can fix the snap (nearest road on the same part) and the far fallback (`unreach` to the floor)
  at no extra cost, since it rereads the roads; T-2214 then never asks a cross-part drive.
- *T-1396* (a car between landmasses by ferry in the market): under "car ferries ignored" it is the opposite rule;
  close it in favour of T-2216, keeping its Canada `market_drives.py` rerun as its own line.

## References (looking outward)

- Transport Fever 2: a town grows with how many destinations its people can reach in reasonable time, so island towns
  need a link before they count ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1066780/discussions/0/1740012731992157798),
  [another](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1066780/discussions/0/5452154443649036128)); a station with no road link
  has no catchment there. Closest to Anita's idea: no link, no trips served.
- Cities: Skylines: a building with no road shows "not connected"; citizens with no path do not travel. Ferries are
  built by the player (T-2177's references).
- MATSim: each network link lists the modes allowed on it (car by default), and a car leg is only routed on car links
  ([MATSim wiki](https://matsim.atlassian.net/wiki/x/WxaBBg), [code examples](https://github.com/matsim-org/matsim-code-examples/wiki/pt)).
  Discrete choice tools carry the same idea as availability conditions (Biogeme's `av`).
- Routing engines count car ferries as roads: OSRM's car profile has ferries as a class one can exclude at query time,
  timed from the way's `duration` tag ([OSRM profiles](https://project-osrm.org/docs/v26.6.1/profiles),
  [OSM help](https://help.openstreetmap.org/questions/37708/can-i-change-a-ferry-route-from-unknown-to-a-trunk-road),
  [OSM wiki route=ferry](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/ES:Tag:route=ferry)). That is option B's world, and why OSM's
  ferry data needs `duration` to be usable.
- Real ferries: Washington State Ferries 19.1M riders in 2024; Fauntleroy - Vashon - Southworth 2.26M riders and
  1.35M vehicles ([WSDOT fact sheet](https://wsdot.wa.gov/sites/default/files/2025-02/WSF-FactSheet-February2025.pdf),
  [WSDOT news](https://wsdot.wa.gov/about/news/2025/back-board-2024-brought-half-million-more-state-ferry-riders)).
  Perama - Salamina runs round the clock every 15-30 minutes, 15 minutes across ([Ferryhopper](https://www.ferryhopper.com/en/blog/featured/how-get-salamis-by-ferry)).
  Hong Kong's six major outlying-island routes are "the main external transport link" for their residents (Transport
  Department papers to LegCo, e.g. [tpcb4-1203](https://www.legco.gov.hk/yr15-16/english/panels/tp/papers/tpcb4-1203-1-e.pdf));
  annual patronage by route was not found.
