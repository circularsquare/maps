- **A's rounds are not the cause.** 40 rounds leave the total where it was (21,875) and lengthen Omi-Shiotsu to Yamashina
  (547 to 616 pitches). A's solves all stop at the 400-iteration cap either way.
- **The long stretches lie between blocks, not in them.** The Kosei line (Omi-Shiotsu, Katata, Omi-Imazu, Makino,
  Nagahara), Tsuruga to Higashi-Maizuru, Obama and Tatsuno are all stations between blocks (`blocks.of` -1); only
  Yamashina (Kansai) and Shimoji (Nagoya) are in blocks. So the stage's placing and its box spacing set their length:
  Kansai's box ends about 220 pitches below Fukui's, and the Kosei line's 20 hops are drawn 547. Next look: the boxes'
  spacing (grown by a fifth plus a 16-pitch gap) between Kansai, Fukui and block 25, and whether the routing of a long
  between-blocks chain detours round a box.
- **Block 25 sprawls**: Iga-Ueno, Shigaraki, Minakuchi, Yokaichi and Hikone, towns chained within 15 km of each other
  (`MERGE_M`), 42 stations in a 315 x 376 pitch box next to Kansai's. A cap on a block's real extent, or merging by
  station density rather than a 15 km chain, may be worth trying.
- **Why B is slower**: B's solves stop converging with blocks (nearly all at 400 iterations, against falling to 200).
  A turn term at a station is scaled by its two hops' mean length, so where one hop is a hop between blocks held at its
  placed length (hundreds of pitches) the turn weighs far above everything else. Scaling those by the shorter hop
  brought B back to converging (9,675 iterations) but not its wall time on this loaded machine, and the stretches got
  slightly longer (22,216), so it was reverted. The whole layout is already faster with blocks (78 s against 93 s here),
  because A runs 10 rounds, not 40.
