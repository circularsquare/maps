# T-1799: interlined lines side by side on the map (design for Anita)

Anita's TODO, 2026-10-29: "show interlined lines better. next to each other. especially for line
traffic view". Research and design only; no code changed.

## What the player would see

- **Line view**: where two or three lines run on the same track, the stroke becomes a ribbon of
  bands touching each other, one per line in its colour, like a transit map. One casing goes round
  the whole ribbon. Where a line leaves, the others close up over a short stretch.
- **Bands keep their order**: a line stays on the same side of its neighbours along the whole shared
  stretch, on the side it leaves towards, so bands do not cross where nothing forces them to.
- **Traffic view**: each band is as wide as that line's riders, so a trunk's width is the sum of its
  lines and each line can be read off on its own.
- **Zoomed out** (lines under about 1.5 px, around zoom 10 and below) the ribbon folds back to one
  stroke, the top line's colour, as today. **Close in** (from about zoom 16.5, where double track
  opens into its two tracks) the bands slide together onto the real tracks, as today, since
  that is where the trains really are.
- Hover and click pick the band under the pointer; trains (pills, zoom 10-14) ride their own band.

## How it is drawn today

- ui-map.md "Lines and track": lines sharing track are drawn **over each other**, one order for all
  (`lineDrawOrder` in `map/network.ts`: no-train lines under, then the shorter route on top). A
  trunk shows only its top line's colour. There is no dash or alternation in the code (the brief
  thought there was); T-1305 left shared trunks "one colour until she has looked".
- Each line is already its own stroke segments along each edge it uses (`networkFromState`, a
  `StrokeBuilder` per line over the edge's `lpts`). So N lines on an edge are already N stroke runs
  on top of each other: side by side needs **a sideways shift per segment**, not new geometry.
- The stroke vertex shader (`STROKE_VS` in `map/rendererShaders.ts`) already shifts sideways along
  the segment's normal for the two tracks close in (`lane_off`, `u_lane_mode`). A band shift is one
  more term there: `off += nrm * shift_px`, where `shift_px` is the shift in line widths times
  `u_width.x`, so it follows zoom with no CPU work.
- Traffic widths are per run of a line's segments on one edge (`buildLayers` in
  `map/demandLayers.ts`, `widthFor`, 0.5 to 6.6 line widths), uploaded as `a_wmul`. Traffic shifts
  are made in the same loop.
- Segment ends are round caps (T-673). Offset segments with per-segment normals leave a small notch
  on the outside of a bend, 2 × shift × sin(half the turn). On fitted curves (many short segments,
  a few degrees each) the caps cover it; at sharp nodes far out bands may notch (stage 4).

## Research

- **Subway Builder** (the game Anita named): v0.9.0 (2025-11-21) added "an experimental setting to
  show interlined routes on the map side-by-side"; v1.7.0 (2026-08-30) made it the normal look,
  "routes that are parallel/interlined now show up parallel to each other", with a setting for "the
  thickness of visually interlined routes". So: bands side by side, the band thickness a setting.
  I found nothing on how they order the bands or handle their traffic view.
- **Mini Metro** draws lines on a shared segment as parallel bands (well known from play; I found
  no written source). **NIMBY Rails** and **Transport Fever 2**: nothing found on drawing shared
  lines apart; TF2 players filter lines by view or highlight one line instead.
- **OpenRailwayMap** does not draw route relations at all (its list, 2016). OSM's transport maps
  draw one stroke per way.
- **Research on transit maps** calls this metro-line crossing minimisation (MLCM): pick the order of
  lines on each edge so as few pairs cross as possible. The general problem is NP-hard (Fink and
  Pupyrev), but the common case is easy: for two lines on an edge, follow them forward until they
  part and put the one that turns left on the left (else look backwards). Nöllenburg gives this
  kind of algorithm in O(lines² × nodes). LOOM (Bast, Brosi, Storandt) does it optimally with an
  ILP on real GTFS networks (New York under a second) and also avoids needless "separations"
  (lines leaving and rejoining a bundle). The greedy pairwise rule is enough for a game; the ILP is
  not worth its weight in a browser.

## Design

1. **Order per edge** (CPU, on line edits, kept in the net cache like the strokes). For each edge
   with two or more lines, sort them with the pairwise rule above, in the edge's own a→b
   direction: follow both paths forward to the node where they part, compare the bearings of their
   next edges there (more to the left goes left); if they never part forward, do the same
   backwards; same route both ways, by line id. The same parting node decides every edge of a
   shared stretch, so the order holds along it. Lines that cross where routes force it cross once,
   at a node.
2. **Slots**: on an edge with k lines, slot i's shift is `i - (k-1)/2` line widths (Line view), or
   the running sum of the lower bands' widths minus half the total (Traffic view). A line drawn
   against the edge's direction (`p & 1`) gets the sign flipped, since the shader's normal follows
   the stroke's direction.
3. **Shift attribute**: a `vec2` per segment (shift at start and end), a tenth attribute in
   `StrokeBuilder`, `strokeCache`, `strokeLod` and `strokeGather`. Where a line's slot differs between
   two edges, the shift ramps over the edge's last and first 30 m (shorter edges: their middle),
   splitting a segment there if needed. `strokeLod` must not merge segments across a ramp.
4. **Shader**: `off += nrm * mix(shift.x, shift.y, along) * bandPx * collapse * (1 - u_lanes.z)`,
   `collapse` a smoothstep on the line's px width (folding under about 1.5 px), `u_lanes.z` the
   existing "stroke grows to the two tracks" factor, so bands hand over to the tracks close in. The
   casing pass needs nothing: all casings are drawn before any colour, so the ribbon gets one
   outline for free, and touching bands have no gap.
5. **Picking, hover, trains**: `pick.ts` measures distance to the shifted stroke (same shift times
   the line's px width); trains use `tripAt`'s existing `shiftM`, set to the band's shift in metres
   at the current zoom.
6. **Traffic marks**: "track at capacity" belongs to the track, so its red band goes round the
   whole ribbon (drawn for the outermost bands only); "riders wait for a train with room" belongs to
   a line, so only that band darkens, with its red dashes inside the band, not outside the casing.
7. **Two-pair trunks** (T-1305's real networks: a four-track trunk is two double tracks 8 m
   apart). At zoom 15, 8 m is about 2 px while each pair's ribbon is 13 px or more, so the two
   ribbons would lie over each other. They need **corridors**: parallel edges within about 20 m of
   each other along most of their length are one ribbon, the left pair's lines on the left, centred
   on the corridor's middle. Player-built double tracks side by side get the same.

**GPU on all of Japan** (T-1644: strokes about 0.4 ms of the 1.7-2.3 ms overlay at zoom 15). No new
segments, since shared lines are already drawn once each; one more attribute (8 bytes a segment)
in the gathered buffer and a few shader ops. Overdraw drops a little, as bands no longer stack.
Expect no measurable change; stage 1 measures it.

## Stages (one agent each)

1. **Line view bands on single edges** (M; Opus: ordering, shader and picking together). Design
   items 1-5. Done when: on the real save (`T-007-real-nyc.save`) at zooms 11, 13 and 15, every edge
   with two or more lines shows each line's colour as its own band with one outline round the ribbon;
   no two bands cross along a shared stretch except at a node where their routes force it (a check
   counts crossings per node against the pairwise rule's own count); no notch or spike a player
   would notice at curves in screenshots; below zoom 10 and from zoom 17 the map looks as today;
   hover and click on a band name that band's line; trains ride their band; smoke passes; Japan
   panning at zoom 15 within a point of T-1644's 23-27% GPU.
2. **Traffic view bands** (S-M; Sonnet, the pattern is set by stage 1). Shifts by riders in
   `buildLayers`, design item 6, the hover unchanged.
3. **Corridors** (M; Opus: a geometry call with quiet wrong answers). Design item 7, checked on New
   York's and Tokyo's shared real networks (staged in `artifacts/trainworld/t1305-shared/`).
4. **Joins, only if stage 1 shows notches** (S): mitred shifts at sharp nodes (each segment end
   carries the bisector, clamped at twice the shift).

Later, if wanted: close in, the pale band under the two tracks striped by line.

## For Anita (my recommendation each)

- **Band width in Line view**: each band a full line width (a three-line trunk is three lines wide,
  like a transit map), or narrower so trunks stay slim. I'd make each band 80% of a line width:
  ribbons read as one track without getting heavy. No setting (Subway Builder has one).
- **When ribbons fold back zoomed out**: I'd fold when a line is under about 1.5 px (around zoom
  10), to today's single stroke in the top line's colour.
- **Most bands before folding**: I'd have no cap; more than five lines on one pair is rare, and a
  wide ribbon there is true.
- **Traffic view, width or colour**: width, each band its line's own riders width as today, so a
  line looks the same on shared and own track and a trunk's width is its total. The cost: the
  busiest trunks get very wide (two busy lines about 13 line widths, 85 px at zoom 15). The other
  way is to scale all widths so the busiest trunk is today's widest line; single lines then get
  thinner. I'd keep per-line widths.
- **Close in** (two tracks drawn): bands slide onto the real tracks, as trains really share them. I'd
  keep that.
