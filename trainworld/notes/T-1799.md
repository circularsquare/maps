# T-1799: interlined lines side by side on the map (design for Anita)

Anita's TODO, 2026-10-29: "show interlined lines better. next to each other. especially for line
traffic view". Research and design only; no code changed.

## What the player would see

- **Line view**: where two or three lines run on the same track, the stroke becomes a ribbon of
  bands touching each other, one per line in its colour, like a transit map. One casing goes round
  the whole ribbon. Where a line leaves, the others close up over a short stretch.
- **Bands keep their order**: a line stays on the same side of its neighbours along the whole shared
  stretch, on the side it leaves towards, so bands do not cross where nothing forces them to.
- **Traffic view**: each band is as wide as that line's riders, so a trunk's width is the sum of its
  lines and each line can be read off on its own.
- **Zoomed out** (lines under about 1.5 px, around zoom 10 and below) the ribbon folds back to one
  stroke, the top line's colour, as today. **Close in** (from about zoom 16.5, where double track
  opens into its two tracks) the bands slide together onto the real tracks, as today, since
  that is where the trains really are.
- Hover and click pick the band under the pointer; trains (pills, zoom 10-14) ride their own band.

## How it is drawn today

- ui-map.md "Lines and track": lines sharing track are drawn **over each other**, one order for all
  (`lineDrawOrder` in `map/network.ts`: no-train lines under, then the shorter route on top). A
  trunk shows only its top line's colour. There is no dash or alternation in the code (the brief
  thought there was); T-1305 left shared trunks "one colour until she has looked".
- Each line is already its own stroke segments along each edge it uses (`networkFromState`, a
  `StrokeBuilder` per line over the edge's `lpts`). So N lines on an edge are already N stroke runs
  on top of each other: side by side needs **a sideways shift per segment**, not new geometry.
- The stroke vertex shader (`STROKE_VS` in `map/rendererShaders.ts`) already shifts sideways along
  the segment's normal for the two tracks close in (`lane_off`, `u_lane_mode`). A band shift is one
  more term there: `off += nrm * shift_px`, where `shift_px` is the shift in line widths times
  `u_width.x`, so it follows zoom with no CPU work.
- Traffic widths are per run of a line's segments on one edge (`buildLayers` in
  `map/demandLayers.ts`, `widthFor`, 0.5 to 6.6 line widths), uploaded as `a_wmul`. Traffic shifts
  are made in the same loop.
- Segment ends are round caps (T-673). Offset segments with per-segment normals leave a small notch
  on the outside of a bend, 2 × shift × sin(half the turn). On fitted curves (many short segments,
  a few degrees each) the caps cover it; at sharp nodes far out bands may notch (stage 4).

## Research

- **Subway Builder** (the game Anita named): v0.9.0 (2025-11-21) added "an experimental setting to
  show interlined routes on the map side-by-side"; v1.7.0 (2026-08-30) made it the normal look,
  "routes that are parallel/interlined now show up parallel to each other", with a setting for "the
  thickness of visually interlined routes". So: bands side by side, the band thickness a setting.
  I found nothing on how they order the bands or handle their traffic view.
- **Mini Metro** draws lines on a shared segment as parallel bands (well known from play; I found
  no written source). **NIMBY Rails** and **Transport Fever 2**: nothing found on drawing shared
  lines apart; TF2 players filter lines by view or highlight one line instead.
- **OpenRailwayMap** does not draw route relations at all (its list, 2016). OSM's transport maps
  draw one stroke per way.
- **Research on transit maps** calls this metro-line crossing minimisation (MLCM): pick the order of
  lines on each edge so as few pairs cross as possible. The general problem is NP-hard (Fink and
  Pupyrev), but the common case is easy: for two lines on an edge, follow them forward until they
  part and put the one that turns left on the left (else look backwards). Nöllenburg gives this
  kind of algorithm in O(lines² × nodes). LOOM (Bast, Brosi, Storandt) does it optimally with an
  ILP on real GTFS networks (New York under a second) and also avoids needless "separations"
  (lines leaving and rejoining a bundle). The greedy pairwise rule is enough for a game; the ILP is
  not worth its weight in a browser.

## Design

1. **Order per edge** (CPU, on line edits, kept in the net cache like the strokes). For each edge
   with two or more lines, sort them with the pairwise rule above, in the edge's own a→b
   direction: follow both paths forward to the node where they part, compare the bearings of their
   next edges there (more to the left goes left); if they never part forward, do the same
   backwards; same route both ways, by line id. The same parting node decides every edge of a
   shared stretch, so the order holds along it. Lines that cross where routes force it cross once,
   at a node.
2. **Slots**: on an edge with k lines, slot i's shift is `i - (k-1)/2` line widths (Line view), or
   the running sum of the lower bands' widths minus half the total (Traffic view). A line drawn
   against the edge's direction (`p & 1`) gets the sign flipped, since the shader's normal follows
   the stroke's direction.
3. **Shift attribute**: a `vec2` per segment (shift at start and end), a tenth attribute in
   `StrokeBuilder`, `strokeCache`, `strokeLod` and `strokeGather`. Where a line's slot differs between
   two edges, the shift ramps over the edge's last and first 30 m (shorter edges: their middle),
   splitting a segment there if needed. `strokeLod` must not merge segments across a ramp.
4. **Shader**: `off += nrm * mix(shift.x, shift.y, along) * bandPx * collapse * (1 - u_lanes.z)`,
   `collapse` a smoothstep on the line's px width (folding under about 1.5 px), `u_lanes.z` the
   existing "stroke grows to the two tracks" factor, so bands hand over to the tracks close in. The
   casing pass needs nothing: all casings are drawn before any colour, so the ribbon gets one
   outline for free, and touching bands have no gap.
5. **Picking, hover, trains**: `pick.ts` measures distance to the shifted stroke (same shift times
   the line's px width); trains use `tripAt`'s existing `shiftM`, set to the band's shift in metres
   at the current zoom.
6. **Traffic marks**: "track at capacity" belongs to the track, so its red band goes round the
   whole ribbon (drawn for the outermost bands only); "riders wait for a train with room" belongs to
   a line, so only that band darkens, with its red dashes inside the band, not outside the casing.
7. **Two-pair trunks** (T-1305's real networks: a four-track trunk is two double tracks 8 m
   apart). At zoom 15, 8 m is about 2 px while each pair's ribbon is 13 px or more, so the two
   ribbons would lie over each other. They need **corridors**: parallel edges within about 20 m of
   each other along most of their length are one ribbon, the left pair's lines on the left, centred
   on the corridor's middle. Player-built double tracks side by side get the same.

**GPU on all of Japan** (T-1644: strokes about 0.4 ms of the 1.7-2.3 ms overlay at zoom 15). No new
segments, since shared lines are already drawn once each; one more attribute (8 bytes a segment)
in the gathered buffer and a few shader ops. Overdraw drops a little, as bands no longer stack.
Expect no measurable change; stage 1 measures it.

## For Anita (my recommendation each)

- **Band width in Line view**: each band a full line width (a three-line trunk is three lines wide,
  like a transit map), or narrower so trunks stay slim. I'd make each band 80% of a line width:
  ribbons read as one track without getting heavy. No setting (Subway Builder has one).
- **When ribbons fold back zoomed out**: I'd fold when a line is under about 1.5 px (around zoom
  10), to today's single stroke in the top line's colour.
- **Most bands before folding**: I'd have no cap; more than five lines on one pair is rare, and a
  wide ribbon there is true.
- **Traffic view, width or colour**: width, each band its line's own riders width as today, so a
  line looks the same on shared and own track and a trunk's width is its total. The cost: the
  busiest trunks get very wide (two busy lines about 13 line widths, 85 px at zoom 15). The other
  way is to scale all widths so the busiest trunk is today's widest line; single lines then get
  thinner. I'd keep per-line widths.
- **Close in** (two tracks drawn): bands slide onto the real tracks, as trains really share them. I'd
  keep that.

## Stage 1 (agent t1799s1, 2026-10-29): Line view bands

Built as design items 1-5, with Anita's calls (80% bands, fold under about 1.5 px, no cap, onto the
real tracks close in).

**Where it lives.** `map/lineBands.ts` (new): the order per edge and each line's shift per path
entry (start, along, end; and the same of `1 - width share`), `bandAlong` for the ease. In
`map/network.ts`, `networkFromState` works the bands out after the edges (7-8 ms on the shared New
York save, 831 shared edges, up to 10 lines), `bandPoints` adds a point where an ease starts or
stops, `StrokeBuilder` writes `Strokes.band` (Int16 x4 a line segment: shift at start and end as a
share of 16 line widths, `1 - width share` at both), and the train samples get `sampleBand`. A line's
cached strokes are rebuilt when its bands change. `strokePost` posts `band` with the line strokes,
`strokeLod` never merges across an ease, `strokeGather` and `renderer.strokes` carry `a_band` (8
bytes an instance), the lane texture is RG32F (lane, band). Shader: `bandW = 1 - u_band * q` on the
line's width and `off += nrm * shift * 16 * u_band * u_width.x`, `u_band` the fold (smoothstep on
the line's CSS px, 1.25 to 1.75) times `1 - lanes.grow`, 0 in Traffic, Elevation and Speed. Trains:
`u_bands` in `TRAIN_VS`, `tripAt(..., bandM)` on the CPU. Picking: `topSeg` takes the bands (the
band under the pointer, else the nearest within the slop), `linesAt` lists past the first only lines
whose band is under the pointer, and a click on a band picks its line (no T-654 track list there).
`?perfOff=bands` (`renderer.lineBands`) draws the old way.

**Calls made on the way** (DECISIONS.md): "ahead" is the lower-numbered line's own direction, not
the edge's a to b, and the never-part tie is the lower number on the left of that line's travel;
with the edge's direction, consecutive edges drawn opposite ways took different parting nodes and
the bands crossed at every such node (115 crossings, then 63, then 9). An edge's order is by how many
of the others each line is left of (Copeland), so a circle of partings among three lines gives a
fixed order rather than what a sort makes of a contradicting comparison. An ease at a node sits on
the side with more lines (the bundle opens out after the node), half and half between equals.

## Stage 2 and T-1807 (agent t1799s2, 2026-10-29): Traffic view bands, stations as wide as the ribbon

**Built.** In the Traffic view each band is its own line's rider width (Anita), in the Line view's
order, so a trunk is the sum of its lines. Every traffic width is drawn at 85% (`TRAFFIC_WIDTH` in
`map/demandLayers.ts`; Anita: "adjust default line width down a tad"; `widthFor` itself unchanged).
- `trafficBands` (`map/lineBands.ts`, called from `buildLayers`): runs of one line on one edge (a path
  entry each, a line's in path order); the order on an edge read back from the Line view's shifts in
  `Strokes.band` (each run's direction against the edge's first run by their chords), so no new field
  comes from the clock worker; each band's shift = half the ribbon less the widths left of it and half
  its own; at nodes the Line view's rule (the side with fewer lines', half way between equals), eased
  over 30 m. Out: per segment the shift at start and end, per train sample (each sample walked along
  its run's segments), and an Int16 copy for picking (shift over 4, `TRAFFIC_PICK_DIV`, as a busy
  trunk is wider than `BAND_RANGE`).
- The Line view's ease points did not exist where the Line view does not ease but the Traffic view
  does (a neighbour's width changing at a stop), so `bandPoints` now cuts every shared edge's line
  segments 30 m from each end and flags those end segments `F_EASE`, which keeps `strokeLod` from
  merging them with the middle.
- GPU: `a_wmul` is a vec3 (width, shift at start and end), so the shifts ride the buffer the widths
  already did: the stroke VAO, the simplified copies (a merged segment's end from its last segment)
  and `strokeGather`; 8 more bytes an instance. `u_band_traffic` picks them in the shader, with no
  width share (bands touch at their own widths); `u_band` folds them as in the Line view. Through a
  station's box (`u_cap`) every view takes the Line view's slots, a line wide before the cap, so the
  narrowed ribbon there still touches.
- Marks: track at capacity is per edge, so every band of the ribbon draws its red band and the union
  goes round the whole ribbon (inner parts are covered by the neighbours' colour). Riders waiting
  belong to a line: on a shared band (bands unfolded) no outer red band; the band darkens, and red
  dashes run inside both its edges (18% of its width, at least 0.5 px), with a thin line of the
  casing colour inside them so they read on red lines (`v_inner` in the stroke shaders).
- Trains: the lane texture's band channel takes the Traffic view's per-sample shifts while it is shown
  (`putLane`), and `tripAt` takes the band array (`renderer.sampleBand`), so picking agrees.
- Picking: `renderer.pickBand` / `pickBandPx` (the Traffic view's copy while shown), `topSeg`'s band
  test uses the band's own width and returns whether the pointer is on it (`linesAt` and the selected
  line's test use that rather than a line's width).
- **T-1807**: `stationRibbons` (`map/lineBands.ts`): per station, half the widest ribbon through it
  (the most of |shift| + half the band over a run of a shared line along an edge that ends within 3 m
  of the station's node), in the view shown; a station attribute `a_ribbon`. A box's half width, or a
  dot's radius, is at least that times the band's px (fold included) plus the rim and a pixel; the CPU
  twin (`stationShape`'s `ribbonPx`) and `stationAt`'s reach agree. The track cap through boxes is
  still worked out from a plain box.

## Stage 3 (agent t1799c, 2026-10-29): corridors, four-track trunks as one ribbon

**Built.** `map/corridors.ts` finds edges that run side by side: an end of one within about 60 m of an
end of the other (the only pairs tested; a grid of ends keeps Japan cheap), at least 60% of each
one's length within 20 m of the other and within 25 degrees of its direction, in either direction.
The relation is passed on, so any number of edges along a track are one corridor; a corridor whose
outer edges are over 30 m apart (`SPAN_M`) is left alone. Per edge: its corridor, which way it runs against the
corridor's direction, its centre line's distance from the corridor's middle. `lineBands` then puts
every line of the corridor in one row, left edge's lines on the left, each edge's own order kept, the
row centred on the corridor's middle (a single-line edge beside another becomes a band too). The
metres between two edges' centre lines become line widths at `BAND_LW_PER_M` (0.05: 0.4 of a line
for 8 m), so the two groups touch. `NetworkBuffers.corridor` (5 numbers per edge) carries the edge's
place to `trafficBands`, which orders and sizes a corridor's runs as one ribbon. No shader, stroke
buffer or picking change: everything rides the existing shift in line widths.

**Results.** New York shared save: 344 corridors on 706 of 1,544 line edges (332 pairs, 9 threes, 3
fives); the real save: 456 corridors on 1,107 of 1,647 (Queens Boulevard's nine lines side by side
where four colours overlapped); Tokyo shared 223; Japan 56 (pairs of Shinkansen and the like). Smoke
20 checks passed (137 s), `checks/t1799-bands.mjs` passes (crossings still 0 against the rule; its

## Stage 4 (agent t1799c, 2026-10-29): splits and merges

Anita: "where they diverge still does not look good... when they're interlined they run in parallel,
but then when they diverge they snap to overlapping and then curve out from there. ideally we

## T-1846 (agent t1846, 2026-10-30): corridors off

Anita, with a screenshot of her Tokyo game: "I think the interlining display is still not quite right.
You can see a line turning suddenly as like disjoint pieces here. So in this way what we have now is
worse than before." A green trunk through central Tokyo stepped sideways in pieces, a pale green line
showing beside it on some stretches and not others.

**Cause: corridor membership changes edge by edge.** Two tracks side by side are cut into edges at
different places (stations, crossovers, junctions on one side only). Stage 3 tests pairs of edges
(ends within 60 m, 60% of each beside the other), so along one track some edges pair and some don't;
an edge in a corridor shifts its lines by its place in the ribbon, the next edge outside it is
centred again, and the 30 m ease between them is a step at zoom 14-15 (4-8 px). A line also steps
where one corridor ends and another starts at the same node (different width and middle). Counted with
scratch `diag2.mjs` (agent scratch t1846): consecutive path entries of one line whose band moves 0.15
line widths or more:

| save | corridors off | stage 3 (20 m) | only 5 m | only 3 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tokyo shared (`tyo_real`) | 94 (real partings) | 446 (99 of half a line or more) | 126 | 102 |
| real New York | 0 | 449 (132) | 455 | 351 |

Stage 4 is not involved (it only moves an ease between edges with different counts of lines of
their own, which are real partings) and stays on.

**Tried and dropped**: (1) an edge in a corridor when 60% of it lies beside its neighbours together
(not one partner), every edge pair tested by a grid along their length; (2) each edge's ribbon made
of its own lines and those of the edges directly beside it, rather than one ribbon for the whole
corridor, so its width only changes where a line joins or leaves. Together: 449 steps on Tokyo, no
better: the union test chained long corridors (up to 17 edges) that then broke at junctions, and
edges still fell in and out. Diff kept at scratch t1846 `local-ribbon-variant.diff`. Only near-
overlapping edges (3 m, which keeps Queens Boulevard's nine lines on nine edges) still has 351 steps
on the real save, since its edges are cut at different places too.

**Now**: `CORRIDORS = false` in `map/lineBands.ts` (`lineBands(..., { corridors: true, within })`
turns it on for a check). The drawing is stage 2's plus stage 4: only lines on one edge are side by
side; parallel tracks, Queens Boulevard on the real save and Tokyo's four-track trunks draw over each

**The proper fix (not done)**: decide side by side along a line, not per edge. Per edge, sample its
length (every 25 m) and per sample record which other lines run beside it within 20 m; then along
each line's path, smooth that record (a line's ribbon only changes where a neighbour track's set of
lines has changed for at least ~100 m, with the change eased over 60-100 m, not 30) and give the shift
per drawn point rather than per path entry (`Strokes.band` already carries a shift at each segment
end; `bandPoints` would cut where the smoothed shift changes). Both tracks' lines must take the same
decision at the same place, so the record has to be symmetric (built per pair of samples, not per
edge). The ribbon's middle stays per place, the midpoint of the tracks beside each other there. Worth
an Opus agent with Anita's screenshot as the test; check it with `diag2.mjs`'s count (should approach
the corridors-off count) before pictures.
