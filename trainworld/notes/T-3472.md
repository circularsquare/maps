# T-3472: long-session memory check (2026-11-16, agent memcheck)

Measure only; no app code changed. Scripts and per-sample JSON: `artifacts/trainworld/t3472/` (`session.mjs` the Chrome
driver, `ff.mjs` the Firefox one over WebDriver BiDi, `snapown3.mjs` who holds a heap snapshot's arrays, `run-*.json`
every sample). Own Vite on 6154 (dev server), headless Chrome pinned to 4 claimed cores, `?demandWorkers=3` (what a
12+ thread machine gets), the all-China save (`t1156-all-china.save`, 2,363 stations, 123 lines, 276 zones). MB are
Windows private bytes summed over the browser's processes; heap figures are CDP `Runtime.getHeapUsage` per target
(JS heap + ArrayBuffer backing stores) and each target's `WebAssembly.Memory` sizes.

## Short version

- **No leak that keeps growing.** All-China on Chrome: 7.1 GB settled 30 s after the load's final, 7.4-8.0 GB from
  the 5th minute to the 45th (slope about 6 MB a minute, inside the swing), 8.05 GB at the end after a forced GC.
  Firefox does the same at a higher level: 8.3-9.1 GB from minute 3 to minute 20, flat.
- **The load is what costs**, not the session: 3 solving workers x 1,296 MB of WASM + the aux worker's 289 = 4.2 GB
  of the 7.1. Anita's 7.4 GB after 43 minutes is this plateau, reached within minutes of loading a big save.
- **What grows during play is about +0.9 GB in the first 10 minutes, then nothing steady**: GPU process +300 (map
  tiles), renderer outside any heap +250, page JS and buffers +190, clock worker +60. On top of that the page's
  ArrayBuffers swing up to +1.2 GB between collections (peak 9.5 GB at minute 17).
- **The base before any city is about 1 GB in Chrome** (start menu) and 1.6 GB once a one-city game starts with 3
  solving workers; 2.2 GB in Firefox. Biggest single parts: the GPU process (0.3), the world's zone outlines held as
  JS arrays on the page (0.15, already at the start menu), the renderer's own overhead (0.3-0.35), each worker's
  isolate (about 80 MB each, 6 of them, about half of it dev-server module source).
- **T-3373**: A and B both act on the 4.2 GB; nothing needs fixing first. Two smaller things are worth their own
  tasks: a solving worker's WASM never shrinks (about 250 MB a worker sits empty once zones go coarse), and the
  page's ArrayBuffer churn.

## What grows, and how fast

| Holder | Final + 30 s | 45 min, after GC | Change | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPU process | 373 | 678 | +305 | rises over the first 10 minutes as the camera visits cities, then flat at 650-710 |
| Renderer outside any heap | 880 | 1,132 | +250 | noisy (630-1,110 between samples), no trend after minute 10 |
| Page JS + buffers | 704 | 893 | +190 | settled level; the swing above it is garbage, see below |
| Clock worker | 379 | 429 | +50 | WASM 193 to 225 once (one 32 MB step), JS flat |
| Demand workers' JS + buffers | 430 | 527 | +97 | flat after minute 1 |
| Demand WASM | 4,177 | 4,177 | 0 | fixed at its high water from the load |

- **Demand WASM never moves after the load**, while what it holds falls: the solving workers' heap in use went from
  1,137-1,144 MB to 1,034-1,061 MB as the fine zones dropped from 109 to 9 (zones not looked at go coarse). WASM
  memory only grows, so about 240 MB per solving worker, 0.7 GB in all, sits unused inside it for the rest of the
  session. Only a worker restart gives it back.
- **The page's ArrayBuffers are churn, not held**: after a forced GC they are back near their settled level, but
  between collections they reach +1.2 GB (backing stores V8 collects late; most likely the buffers the workers send,
  not traced to a sender). This
  is what makes the 9.5 GB peaks.
- **MapLibre**: 20-58 tiles in view and up to 202 kept out of view (its own cache cap); its worker 20-90 MB. Bounded.
- **The app's own collections** (every array, map, set and buffer two levels into the demand client, renderer,
  overlay, labels, capacity, world and clock client, sampled each minute): nothing grows with time except debug-only
  logs (`twDemand.log` 2 to 50 entries, `qlog` 4 to 186, `editLog` 33, `boardLog` 16 to 40) and
  `client.cur.coarse` 62 to 162 zones, all small. `clockClient.worldBefore` (the world before the last edit, for
  undo) is one copy of the world, about 2,400 nodes. DOM nodes 2,800-3,200.
- Twenty-eight `net::ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RESOURCES` load failures during the session: Chrome refusing requests when too
  many are in flight while flying between cities. Not a memory effect.

## The base, before anything is built (Anita's question)

Chrome, after a GC, 3 solving workers asked for:

| Moment | All | Renderer | GPU | Browser + utility | Page JS + buffers | Workers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Start menu | 975 | 590 | 294 | 84 | 209 + 70 | none yet |
| New game, New York | 1,588 | 1,163 | 336 | 82 | 202 + 67 | 4 demand, clock, shade: 26-28 JS + 18-34 buffers each; WASM 33 x 3, aux 65, clock 33 |
| New game, Shanghai | 1,594 | 1,169 | 336 | 82 | 202 + 67 | same |
| All-China loaded, untouched (final + 30 s) | 7,116 | 6,650 | 373 | 86 | 282 + 422 | see the session table |

Firefox: a new game is 2,186 (main process 794, tab 1,367).

What the 1.6 GB of a new one-city game is made of:

- **GPU process, 0.3 GB**: MapLibre's and the overlay's textures and buffers (headless d3d11 here).
- **The page's JS, 0.2 GB, already at the start menu**. From a heap snapshot of the menu: about 150 MB is the world's
  zone outlines held as GeoJSON-style nested arrays: `cityBoundaries.ts`'s outlines (61 MB of coordinate arrays,
  3.7 million small arrays), its `OutlineEdges` index (holders map 40 MB, vertex ids 23 MB, per-zone rings and keys
  14 MB, drawn features 5 MB) and water outlines (9 MB). A further 60 MB of module source text sits outside the JS
  heap; that is the dev server (production serves a few bundles). The same as typed arrays would be a fraction; this is the clearest cut in the
  base that is not a worker.
- **Each worker, about 80 MB plus its share of the renderer's overhead**: 26-28 MB JS, 18-34 MB buffers, 33-65 MB
  WASM, and about 34 MB of module source outside the heap on the dev server (by the snapshot). A new game starts six (three
  solving, the aux, the clock, the shade worker) plus MapLibre's: about 0.45 GB before a zone is open.
- **Renderer overhead outside any heap, 0.3-0.35 GB** (Blink, compiled code, isolates, allocator).
- **Browser and utility processes, 0.08 GB.**

## Edit time on the big save

Chrome, all-China, 4 cores: a train count change answered in 113-258 ms, its undo in 184-188 ms, a 1 km track piece
off a station in 130-291 ms, its undo 65-838 ms. Firefox: 57-885 ms. Nothing near the reported 5 s per piece; this
save's track pieces add to a network the clock worker already holds, and the player's 246M-point save may have more
track, more trains or fewer cores. A per-step timing of their save (`?debug=1` edit log) would say where the 5 s goes.

## What this means for T-3373

- **There is no leak to fix first.** Memory reaches its level at load and in the first ten minutes of looking around,
  then holds; the same in Firefox.
- **A (fewer solving workers on big saves)** removes 1.3 GB of WASM and about 0.15 GB of JS and buffers per worker
  dropped on all-China: from 3 workers to 1 is about -2.9 GB of the 8. It is the biggest available cut and acts on
  exactly the part that is large.
- **B (zones split between workers)** cuts the same 4.2 GB towards one copy plus each worker's market and set-up;
  how much depends on how much of the 1.3 GB is the market and set-up, which every worker would still hold
  (T-3071: the market alone was 185 MB of a NYC worker).
- **Neither touches the rest**: the base 1-1.6 GB (outlines on the page, worker isolates, GPU), the GPU's +0.3 GB
  of tiles, the page's ArrayBuffer churn, and the WASM high water. Candidates for their own tasks, smallest first:
  1. the zone outlines on the page as typed arrays (about 150 MB, every game, from the start menu);
  2. return a solving worker's empty WASM once its zones go coarse (about 0.7 GB on all-China after 10 minutes),
     e.g. a restart of the worker when it is idle and most of its memory is free;
  3. find which worker messages make the page's buffer churn (up to 1.2 GB of peak).
