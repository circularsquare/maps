# T-3310: do high-speed lines still lose money after the fare fix?

Agent hsrprofit, 2026-11-12. Measurement only, no code changed. Anita: "people are still complaining about
shinkansen losing money. since the fare fix went live already, do we think that its just a skill issue / that
theres nothing left to do here? ... idk if theres anything else that we were planing to do regarding high speed
rail balance".

## Short answer

Mostly skill and one default, not a bug. Neither v0.3.1 nor master loses money on a lone high-speed line by
itself; the two versions give the same figures to the dollar in every case below (option A and the world
rebuild change nothing for a line with no ordinary line in its way). What loses money:

1. **Train length and frequency.** Cost is cars times trains (running 90% of the operating cost at 16 cars).
   A Tokyo - Osaka line with 16-car trains every 10-15 minutes spends $360M a game day on running and takes
   $297M: -$97M a day. The same line with 5-car trains (what a new line starts with) at the same timetable
   makes +$125M a day, and riders do not drop (the trains are not full at 55,000 riders a day).
2. **The 1.5x default is above the fare that earns most.** Fares a weekday peak at 1.0x (alone: $387M against
   $295M at 1.5x). Beside a parallel ordinary line that stops at every town, 1.5x leaves the high-speed line
   26,000 riders against 82,000 at 1.0x: with 5-car trains it breaks even (-$1M a day) at 1.5x and makes
   +$183M at 1.0x. This is the case players meet (a Shinkansen next to the old line) and the one the default
   hurts.
3. **Construction can't be paid back by anyone.** $100B for 456 km ($220M a km, trains $1.2B of it). Best
   case measured pays it back in 463 game days (127 years at 100 real days a game day). A player on $10B plus
   a $10B loan can't build it at all; a short line pays upkeep and little else (Tokyo - Nagoya alone never
   covers its own upkeep plus the thinnest timetable).

Demand is the gap against real life (below), but it is not something a balance number fixes: the isolated
stations here have no feeder network.

## Method

Sandbox game (`?new=1&mode=sandbox&paused=1`): a Tokyo - Osaka line costs more than a normal game's cash and
loan (every price, fare and cost is the same in a sandbox, docs/track-and-service.md 6.4). Built by script
(`hsr.mjs` in the agent's scratchpad `hsrprofit/`, not kept): `route` edits with `through`, `hsr`, design 320
(160 for ordinary), level +1 viaduct unless said, between Tokyo (139.7671, 35.6812), Shin-Yokohama, waypoints
at Odawara, Mishima, Shizuoka, Hamamatsu, Toyohashi (track only), Nagoya (136.8815, 35.1709), Kyoto,
Shin-Osaka (135.5003, 34.7335); 456 km; stations at the five names with 320 m platforms; one line, high-speed
trains, 16 cars, every 10 / 15 / 20 minutes at high / medium / low demand (6 / 4 / 3 an hour), multiplier 1.5,
then edited through the sweeps; demand waited to a final that stayed the newest for 30 s. Read in the page:
riders and fares by kind of day from `incomeByDay`, running cost from `runningToday`, the line's upkeep share,
construction from the ledger's `build` + `trains`. Average day = (5 weekdays + Saturday + Sunday) / 7; Friday
runs about 15% above Tuesday-Thursday (ledger), left out. Figures are game dollars (fares and running costs
x100, upkeep and construction as the game shows them). **Ledger check**: with the clock at 960x for 7 game
days the ledger's day 2-4 read fares $256.98M, running $135.70M, upkeep $34.48M, the model's figures to the
cent; Saturday $396M against the model's $414M (a holiday or Saturday calendar detail).

Versions: v0.3.1 = `.tw-trees/hotfix031` (71009033), Vite 6163. Master: first runs on the main tree's
working tree through Vite 6164; the main tree then broke under another agent's half-written edit
(`i18n/places.ts` imports a file that did not exist yet), so the mul sweeps and the ledger check ran on a
clean worktree of master HEAD 633dd492 (`.tw-trees/hsrprofit-master`, same packs, Vite 6165). Both master
runs agree (cars 5 at 1.5x: $297.1M / $135.7M). Port 6162 was held by someone else's older Vite (pid 22556),
serving old code; not touched.

## Where the money goes (Tokyo - Osaka, 16 cars, 6/4/3)

- **Running**: $360M a day, 91% of the daily cost. Per train-km high-speed is $5 + $3 a car, times Japan's
  0.63: 16 cars is $33 a train-km, 91% of it the cars. Cost is linear in cars and in trains an hour; the fares
  are not (riders barely move until the trains are full), so shorter trains cost-save without losing a rider
  until the 5-car train fills (it does not at 55,000 riders; 800 seats a train).
- **Upkeep**: $34.5M a day for 456 km of viaduct (1.5 x $50k a km) plus five stations. 9% of the cost. Tunnel
  track makes it 2.6 times ($90M); ground is the same as viaduct.
- **Build**: track about $99B, trains $1.2B (320 cars at $3.75M), stations under 1%. $220M a km is twice the
  flat viaduct price ($112.5M a km): Japan's hills and the water crossings (terrain surcharges up to +3x,
  water +2.5x) carry the rest. Ground, viaduct and tunnel come out within 10% of each other here
  ($110B, $100B, $97B), so the route, not the level, sets it.
- A game day's loan payment on $10B is $222M for 60 days; the best lone line above earns $217M a day.

## Against real life

JR Central's Tokaido Shinkansen: about 460,000 riders a day in FY2024 (46.0 ten-thousands a day, integrated
report 2024 [global.jr-central.co.jp/en/company/ir/annualreport/_pdf/annualreport2024-06.pdf]); the line is
about 93% of JR Central's transport revenue, which is 81% of the ¥1,831.8bn group revenue, so about ¥1.38
trillion a year, about ¥8,200 a rider, about US$55 at ¥150. Group operating income is roughly 38% of revenue
(forecast ¥746bn on ¥1,940bn for the year to March 2026, quartr.com summary of the half-year results); I found
no Tokaido-only margin, so treat 38% as the group's.

| | real | game, lone Tokyo - Osaka line |
|---|---|---|
| riders a day | 460,000 | 55,000 (8.4 times fewer) |
| revenue a rider | about US$55 | $53 (16 cars and 5 cars alike) |
| operating margin | about 38% (group) | 42% at 5 cars (profit 125 of 295 in fares), 63% at 8 cars every 20-45 minutes |

So the price per rider is right, the margin with sensible trains is right, and the number of riders is one
eighth. The cause here is demand and not price or cost: an isolated Tokyo Station and Shin-Osaka with only
their walking catchments. The market's Tokyo - Osaka pair is 160,800 trips a day by all modes (notes/T-1508.md);
the line gets 55,000 riders over all its pairs. A player with feeder metros would see more; I did not test a
feeder-rich game (the jpn7 save carries the Shinkansen as ordinary lines, so a high-speed line added there
competes with itself). The world rebuild (long-trip pull, more airport trips) is in the shared data and made
no difference between master and v0.3.1 here, because the packs are the same files for both Vites; it is not
separated out. What a pre-rebuild pack would give is not measured.

## Demand, price or cost

- Lone line, a sensible timetable: profit is positive in every case above except the 16-car 10-minute one and
  Tokyo - Nagoya. So not "the fare fix failed".
- Price: yes, in one place. 1.5x loses 20-30% of fares against 1.0x alone and loses nearly everything
  beside a parallel local line (riders 82,000 -> 26,000, the cliff of T-3239). The default sits on it.
- Cost: running cost is the lever the player holds, and the starting default (5 cars) is fine. A player who
  copies the real thing (16 cars, a train every 5-10 minutes) overbuys by 3x: 236 train runs a day for 55,000
  riders is 235 riders a train in 2,560 seats.
- Demand: 8 times below life, but with no feeders. Not tuned here.

## Recommendation

**Skill issue, plus one default.**

1. *What a player should do* (a hint, not a rule): start a high-speed line with 5-8 cars and 20-30 minutes
   between trains, raise it when the line panel shows full trains; fewer stops don't matter for money, cars
   and trains do. Check the line's profit after the first day with riders, not before.
2. *A balance change worth deciding* (Anita's call, since it changes what the game is): lower the default
   high-speed multiplier from 1.5 to 1.25 (or 1.0). Measured effect: lone line profit at 5 cars +125 -> +195
   (+57%) at 1.25, +217 at 1.0; beside a parallel line -1 -> +143 at 1.25, +183 at 1.0; riders +36% / +72%
   (lone) and about 2.2x / 3.2x (beside a local line). Effect on other games: only lines with high-speed
   trains, and only where the multiplier was left at its default (saves that stored a multiplier keep it);
   ordinary lines, buses and ferries unchanged; a high-speed line then earns about the same per rider as
   before minus 9% (1.25x) or 24% (1.0x), on 36-72% more riders. Option B of T-3239 proposed this and it was set aside for A;
   A is in and the cliff is still there (A moves where the corridor flips, not whether a line this
   price-sensitive keeps its riders).
3. *Nothing to do on cost*: the 1.5x high-speed car and track prices are not what loses money; running cost
   follows the cars the player picks. The 3-tier wealth split (set aside) would spread riders by group
   around the same cliff (T-3239: within about 20 points of A either way); no case here needs it.
4. *Construction* ($100B for Tokyo - Osaka, $220M a km in Japan) is a different complaint: no timetable pays
   it back inside a human timescale (463 game days at best). If players say "losing money" about the whole
   line including building it, that is the price list, not demand; changing it is Anita's.
