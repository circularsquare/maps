# T-334 Crossovers, and what express running still needs

A design note for Anita (her TODO: "think about express (added partially, with quad track, still
needs crossover stuff) / think about crossovers in general?"). No code. Folds in T-257's "crossovers
between the pairs mid-stretch". Choices marked **open** are hers; the rest I have decided.

## How parallel tracks join today

**The two tracks of a double track** never need joining while running. An edge's direction picks
its track (running side from the city pack), so a train in one direction is always on the right
track. The one place a train changes track inside a pair is reversing, and that is already
automatic: a line ending at a through station on double track reverses there over an implied
crossover, charged 90 s per crossing move against opposite-direction trains (6.2, "double-track
station reversal"). A dead end pools its platforms. A line cannot reverse at a stop in the middle
of its route (`find_path` makes it leave on the other side).

**Quad track's two pairs** are two ordinary double-track edges that meet only at the two ends of
the stroke, where they form a junction. Between the ends nothing joins them; the twin stations are
two separate nodes linked as one complex, so riders change on foot but trains cannot. So a line on
quad picks local or express for the whole stretch.

**Two routes drawn side by side** (two separate strokes a few metres apart) are not joined at all.
Nothing stops them being close, and nothing connects them. (T-062's "side by side" was only how
lines sharing one track were drawn, and T-190 removed it.)

**Joining by hand** works in principle, untested: draw a short route from one track to the other.
Each end becomes a flat double-track Y, with the existing junction rule, price (0.25 km of track
each) and "Build a flyover" button. In practice it is poor at 8 m spacing: the clicks are hard to
place, the 100 m minimum radius and the lead-in PIs (which leave a node on its heading) make a long
S, the speed limit falls to about 38 km/h, and each Y is cut and priced like a branch.

## What express and local running is missing

1. **Changing pairs mid-stretch**: an express that runs local in the outer part of its route
   (New York's 2 and 3 in the Bronx and Brooklyn) needs to join the express pair partway along a
   long quad stretch, not only at its ends.
2. **A clean way to place that join**, with a sensible speed and price.
3. **Overtaking has no cost on plain double track.** Trains do not block each other: an express
   behind a local on the same track simply passes through it, and the section's occupancy charges
   nothing for the speed difference beyond the small "mix" term in the delay formula. So today
   express service on plain double track works as well as on quad, and quad buys only separate
   section capacity. This is outside crossovers, but it decides how much quad and passing tracks
   matter (open question 5).

Cross-platform changes at express stops already work, through the twin stations' complex.

## What real railways do

- **Crossover**: two turnouts back to back joining two parallel tracks, about 60-120 m long, taken
  at 25-80 km/h (faster with long high-speed turnouts). Two crossovers laid over each other in the
  length of one are a **scissors**, letting trains go either way. Railways put them before and
  after terminals and turnback stations, at express stations on four-track lines, and every few km
  on main lines so trains can work round a blocked track.
- **Interlocking**: the signalling that sets and locks a route through a group of points so two
  conflicting routes are never set at once. A cluster of crossovers and junctions at one place is
  "an interlocking"; its capacity is set by which routes conflict, which is what the game's
  junction rule already counts.
- **How a four-track line is paired matters.** *Paired by direction* (New York: local, express,
  express, local, with each direction's local and express side by side) lets a train change
  between local and express without crossing any opposing track, and gives cross-platform changes.
  *Paired by use* (British main lines: a slow pair beside a fast pair) means every change of pair
  crosses an opposing track, so busy places get a **flying junction** or dive-under instead. The
  game's quad is paired by use: travelling the drawn way with right-hand running, the tracks are
  express back, express forward, local back, local forward, so a forward train moving from local
  to express crosses the local back track.

## Options for the game

**A. Crossover tool.** A tool in the Build tab: click on quad track (or on any track with a
parallel pair within about 15 m) and the game places a crossover there.
- *Track model*: both pairs are split at the click, and two short edges join the new nodes in an
  X, one per diagonal, so trains can change either way in either direction. The edges carry a
  "crossover" flag: fixed shape (two turnouts and the diagonal, about 120 m along the track), no
  PIs to drag, a 50 km/h speed limit, drawn as an X. Routing needs nothing new: each crossover end
  sits on one side of its node like any branch.
- *Capacity*: nothing new. Each split node is a junction, and the existing crossing rule finds the
  real conflicts from the track positions (a forward local-to-express move crosses the local back
  track; staying on your pair crosses nothing, so it costs nothing). The junction inspector and
  its flyover offer work as they are.
- *Price*: two junctions' worth (0.5 km of track at the level).
- *Player*: one click, one undo step, delete like any track. Works for quad and for two routes
  drawn side by side.

**B. Automatic crossovers at quad stations.** Every twin station on quad gets a scissors on each
side, as at a New York express station.
- *Track model and capacity*: the same edges as A, placed by the station edit. Nothing new in
  capacity: an unused crossover makes no conflict.
- *Player*: nothing to do, but cannot put one between stations, pays for ones nobody uses, and
  every quad station becomes four junction nodes.

**C. A four-track station node.** Instead of twins, a quad station is one node that both pairs pass
through, with a station setting "Trains can change pairs here".
- *Track model*: a node with four track ports; platforms keyed by port rather than by side, so
  the platform resource, `active_node_tracks`, the twin logic and Express copy all change.
- *Capacity*: the junction rule works on the combined ports.
- *Player*: a tidy one-click setting, but only at stations, and it is the biggest change of the
  three to code that is already built.

**D. Passing tracks at stations** (Japanese private railways, Keio or Odakyu: locals wait in a
loop while an express passes). A cheaper alternative to quad, not a way to join pairs. It only means
something once overtaking costs time (missing item 3), so it belongs with that question, later.

## Recommendation

**A, the crossover tool**, kept paired by use. It adds one edge flag, a fixed shape and a tool,
and leaves routing and capacity untouched, because the junction rule already charges exactly the
moves that cross opposing traffic. It covers mid-stretch changes on quad and joins two routes
drawn side by side with the same click. B can be added later as a default on top of A if placing
crossovers at every express stop gets tedious. T-257 stays on hold until Anita says.

Decided here, not open: 50 km/h through a crossover; one crossover is both diagonals (a scissors
spread over about 120 m), since a single diagonal only lets one direction change each way and
players would not expect that; the tool works wherever two pairs run parallel within about 15 m.
