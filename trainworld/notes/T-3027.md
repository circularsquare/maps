# T-3027: ramp length sets ramp speed (agent rampspeed, 2026-11-20)

## Rule (Anita 11-20)
- A ramp's speed limit: 0.8 km/h per metre of ramp per level (`RAMP_KMH_PER_M`): 200 m 160, 250 m 200,
  400 m 320. It holds over the whole ramp; a piece runs at the lowest of curve, ramp, track cap, train top.
- Drawing sizes ramps to the target speed (`ramp_for(min(target, kind cap))`): 400 m at 320 on high-speed
  track, 200 m on ordinary track (its cap is 160), 100 m on tram track (cap 80). Where the legs at the
  ramp's two levels have less room, the ramp takes all of it, down to the floor: 160 m a level on rail
  (5%, 128 km/h), 100 m on tram track (8%, 80). Shorter is `RampTooShort`, as before.
- A route with a level change that is refused at its target's length (platform or crossing on a long
  ramp, bridge ramps with no room, an over-track ramp with no room) is tried again at 200 m, then the
  floor (`draw.rs` `route_op`, `ramp_tries`); a ramp over track tries every length before giving way to a
  flat crossing.
- Old track (`EdgeData::ramp` 0, every piece in today's saves): 200 m a level, no speed limit; a ramp
  squeezed under 200 m by a reshape takes the rule. Kept through reshape, split, join, rebuild.

## Brief vs code
The brief said today's high-speed ramps are capped at 160 by a bug. They are not: no ramp limits speed
in master (T-3421's notes: "Re-checked how a ramp could slow a train: nothing does"). Applying 200 m = 160
to old ramps would slow existing high-speed lines: Anita's North America save (na-day-491) has 492
high-speed pieces with ramps, about 2,773 s of extra running summed over them (scratch count). So old
pieces keep no limit (DECISIONS 2026-11-20). No advisor was available in this session.

## How it is built
- `EdgeData::ramp: u16` (metres a level, 0 old), `Network::edge_ramp`, saved in a new trailing block
  `LRA1` (save level 31). Drawn pieces store 0 for 200 m off high-speed track (`stored_ramp`), so an
  ordinary network's save is byte for byte as before.
- `geom::Ramps { per_level, floor, old }` in a thread-local scope (`with_ramps`, `ramps_scope`), like
  `bridge::with_min_radius`: `vertical` reads it. `Network::fit_edge`/derive, `check_edge`, `split_plan`,
  rebuild's `ramp_in`, `raise_end` and edge previews run in the piece's own scope; the drawing tool in the
  route's; flyover planning in `Ramps::exact(200)`. Outside any scope a fit is as before (200 m, no
  shortening).
- `bridge.rs` `RAMP` is now `ramp()`, the scope's length.
- Speeds: `geom::limits` (curve, cap, ramp spans), `geom::run_time` (piece times as before plus what
  slower ramps add, so track without them is timed to the bit). Used by `service::build_run`, derive's
  `edge_time`, `render_speeds` (ghost, Speed display, hover), `edges_info` vmin, and
  `line_stretch_speeds` (why 5 = ramp, `lineInspector.whyRamp`).
- Preview: `preview_ramp_labels` (x, y, km/h of each ramp under the track's cap), passed as
  `PreviewAnswer.ramps` and labelled like a curve (`rampLabels`), drawing and reshaping.
- split_plan's T-622 ramp-holding vertices now use the ramp as laid (vertex pair in `vert`), not 200 m
  about the leg's middle.
