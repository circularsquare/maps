## The short version

- **A ferry line is a bus line on water.** The Ferry button sits beside Bus (key O). Click on land by the water to
  put a port on the shore, or click an existing port to share it. The player places only ports: between them the
  route finds itself (Anita, 2026-11-03: one button, for placing ports).
- **Ports are stations** of a new kind: on the shore, shared by any ferry lines, walked to like a station, with
  changes to trains and buses near them. A port costs US$10M; boats US$8M each; the route is free.
- **The water is the basemap's own**, the vector water the player sees, read from its zoom 12 tiles in the app as
  the bus tool reads roads. It has Venice's and Amsterdam's canals, needs no data build, covers the whole world,
  and is 0.1 to 2 MB a leg. The rail mask (25 m, water under 50 m wide dropped) loses the canals.
- **The route**: the shortest water path where every metre nearer land than 400 m costs up to 4 times as much,
  eased off if that makes the route more than 1.3 times the plain shortest; at least a 10 m channel; bends as
  wide as the water allows up to 300 m. A narrow river is all "near land", so the boat keeps to its middle.
  Measured: 0.5 to 2.5 s a leg in plain JavaScript, in a worker.
- **Boats**: one kind, 320 riders, 25 km/h in open water down to 10 km/h in channels under 40 m wide,
  accelerating at 0.15 m/s². Slow on purpose; real harbour ferries run 8 to 30 km/h.
- **Demand**: ferries are ordinary lines (they win where they are quickest, as at Staten Island). On top, Anita's
  rule: 10% of visits and attractions trips are ferry fans, for whom minutes aboard count a tenth. This is a new
  demand factor, so it waits for her OK (the last section).
- **Fares**: a Ferry row under High speed in the Money tab, a multiple of the curve's part above the base like
  high speed's, 1.00x by default, shown once a ferry line exists.
- **Buses** (T-2178): a straight bus stretch (no road found) over more than 50 m of water is refused.
- **Stages**: five tasks, T-2192 to T-2196, plus T-2178; two need their own worktree.

## 1. What there is to build on

### The bus line kind

Built as T-1464, T-1465, T-1514, T-1577 (docs/track-and-service.md 6.7, docs/ui-building.md "Drawing a bus line").

- **Drawn** in `map/busDraw.ts`: each click a point; between two points the app finds the shortest path over the
  basemap's zoom 14 roads (`busRoads.ts`, `roadGraph.ts`), fetched straight from the basemap's tile server, or
  runs straight where there is no road. Dragging, deleting and finishing points, touch, Edit route.
- **Stored** in the sim as points with two bits each (a corner, a road stretch), at most 8,000, in `LineData.bus`;
  saved in a trailing `LBS2` block. The sim takes the points as they come; it never sees the road graph.
- **Run**: stops place themselves (`bus::plan`: train stations within 200 m, both ends, crossings, about every
  2 km); each 100 m has its own speed, min(18 km/h, 0.7 x the car's street speed there) from the zone's pack
  (`bus::Pace`, `streets.rs`); no acceleration, no capacity at stops.
- **Demand**: a bus line is a line in the network, 0.5 of a 160-rider car; a 300 m corridor reaches its stops; a
  boarding costs 6 perceived minutes (`BUS_BOARD_MIN`).
- **Priced**: US$1M a road-km and US$0.6M a bus on Construct, US$9 a bus-km to run, upkeep US$30 a road-km a day
  (real figures, times `ECONOMY` in the game). Its own fleet, spares shared between bus lines.

A ferry differs in four ways: its stops are the ports the player placed (not placed by the sim), its route is on
water, its speed comes from how much room the boat has, and it accelerates. Everything else (line ids, schedules,
fleets, the boarding account, fares, panels) carries over.

### The water the game has now

- **Each zone's pack mask** (`pipeline/water.py`, `tier0.py`, `plan_config.water_cell`): a raster at 25 m (50 m
  where the zone's box would pass 150M pixels), water narrower than 2 pixels dropped by an opening
  (`without_narrow`), so **water under 50 m wide (100 m in big zones) is gone**. Source: OSM coastline polygons
  (osmdata.openstreetmap.de) plus Geofabrik inland water (`natural=water`, riverbanks, docks, canals); TIGER in
  the US; StatCan in Canada. Stored as row runs in the pack's `.bin` (Venice's: about 170 KB over a 93 km box).
  The clock worker fetches it for zones near the view (zones.md 6) and the sim decides "no track at ground over
  water" with it (`cost::GeoMask`, `MaskSet`). Demand's walks route round it (each metre of water 3 metres of
  walking, demand-worldwide.md).
- **The world's coarse water** (`world_water.py`, `packs/world.water`): 1/60 degree cells (about 1.85 km by 1.4
  km at 40 degrees), the sea and big lakes, where no pack mask covers.

For ferries both fall short. The Sumida (150-200 m), the Seine in Paris (100-200 m) and the Bosphorus survive in
the pack mask, but Venice's Grand Canal (30-70 m) only in part and the rii, Amsterdam's grachten and most city
canals not at all. Between zones, and in places with no zone yet (Istanbul), there is only the 1.85 km grid.

### A finer mask: the options

| Source | Resolution | Narrow rivers and canals | Where | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pack masks rebuilt finer (OSM, 5 m, no opening) | as chosen | yes (OSM's polygons) | zones only | a pipeline pass over every pack (hours; `data/packs` is held by runs), masks 4 to 25 times bigger |
| JRC Global Surface Water | 30 m Landsat | rivers over about 60 m; no canals | world | a raster download per zone, coarser than the pack mask |
| **The basemap's vector water** (OpenFreeMap, OpenMapTiles `water` layer, from OSM) | vector; at z12 a tile unit is 2.4 m at the equator | yes, Venice and Amsterdam included | world, zones or not | none: tiles the map fetches anyway |

**Pick: the basemap's water at zoom 12.** It is exactly the shore the player sees, so a port clicked on the drawn
shore is on the shore; it costs no data build and works in places with no zone; and the bus tool already fetches
the same server's tiles. Zoom 12 carried the same canals as zoom 14 in every test, at a tenth of the bytes (z14
tiles hold buildings and house numbers):

| Leg | z14 tiles, MB | z12 tiles, MB |
|---|---|---|
| Sumida, Asakusa to Hinode (7.4 km) | 35, 17.7 | 4, 1.2 |
| Seine, Eiffel Tower to Notre-Dame (4.4 km) | 15, 10.8 | 4, 1.2 |
| Bosphorus, Eminonu to Sariyer (17.7 km) | 187, 19.3 | 20, 2.1 |
| Amsterdam canals (2.1 km) | 15, 7.8 | 2, 0.5 |

**Bridges cut the water.** In Amsterdam (and in places in Venice) OSM's water polygons stop at each bridge, so a
boat could not pass under one. The fix in the prototype: a cell under a bridge (the `transportation` layer's
`brunnel=bridge` lines, 8 m either side) counts as water where closing the water by 25 m would fill it. With it the
Amsterdam canal route goes through; without it there is none. A gap that is not a bridge (a quay, a lock wall)
stays land.

## 2. References

**Games.**
- *Cities: Skylines* (Mass Transit, 2017): ferry stops or piers on the shore and a depot; the player draws free
  "ferry pathways" over water (canals come with them) and the line is an ordered list of stops that must loop;
  ferries follow the pathways. [Dev diary 2: ferries](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/cities-skylines-mass-transit-dev-diary-2-ferries.1008076),
  [a player guide](https://www.fandomspot.com/cities-skylines-ferries/). Players report the game's water test
  sometimes refusing paths in wide water. The lesson: drawn paths are a chore; we search instead.
- *Cities: Skylines II* (Bridges & Ports, 2025): passenger ship lines over "seaways" the player builds, with
  "pathfinding failed" the usual complaint ([Paradox news](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/cities-skylines-ii/news/bridges-and-ports-coming-october-29),
  [a fix guide](https://dotesports.com/general/news/how-to-fix-pathfinding-failed-issue-for-the-cargo-harbor-in-cities-skylines-2)).
  The lesson: say plainly when there is no water route, and let the player add a point.
- *OpenTTD*: docks on sloped coast tiles; ships path-find over water tiles, and for years needed **buoys** as
  waypoints on long routes because the pathfinder hit its node limit; OpenTTD 14 (2024) has a new ship
  pathfinder that goes far without them, buoys kept as waypoints.
  [Water transport tiles](https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Manual/Water%20Transport%20Tiles),
  [Building buoys](https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Manual/Building%20buoys),
  [the new ship pathfinder](https://www.openttd.org/news/2024/02/24/new-ship-pathfinder). Our water clicks are
  buoys: the search finds the route, a click steers it.
- *Transport Fever 2*: harbours and automatic ship paths over water; players report zigzags on straight rivers
  and "no path" for big ships in shallow water ([a Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1066780/discussions/0/2639605226291109533/)).
  The lesson: smooth the raw grid path.
- *Mini Metro*: no ferries; rivers are crossed by a limited stock of tunnels or bridges. *NIMBY Rails*: rail
  only, nothing found on boats.

**Routing boats off land.**
- Eurostat's [searoute](https://github.com/eurostat/searoute) (and the Python [searoute](https://pypi.org/project/searoute/)):
  Dijkstra over a fixed network of sea lanes, endpoints snapped to it; for drawing plausible routes, not for
  steering ships. Too coarse for a harbour.
- Path planning for ships on a grid: A* with a **safety distance** and a **turn radius** (An, Aksakalli, Aydogdu
  and Kum, *European Journal of Operational Research* 2013, "Optimal ship navigation with safety distance and
  realistic turn constraints", [record](https://avesis.itu.edu.tr/yayin/54a996fc-fccc-4329-93d1-342983ddc3b8/optimal-ship-navigation-with-safety-distance-and-realistic-turn-constraints));
  later work draws an offset line round the shore, runs A* and smooths the result
  ([Journal of Navigation, multi-factor A*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-navigation/article/abs/ship-path-planning-based-on-multifactor-and-multiscale-a-algorithm/81A2C8495D36C6B59F40230B9E5AC12F)).
  That is the shape of the design: a cost that rises near land (a soft safety distance, because narrow rivers
  cannot keep 400 m), A*, then smoothing with a bend radius.

## 3. The design

### 3.1 Ports

- **A port is a station** (a new station kind), so it gets what stations have: a name from the place list, the
  inspector, walking access, the waiting crowd in the boarding account, changes on foot to any station within
  300 m after a ride, and the passage upgrade to another station. No platforms, track, levels, terminal or park
  and ride. Bus lines stop at ports as they stop at train stations (within 200 m of the route).
- **Placed by the Ferry tool only**: a click on land within 150 m of water puts a port at the nearest point of the
  shore. It has two points: the **landing** on the shore (where it is drawn and where riders walk to) and the
  **berth**, the nearest water with room for a boat (5 m off land, within 300 m), where routes start and end. A
  click with no such water within 150 m is refused ("Too far from water").
- **Shared**: a click on an existing port, while drawing any ferry line, stops there. Any number of lines; no
  berth limit (like bus stops; a lie a player will rarely meet).
- **Cost**: US$10M a port on Construct (a NYC Ferry landing barge runs about that), upkeep US$10k a game day (a
  fifth of a station's). Removing one refunds nothing, as for stations.
- **Demand's position** of a port is its landing, moved inland up to 50 m where the pack's 25 m mask calls it
  water, so walks to it route as walks to any station.

### 3.2 Drawing a ferry line

The bus tool's handling, unchanged where it can be: the Ferry button (key O; F is the Money tab) or the Lines
tab's ferry icon starts it; each click adds a point; dragging, right click to delete, Escape, Enter, double
click, touch and Edit route as for buses. What a click makes (Anita, 2026-11-03: "i dont think we should have 2
construct buttons for ferry btw. just one for placing ferry ports. the ferry will route between them. if we want
ferries to share a port, we just click on an existing port rather than a new port spot."):
- on land by the water: a new port;
- on an existing port (or within 30 m of one): that port;
- on water, or land far from water: nothing (the tag says why).

There are no waypoints the player places: every point of a ferry line is a port, and the router finds the path
between consecutive ports by itself (3.3; the router can take waypoints internally, but the tool sends none).
A line needs two ports at least. Between two ports the route is searched (3.3); while it is searched the leg is
straight and grey, as a bus leg is while its roads load. The tag by the pointer gives the route's length and its
price, as the bus tool's does.

### 3.3 The route

All in the app, in a worker (`workers/ferryWater.worker.ts`), because the tiles are the app's and the bus tool
already works this way; the sim gets the finished points and never searches.

1. **Water raster for the leg**: the zoom 12 tiles under the leg's box (the two points plus 35% of their distance,
   at least 1.5 km round), water polygons filled at pixel centres (no swimming pools, tunnels or intermittent
   water), bridges filled as in section 1. The cell is 5 m, or coarser for a long leg so the raster stays under
   about 2M cells (box side / 1,400). Decoded tiles are kept, as `busRoads.ts` keeps its tiles.
2. **Distance to land** for every water cell (an exact Euclidean distance transform, 0.1-0.4 s).
3. **The search**: A* over 16 neighbours. A cell is passable if it is at least 5 m from land (a 10 m channel) and
   at least one cell. A step costs its length times 1 + 3 x (1 - d/400 m)² where d is the distance to land under
   400 m, so a metre along the shore costs 4 and one 400 m out costs 1. The penalty fades out within 300 m of each
   end, so a boat may leave its berth straight. First the plain shortest path; then the penalised one, and if
   that is over 1.3 times the plain length, again with the penalty at a third, then a tenth, then none. In a
   narrow river every cell is "near land", so the penalty only pushes the boat to the middle; in a harbour it
   keeps about 400 m off where it can; a channel nothing can avoid is used.
4. **Smoothing**: Douglas-Peucker at 25 m (less where the channel is narrow), then an arc at every corner,
   the widest that fits the two legs and stays in water, at most 300 m (tried at 0.6 times the radius until it
   fits). Narrow bends get tight arcs; open water gets wide ones.
5. **Speed along it** (stored per point, 3.4): from the distance to land and the bend.
6. **No water path** (the ports are on separate water, or joined only by a channel under 10 m): the leg stays
   straight and red, and the tag says "No water path found" (Anita, 2026-11-03). The line cannot be made until
   every leg has a route. Since the player places no waypoints, the router widens its search itself before it
   gives up: a wider box, and a coarser plan for long legs (T-2192 builds it; "F1 -> F2 route format").

**One rule** (one boat kind, as one bus kind):
- **320 riders at crush** (2 of demand's 160-rider cars), so the crowding rule and the 30-minute queue apply as
  they are. Between the NYC Ferry boats and an Istanbul ferry; a busy line runs more boats.
- **Speed by room**: 25 km/h (13.5 knots) where the boat is 200 m or more from land, 10 km/h where it is 20 m or
  less (a channel under 40 m: the Grand Canal, the grachten), straight between; through a bend at most
  √(0.3 m/s² x radius) (a 50 m bend 14 km/h, a 20 m one 9). The app stores each route point's speed, the sim
  takes it as it takes a bus's.
- **Acceleration and braking 0.15 m/s²**: 46 s and 160 m to reach 25 km/h, so each stop costs about 46 s more
  than passing it at speed. Trains have 1.0.
- **At ports**: the line's dwell, 60 s for a new ferry line; turnaround 300 s.
- **A new ferry line** starts at a boat every 15, 20 and 30 minutes by level.
- Checks against real trips: Staten Island 8.1 km, about 22 min (real 25); Eminonu to Kadikoy about 16 min
  (real 20); Asakusa to Hinode about 32 min without stops (real 35-40 with them); Grand Canal at 10 km/h.

### 3.5 Money

- **Ports**: US$10M each, paid on Construct; upkeep US$10k a game day.
- **Boats**: US$8M each (a NYC Ferry 350-seat boat is US$7-9M), bought as the line's fleet grows, a fleet of
  their own with spares shared between ferry lines, as buses.
- **Running**: US$40 a boat-km (real, times `ECONOMY` as buses): about four buses' worth for four buses' room,
  dearer per place than rail (a rough reading of NYC Ferry's costs; boats are dear to run).
- **The route**: free, and redrawing it is free (no track); only new ports cost. Removing a ferry line needs no
  confirmation: its ports stay (they are stations) and its boats go to the spares.
- So a two-port harbour line with 6 boats is US$68M, against US$16M for a 10 km bus line and about US$4B for a
  first metro line.

### 3.6 Demand

**Ferries as lines.** A ferry line is a line like any other: ports are stations, in-vehicle time is its run time,
waits are half the headway, changes cost what any change costs. No boarding cost like the bus's 6 minutes (a
ferry is boarded like a train, from a waiting room on the quay). Trips by ferry count as the player's network
(train share, points, stars), as buses do. Ferry kilometres pay the fare curve, the part above the base times
the ferry multiplier (3.7). Where a ferry is the quickest way it wins on these rules alone: Staten Island,
Hong Kong's harbour, the Bosphorus, Manly. This also answers T-280 (Staten Island's walks blocked by water).

**The ferry fans** (Anita's rule, made concrete; needs her OK, below):
- **Who**: 10% of every **visits and attractions** trip (the `social` and `attractions` purposes, and their
  weekend and holiday forms). Not commutes, errands, school, medical, university or airports.
- **What they feel**: each minute aboard a ferry counts 0.1 of a minute (an hour's cruise feels like 6 minutes).
  Their walks, waits, changes and fares count as anyone's. They choose as everyone does (walk first, then the
  network against the car on the ratio curve), so a fan still drives if the port is far or the boats rare.
- **No new trips**: trips are made before the network is known (demand.md), so fans only move trips that exist
  onto ferries; at most 10% of the leisure trips between places a ferry links.
- **How to compute it**: either a second station-to-station search for fans, only while any ferry line exists,
  or (cheaper) composing through ports: a fan's best cost is the lower of the ordinary cost and, over every ferry
  ride p to q, the cost to p, the ride at 0.1, the cost from q (one ferry ride a trip). T-2196 measures both and
  takes the cheaper that meets its bound. A network with no ferry solves to the bit as now.

Does it need Anita's OK? Yes: CLAUDE.md asks that any complicating factor in demand go to her first, and this is
one (a share by purpose, a weight by mode). Her message proposes it; what she OKs is the exact form above. It
holds worldwide (one share, one weight, no country constant) and gets an entry in `docs/demand-worldwide.md`
and a line in `docs/demand-plain.md`.

**In places with a lot of water.** Hong Kong, Sydney and New York are built; Istanbul (Turkey) is not yet. Each has
harbour crossings where the ferry is already competitive for anyone, so most riders there are ordinary ones and
the fans add to them. The risks a player could see: a ferry beating a parallel subway for commuters (fans are
leisure only, so no); a long scenic ferry full of fans while a direct train runs beside it (bounded by the 10%);
ports crowding a shore cell's 6 nearest stations (ports are few; T-2195 checks it on Hong Kong). Demand's
walks over water (3 metres of walking a metre of water, standing for small ferries and bikes) stay as they are.

### 3.7 The fare multiplier row

A row "Ferry" under "High speed" in the Money tab (`MoneyTab.tsx`, as `HsrFareRow`): a stepper by 0.05,
default 1.00x, a reset button once set, shown once a ferry line exists, saved with the game. Rides on a ferry pay
the curve's part above the base times it; a trip that changes pays the base once and the rest times its
km-weighted multiple, ferry and high-speed legs together, in demand and income alike (T-1626's rule). Default
1.00x because the player can raise it; real systems vary (Istanbul's ferry fare is the metro's, NYC Ferry's is
dearer than the subway's).

### 3.8 What the map and panels show

- **The route**: dashed in the line's colour on a white casing, the bus line's width (half a rail line's), drawn
  under rail track; hovering a line lights it as for buses.
- **Ports**: a station circle on the landing with a short line to the berth; a port's name like a station's.
- **Boats**: short pointed hulls in the line's colour, moving along the route like buses.
- **Badges**: a ferry line's badge is a pill (rail a circle, bus a rounded square), "Ferry 1" by default.
- **The ferry panel**: the bus panel, with ports in place of stops: schedule (boats or headway by level), average
  speed, the ports list, the past 24 hours, Edit route, Copy line, Delete line.
- **The Build tab**: the Bus button becomes half width, Ferry beside it (mockup). The Lines tab gets a ferry icon
  beside the bus icon.

### 3.9 Saves

A trailing block (`LFR1`) holds each ferry line's points (each with its kind: port, waypoint or route; its speed),
its ports and whether it is constructed; ports are station records with a kind flag. Routes are never searched
again on load, so a basemap update never moves a saved route. Older saves load as they are (they have no ferries).

### 3.10 Buses over water (T-2178)

A bus route's straight stretches (no road path, a path over 3 times the line, no road within 100 m) are checked
against the same zoom 12 water: one with more than 50 m of water along it (in all) is refused, the leg drawn red
with the tag "No road across the water here", and the line cannot be finished until it is moved or a point
added on a bridge. Road stretches are never refused: they cross on the roads' own bridges. The 50 m lets a
quay or a culverted stream through where the basemap and the roads disagree a little. While the water tiles load
the leg waits, as it waits for roads. Saved routes load as they are; the sim does not check.

### 3.11 Left out

Car ferries in the market (T-1396 and T-279's rule stay as they are), long sea crossings (a leg's raster is
capped, so a 100 km crossing needs points along the way), port capacity, boat sizes, tides and depth.

## F1 -> F2 route format (T-2192, written 2026-11-03)

What the ferry worker (F1) gives the tool, and the tool gives the sim (F2). As with buses, the app routes and the
sim takes the points as they come; it never searches water.

Changed 2026-11-03, after Anita's call that the player places only ports: the tool sends no waypoints, so a line's
points are all kind 1 to start with and kind 2 is not used by the tool (the router keeps it internally); the
refusal is worded "No water path found"; long legs are planned coarse first, so the 40 km cap is gone (150 km now).

- **One polyline a line**, from its first port to its last, through every port in the order placed
  (the first click first). The worker gives longitude, latitude pairs; the tool converts each with
  `lngLatToPack` (game/coords.ts) as `busDraw.ts` does, so **the sim gets x, y in the network's frame**, flat
  `[x0, y0, x1, y1, ...]`, the same frame and convention as `BusRoute.points`. Ground metres between points as
  for buses (the frame's scale).
- **`kinds`, one u8 a point**: 0 a route point; 1 a port's berth, where boats stop (the first and last points are
  always 1); 2 a waypoint, passed without stopping (none from the tool now; a sim should still run through one
  at its speed). A port's point is exactly its berth, so Edit route recovers the ports from the kinds. Which port a kind 1
  point is (its station id) is the tool's and F2's to carry beside it; the worker does not know ports' ids.
- **`speeds`, one f32 a point, m/s**: the most a boat may do at that point from room and bend alone: 25 km/h
  (6.94 m/s) 200 m or more from land, 10 km/h (2.78 m/s) 20 m or less, straight between, and at most
  √(0.3 m/s² x radius) on a bend. Between two points the limit is the lower of their two speeds (points are
  close, at most 25 m apart, wherever the speed changes). **Stops are not in it**: the sim brakes to 0 at kind 1
  points and accelerates away at 0.15 m/s², and runs through a kind 2 point at its speed.
- **Ends**: the route runs first click to last; boats run it out and back as buses do (dwell at kind 1 points,
  turnaround at the ends).
- **Size**: about one point every 10 to 25 m on bends and wherever the speed changes, fewer on straight open
  water; a 20 km line is a few hundred points, within the bus's 8,000.
- **Refusals** come before the sim: a leg with no route gives the tool `reason: "no-water-path"` (the UI words it
  "No water path found"), a port click with no water near `"far-from-water"` ("Too far from water"), a leg over
  150 km `"too-long"`, tiles that would not load `"tiles"` (try again). The sim is never sent a route with a
  refused leg.

The worker's API, for F3: `ferry/ferryWater.ts`, `FerryWater` (`port`, `line`, `probe`); the routing itself is
`ferry/route.ts`, pure.

## F2 -> F3 sim interface (T-2193, written 2026-11-03)

The sim takes the route above as it comes: flat `[x, y, kind, m/s]` per point (kinds 0 route, 1 port, 2
waypoint), with the line's ports as their own `Uint32Array` in route order, one per kind 1 point, each within
50 m of that port's berth. Rules in docs/track-and-service.md 6.8; `TrackApi` exports in `sim/src/track/wasm/ferry.rs`:

- `add_port(x, y, bx, by, name)`: landing on the shore, berth in the water (within 320 m); 0 when added, then
  `last_port()` is its node. `is_port(node)`, `port_berth(node)` [x, y], `ports()`. Ports are stations: they
  appear in `nodes_info` (platform 1) and `line_stops`; `remove_station` removes an unused one.
- `set_ferry_line(id, ports, points, high, medium, low, dwell_s, turnaround_s, colour, name)`: headways in s
  per level; a new line passes 900, 1200, 1800, 60, 300 (`ferry::FERRY_*`). `set_ferry_route(id, ports, points)`
  redraws it (free; a constructed line pays for new ports at once).
- `is_ferry(line)`, `ferry_lines()`, `ferry_route(line)` [constructed, then x, y, kind, m/s per point],
  `ferry_m(line)`; `bus_stops(line)` gives its ports as its service has them (8 numbers a stop, as for buses);
  `ferry_quote(line, ports, points, dwell_s)` [ground m, a boat's run s, US$M of unconstructed ports, 1 if ok].
- Money: `fleet_ferry()`, `set_fleet_ferry(n)`, `boats_needed()`; the game header must save and restore the boat
  fleet as it does `fleetBus` (not done yet). `construct_line`, `construct_all` and `construct_with_buses`
  take ferry lines. `money_params` ends with boat price, boat-km cost, port upkeep a day, port price;
  `upkeep()` ends with ports' upkeep US$M and their count; Construct's quote rows are kind 12 ports, 13
  boats, 14 the route's metres (free).
- Refusals the tool may word: `NotAtPort` (track or an upgrade at a port), `PortBerth` (berth out of reach),
  `LineStops`/`LinePath` for a route that does not fit its ports.
- Trips, positions (`trips`, `run_polyline`, the phases) work as for buses: the boat is drawn along the route.
