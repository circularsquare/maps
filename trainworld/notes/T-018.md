# T-018, T-016, T-017: MapLibre 6, keyframe re-basing, unfocused frame rate

Done 2026-10-08, one session, on the T-002 spike in `app/src/` (`main.ts`, `trains.ts`).
Measured in headless Chrome on Anita's desktop (ANGLE on the RX 6600, real GPU), 1280x800,
dpr 1, Chrome pinned to 2 cores, 10k trains on 500 lines, whole-network view (zoom 10.2).
CPU is the whole Chrome process tree, % of one core, 8 s windows.

## T-018: MapLibre 6.13

`maplibre-gl` 5.24.0 -> 6.13.0. `npm audit`: 0 vulnerabilities (was 1 critical,
GHSA-jrc7-96c5-q579, fixed in 6.4.1+).

What 6 changed that touched us, and what the app does now:

- **ESM only, no default export.** `import * as maplibregl from "maplibre-gl"`.
- **Worker URL.** 6.x builds its worker URL beside its own module (`./maplibre-gl-worker.mjs`),
  which Vite bundles away. `main.ts` imports `maplibre-gl/dist/maplibre-gl-worker.mjs?url` and
  passes it to `maplibregl.setWorkerUrl()`; Vite emits it as `assets/maplibre-gl-worker-*.mjs`
  (508 kB). serve.py serves `.mjs` as `application/javascript`, which a module worker needs.
- **`map.transform` is gone** (Map now composes a Camera; `map._camera.transform` is internal).
  The spike used it for `worldSize` and, in overlay mode, the matrix.
- **Matrix.** Both modes now use the custom layer's `args.defaultProjectionData.mainMatrix`:
  mercator 0..1 to clip space, float64 (MapLibre builds it with `createMat4f64`). Our local-to-
  mercator matrix is composed with it in float64 on the CPU as before, so no `worldSize`. The
  custom-layer `render(gl, args)` signature and its fields are the same as 5.x
  (`modelViewProjectionMatrix` is still world pixels); 6.13 adds optional terrain-draping hooks
  (`renderToTerrainTile`) we do not use.
- **Overlay mode's matrix** comes from an empty custom layer `camera` that copies `mainMatrix`
  on every frame MapLibre draws. That is the camera the basemap was actually drawn with. It costs
  nothing while MapLibre is idle (0 map renders/s while playing in overlay mode, as before).
- **`map._render`** still exists in 6.13 as an internal method. There is no public frame-start
  hook or `prerender` map event, so "Map JS" still wraps `_render`, now behind a
  `typeof === "function"` guard (if a release renames it the readout says "idle"). Render count
  now comes from the public `render` event.
- **Unchanged and checked:** `style.load`, `setPaintProperty` on symbol layers, `getPixelRatio`,
  `triggerRepaint`, the `#zoom/lat/lng` hash (6 reparses it with URLSearchParams; our form loads
  fine), the CSS import.
- **MapLibre's per-frame JS is the same as 5.24**: 2.2 ms (T-002: 1.8-2.3 ms).
- **Globe projection does not work with our layer.** `setProjection({type: "globe"})`: at zoom 3
  the whole network collapses to a speck near Quebec, at zoom 6 and 10.2 nothing draws. Our
  shaders assume mercator and `mainMatrix` means something else under globe. The fix is
  MapLibre's `shaderData.vertexShaderPrelude` (`projectTile`) with per-tile projection data, while
  keeping centimetre precision at street zoom (a mercator 0..1 float32 is only ~1 m). Follow-up
  T-033; the spike stays mercator, so nothing is broken today.

## T-016: keyframe re-basing

Before: one `vec3 (offset, speed, t_ref)` per train with `t_ref = 0` and `u_time` = absolute sim
seconds, so `offset + speed * (t - t_ref)` is evaluated in float32 on numbers that grow without
bound.

Now:

- **Render epoch.** The time uniform is sim seconds since the start of the current game hour
  (`EPOCH_S = 3600` in `trains.ts`), so it is always under 3600 and float32 holds it to ~0.25 ms.
  The clock stays float64 in JS.
- **Re-base when the hour changes.** For every train, in float64 from its master keyframe:
  `offset' = (offset + speed * (epoch - t_ref)) mod (2 * length)`, `t_ref' = 0`. Always from the
  master, never from the previous re-base, so nothing accumulates.
- **Only the changed fields go up.** Train attributes are now three buffers: `a_line` (uint) and
  `a_speed` (float), uploaded once, and `a_kf = (offset, t_ref since epoch)` (vec2), re-uploaded
  with `bufferSubData` on a re-base. Still 16 bytes per train.
- **Clamp.** Float32 `mod()` can land a hair below 0 or above `2 * length`; unclamped, the sample
  index then read the neighbouring line's samples and the train jumped kilometres for one frame.
  The shader now clamps `d` to `[0, length]` and the sample index to the line. This was the cause
  of the old scheme's worst cases below.
- **In the game, the clock worker should do the re-base** (T-025): it owns the keyframes, can
  produce the next hour's `(offset, t_ref)` buffer ahead of time and transfer it; the main thread
  then only uploads.

### Check: GPU positions against a float64 reference

`spike.checkPrecision({t, dt, frames, rebase})` builds a second renderer on its own WebGL2
context (the probe changes GL state MapLibre's context must not see), reads every train's GPU
position back by transform feedback (the same GLSL `trainPos()` the draw shader uses), and
compares it with `cpuTrainPos()`: the same steps in float64 from the master keyframe and the same
float32 samples. Error in real metres; jitter is the largest frame-to-frame change of a train's
error vector, which is what the eye sees as shaking. 10,000 trains, 120 frames per case, `dt` =
0.5 s (30x at 60 fps) and 10 s (600x). `?rebase=0` / `rebase: false` is the old scheme.

| Game time | Scheme | Error p50 | p99 | max | Jitter max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| day 0, 08:20 | absolute | 1.8 cm | 9 cm | 14 cm | 24 cm |
| day 0 | re-based | 0.1 cm | 0.7 cm | 1.4 cm | 2.1 cm |
| day 30 | absolute | 1.5 m | 6.9 m | 9.1 m | 17 m |
| day 30 | re-based | 0.1 cm | 0.6 cm | 1.4 cm | 2.2 cm |
| day 365 | absolute | 20 m | 101 m | 171 m | 262 m |
| day 365 | re-based | 0.1 cm | 0.7 cm | 1.6 cm | 2.1 cm |
| day 30, across 09:00 | re-based | 0.1 cm | 0.9 cm | 1.8 cm | 2.7 cm |

(Before the clamp, the absolute scheme's max was 31 m on day 0, 5.4 km on day 30 and 55 km on
day 365: trains teleporting onto other lines.) Re-based numbers do not change with game time.
The live page at `?day=30&speed=600`, zoomed to street level, shows trains on their lines
(`T-002.png`).

### Cost of a re-base (main thread, live page)

| Trains | Re-base |
|---|---|
| 10k | 0.1-0.8 ms |
| 100k | 0.7-1.5 ms |
| 1M | 9-13 ms (one frame stretched to 26-30 ms) |

Once per game hour: every 6 s at 600x, every 2 min at 30x. Fine at 10k; at a million trains it
belongs in the clock worker.

## T-017: unfocused but visible window

- `blur` -> frames drop to 10 fps (`UNFOCUSED_MS = 100` in `main.ts`); the clock keeps running.
  `focus` -> full rate again. Hidden (`visibilitychange`) -> no frames and the clock freezes, as
  before. Paused -> no frames.
- Layer mode: after each frame the custom layer asks for the next one, by `triggerRepaint()` at
  full rate or from a timer at 10 fps. Overlay mode: the same with its own rAF. MapLibre's own
  frames (panning, tile loads) still happen at their own rate; in overlay mode the trains are
  redrawn on each of them while throttled, so a pan in an unfocused window does not leave the
  trains behind.
- Headless pages report `document.hasFocus() === false`; the checks turned on CDP
  `Emulation.setFocusEmulationEnabled`, dispatched `blur`/`focus`, and simulated hidden by
  overriding `document.visibilityState` and dispatching `visibilitychange` (headless cannot hide
  a page). That exercises the page's handlers; it does not test Chrome's own background throttling.

| State | Layer CPU | Layer frames/s | Overlay CPU | Overlay frames/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Focused, playing | 30-36% | 60 | 17-21% | 60 (map 0) |
| Unfocused, playing | 3-9% | 10.4 | 3-4% | 10.4 (map 0) |
| Hidden | 0-0.6% | 0 | 0-0.6% | 0 |
| Paused | 0.2-0.4% | 0 | 0% | 0 |
| about:blank | 0% | | 0.2% | |

Three runs of layer mode, two of overlay; one hidden window read 16% once and 0-2% on four
repeats, so that one is noise. Focused CPU is higher than T-002's (26% layer, 15% overlay) with
both modes up by a similar share, while MapLibre's per-frame JS is unchanged; the sampler and a
busier machine (other agents building) differ from T-002's run, and 5.24 was not re-measured
side by side, so treat it as unconfirmed rather than a MapLibre 6 regression.
