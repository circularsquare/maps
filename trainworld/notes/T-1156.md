# T-1156: a big Chinese network, measured

Measured 2026-10-24 (perf agent), dev server 6126, headless Chrome, 2 demand workers unless said.
Scripts in the agent's scratchpad (`t1156-build.mjs`, `t1156-measure.mjs`, `t1156-prof.mjs`,
`t1156-patch.mjs`), not kept; logs, JSON and CPU profiles in `artifacts/trainworld/t1156/`.

## The save

`artifacts/trainworld/t1156/all-china.save`, copied to the maps root as `t1156-all-china.save`
(22 kB gzipped; Load a file in the start menu). Built through the edit API in a sandbox started in
Shanghai, then made a normal game with US$1T cash (`t1156-patch.mjs`):
- Metro in China's 24 biggest zones (Shanghai, Beijing, Guangzhou, Shantou, Shenzhen, Suzhou,
  Chongqing, Chengdu, Nanjing, Wuhan, Dongguan, Tianjin, Hangzhou, Xi'an, Shenyang, Quanzhou,
  Zhengzhou, Foshan, Qingdao, Hefei, Xiamen, Harbin, Jinan, Nanchang): 3-12 straight lines each
  through the centre at spread angles, underground on levels -1 to -3, a station every 1.4 km,
  24-30 km long, 10/6/4 trains an hour. 113 lines.
- High speed on level 2 between them: Beijing to Shanghai, Beijing to Shenzhen, Shanghai to
  Ningbo, Nanjing to Chengdu, Zhengzhou to Xi'an, Beijing to Harbin, Guangzhou to Nanning,
  Hangzhou to Shenzhen by the coast, Jinan to Qingdao, Shenyang to Dalian; a station every
  ~60 km, 6/3/2 trains. 10 lines (Tianjin to Beijing refused: StationPorts).
- In all 123 lines, 2,363 stations, 2,266 edges, 123,200 track segments (Japan's T-737 network:
  709 lines, 10,171 stations, 860,000), every line built, 12,500 cars. Its stations and track reach
  169 zones, so demand opens 169 packs.

Building it: 4 minutes 17 s for 5,300 edits, each line constructed as it was finished. Building
everything as blueprint first and constructing at the end was abandoned at 16 lines: each edit
then took 125 ms and growing (see item 3).

## Ranked: what stands between this and "smooth"

0. **Fixed here: every edge's stroke arrays started at 1,024 segments** (`map/network.ts`
   `StrokeBuilder`): a fresh builder per edge allocated eight arrays of ~50 kB and sliced them, so a
   full rebuild (a load, a new anchor) allocated ~110 MB. Now a fresh builder takes the size asked.
   Load: the clock worker's `networkFromState` 7.5 s to 1.9 s, the longest main-thread task in the
   first minute 2,378 ms to 323 ms; edits 2 and 3 after the load 3.3 s and 1.7 s to 0.84 s and
   0.13 s. Same drawn output (same arrays, only their spare capacity changed).
1. **Demand over many zones** (T-898, T-782, new). 169 zones opened for one network: about three
   minutes of downloads and pack opening before the solve is even sent, then a minute to the final;
   2.4-2.6 GB of WASM memory per solving worker, against a 4 GB ceiling a worker cannot pass. All
   745 Chinese zones would not fit at today's memory per zone. Likely gain: the largest of all
   (minutes, and whether all of China can load at all). New task: memory per opened zone (which
   tables a reach-only zone needs, sharing the market between workers), measured natively first.
2. **The draw anchor moving** (T-1028, T-1029; T-1162's freeze on a big network). Arriving in
   another city re-expresses every edge in the new local units: the worker re-renders every stroke,
   the main thread uploads all of it (699-865 ms task), and a click in that window waits (first
   piece in Beijing 1.4-1.6 s). New task: strokes kept per region anchor, or the anchor move done as
   a GPU-side shift plus a background rebuild, so a jump costs a frame. Likely gain: the freeze on
   arrival, about 1.5 s to under 100 ms.
3. **Unbuilt lines quoted on every state** (new). `blueprint_cost_items` runs a trial construction
   of the whole blueprint (to count cars to buy) and `line_build_cost` prices each unbuilt line, on
   every state: with 12 unbuilt lines 137 ms an edit (65% the first, 34% the second), about 8-10 ms
   more per unbuilt line. A player planning a network before building it pays this on every click.
   Fix: quote when a panel asks (the Build tab's quote, an unbuilt line's panel), or only the lines
   an edit dirtied; Rust and the two panels. Likely gain: planning edits back to ~20 ms.
4. **The first edit after a load** (T-1029): 5 s, cold caches in the worker and main thread
   (state 960 ms, buffers 1,182, main 490). Warm the caches in idle time after the load.
5. **Loading progress on the main thread** (new, with T-1161's loading card): `jobProgress` and
   `zoneProgress` and the card's redraws are 5-7% of the main thread through the three-minute load
   (456-553 ms per 10 s); the pack copies per worker 1.6 s and the packs' sha256 0.9 s. Likely gain:
   smoother panning while a big game loads.
6. **Pan** (T-1030, T-056): the pick grid built on the first move after an edit (`CellIndex`, 300 ms
   on the main thread), station labels placed (234-286 ms over the drag), MapLibre drawing CJK glyphs
   in the page (`measureText`, 441 ms over the drag on fresh tiles). The grid wants T-1030's patching;
   the glyphs are T-056's choice (local CJK) and Anita's to revisit.
7. **A train count's first change** (T-1029, T-747's kind): 489-531 ms in the worker's apply twice,
   then 37 ms. Worth a profile: probably the count settling over every line once after the load.
8. **The clock worker's load** (T-1021, new): `TrackApi.load` 4.3 s of WASM for 2,266 edges, the
   masks of every zone the track meets 2.5 s, `buildState` 3.5 s (per line `stop_times`,
   `run_polyline`, names decoded one call each). Native profile first.
9. **A solve after an edit** (T-782): 29-31 s to the final; the market's two sweeps.
10. **A bubble selection's longer trips** (from T-1165): `selection_all_day` 100-180 ms per query
    on each solving worker in China, a sum over every market place.

Found on the way: during the build a solving worker panicked twice ("recursive use of an object
detected which would lead to unsafe aliasing in rust", then "unreachable"), while the network grew
and demand re-solved; the game carried on. A wasm-bindgen borrow held across a re-entrant call:
new task, P2 (the clock worker's version of this is T-1007's recovery).
