# T-818: crowding on long lines (and T-819's capacity outline)

## The complaint

Anita, 2026-10-21: the Eurostar line read red for crowding while its middle stretch was nearly
empty.

## What the colour was

The line panel's dot per demand level (`CrowdDots`, `crowdColour`) and the Lines tab's train
count (`crowdClass`) came from `crowding` in `game/today.ts`: rider-seconds waited after full
trains (each left-behind rider waits the gap to the next departure of that platform) over riders
arriving, every rider the same. `Boarding::crowding` in `sim/src/track/boarding.rs` sums both
per period over every stop of both directions.

## Reproduction

New sandbox game in London on my own Vite (6153), a line built through the tunnel (deep level -2,
200 m platforms, 10 cars, 1,600 riders a train): Paddington, St Pancras, Stratford, Ebbsfleet,
Ashford, Calais, Lille, Roissy, Gare du Nord, Chatelet, Gare de Lyon (465 km), trains 16 / 12 / 6
(2.5, 1.9, 0.9 an hour). Weekday after three crowding rounds: 215,700 boardings a day.

## The rule

A rider's fare on the line is base + per-km x the km they ride. The account has no origin and
destination per rider, only boardings and alightings per stop, and it lets off a share of whoever
is aboard at each stop; so the km a boarder at stop k rides is the next stretch plus, with the
chance of not getting off at the next stop, the km from there (`ride_km`). The waits and arrivals
are summed times those km in the Rust account (`PERIOD_STRIDE` 6 to 8), and the app combines them
with the save's fares, so a fare change needs no new account. The ride in the score (square root
of minutes aboard per rider) stays unweighted.

The figure still goes orange on this line: riders going through the tunnel board at the same city
platforms as the locals and are left behind with them. That is what the account says happens, so
it stays; the hover's stop name says where.

## T-819: lines at capacity in the traffic view

`TrackApi.full_track(level)`: per edge of a track or single-track section at `FULL_RHO`, or of
either track through a full flat crossing, edge, utilisation, trains an hour. The real save has no
full section at any level; what is full there is six flat crossings at high demand (104-113%,
41-45 trains an hour), so crossings are included: the track through them takes no more trains. The worker sends it for the three levels with each state; the main
thread hands the renderer the edges at the level in force while the traffic view is on. A pass
before the lines' casings draws a red band 3 px wider than the casing under the line strokes on
those edges (a per-segment attribute, rewritten when the set changes); a first try that only
coloured the casing red vanished against red lines (the 1, 2, 3).

Real save, 07:00 (high): 12 edges full, all from the six flat crossings (G, L, 2, 6, 7, A, D,
N). Cost with the outline on against off, 60 frames each, twice: GPU 1.61 against 1.50 ms a frame,
CPU frame median unchanged (16.6 ms, vsync), rewriting the attribute 2.7 ms when the set changes
(107,646 line segments). Hover on the G between Nassau Av and Metropolitan Av: "subway G / 68,800
riders per day / Track at capacity at high demand". Screenshots `artifacts/trainworld/t819-light.png`,
`-light-hover`, `-light-z12`, `-light-z17`, `-dark`, `-dark-hover`, `-dark-lineview` (no outline in
the Line view).
