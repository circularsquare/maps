# T-2625 The network diagram condenses in two dimensions at continent scale

After T-2609's cut, land in one network's column and another's row stayed at the floor, so on Anita's North America
save the networks were still small spots. Rule now: docs/ui-diagram.md "The rubber sheet", the floor's fall. Code:
`sheet.ts` (`farP`, `farKm`, `farMin`, `farS`, `scheduleFar`; the block before the field, `LfN`).

## What was wrong, and why the fold guard was not it

- A map can draw a network S times bigger than land far off only with uneven stretch in the ring round it. The
  cheapest such map is the radial stretch r^(1-p) (scale falling as r^-p), whose stretch is 1 / (1 - p) everywhere:
  p 0.5 is 2:1, inside the sheet's fold guard (2.33:1). Over a ring 15 times the network's size that buys a scale
  ratio of 15^0.5 ~ 4. So the guard was not what held the networks small; the plan was (the floor everywhere).
- T-2609's tries lowered the floor with distance from the floor itself at 25 km. The networks ask 1.5-2 times the
  floor, so the ring round each network had to fall from the network's scale to the floor in a few km and then on: it
  asked for more than 2:1 and the sheet answered by shrinking the networks (to 0.5-0.8 of their ask). Measured here
  too: floor falling as (25 km / d)^0.5 gave a box share 0.126 with Los Angeles at 3.0 of its 4.8, Seattle 1.9 of
  4.8; with the guard loosened to 4:1 (`foldFree` 0.6, since removed) nothing better and 5 folds; all Japan stopped
  settling (stations moving 5 pitches a round at the end, 1.8 before).

## The design

- **The floor falls from the networks' own scales.** Per lattice node: the best over network stations (2 others
  within 10 km, the cut's) of the station's own asked scale times (1 + d / 2 km)^-0.5, carried node to node like a
  chamfer distance (four sweeps), then never above the old floor and never under 0.1 of it. So the plan round a
  network is the radial power law the sheet can draw, starting from that network; far land and water go to about
  0.9 pitches a km instead of 1.4.
- **A station of no network** (a lonely stop on a long line) asks at most the floor where it is, so it makes no
  bubble.
- **Networks hold their size**: the size pull on land near lines is 3 times as strong on such a map (`farS`), and a
  second "raise" round (`scheduleFar`) gives cores that came out small their wish again.
- Country maps only (600 km across or more, with network stations); a city map is the same to the bit.

## What it does not do

- Far land is drawn about 4.5 times smaller than the networks, not 10-20 times: past p 0.5 the ring needs more uneven
  stretch than the guard allows and the West's networks shrink. A further step would need the guard looser where
  nothing is drawn and a way to keep networks at their ask (proposed, not filed: the handoff offered T-2724 to
  another agent too).

## Looked outward

- Wu, Niedermann, Takahashi, Roberts and Nöllenburg, "A survey on transit map layout: from design, machine and human
  perspectives" (CGF 2020, https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/files/pub/metromapsurvey-2020.pdf; its 2022 sequel on
  computing them, https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07301): focus+context and space-efficient layouts are least-squares
  energies over a mesh (Wang and Chi), as the sheet is.
- Wang and Chi, "Focus+context metro maps" (TVCG 2011, https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/bitstream/11536/14562/1/000296241900086.pdf):
  focal stations get room, the rest is drawn closer, solved by least squares; the sheet's basis (notes/T-2566.md).
- Keahey and Robertson, "Nonlinear magnification fields" (InfoVis 1997,
  https://legacy.cs.indiana.edu/ftp/techreports/TR460.pdf): a distortion described by its field of scalar
  magnification, and a field is not always reachable by a map. That is the point here: the plan must be a field a map
  with bounded stretch can draw.
- Bounded distortion maps: Lipman 2012; Kovalsky, Aigerman, Basri and Lipman, "Large-scale bounded distortion
  mappings" (SIGGRAPH Asia 2015, https://shaharkov.github.io/LargeScaleBD.html); Garanzha et al., "Foldover-free maps
  in 50 lines of code" (SIGGRAPH 2021). They bound each triangle's stretch (condition number) and project onto that
  set; the sheet's fold guard is a soft form of the same. With stretch held to K, a radial map can change scale only
  as r^(1 - 1/K) (the quasiconformal radial stretch), which sets how fast the floor here may fall.
- Gastner, Seguy and More, "Fast flow-based algorithm for creating density-equalizing map projections" (PNAS 2018,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.07625): cartograms reach any area plan by diffusing density, at the cost of shapes
  everywhere; not used, since the networks' own shapes must stay.
