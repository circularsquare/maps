# T-2488 spike: zone borders drawn "like the lines"

Anita's ask (2026-11-04, after T-2464): borders hug the network like a skin; could a border be drawn the way a rail
line along it would be (smooth, orthogonal and diagonal), by the same logic; failing that, a hull-like outline with
more room from the lines; and ideally every built zone plus its neighbours, distorted to fit. Spike only: nothing
landed. Code in tree `diaglines` (`.tw-trees/diaglines`): `app/src/diagram/borderLines.ts` (new),
`app/src/diagram/borders.ts` (an options argument and four exports), `checks/t2488-borders.mjs`,
`checks/t2488-inlayout.mjs`, `checks/t2488-shots.mjs`. Pictures and figures: `artifacts/trainworld/diaglines/`.

## Three things tried

1. **Borders inside the layout** (the literal idea; `t2488-inlayout.mjs`). Each real border arc, sampled every median
   real hop (Japan every 3), added to the graph as a line with no stops, laid out and routed with the rail lines.
2. **Borders routed as lines on the finished drawing** ("lines"; `borderLines.ts`). The rail drawing is fixed. The
   real boundaries between zones (and the zones' coasts and outer edges) are split into arcs where three zones meet;
   each rail hop a real boundary crosses an odd number of times is crossed once, square to its bundle, at about the
   real share of the hop (clamped to 25-75%), from the side the boundary comes from. Between crossings the border is
   routed on a grid (1 pitch) as a line would be: runs at 0, 45 and 90 degrees, a 45 degree bend costing 8 pitches of
   length and a 90 degree one 16, cost rising within 2.5 pitches of a casing or pill, never through one (the search
   widens 3 times, then may cross at a high cost), near a course. The course is the real arc laid out as a chain (the
   layout's stage A on the border alone: each step its real offset through the local map of the nearest stations,
   weak pull to T-2168's warp, each point kept on the side of each near station it is on in reality), simplified to
   2 pitches. Corners rounded as T-2464's. Neighbouring zones (no stations) are drawn by the borders they share with
   zones that have stations; their far edges were tried and left out (concentric rings round the network).
3. **The fallback** ("wide"): T-2464 with the region 5 pitches past the casings (2 now), gaps and bays under about 60
   pitches closed (8), the outer edge fitted within 3 pitches (0.8), corners of 3 pitches (2).

## What was learned

- **In the layout, borders float.** Nothing ties a border to the stations but the pushes that keep any two nodes
  apart, and the layout shrinks long hops (length to the power 0.3), so the network comes out small inside borders
  drawn at their own scale (`nyc-all-0.6-inlayout.png`). Their shapes do come out smooth and octilinear: the routing
  makes nice borders. To place them, every border point needs terms tying it to the stations near it (a warp inside
  the solve), and border points must be kept out of cities, blocks, lonely hops, the pitch and the separation, all of
  which treat them as stations now. Crossings are not represented at all (the layout has no crossing nodes; pieces
  cross freely), so nothing keeps a border crossing the right hop; splitting rail hops at crossings would add key
  nodes and bends to the rail lines. Cost even naive: layout time x1.4 (Japan) to x22 (Toronto), the rail drawing
  changed (more pieces and bends, longer lines, a different pitch). And zone outlines reach the worker after the
  first layout, so it would wait for them or lay out twice.
- **On the drawing, it works where geography and drawing agree.** Land borders between metros or prefectures that
  lines cross cleanly come out as Anita pictured: smooth runs, crossing each line once between the right two stations,
  clear of the lines (New York's border with New Haven, Osaka with Nara by Ikoma). Far from the network they keep the
  real shape, squeezed.
- **It fails where they disagree, which is near dense networks and coasts.** The diagram stretches a city core 10-50
  times against its suburbs; the real boundary passing a core (Toronto's lakeshore, Brooklyn's coast, the Hokuriku
  coast) has to be bent through that, and no warp tried (T-2168's blend, a least squares chain, side terms, a cap on
  magnification) keeps every station on its side: 9% of Japan's stations end up outside their zone, mostly coastal
  and between blocks. The coarse outlines make it worse (they cut corners at coasts, 59 Japanese and 32 Toronto
  stations lie outside every outline). Where the border is right it can still sit oddly (a coast squeezed into a bay
  of the network because the real coast lies there).
- **The fallback is cheap and safe.** Same guarantees as T-2464 (every station inside its own zone), twice the room
  from the lines, bays filled; still an outline of the network, not of the zone.

## Recommendation

Do not put borders in the layout. Draw real borders as routed lines only where a border crosses the network or lies
clear of it, and keep the network-shaped outline (the fallback) where real geography would fight the drawing:

1. Land the fallback now (small: the options in `borders.ts` with the "wide" values; T-2464's check passes as is).
2. Then, if wanted, the hybrid: real borders between zones that have stations routed as in `borderLines.ts`, taken
   only where they keep every station on its side (checked per arc; an arc that fails falls back to T-2464's border
   for that pair of zones), coasts left to the fallback outline (or to the water task, T-2169). Neighbouring zones
   shown by their shared borders with built ones (their names could go beside them, T-2438). Needs: the per-arc check
   and fallback, routing kept under about 2 s for Japan (now 4.8 s), the borders redone when outlines arrive (as now),
   docs. About a day of agent work; the part that is not known to work is how many arcs survive the check in Japan.

## Looking outward

- Metrochrones (Dirsch and Haunert, 2023): region boundaries on an octilinear transit map routed on an octilinear grid
  so they include the reachable stations and exclude the rest: the closest published match to item 2
  ([summary](https://timetableworld.com/?p=29179)).
- Octilinear metro maps on grid graphs (Bast, Brosi, Storandt, EuroVis 2020): lines routed as shortest paths with bend
  costs on an octilinear grid, the model for the router here
  ([slides](https://ad-publications.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/EuroVis_octi-maps_slides.pdf)).
- Territorial outline schematization keeping topology and area, with restricted orientations (Buchin, Meulemans,
  Speckmann, ACM TSAS 2016, [record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145%2F2818373)); topologically safe curved
  schematization keeping points on their side by a Voronoi diagram (van Goethem et al., Cartographic Journal 2013,
  [record](https://heibib.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/search/Record/1746584645)); chorematic outlines and octilinearity
  (Reimer and Meulemans 2011, [pdf](https://kartographie.geo.tu-dresden.de/downloads/ica-gen/workshop2011/genemr2011_Reimer.pdf)).
  These schematize outlines alone (or with points), not inside a distorted network drawing; none found that does.
- London's zone bands (notes/T-2168.md) remain the model for borders decided by stations, which T-2464 already is.
