## 1. Worldwide ranking

`venv\Scripts\python.exe -I trainworld\notes\T-4364\rank_zones.py --jobs 4` (or `powershell -File
trainworld\notes\T-4364\rank_zones.ps1`, which claims 4 cores under the run's own pid; about 10 minutes at 4). Output
`artifacts/trainworld/zone-ranking.csv`, every registered pack (4,830), sorted worst first.

- **Compactness**: area over convex hull of the pack's own boundary (`data/packs/<id>.boundary.geojson`, as the game draws
  it), in Mollweide as world_plan. `hull` is the merge rule's measure; `land_hull` divides by the land in the hull only
  (the hull less OSM's coastline sea), so coasts, bays and islands do not count against a zone. The ranking uses land_hull.
- **Crossing**: the share of commutes with an end in the zone that cross its line, from the joint commute solve's figures
  in the pack header: `gravity.trips` (within) and `gravity.joint.commuters_out` / `commuters_in` (long commutes included):
  (out + in) / (within + out + in). Commutes only (not everyday outings or market trips), and it rises as zones get
  smaller, so small zones beside big ones rank high; that is the point (a remnant beside a city is the usual misfit).
- **Score**: the mean of the two percentiles (1 = worst). 41 packs have no joint solve (no crossing, no score): islands and
  lone packs. 19 outlines first failed on invalid geometry (Kolkata, London, Essex ...); fixed (make_valid per feature),
  all 4,830 now have a hull.

### China and prefectures

Source: GADM 4.1 CHN level 2 (368 prefecture-level features, from 2022; some are dated, e.g. Chaohu, abolished 2011;
licence free for non-commercial use, used here only to measure). geoBoundaries' CHN ADM2, which the plan has on disk, is
county-level (2,391 features), so the plan knows no prefectures today. Each plan unit went to the prefecture holding most
of its area (`notes/T-4364/scripts/prefectures.py`).
- 721 Chinese rows (84 metros, 637 zones): 430 (60%) have 90% of their people in one prefecture (zones 358 of 637).
- 344 prefectures with people: 277 (81%) are split over 2+ rows each holding 10% of them. That is mostly size, not bad
  lines: the median prefecture has 3.2M people against the zone cap of 2.5M; 217 prefectures are over the cap and 38 under
  the 1M floor.
- 39 rows hold 10% or more of their people in another province (Tangshan, Wei/Neihuang, Longchang, Panzhou, Baoying ...),
  though zones merge within their province; most are zones under 80% of the floor, which the rule lets cross.
- The clearest misfits are a city's own districts in a neighbour's zone (Guilin, Wuhan's Huangpi, Doumen, Qingxin,
  Yongning, Liaoyang's districts), which the proposals above take; the ranking finds more of the same kind.

**A full re-plan by prefecture** would mean: a prefecture source in world_plan (GADM is dated and non-commercial; OSM's
admin_level 5 relations would be the global choice), a CHN rule grouping units by prefecture as Korea's `group_level` does
by si/gun/gu, and a decision on size: whole prefectures average 4M, so either the cap rises to about 5M (fewer, bigger
packs, about 344 rows instead of 721: more memory per zone, longer builds) or big prefectures split at the cap (much of
today's pattern again). Then `zones --countries CN --replan` and a metros replan, all 721 rows rebuilt (recorded steps 12.3
core-hours today), most ids new (zone_successors for every removed id), and one join per solve region: 28 regions at
about 1 hour each (China's 11-row batch join on 10-29 took 139 min), so 1.5-2 days of machine time at 4-8 cores, plus
place names, zone-names (China's were checked by hand in T-847) and start tiers. Not proposed: the targeted edits fix
what players noticed for about a tenth of that.
