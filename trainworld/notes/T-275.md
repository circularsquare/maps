# T-275: routed walking and driving

Done 2026-10-13. Anita: "maybe some real pathfinding for driving and walking would be nice? do we
already have that? can people currently walk across the hudson? seems like a kinda prereq. maybe
tolls after?" Before this, every walk and drive was the straight line x 1.3, so a Hoboken home
walked to a Manhattan station 2 km away across the Hudson, and drives crossed rivers and bays
anywhere. The design as built is in docs/demand.md 4.3 ("Routed walking and driving"); this file
keeps the working, the numbers and the checks.

## What was built

- `pipeline/routes.py <city>` (any pack, any country given its OSM extracts), run after
  `build_city.py` and after anything that re-solves a pack's zones. Two stages, `--stage drive`
  (Python) and `--stage walk` (the sim's `pack_walk` bin), `all` by default. `--check` lists which
  packs have current routes.
  - OSM ways from Geofabrik extracts, one per first-level region of the pack plus, for US packs,
    every state whose TIGER polygon meets the pack's water grid (bridges and roads just over a
    state line: Enfield CT for Springfield, Pennsylvania for Washington). Read with GDAL's OSM
    driver (pyogrio, already in the venv; no new installs), cached as `data/raw/osm/*.ways2.npz`.
    The .osm.pbf files are kept with `--keep-pbf`, deleted otherwise (2.3 GB for the 13 states).
  - Walking barrier raster, 50 m: a pixel is a barrier when 3 of its 4 water-mask pixels are
    water; every OSM way a person may walk on that is a bridge (footways, paths, streets; not
    motorways, not trunk roads unless `foot=yes`, not `foot=no`) is burnt back in as land,
    4-connected, its ends carried 60 m onto the bank; land enclosed by water smaller than 40
    pixels (0.1 km2: rocks, marsh hummocks, pier ends) becomes water, so a cell centred off the
    shore joins the real shore.
  - Drive graph: every public road but service ways (motorway to residential, living streets),
    junctions only (points shared by ways, way ends), parallel edges deduplicated, clipped to the
    pack's grid plus 20 km; pieces in components under 200 junctions are not joined to. Each
    place joins its nearest road point (not junction) whose straight line crosses no water,
    `ROAD_DETOUR` x that line, then along the road to the edge's two junctions.
  - Per zone, from a representative cell (nearest the commuter-weighted centre) on a virtual
    node: Dijkstra (scipy, limit 50 km) to every cell of the zones within 6 squares; the factor to
    a zone is its cells' road metres over their straight metres, commuter-weighted; the two
    directions averaged. Beyond 6 squares: one full Dijkstra per supercell (4 x 4 squares per
    region, its heaviest zone) to every zone, aggregated to supercell pairs. Stored as bytes,
    `round(64 ln f)`.
  - `pack_walk` searches each zone's walk with the game's own search and writes the walk factors
    to the zones within 2 squares.
- `sim/src/demand/routes.rs`: `Routes` (from the pack; dense tables per zone, slot and region),
  `WalkGrid::field` (Dial's bucket queue over the window's pixels, 8 neighbours, no corner
  cutting, side 70 and corner 99), `Field::eff` (the straight line x path / free path between
  the same pixels: exactly the straight line where nothing is in the way), `station_walks`
  (thread-local cache by pack and station position), tests (river with and without a bridge, a
  diagonal line of water, a station on a pier).
- Kernel: `Params::routes` and `road_routed_scale`; `drive_factor`, `drive_min_f`;
  `build_access` takes each station's exceptions (cells whose walk goes round water, with their
  metres, or cut off) and `build_subzones` uses them; `Gravity::walk_share_at` / `walk_share_pair`
  for the walk share between zones; `mode_choice`'s zone loop gets the drive factor and walk
  share per zone pair; everyday's sample walk share and pair drive, the trip explorer's walk share
  and drive, and `flows`' walk end all go through the same. `DemandApi::new` reads the routes.
  The regional market (intercity.rs) is unchanged: straight line x 1.3 (its redesign is T-276).

## Decisions and why

- **Distance from the roads, speed from the density model.** The road's length comes from the
  routes; its speed stays `place_min_per_km` (T-090), as Anita asked unless routed speeds were
  clearly better. They were not tried: OSM maxspeed is sparse in the US and congestion is what
  the density curve stands for.
- **One scale on the routed factor, 1.3 / 1.210 = 1.074** (`road_routed_scale`). Shortest-
  distance routes over the roads are shorter than 1.3x on average (commute- and distance-weighted
  over every zone pair: New York 1.210, Philadelphia 1.177, Boston 1.168, Washington 1.214,
  Baltimore 1.232, Providence 1.262, New Haven 1.223, Hartford 1.226, Springfield 1.246), while
  the density speeds were fitted to ACS car commute times on New York with 1.3 (road_speed.py).
  Unscaled, New York's drives got 7% faster everywhere and commute rail fell from 19.67% to
  18.43%, mostly in the city (Kings 41.7% to 39.8%, Bronx 38.3% to 35.6%), not where water is.
  Scaled once, with New York's own level, New York keeps its fitted level and every pack keeps its
  differences from New York (Boston's roads 3.5% shorter, Providence's 4% longer). It also stands
  for drivers taking faster, longer roads than the shortest.
- **Residential streets are in the drive graph.** Without them (first try) rural places joined
  the network kilometres away: Springfield's near pairs had a median factor 1.66, 27% over 2.
- **Window sizes**: drive 6 squares (to 13 km), where water detours are large against the
  distance; supercells beyond, where the factor varies slowly (a supercell splits by region, so
  the Hudson's two banks are apart; the Sound's two New York shores share supercells only where
  it is narrow, by the bridges). Walk 2 squares: the walk share is 0.3% at 4 km.
- **Walking transfers between stations stay straight x 1.3** (T-161's 300 m and complexes). On
  the real New York network none of the 80 station pairs within 300 m has water between them,
  complexes are the player's own links, and every population must build the same graph from the
  same stations or their strategy searches stop being shared (T-119): the commute APIs of two
  cities and the market would each search again. Revisit if a city with transfers across water
  appears.
- **Ferries are not walks or drives** (the Staten Island Ferry, NY Waterway, Boston's water
  taxis): a ferry is its own mode, not modelled (T-280).
- **A pack whose zones change loses its routes silently, not with an error**: `Routes::from_pack`
  checks the zone count and a sum over zone positions (`zones_check`) and uses nothing if they
  differ, so a gravity re-solve never stops a city opening; `routes.py --check` reports it.

- Walking across the Hudson, the East River away from its bridges, the Kills and Newark Bay is
  gone; North Hudson's rail share falls from 2.8x ACS to 2.0x, Bergen's from 1.7x to 1.6x.
- The Hudson waterfront moves only from 26.9% to 28.1% (ACS 40.9%): by road Hoboken to Midtown

- County error 2.41 to 2.49 points: the city's counties lose about half a point each to drives
  through the grid being shorter than 1.3x; the waterfronts gain.

## Room for tolls (T-270)

Not built. The way in: `read_ways` keeps one more flag per way from `toll=yes` (OSM tags the
Hudson tunnels, the Verrazzano, the turnpikes and most US toll bridges); the drive stage runs
each zone's Dijkstra a second time with tolled edges dropped and stores, in the same window
layout, whether the shortest route uses a toll and how much longer the toll-free one is
(`drive_toll`, a byte per pair). The kernel would then price a pair as the cheaper of the tolled
route plus its toll in perceived minutes and the free route. Toll amounts are not in OSM for
most roads; a per-country flat charge per crossing (US: about $15 eastbound on the Hudson
crossings, T-171 1.4) is the simple start.
