# T-2988 Turnback loops at a line's end: scope and design

Agent loopend, 2026-11-10, read-only on code. Anita's asks are in TASKS.md's T-2988 line; her screenshot is
`artifacts/trainworld/t2988-anita-pittsburgh-loop.png` (line 5 in Pittsburgh: Moore Av, then a junction, then a
loop through Louisa Way and Brandon Way whose unused track runs from Brandon Way back into the junction). Her two
later notes (2026-11-10, through the orchestrator): a **manual mode** listing every stop a train makes in order
("A 1, B 2, C 3, D 4, C 5, B 6, A 7 ... then they can just remove that stop one way ... or loop A->B->C->D->B->A ...
we just pathfind between every pair of stations, avoiding reversals"), and "we can probably also do the easier way
to add a little terminal loop, to be clear, just think it would be good to have a catch-all manual way". So: both,
the quick loop first, the manual mode as a later stage.

Experiment: `scratchpad/loopend/balloon.mjs` (new game, Brooklyn, `balloon_fixture`'s shape built with the edit
API: A, B, M on a stem, junction J, a loop through K). Screenshot `artifacts/trainworld/t2988-turned-today.png`.

## 1. What a balloon is today

**In the sim it already exists, fully.** A line whose stops are `[A, B, M, K, M, B, A]` (out along the stem, round
the loop, back down the stem stopping again, closing at A) is a T-798 balloon: `loop_kind` sees the closing turn at A,
`check_line` and `match_stops` accept a station twice (not twice in a row), each visit is its own route node in
demand, the boarding account runs it, the closing stand at A is the turnaround. T-2981's One way turns run 1 off, so
every train goes round the loop the same way and **the stem is still served both ways**: that is exactly "the loop
part one way, the tail both ways", with no new sim work. Sim tests already cover it: `a_balloon_turns_back_at_its_first_stop`
and `a_one_way_balloon_turns_once_a_round_trip` (`track/tests.rs`). T-2991's chevrons are drawn only where a path runs
once, so the loop gets them and the stem does not. A loop with no station on it works too: `[A, M, A]` runs M, round
the loop, past M, back to A (`stop_edit_tests.rs`).

Measured in the game (the experiment): open line `[A, B, M, K]` round trip 1,216 s; the turned form
`[A, B, M, K, M, B, A]` one way, lap 1,232 s (16 s more, and no turnaround at K); the line panel shows it with
Direction, Lap, "Turning at the end", and every visit as a stop row. `orderStops` also took `[A, B, M, K, M, A]`
(B skipped on the way back), lap 1,148 s.

**But a player cannot build it.** Every way of adding a stop refuses a station the line already stops at
(`best_slot`: `old.contains(&station)`; `passed_stations` leaves stops out; the T-2895 drag only moves rows). The
only loop a click makes is T-798's: the **first** stop's marker says "Click to close the loop". In Anita's case
that is Terrace Village, at the other end of the line. The experiment confirms the tier: A is `close` on the open
line, and the click gives `[A, B, M, K, A]`, a balloon whose way back runs express past every stem station. It runs
both ways (the train default), so each direction needs its own trains: round trip 2,126 s against 1,216 s. That is
worse than no loop at all, and nothing about it says "turn round the loop at the far end". The stop just before the
branch (Moore Av in her case) is a plain stop: clicking it removes it.

## 2. Design

### 2a. The quick terminal loop (first cut)

**Where the offer shows.** In stop editing, for each end of an open line (and for the free end of a line already
turned at one end), the worker looks for a loop: track leading on from the end stop **in the direction of travel**
(no turning back there) that comes back onto the line's own path, arriving so the train carries on back toward the
other end (no turning back at the junction either; a junction facing the wrong way gives nothing). The stop marked
is **the last stop before the branch**: the line's last stop whose place on the path comes before the point where
the loop rejoins (Moore Av in her case; the end stop itself when the loop leaves beyond it and has no station of its
own). Its marker draws as the stop it is, and its hover says what the click does (drafts for her below). This is her
gesture: "i click the point right before it branches ... it should recognize that oh we could close this into a loop".

**What the click does.** One edit, one undo: the stops become the forward stops, then the stops before the rejoin
point again in reverse (the end stop not repeated), closing at the line's first stop; the line is set One way. Her
line: `[Terrace Village ... Moore, Louisa, Brandon, Moore, Michigan ... Forbes, Terrace Village]`. Track facts decide
the rest as now (a balloon closing at Terrace Village turns there as a terminus).

**Both ends.** A line turned at one end is a balloon closing at its first stop. Turning the other end too makes the
closing hop go round that end's loop instead of reversing: the stops are rotated so the first stop is a stem stop
just past the second loop, and the line becomes a T-798 **circle** (closes carrying straight on), one way, stem stops
listed twice. The same search and the same op, from the other end; demand wraps a circle already.

**Not much longer or slower.** Offered only when the lap with the loop is at most **2 minutes or 10% longer**
(whichever is more) than the line's round trip now. A loop replaces a turnaround (3 min by default) and the run back
over the loop's first stretch, so most real loops come out shorter, and the rule only stops detours. Behind it the
route search's own 600 s reversal penalty already makes a much longer loop lose to reversing (T-1273 then folds the
line back to a plain end, `long_balloon` test), so a later reroute cannot quietly keep a bad loop.

**Cheap.** The search runs inside `stop_tiers`, which the worker already runs after each stop edit, off the click's
path: one bounded Dijkstra from each open end (two at most), stopped at the time budget above, so it stays local.
`stop_tiers` already runs a route search per station in the city, so two more are noise. Adding stops is untouched:
`insert_stop` does not search for loops.

**Undo and opening.** Ctrl+Z takes the whole turn back. On a turned line the same branch stop's marker offers the
reverse ("stop looping"), which cuts the stops back to the forward ones and leaves the end open. The closing row's
cross in the stop list ("Open the loop" today) would do something useless on a turned line (it leaves the reversed
stem as a long tail), so on a turned line it does the same as the marker.

**After the turn.** A new station clicked on the stem goes in on both passes (`best_slot` adds the second visit
where the path passes it again); one clicked on the loop goes in once. A stem stop clicked off goes from both passes
(the map click already removes every visit). T-798's "Click to close the loop" on the first stop is kept only where
it makes a **circle**; where it would make an express-return balloon, the turn at the far end replaces it (it is the
same line when the stem has no stops).

**The panel.** Nothing new beyond what T-798 and T-2981 show: the Direction row (One way set by the turn), Lap,
"Turning at the end", and the stop list with every visit as a row (as the experiment shows; Anita's manual-mode list
in 2b numbers them). Folding the two passes into one row each is possible later but is not needed for the first cut.

**Hover drafts** (for Anita's reading when it is built; marker tags, under the station name):
- "Click to loop back here" (the branch stop; the click turns the line round the loop)
- "Click to stop looping back here" (the same stop on a turned line)

### 2b. Manual mode: every stop in order (later stage)

**How it maps onto today's line model.** Her list is a one-way loop line whose stops are the whole cycle, kept in the
player's order. All three parts exist: a closed stop list (T-798), One way (T-2981: one run, run 1 off), and an order
the game never re-sorts (`LineData::hand`, T-2895, with `orderStops` routing each leg between consecutive stops and
refusing an order that would turn back at a junction or a passed station). So "manual mode" on a line `[A, B, C, D]`
writes `[A, B, C, D, C, B, A]`, hand ordered, one way; each row is one visit. Skipping C on the way back is removing
row 5; "loop A->B->C->D->B->A" is removing row 5 where track allows. Each leg is the route search's, avoiding
reversals (600 s penalty) and allowing them only at a stop or a bare track end, as now.

**Sim.** Runs and timetables need nothing new: one run, departures from A at the headway, every reversal inside the
run standing the turnaround (T-797), round trip = the lap, fleet steps by 1. Two rule changes:
- a reversal at a stop on a manual line is a **terminus** (platform and turnback, as a line end now), not the
  stopgap limiter of 12 an hour a side; otherwise a plain out-and-back written as `[A ... D ... A]` would be capped at
  12 trains an hour at D, which a line ending at D is not;
- the large amber U-turn icon (there because "most reversals are accidents") is left off a manual line's reversals.
Skip-stop is new to the game: today's two-run line has one stop list for both directions, so "C one way only" can't
be said at all; in manual mode it is just a visit that isn't listed.

**Demand and the panel for a stop served one way.** It is one visit in the cycle, so it has one route node: riders
board or leave there going that way only; a trip the other way rides through the turn and pays the stand, or walks
or drives, which the shortest-path search already chooses. The stop list row shows that visit's boardings; the
station panel already sums a line's visits (`complexLive.ts`).

**The list.** The stop list, while editing a manual line, shows every visit numbered, as in her mock. Rows drag
(T-2895), a row's cross removes that visit only (`drop_stop`, which already refuses a removal that leaves a station
twice in a row or a reversal on no stop), and a new control puts a visit in after a chosen row (a "+" between rows,
then a station click on the map), which is the one big new piece. A toggle at the top of the list switches modes
(draft: "Edit every stop in order"); switching back is offered only when the list is a plain out-and-back that folds
into an open line. The Direction row is hidden in manual mode (the list is the direction).

**The quick loop inside manual mode.** A turned line already is a manual-form line, so the loop marker becomes a
shortcut that writes the loop into the list (rows 5 to 6 of her mock replaced by the loop), and the player can then
adjust any row. Both entries end in the same line.

**Risks for 2b.** Trains enter and leave service at A only, so at the start of the day the far end waits one run for
its first train (real-ish, but visible); direction words in the schedule and trip views ("towards X") assume two runs
and need a look for a one-way line that serves both ways; long lines get long lists (a 30-stop line becomes 59 rows).

## 3. Other games

- **Transport Fever 2**: a line is a cycle of stops in order; players write an out-and-back as A>B>C>D>C>B>A, the
  vehicle running from the last stop back to the first
  ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1066780/discussions/0/3127163422389936708)). Each leg is pathfound;
  players build balloon loops as track to turn trains without the default flip in the platform (forum talk, not
  checked against the game). Her manual mode is this model.
- **Cities: Skylines**: a line is closed by clicking back to its first station; to use a terminal loop behind a
  station a player clicks A, B, then A again
  ([Steam thread, 2015](https://steamcommunity.com/app/255710/discussions/0/613956964594021857)). Like T-798's
  close-at-first-stop, with no help finding the loop.
- **NIMBY Rails**: a line is an ordered list of stops (platforms or waypoints), run in order and repeated; reversals
  happen at a stop in the list ([wiki: Stop](https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/Stop);
  [Steam: backtracks at last stop](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1134710/discussions/0/4039228371164050216)). Again the
  manual model; loops are the player's to list.
- **Mini Metro**: lines are paths whose ends can be joined into a loop; there are no terminal loops. Subway Builder:
  not checked.
None of these finds a turnback loop for the player; all of them let the player write the cycle out. The quick
gesture in 2a would be ahead of them; 2b is what they all do.

## 4. Cost and stages

| Stage | What | Where | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1a | Loop search in `stop_tiers` (tiers 7 "loop back", 8 "stop looping"), `turn_end` / `unturn_end` (one `Op::Line` with stops and One way, one undo), both ends (balloon to circle), tier 5 only for circles, `best_slot` second visit on a turned stem, Rust tests on `balloon_fixture`, `long_balloon` (not offered) and a two-loop fixture | `track/wasm/lines.rs`, `track/wasm.rs` (`close_path`, `best_slot`, a bounded `find_path_side` variant in `net.rs`) | ~250 lines Rust; a few hours. Changes the WASM interface: own tree |
| 1b | Marker tiers and hovers, the click (`turnEnd` / `unturnEnd` ops), the closing row's cross on a turned line, strings and l10n pass 1, a check script like the experiment | `map/stopMarkers.ts`, `map/tools.ts`, `workers/protocol.ts`, `workers/clock.worker.ts`, `ui/inspector/LineInspector.tsx`, i18n | ~150 lines TS; a few hours |
| 2a | Manual mode in the sim: terminus rule for a manual line's reversals, no U-turn icon there, `set_manual` (open line to the full cycle and back, one undo) | `track/capacity.rs`, `wasm/lines.rs` | ~120 lines Rust; a few hours |
| 2b | The numbered list, remove one visit, put a visit in after a row, the toggle, direction words checked | `LineInspector.tsx`, `map/tools.ts`, stop list components, i18n | ~300 lines TS; most of a day |

Stage 1 is about a day of agent work in all and needs nothing from stage 2. Stage 2 is a day to a day and a half,
and the riskiest part is the UI for putting a visit in, not the sim.

**Risks in stage 1.** The loop search must say no at junctions facing the wrong way (it uses the same move rules as
the route search, so it should); a turned line's stem stops appear twice everywhere stops are listed (stop list,
line copy, the diagram), which the experiment shows works but is long; the rotation for both ends changes which stop
is the line's first (its run 0 start), which only moves where trains enter service.

## Recommendation

Build stage 1 first (1a then 1b): it is her Pittsburgh case, it's cheap because the sim already runs one-way
balloons, and it makes no new kind of line. Then 2a and 2b as the catch-all, with the loop marker kept as the
shortcut inside it.

### Fixes after Anita's try (T-3079, tree exact2)

- Toggle is "Exact edits" and always switches back: a list that folds (reads the same both ways) goes to its open line, any other
  stays as a normal line with the same stops (a turned line keeps its loop, `hand` and one way stay).
- A manual line's route search turns back at a listed stop for 0 s (`route_hops_turn`, `find_path_turn`), closing hop quickest
  with no carry-on. Line 1a's leg after De Ruijterkade ran round Line 1's loop because the 600 s penalty beat turning; those
  rows were the "passed" stations. `order_fault_line` applies the same rule to a drag's check.
- Exact edits lists the visits unflipped (Line 1's list had been drawn last-row-first) and draws no related-lines side view, so no
  loop and no crossing strip beside it. Whether the crossing strip is wrong in normal mode too was not looked at.

## Round 2: hover, ends, half dot, numbers on the map (T-3089, T-3080, agent exactmap, tree exact3)

- Row hover (every line's list, both modes): `setListHot({stations: complexOf(s)})` on the row's enter and leave (`map/hovered.ts`),
  so the shader's hovered-station light shows, and `StopMarkers` adds `.hot` (white ring and glow in the line's colour) to the
  marker over the station, which covers the shader's. Selection and camera untouched.
- `drop_stop` on a manual line is `drop_visit`: the list is a cycle (last stop = first), so a row's visit leaves the cycle, the
  first and the closing row being the same visit, and the cycle closes up (`manual_stops` finds the legs again). A removed turn's two
  neighbours are one visit (`[A,B,C,B,A]` less C is `[A,B,A]`: the line is shorter). Refused below two stations or with no route.
  The old refusals (ends, reversal stops) were `dropped`'s: it keeps the path and cuts it, which a cycle cannot.
- Cross, map click and map number are one edit, `dropStop`. Before: the row's cross was `dropStop` (one visit; no cross on the ends or
  a turning visit); a click on a stop's dot on the map was `setStops` with the station filtered out of the list, every visit of it at
  once, both ways, the route found again; the numbers did not exist. Now a click on a stop takes out its first visit only (a second click
  the next), so the map can drop one direction. Dot click in the list is the same.
- Half dot: `StopMarkers.manualMarks` sets `oneWay` for a station a manual closed list visits once with different neighbours either side
  (a turn, `prev === next`, stays whole); T-3041's rule read `turnedLine`, which gave a manual line's dropped-visit station nothing.
- Map numbers and circles (`StopMarkers`, DOM in the marks layer, `pickVisit` ahead of `pick` in `Tools.lineClick`): per visit its
  list number (the first stop's two rows share one label "1, n"), placed on the right of the way the train goes, (-dy, dx) of the
  direction from the station before to the station after along the route (stops and passed stations in path order), at marker radius + 9 px;
  a turn uses the way in. Left-hand running: `side` in `placeVisits`. Hidden below zoom 12.5. Empty circle: each pass of the route over a
  station of its own legs with no stop there, on the right of that pass; `passed_stations` for a manual line now also lists a stop of another
  visit (once per leg), and the list's "passed" rows filter those out. A circle puts the station in after the stop before the pass
  (`orderStops` with the splice, as "+"): the route already passes there, so it is where the cycle already runs. A station the route never
  passes in a direction has no circle (the "+" or a click on the station adds it, as before).
- Strings: `stopMarkers.removeVisit`, `stopMarkers.addVisit` (hover tags on the numbers and circles), pass-1 in 10 languages.
- Check: `checks/t3023-manual.mjs` (39 PASS, `dark` argument). Left: direction is taken from the stations either side, so on a bendy leg the side
  can be off near a curve; two numbers of the same station on one side (two visits one way) overlap.
