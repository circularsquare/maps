# T-104: Whole trains on a line

The schedule controls allocate integer trains to the entire line, across both directions, at high/medium/low demand. Headway is a derived result of allocated trains and the capacity-adjusted round trip. Zero pauses departures for that demand level. New lines start with 8/4/2 trains.

Rust stores optional allocations on LineData and Network. Legacy frequency inputs remain supported internally for existing saves and calibration fixtures; their displayed initial counts are the rounded-up old fleet. Editing the schedule converts all three levels to count allocation. Allocations survive rerouting, platform/geometry edits, breaking/reconnecting a line, save/load and undo/redo. Car purchases still use the largest period allocation times train length, use spare owned cars first, and charge actual construction/extra cars.

TWT4 network saves add an optional 3-integer allocation after historical frequency slots. Allocated lines write zero to those slots because frequencies are derived. TWT2/TWT3 still load with their original frequencies. Existing lines change scheduling model only when edited. This preserves historical calibration saves and makes quotes input-pure.

The edit-time solver uses the existing incremental capacity pass. A common 1% frequency probe measures a positive congestion derivative bound, giving stable rate updates around saturation; at most 80 iterations, with early exit at 0.0001 train residual. Capacity resources used by allocated lines keep continuous delay rather than rounding each delay to seconds; legacy-only resources retain their prior rounding. Shared lines whose profiles move are included in renderer/demand dirty sets. No per-frame work or polling. Reverse-direction departure phase follows the forward turnaround, keeping odd fleets evenly spaced around the full circuit.
