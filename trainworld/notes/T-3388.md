# T-3388: London edits take about 4 s (londonedit, 2026-11-17)

Status: done (tree `.tw-trees/londonedit`, branch tw/londonedit). Scripts in `notes/T-3388/`.

## Where the time went

Native, release, one core, `t3388_probe` (below) on the shipped London save (`data/packs/real/lon.save`,
171 lines) and the real NYC save. Every London edit that reached the big merge group ran the settle four
times (the edit's own, then `PACE_ROUNDS` = 3 paced ones), about 1 s each:

- 130 of London's 171 lines meet at merges through each other, so they are one `pace_merges` group. The
  greedy split into sets within 3% (`PACE_SHARE`) runs down a near-continuous list of headways, so a
  line moving by 0.05% at the top moves every set edge below it.
- Each pacing slows the paced lines a little, which moves round trips by up to about 0.2%, which moves the
  next pacing back: a two-state cycle, 121 lines changed every round, five of them in and out of sets.
  The loaded save sits in that cycle too: the first edit anywhere in the group repaced 138 lines.
- Each paced settle steps about 117 lines for 4-6 rounds; a round is mostly `update_scoped` working out
  profiles again for the 100-190 lines whose holds moved (about 1 ms a profile).
- A train-count edit on a line outside the group (Jubilee) was already fast (17 ms). The brief's 3.5 s
  train-count figure is a line inside the group (Bakerloo, windrush).
- A side effect: a repacing applied to lines outside the edit's settle scope left them paced to rates
  they were never settled to; the next edit with a wider scope paid for it.

## What changed (`sim/src/track/world.rs`)

`pace_merges` holds still (`TrackWorld::pace_in`, `PACE_HOLD` 0.5%):

- A group's lines at one level are paced again only when something it was paced from changed: a count
  line's headway by more than 0.5% or past its own pace (so a held line can run its pace; line-levels
  paced under their headway after an edit: 1-2 of 469, as before, 0-3, mostly from the loaded save), a
  headway line's set headway, a line joining or leaving, the merge filling or emptying (98%).
- When it is paced again, a line that has not moved keeps the headway it was paced from and its set:
  the greedy split stops at the edge between two sets of unmoved lines (only a moved line can cross an
  edge), and a set whose lines all ran one pace keeps it while that pace is at least its longest
  headway and within 0.5% of the new one.
- After a pacing, the next round of the same settle takes each line's headway as it now is (as long as
  the line can run its pace): pacing a line moves its own round trip (by about 1% on the T-2973 two-line
  test, which without this went 305 / 302 s back and forth from edit to edit), and that move is the
  pacing's, not a reason to pace again.
- A load (`LPC1`, `pace_from_save`) takes the saved pacing and its sets as they are (`adopt`): count
  lines at one pace are a set, a headway line whose headway is that pace heads it.
- An edit refused for money leaves the pacing and rates as they were (new test in
  `count_lines_at_a_merge_run_in_step`, with a no-op edit after the pacing has held changing nothing).
  An earlier version that put the pacing back before the refusal's restore made it worse and was dropped.
- `pace_in` is not saved (save.rs is another agent's): a load starts from the saved pacing, which the
  rule then treats as just paced.

## Figures

Native, the same fresh-load edits on London (34 dumps, 469 line-levels each with service), before vs
after, per line and level (`cmp.py`):

- Train counts: identical everywhere.
- Edits with no pacing round before (Jubilee, the refused spurs and stations, lone track, every NYC
  edit): identical, every rate to the bit.
- Edits where the old rule paced: the old rule's own answer is whichever state its third round lands on,
  so the tolerance is the range of trains an hour the old rule visited in its pace rounds for that line in
  that edit. Of 16,884 line-levels, 185 land more than 0.1% outside that range, 70 more than 0.5%, 58 more
  than 1%, the largest 3.1%. All of those are lines on the edge of a 3% set: paced with the set in one rule
  and at their own headway in the other (line 34 medium 4.36 or 4.50 trains an hour, line 139 medium 2.08
  or 2.14), which the old rule also flips between from one edit to the next (line 34 medium sat at 4.4986
  after some old-rule edits and 4.3622 after others). No line moves by more than the pacing can move it
  (3%, `PACE_SHARE`, plus slack). Against every value the old rule took for that line and level at
  the same trains in any London run (all dumps and pace rounds), 32 of 42,210 line-levels are more than
  0.1% outside, 4 more than 1% (line 104 medium, 2.194 against 2.135-2.169, 1.2%).
- Paced line-levels (of about 470): 329 in the loaded save; after edits 320-328 against the old rule's
  324-327; along the edit sequences they go down and up again, not only down.
- Largest pace over a line's own headway: 3.32% (in the loaded save itself); the old rule reached 3.41%.
  So "at most 3% fewer trains an hour" in the doc was already about 3.4% and stays so.
- Totals per level: within 0.5 trains an hour of about 664 / 583 / 189 (0.08%).

Tolerance used and why: per line, within the range the old rule itself visits, or failing that within the
3% pacing cap; totals within 0.1%. The old rule never had one answer for these lines, so a tighter test
compares against a coin toss.

   (and `trainworld/T-007-real-nyc.save nyc.net`).

   busiest) and 0 (1).
