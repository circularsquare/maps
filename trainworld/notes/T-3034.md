# T-3034: road gaps at junctions, stage 1 (measure and propose)

Anita 2026-11-10: buses and trams sometimes cannot cross small gaps near intersections that look joined on
the basemap. No game code changed. Scripts: `notes/T-3034-scripts/` (run order: `analyze.mjs`, `osm2.mjs`,
`judge.mjs`; they import `decodeRoads` and `joinRoads` straight from `app/src/map/*.ts` with Node 22's type
stripping, and write next to themselves). Data: `artifacts/trainworld/t3034-cases.json` (every case),
`t3034-judged.json` (the OSM-checked sample).

## Method

- 12 cities, a 3x3 block of zoom-14 tiles at each centre (108 tiles), fetched from the tile source the game
  uses (OpenFreeMap planet, `20261101_080013_pt`). New York, Pittsburgh, Paris, Prague, Tokyo, Ho Chi Minh
  City, Mexico City, Lagos, Mumbai, Sao Paulo, Melbourne, Istanbul.
- Decoded with the game's `busRoad` keep, run through the game's `joinRoads`. A road end is a line's first
  or last vertex not on the tile edge: 102,565 of them. It counts as joined when another line has a vertex at
  the same point (what the graph merges), or the line is a closed ring. 95,758 (93.4%) are joined. 3,616 have
  no road of any kind within 12 m (real dead ends). The rest are the cases below.
- For each unjoined end: the nearest other road in the graph, and its kind: street at the same level, link,
  motorway (a street end near one is not joined by rule), bridge or tunnel piece. If the graph has none within
  12 m, the nearest road the game leaves out (a second decode: alley, driveway, parking aisle, tagged or
  restricted service, path, pedestrian, track).
- **Spur.** An end whose own line meets another road's vertex within 15 m along it is a spur tip past a
  junction that is already joined (the first sample was full of them), so it is not a gap. They are counted
  apart.
- **Truth.** For a sample the cases were checked against OSM (Overpass, ways within 45 m, 15 batches): a real
  junction means an OSM node used by 3 or more way ends (a split road is 2, not a junction) within the distance
  to the nearest road plus 3 m of the end. That test is generous: it also says yes when a different junction
  is close. I looked at 36 of the cases as pictures (below) to check it.
- Seams were checked too: the end of a road on a tile edge against the other tile's edge ends.

## Samples as pictures

`artifacts/trainworld/t3034-sheet-2-3.png`, `t3034-sheet-3-5.png`, `t3034-sheet-5-8.png`: 12 cases each, half
that OSM calls a junction and half not (so the sheets do not show the rate; the table does). Black is the
game graph with its vertices, blue the OSM roads, green a junction in OSM, dashed grey roads the game leaves
out, a red ring the unjoined end. `t3034-problem.png` is four real cases.

Judged by eye from those sheets:

- OSM "no" (all of those on the sheets): all real dead ends or an end meeting only roads the game leaves out. Stems
  that stop short and point at a road with no OSM junction are real in OSM too: the OSM way ends there.
- OSM "yes", 3-5 m: 4 of 6 are real gaps (a T stem stopping short; a street meeting a roundabout; a street cut
  in two at an alley crossing, twice). The other 2: the stem runs 5 m past a junction that is already joined, and
  one unclear.
- OSM "yes", 5-8 m: about half. Several are an end sitting at a junction with a left-out alley, where the
  "nearest road" 6 m away is not what it meets. So the 31% at 5-8 m is a ceiling; by eye it is nearer 15%.
- 2-3 m: mostly real: a T stem 2.8 m short (Prague), three roads meeting within 2 m (Tokyo), an open block ring
  (Mumbai), plus some parallel stubs that are not.

What the real ones look like, in order of how often I saw them: a stem that stops 2-5 m short of the road it
joins (OSM has the junction); a street cut in two where an alley or path crosses, its two ends 3-5 m apart; a
ring that is open where its first and last vertex meet; a street meeting a roundabout.

## Does a rule beat a plain distance?

No, none I tried. On the OSM-checked sample, street cases only:

| Feature | 1-3 m | 3-5 m | 5-8 m |
|---|---|---|---|
| everything | 8 of 15 true | 8 of 34 | 14 of 45 |
| end points at the road (cos 0.9) and the road is crossways | 5 of 6 | 2 of 10 | 4 of 21 |
| last segment 15 m or more long and pointing at the road | 4 of 4 | 1 of 11 | 4 of 22 |
| end touching a left-out road (alley, path) | 3 of 4 | 4 of 17 | 7 of 23 |
| the nearest point is the end of another line (end to end) | 2 of 4 | 3 of 16 | 2 of 9 |

Aimed and crossways is good at 1-3 m but then there are only 7 such ends in the 12 cities. Past 3 m nothing
separates the real ones from the dead ends that happen to point at a road.

## Proposal

1. **`JUNCTION_GAP` to 3 m, written in metres** (units = 3 / metres per unit, about 5 at the equator and 6-7
   at 45 degrees; today it is a fixed 2 units, 1.2 m at the equator and 0.6 m at 60 degrees). Expected over the
   12 cities: +32 joins, about 15 real and 17 false. By city, joins/false: Mumbai 27/15, Melbourne 2/1, Pittsburgh
   1/0, Prague 1/1, Tokyo 1/1, the other seven none. A false join here ties a dead end to a road less than 3 m
   away, which the basemap draws as touching anyway.
2. **No higher.** Joins/false for the same rule at 4 m: 72/48; 5 m: 113/79; 6 m: 170/118; 8 m: 286/198; 12 m:
   652/480 (Mumbai alone is 345/250 at 12 m). At 5 m that is about 7 false joins per city block of 3x3 tiles
   for 3 real ones, mostly dead ends and parallel roads. The OSM test flatters these, so the real false counts
   are higher. An angle gate (cos 0.85) halves the joins at 5 m (36, of which 24 false) and still leaves two
   false for every real one.
3. **Same 3 m for the `LINK` end test** (`onStreet` uses `gap2` as well): +24 short service lines kept in the
   12 cities. Not checked against OSM; their ends are the same kind of gap.
4. **Cheap and optional**: an excluded service piece (alley, driveway, parking aisle) of 15 m or less with
   both ends on two different streets becomes a link: 15 pieces in 12 cities, 13 of them in Tokyo. Leave the
   restricted ones out. This is a design call (buses in alleys), so it stays a P3.
5. **An open ring**: close a line whose two ends are within 5 m of each other (14 in all). Tiny.

What this does not do: it will fix about 1 real gap per 49 km2 downtown block in most cities. If Anita is
still seeing many, they are not coming from these gaps in the tiles. Candidates still to look at:
- She sees a bus refuse a gap that really is a dead end in OSM but looks joined because the basemap draws
  roads 5-10 m wide. At 3-5 m, 76% of the sampled cases have no OSM junction. Joining them is a choice between
  visual agreement and OSM truth; this note does not take it.
- Cases that are not tile gaps: turn limits on trams (`turnPath`), `LINK` and the 60 m cap, divided-road hops.
- A debug view in the game: red rings on unjoined ends within 8 m, drawn under a `?roadgaps=1` flag, so she
  could point at one and we would see what the graph sees. I would do this next. About half a day.

## What was not done

- The 36 eye judgements are mine, from the pictures, with the OSM data drawn in; no second reader.
- No headless screenshot of the game's own map; the pictures are drawn from the same decoded tiles and OSM.
- Only the centre 3x3 of 12 cities. Mumbai behaves unlike the rest, so a rural or smaller city may too.

## Follow-up: the built 3 m gap added far more than predicted (2026-11-10)

Tramspeed's recount found +206 joined ends against my ~32. Re-counted with `T-3034-scripts/recount.mjs`
against the HEAD `joinRoads` (not `joinRoads(raw, 1.5)`, which also changes the `LINK` length test and drops
every link over about 23 m, so it is no baseline). Same 108 tiles.

| | naive 3 m (every vertex) | built rule |
|---|---|---|
| ends newly joined | 200 | 158 |
| of those, spur tips (already joined within 15 m along the line) | 115 | 103 |
| of those, ends of newly kept links (28 links) | 50 | 44 |
| of those, street ends, real candidates | 35 (25 run along the road) | 11 |
| interior vertices newly joined to another road | 4,254 | 156 |

So the +206 was mostly spur tips and link ends, which I had left out of the 32 (spurs) or counted in a
separate line (links). The real trap was the 4,254: with the gap applied to every vertex, roads running within
3 m of each other (dual carriageways, parallel tram and street lines) were joined along their length.
(`11506/7307` and `11507/7307` are Mumbai, not Tokyo.)

OSM check of the street ends and link ends the naive rule joins (an OSM junction within 6 m, 77 cases, all of
them):

- street ends the built rule keeps: 6 of 9 have an OSM junction
- street ends it drops (running along the road): 4 of 26
- link ends: 39 of 42

Fix, in `joinRoads` (`app/src/map/roadGraph.ts`): the 3 m gap is for line ends only, and only where the end is
not running along the road it joins (angle between the end's heading and the road more than about 37 degrees,
`ALONG_COS` 0.8); every interior vertex, and an end running along a road, keep the old 2 units (`NEAR_UNITS`).
The link end test keeps the 3 m. Typecheck passes; smoke on my Vite passed (27 checks).
3 m stays: it adds 11 street joins in 108 tiles (about 7 real), 44 link ends (nearly all real), and spur tips
that change nothing.
