## What NEC FUTURE counts

- **Distance**: the Final EIS (chapter 5, 5.2.2) says its Interregional model "was estimated using
  only trips that were 50 miles or longer", and the base trip table was adjusted "to exclude these
  zone pairs" under 50 miles apart (24% fewer trips). So T-299's cut, zone pairs over 80 km centre
  to centre, is NEC FUTURE's own rule.
- **Purpose**: intercity travel only; "does not include auto commuting" (Draft EIS Table 5-1).
  The market's inter-region trips are non-commute too, so commutes between zones are left out of
  the comparison, as above.
- **Places**: metro areas "based on" OMB's MSAs, with boundaries "drawn based on markets served"
  (not published by county). The Draft EIS lists Trenton-Ewing (Mercer) as its own metro, apart
  from New York-Northern New Jersey-Long Island; Mercer is in the game's New York.
- **Days**: annual trips (Figure 5-1, 2012, Moody's-based model), so a mean day with weekends.
- **A cross-check that it is not low by definition**: NEC FUTURE gives the pair 7.8% by rail (FEIS
  Table 5-4, 2040 No Action) and 1.45M rail trips a year (Table 5-5), about 4,000 a day, which is
  Amtrak's real order; 4,000 / 7.8% is about 51,000 a day all modes, NEC FUTURE's own figure.

## Why it is high: destination choice, not the trip rate

NHTS 2017 by household CBSA (`T-379/nhts_cbsa.py`, the same linking and distances as
research/intercity/nhts_long.py), weekday non-commute trips a person, against the market's ground
departures per resident by band over the world field (`T-379/model_bands.py`, `fit_compete.py`):

| Band | NHTS New York CBSA (trips sampled) | Model, NY MSA zones | NHTS Philadelphia CBSA | Model, Philadelphia MSA zones |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50-80 km | 0.0282 (329) | 0.0294 (x1.04) | 0.0355 (39) | 0.0236 (x0.67) |
| 80-150 km | 0.0096 (222) | 0.0176 (x1.83) | 0.0324 (24) | 0.0306 (x0.94) |
| 150-300 km | 0.0100 (92) | 0.0066 (x0.66) | 0.0062 (16) | 0.0126 (x2.02) |

(Model figures at kappa 0, which is today's rule refitted on these zones: A 2.25, R 0.102, close to
2.37 and 0.107.) Each origin's trip rate in each band is within about 2x of NHTS, so the market's
origins are not making far too many long trips. What is off is where they go. NHTS's own budget
bounds the pair: New York's residents make about 0.020 trips a day of 80-300 km (430k a day for the
pack's 22M), Philadelphia's about 0.039 (240k), to everywhere. The market sends about 125k of New
York's (29%) and 176k of Philadelphia's (74%) to the other place. Three quarters of Philadelphians'
80-300 km trips going to the New York pack leaves almost nothing for the Jersey Shore, the Poconos,
Lancaster, Baltimore and the rest; NEC FUTURE's figure implies about 10% and 6%. A plausible
all-mode figure is perhaps 50,000-120,000 a weekday over 80 km, so the market is about 2.5-6x high.

The cause is the ground curve's destination rule: attraction is people, at `max(d, 25)^-2.37`,
production constrained. New York's 22M people 130 km from Philadelphia outweigh everything else
Philadelphians could reach at that distance, and population is a poor stand-in for where 100-200 km
leisure trips go (beaches, mountains, second homes). The same shape gives Washington - Baltimore
7.5x over 80 km; pairs with no giant neighbour (New York - Boston 1.2x, Boston - Washington 1.3x)
are close. New York - Washington's 2.6x is the ground curve at 300-400 km, 2.4x NHTS in T-376.

## What was tried

**Nearby destinations competing in the normaliser** (`fit_compete.py`): the normaliser also counts
destinations under the onset at kappa x (1 - onset) x max(d, 25)^-A, the trips themselves keeping
the onset (intervening opportunities: a Manhattan resident has 12M people within 25 km that the
present normaliser ignores). Refitted R and A to NHTS's Northeast bands for each kappa:

| kappa | A | R | NY 80-150 km against NHTS | Philadelphia 150-300 km against NHTS |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 (today) | 2.252 | 0.102 | x1.83 | x2.02 |
| 0.1 | 2.248 | 0.123 | x1.68 | x2.11 |
| 0.4 | 2.235 | 0.169 | x1.57 | x2.14 |
| 1.0 | 2.221 | 0.242 | x1.57 | x2.10 |

The Northeast's band fit pins the curve's shape, so the near competition barely moves either metro
(New York's 80-150 km at most 1.83 to 1.57x) and does nothing for the destination split. Rejected.

## Why the model was left

Neither of the brief's suspected faults is there (no double count, the seam is a quarter of the
trips and outside the 80 km cut), and NEC FUTURE's definition matches T-299's comparison closely
enough that the gap is not definitional: weekday against mean day is 1.17x, Mercer 38k. What is
wrong is a fitted part of the rule (the destination weight), and fixing it means a new term fitted
to pair data worldwide, which Anita's 2026-10-15 decision (no precise calibration until more cities
give data) puts later. A per-pair or Northeast factor is not allowed. So: T-438, a competing
destinations term (a destination cell's pull damped by the people already around it, Fotheringham's
form, `a_j x (people within ~50 km of j / ref)^delta`), or a non-residential attraction for leisure
(OSM tourism and holiday homes), fitted to NEC FUTURE's pairs and one non-US check (Great Britain's
NTS or MiD), with New York - Philadelphia and Washington - Baltimore as the tests.

Game effect meanwhile: the New York - Philadelphia corridor carries 2.5-6x the plausible
inter-region trips, all by car until a player builds; a player's line between the two earns that
much more than it should (its rail share of one trip, 59%, is about right). Commutes across the seam
(397,554 legs) are 0.56x LODES and are not part of this.
