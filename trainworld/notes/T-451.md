# T-451: are Tokyo's everyday trips too local?

2026-10-15. Anita, weekend view of a Kichijoji / Inokashira cell: far more trips to nearby Suginami
cells than to Shinjuku, Shibuya or Ginza; "maybe long trips are underemphasized in tokyo".
Measurement only; nothing built. Scripts: `everyday_dest.py` and `region_od.py` in the session
scratchpad (they redo the sim's rule in numpy on the `tyo` pack, read only).

## The rule being measured

`sim/src/demand/everyday.rs`, docs/demand.md 4.1: each outing picks a 2 km zone by zone attraction
x exp(-d / scale), d the grid distance between zone centres (1.04 km inside the zone), then a cell
inside the zone by its weight. Scales: errands 3 km, social 6 km, medical 5 km (T-138's "candidate"
values for New York, never fitted). Attraction in `tyo` (tier 0): OSM shops and services for
errands, and for social 46% population, 46% OSM restaurants, bars, venues and hotels, 8% parks;
every OSM place counts as one, whatever its size. Weekends multiply the totals (errands x1.22,
social x1.60) and change no destination.

## 2. Against the 2018 Tokyo Person Trip Survey

Tokyo-to's analysis of the 6th survey (weekday), "trips between areas" by purpose, ref 2-5,
https://www.toshiseibi.metro.tokyo.lg.jp/documents/d/toshiseibi/pdf_bunyabetsu_kotsu_butsuryu_pdf_chiiki_koutsu_kentoukai02_5-2
(page 6). The areas are the Tokyo Grand Design's: central core (roughly inside Ring Road 7, 4.6M
people), new urban (the outer wards and the eastern Tama cities, 6.0M), Tama (2.6M), the mountains.
Private trips (shiji, which here includes shopping) by where they end:

| From | central core | new urban | Tama | outside Tokyo-to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| central core, PT | 84.4% | 8.2% | small | about 6% |
| central core, game | 70.1% | 22.5% | 0.6% | 6.8% |
| new urban, PT | **15.6%** | 76.9% | small | 5.1% |
| new urban, game | **34.7%** | 44.0% | 4.0% | 17.3% |
| Tama, PT | **3.5%** | 4.7% | 83.7% | 6.4% |
| Tama, game | **3.1%** | 12.5% | 62.9% | 21.5% |

Game areas by municipality (`region_od.py`): central core = the 15 wards mostly inside Ring 7
(Chiyoda, Chuo, Minato, Shinjuku, Bunkyo, Taito, Sumida, Koto, Shinagawa, Meguro, Shibuya, Nakano,
Toshima, Kita, Arakawa; 4.27M residents); new urban = the other 8 wards plus Musashino, Mitaka,
Chofu and Komae (6.14M); Tama = the rest of Tokyo-to (3.62M). The PT lines run through some wards,
so a few points either way are definition, not model. Game mean straight-line outing length:
central 6.4 km, new urban 7.6, Tama 8.3.

**The game already sends more everyday trips into the centre than PT shows, not fewer**: from the
outer wards and Kichijoji's ring, 35% against 16%; from Tama, the same 3%. What the game gets
wrong is the other way: too few trips stay in their own area (44% against 77% from the new urban
area) and too many cross into Saitama, Kanagawa and Chiba (17-22% against 5-6%). On PT's evidence
Tokyo's everyday trips are, if anything, too long and too spread out in the game, not too local.
Caveat: PT counts every trip by where it starts, chained trips included (shop to shop, work to
restaurant), which are mostly short and local; the game's outings all start at home. That
explains some of the own-area gap, not a factor of two in the centre share.

**Weekends.** PT 2018 is weekday only. In the game the weekend changes only the purpose mix: more
social, which travels further, so the new urban area's centre share moves from 34.7% to about
35.5%. The one Japanese weekend survey to hand, Keihanshin's 6th PT (holiday), has its free-purpose
(private) trips at 8.2% rail on a holiday against 8.5% on a weekday, the car taking the difference
(MLIT road statistics table 15-5, p. 2,
https://www.mlit.go.jp/road/toukei_chousa/road_db/pdf/2024/15-5.pdf): no sign that holiday private
trips head for the rail-served centres more than weekday ones. A Tokyo holiday survey would settle
it; none was found.

## 3. Which part of the rule drives it

- **Distance curve**: exp(-d/3 km) errands and exp(-d/6 km) social, judgement for New York (T-138),
  never fitted anywhere. It sets how local trips are. Against PT it is not too short; the spill
  across prefecture lines points the other way.
- **Attraction measure** (tier 0, general): OSM places counted one each, so a department store or a
  station building with 200 shops can be one node, while a residential street of small shops is
  many. The four terminal circles hold 0.7-1.4% of errand attraction each, against 0.9-4.1% of the
  economic census's jobs. Blending jobs in at half (`region_od.py` "half jobs") moves Kichijoji's
  errands to Shinjuku from 1.1% to 2.5% and to Ginza from 0.15% to 0.48%, but the new urban area's
  centre share only from 34.7% to 37.1%: it moves trips onto the terminals inside the centre, not
  into the centre.
- **2 km zones**: not a factor at these distances (the decay table uses the grid; the same-zone
  1.04 km only matters under 2 km).
- **Weekend factors**: change the mix only, under a point of centre share.
- Variants tried for scale: doubling both decays sends 37.8% of the new urban area's outings to
  the centre and 23.5% outside Tokyo-to, further from PT on both counts.

## Proposed change (not built)

1. **No change to the distance rule for Tokyo now.** PT says the centre share is already high; a
   longer decay makes it worse. When calibration starts (T-450's turn), fit the everyday scales to
   a survey's trip lengths, starting with this table: the cross-boundary spill suggests the scales
   are, if anything, too long for Tokyo. General, not Japan-only: every city's scales are New York
   judgement.
2. **Attraction with size (general, tier 0)**, worth a task at P3: weight OSM places by floor area
   where mapped (building footprint x levels for `shop=department_store`, `shop=mall` and shops
   inside station buildings) or blend in the jobs proxy, so that terminal districts outdraw
   residential shop streets. It changes where in the centre trips go (towards Shinjuku, Shibuya,
   Ginza), which is what Anita sees missing, without lengthening trips. Its check is a city with a
   measured retail destination split (a PT survey's large-zone OD, or footfall).
3. **Display (for the interface owner, not demand)**: the destination bubbles are per cell, so a
   centre's many small cells read as weak however much they sum to. Bubbles summed to a coarser
   grid or by place when zoomed out would show the centre's draw as the trips have it. Not filed;
   for the orchestrator to judge.
