Stage 1b (T-426, below the checks) added Ibaraki and dictionary romaji; the tables before it are

2026-10-15. Greater Tokyo (`tyo`) built by `pipeline/tier0.py` with Japan's inputs from
`pipeline/japan.py` (new): census 500 m mesh population, economic census 500 m mesh jobs scaled

## Decisions

- **500 m mesh**, not 250 m: a 500 m mesh (0.25 km²) is a little larger than a cell (0.1 km²), so
  nearly every mesh holds cells and GHSL only shapes inside it; the 500 m file also has 5-year ages.
- **Jobs scaled to the census's workers by place of work**, as the brief said: the economic census
  counts 17.86M persons employed inside against the census's 16.43M employed people (it counts
  dispatched workers where they work and second jobs, and leaves out farms and households). Per
  ward the scaling runs from -38% (Yokohama Nishi-ku, Minato Mirai) to +38% (Minamiboso).
- **Work at home 0.23**: the census's 9.5% working at home (shopkeepers, farmers above their work)
  plus a telework day share of 0.15 (MLIT 2024: 37.5% of Greater Tokyo's employed telework; 2 days
  in 5, judgement) on the rest.
- **Curve d^-2.2 exp(-d/80 km)** (below): steeper than any sampler city.
- **Schools**: P29 points get the children 0-14 x 0.9 of the meshes nearest them (elementary,
  junior high, compulsory, secondary schools; high schools, special schools and kindergartens none).
- **Narita's O&D share 0.8** (judgement); Haneda 1.
- **Outline**: N03 parts under 2e-6 deg² (about 0.02 km²) dropped (3,484 parts otherwise, mostly
  piers); `所属未定地` (13000) kept only beside the wards (Tokyo Bay's Chuo breakwater landfill).

## Curve: the 2020 census flows, municipality to municipality (`t420_curve.py`)

14.0M commuters with both ends inside (at-home workers out), 244 municipalities, 3,389 zones of
2 km. 33.5% work in their own municipality (a ward of a designated city counts as one).

| Curve | Misplaced between municipalities, pack margins | Own municipality | Mean km (pack) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **d^-2.2 exp(-d/80)** (applied) | 16.9% | 33.0% | 9.2 |
| London's d^-1.85 exp(-d/30) | 17.5% | 28.5% | 9.7 |
| tier 0 rule, power 1.4, length to the census median (d^-1.4 exp(-d/16.5)) | 20.3% | 22.9% | 10.5 |
| Paris's d^-1.3 exp(-d/20) | 22.3% | 19.5% | 12.1 |
| Toronto's d^-1.2 exp(-d/20) | 23.8% | 17.6% | 12.9 |
| US d^-0.8 exp(-d/30) | 36.0% | 8.3% | 19.9 |

With the census's own margins (flows placed at each municipality's population centre) the best
is d^-1.1 exp(-d/16) at 16.5%: placing a ward's flows at one point makes its own trips 1 km long, so
those margins favour flat curves; the pack's margins spread them and are the fair test. The
surface is flat from 1.85 to 2.3 (16.9-17.5%); only the steep end keeps the own-municipality share.
The distance histograms are not comparable for the same reason (the census's first bin is the
own-municipality share).

## A dense network on the pack (research/japan.md 5.1)

A native probe of one solving worker (a scratch copy of the crate with `t420_probe.rs`, the T-398
probe's method without the market, since a `market: -1` city's cluster has none): commute and
everyday engines, periods 0, 1, 4, free flow and three crowding rounds, then a layout edit (one
station 300 m), a train-count edit and the first network again. Two synthetic networks around the
pack's origin: **grid**, 16 + 16 lines 3 km apart over 48 km with a stop every 1.5 km plus 12
radial lines to 60 km (44 lines, 1,308 stops, about 1,050 stations after merging); **grid2**, 24 +
24 lines 2 km apart with a stop every 1 km plus the radials (60 lines, 2,604 stops, about 2,050
stations, real Tokyo's order). New York's real save (109 lines, 1,756 stations) for reference.

| Pack, network | Steady heap | Peak | Solve, first network | Layout edit | Train-count edit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| nyc, real save | 114 MB | 145 MB | 22.3 s | 22.2 s | 7.4 s |
| nyc, grid | 114 MB | 149 MB | 18.0 s | 18.3 s | 6.2 s |
| tyo, grid | 91 MB | 126 MB | 13.4 s | 13.7 s | 6.7 s |
| tyo, grid2 | 124 MB | 242 MB | 37.8 s | 36.7 s | 15.4 s |
| nyc, grid2 | 152 MB | 274 MB | 35.1 s | 35.7 s | 15.4 s |

Opening Tokyo's engines: 20 ms commute, 340-430 ms everyday (New York 40 and 590 ms). So Tokyo's
pack costs less than New York's on the same network (fewer cells, a steeper curve); what grows is
the network: doubling the stations doubles the peak (the crowding rounds' strategy tables) and
nearly triples the solve, on either pack. No per-pack limit breaks. A Tokyo-scale network would
put a worker near 250 MB and an edit near 35 s, which the performance work (T-362's loose
coupling, strategy tables) should take on before Japan's stage 2 joins more packs to one solve.

## Stage 3 (2026-10-15/16): the rest of Japan as 38 zones

Every prefecture outside the seven metro packs is one non-city zone (31), and Hokkaido's
subprefectures outside Sapporo's pack make 8 more after the under-100k rule (docs/zones.md 2):
Hiyama joins Oshima, Rumoi and Soya join Kamikawa, Hidaka joins Tokachi, Nemuro joins Okhotsk,
Shiribeshi less Otaru (85k) joins Iburi, each to the neighbour with the longest shared border;
Kamikawa, Sorachi, Okhotsk, Tokachi, Kushiro, Iburi and Oshima stay. The design's "about 6" was an
estimate; the rule gives 8. Okinawa is in; Tokyo's islands stay out (they are `tyo`'s prefecture).
With the metros, all 47 prefectures play. Logs and images: `../artifacts/trainworld/T-420s3-*`,
outlines `T-420-<id>-outline.png`.

**How.** `pipeline/japan_zones.py` (new): `plan` writes `pipeline/japan_zones.json` (units,
neighbours, extract, water extracts, origin, census at-home share, wfh); `build <id>` runs one zone
from outline to checks (curve fit, tier0 build, places, routes, merge, `read_pack`, water picture);
`run` builds zones in parallel, one process each; `fold` writes the fitted curves into the JSON.
`build_city.py` makes a `CITIES` entry per zone from the JSON (`kind` "zone", `utc_offset` 9).
Registration, boxes, market packs and local trips in one call: `pipeline/register.py <ids>
--market --trips` (T-441).

**Decisions** (DECISIONS.md 2026-10-16):
- **Units** as above; a zone is named after its most populous unit (the prefecture, or "Kamikawa",
  "Okhotsk" and so on); its origin is the centre of its most populous 500 m mesh.
- **Curve per zone**, fitted on table 4's municipality flows with the pack's own margins over a
  fixed grid (power 1.2-2.4, length 16-160 km), the lowest misplaced share, ties within 0.3 points
  to the own-municipality share nearest the census's. A few minutes a zone, not the metros' 4-25.
  Zones fit flatter and shorter than the metros (d^-1.4 exp(-d/16) is the commonest): most work in
  their own municipality (52-91%).
- **Work at home**: the census's at-home share plus MLIT's 16.8% telework outside the big three
  metros at 2 days in 5 (0.138 Okinawa to 0.231 Okhotsk).
- **Outlines leave out** the six Russian-administered villages of the southern Kurils (N03 lists
  them under Nemuro; the census counts no one) and uninhabited far islets: parts under 5 km² more
  than 50 km from every larger part (Takeshima, the Senkakus). Stage 3 only (`far_islets=False`);
  the metro packs' cached outlines are unchanged.
- **Hamamatsu's wards** were redrawn in 2024 (seven became three, old Kita-ku split), so N03 2025
  and the 2020 census disagree: both are read as the whole city (`japan.CITY_OF_WARD`). Before
  that fix Shizuoka's jobs came out 0.39M short and its curve misplaced 24.7%. No other
  municipality code differs between table 4 and N03 in the 31 prefectures and Hokkaido.
- **Water pixels of 50 m** where a zone's box would pass 150M pixels at 25 m: Kagoshima (Amami) and
  Okinawa (the Daito and Sakishima islands, 860 km across).

**Inputs, fetched once** (`japan.py`'s fetch, as a batch): N03 for all 47 prefectures (379 MB of

Not worth its cost:
- **Three failures found by the batch, not before it**: tier 0's university density NaN when the
  only counted campus is denser than 1 a m², then the same fix failing where none is counted,
  and Wikidata's 429 when builds query at once (9 of 38 zones rebuilt). A first batch of the 3-4
  zones most unlike each other would have caught them in one round.
- **Hamamatsu's redrawn wards** showed only in the per-pack summary (Shizuoka's 24.7% misplaced),
  and the curve it kept was then reused by a rebuild until the JSON's copy was removed. A check
  of table 4's codes against N03's belongs in `plan` (now fixed in japan.py, not yet checked
  there).
- **`japan.municipalities` took 53 minutes** for 47 prefectures: the buffer of every unit's union
  for "land of no municipality". A distance test instead would take seconds.
- **Geofabrik's downloads were slow** (Shikoku's 87 MB in 32 minutes, while Chugoku's 227 MB took
  21 s): nothing to do but start them first.

Proposals:
1. `japan_zones.py plan` checks table 4's municipality codes against N03's and fails on a
   mismatch; `build` refits when the JSON's curve predates a change (or `fold` runs only at the end).
2. Before any batch: build the 3 zones most unlike each other (an island zone, a big one, a
   Hokkaido merge) one at a time, then the rest in parallel.
3. `japan.municipalities`: test "000" parts by distance, not by buffering the union (T-441's
   follow-up, small).
4. A zone needs no browser line beyond the generated one; the row maker (a water pair from the
   mask, a line through the densest cells) could become `city-browser.mjs --auto <id>` for every
   future zone.
