# T-420 stage 1: Japan's country module and Greater Tokyo

2026-10-15. Greater Tokyo (`tyo`) built by `pipeline/tier0.py` with Japan's inputs from
`pipeline/japan.py` (new): census 500 m mesh population, economic census 500 m mesh jobs scaled
per municipality to the census's workers by place of work, a curve fitted on the census's
municipality flows, at-home workers from the census plus MLIT's telework share, KSJ P29 schools.
Registered, `market: -1`, `utcOffset` 9. The design is research/japan.md; stages 2 and 3 wait
(TASKS.md T-420).

Scripts: `notes/t420_curve.py` (curve fit; makes the signals cache), `notes/t420_network.py` (the
synthetic dense network measurement). Logs and images in `../artifacts/trainworld/T-420-*`.

## Footprint

Saitama (11), Chiba (12), Tokyo (13) and Kanagawa (14) as whole prefectures, dissolved from their
KSJ N03 municipalities (designated cities by ward). Tokyo's Izu and Ogasawara islands (codes 13360
and up) are left out; of N03's unassigned land (13000), only the Tokyo Bay landfill beside the
wards is kept (the far islets went with the islands).

## Decisions

- **500 m mesh**, not 250 m: a 500 m mesh (0.25 km²) is a little larger than a cell (0.1 km²), so
  nearly every mesh holds cells and GHSL only shapes inside it; the 500 m file also has 5-year ages.
- **Jobs scaled to the census's workers by place of work**, as the brief said: the economic census
  counts 17.86M persons employed inside against the census's 16.43M employed people (it counts
  dispatched workers where they work and second jobs, and leaves out farms and households). Per
  ward the scaling runs from -38% (Yokohama Nishi-ku, Minato Mirai) to +38% (Minamiboso).
- **Work at home 0.23**: the census's 9.5% working at home (shopkeepers, farmers above their work)
  plus a telework day share of 0.15 (MLIT 2024: 37.5% of Greater Tokyo's employed telework; 2 days
  in 5, judgement) on the rest.
- **Curve d^-2.2 exp(-d/80 km)** (below): steeper than any sampler city.
- **Schools**: P29 points get the children 0-14 x 0.9 of the meshes nearest them (elementary,
  junior high, compulsory, secondary schools; high schools, special schools and kindergartens none).
- **Narita's O&D share 0.8** (judgement); Haneda 1.
- **Outline**: N03 parts under 2e-6 deg² (about 0.02 km²) dropped (3,484 parts otherwise, mostly
  piers); `所属未定地` (13000) kept only beside the wards (Tokyo Bay's Chuo breakwater landfill).

## Curve: the 2020 census flows, municipality to municipality (`t420_curve.py`)

14.0M commuters with both ends inside (at-home workers out), 244 municipalities, 3,389 zones of
2 km. 33.5% work in their own municipality (a ward of a designated city counts as one).

| Curve | Misplaced between municipalities, pack margins | Own municipality | Mean km (pack) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **d^-2.2 exp(-d/80)** (applied) | 16.9% | 33.0% | 9.2 |
| London's d^-1.85 exp(-d/30) | 17.5% | 28.5% | 9.7 |
| tier 0 rule, power 1.4, length to the census median (d^-1.4 exp(-d/16.5)) | 20.3% | 22.9% | 10.5 |
| Paris's d^-1.3 exp(-d/20) | 22.3% | 19.5% | 12.1 |
| Toronto's d^-1.2 exp(-d/20) | 23.8% | 17.6% | 12.9 |
| US d^-0.8 exp(-d/30) | 36.0% | 8.3% | 19.9 |

With the census's own margins (flows placed at each municipality's population centre) the best
is d^-1.1 exp(-d/16) at 16.5%: placing a ward's flows at one point makes its own trips 1 km long, so
those margins favour flat curves; the pack's margins spread them and are the fair test. The
surface is flat from 1.85 to 2.3 (16.9-17.5%); only the steep end keeps the own-municipality share.
The distance histograms are not comparable for the same reason (the census's first bin is the
own-municipality share).

## A dense network on the pack (research/japan.md 5.1)

A native probe of one solving worker (a scratch copy of the crate with `t420_probe.rs`, the T-398
probe's method without the market, since a `market: -1` city's cluster has none): commute and
everyday engines, periods 0, 1, 4, free flow and three crowding rounds, then a layout edit (one
station 300 m), a train-count edit and the first network again. Two synthetic networks around the
pack's origin: **grid**, 16 + 16 lines 3 km apart over 48 km with a stop every 1.5 km plus 12
radial lines to 60 km (44 lines, 1,308 stops, about 1,050 stations after merging); **grid2**, 24 +
24 lines 2 km apart with a stop every 1 km plus the radials (60 lines, 2,604 stops, about 2,050
stations, real Tokyo's order). New York's real save (109 lines, 1,756 stations) for reference.

| Pack, network | Steady heap | Peak | Solve, first network | Layout edit | Train-count edit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| nyc, real save | 114 MB | 145 MB | 22.3 s | 22.2 s | 7.4 s |
| nyc, grid | 114 MB | 149 MB | 18.0 s | 18.3 s | 6.2 s |
| tyo, grid | 91 MB | 126 MB | 13.4 s | 13.7 s | 6.7 s |
| tyo, grid2 | 124 MB | 242 MB | 37.8 s | 36.7 s | 15.4 s |
| nyc, grid2 | 152 MB | 274 MB | 35.1 s | 35.7 s | 15.4 s |

Opening Tokyo's engines: 20 ms commute, 340-430 ms everyday (New York 40 and 590 ms). So Tokyo's
pack costs less than New York's on the same network (fewer cells, a steeper curve); what grows is
the network: doubling the stations doubles the peak (the crowding rounds' strategy tables) and
nearly triples the solve, on either pack. No per-pack limit breaks. A Tokyo-scale network would
put a worker near 250 MB and an edit near 35 s, which the performance work (T-362's loose
coupling, strategy tables) should take on before Japan's stage 2 joins more packs to one solve.

Not worth its cost: finding e-Stat's statsIds and file ids by hand (0.6M; now in japan.py and these
notes); the build's parks step, 35 minutes on an unprepared outline (fixed for the next pack);
waiting out the hour-long build in 4-minute waits (about 30 wakes, 1.1M); a first, slow
mesh-share method.

Proposals:
1. **Prepare the boundary in every enrichment that tests points against it** (parks done here;
   medical took 15 minutes and likely has the same cause; and `enrich_parks`'s ring intersection
   with the whole outline is 23 ms a park). A task: T-425. For hundreds of metros with long
   coastlines this is the largest build cost after the OSM read.
2. **Playbook step 4: a build over 15 minutes is waited on in one background wait** on the
   process, not repeated 4-minute foreground waits; the brief's 4-minute rule pushed toward polling.
3. **Japan's next packs (stage 2)** need no new research: japan.py takes a prefecture list. Read
   the whole Japan extract once (research/japan.md 4); give each pack its `neighbours` (land beyond
   the outline) and its region's telework figure.
4. **Names**: OSM's `name:ja-Hira` is almost only on places that already have `name:en` (1.3%
   gained); a third of Tokyo's places show kanji. A kanji reading dictionary would be the next
   step, but it is not a fixed table (decision 3 for Anita).
5. **Brief**: right and complete; the water test places from the cache worked first time.
