**Result**: each demand worker's corridor table (the first time it has market figures) took 2.6-4.2 s; now

`demand.worker.ts`'s `tableColumns` and `corridorTable`, removed after; env as its head says); JSON and logs in

## Where it stood after T-2122

T-2122 already built the table from its rows (`corridor_rows*`, 2.77M of 10.5M place pairs with today's
4,573 places) rather than whole-world arrays, but `corridor_rows_modes` was still 2.0-3.5 s of it: each row's two
ways each ran the whole `pair_mode_rule`, and its flight search scanned every airport's list of direct places
in a line (239 a place on average, up to 3,360): 4.7 billion entries for one way of every row, 0.6 s natively.
Each `corridor_rows*` call also walked all 10.5M corridors twice (a count, then the rows).

## What changed (sim `demand/intercity.rs` only; no interface change, no app change)

1. `pair_ways`: what a pair's two ways share (car, flight, crossings, routed drive, the car's running cost) is
   worked out once per corridor, then each way's share that flies (`PairRule::flown`, over its own income
   groups). Each part is the same both ways to the bit (the flight's minimum: every airport pair's fare is
   the same, so ties are the same cost; km, parking mean and drive lookups are symmetric).
2. `Directs`: for the rows pass, every airport's direct places as bits (2.5 MB, made for the pass in 3 ms and
   dropped). Elsewhere (`pair_mode_rule`, the open places' rules) the lists are kept sorted at load
   (`sorted`; the rows and packs already write them sorted) and binary-searched.
3. `table_rows` merges the sorted `trips_at` and `commute` lists instead of walking every corridor, and the
   row count is kept once per corridor set (`Corridors::rows`).
