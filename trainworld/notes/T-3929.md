# T-3929 Expresses drawn through locals on count and headway lines

## The cause, restated

`Service::lattice`'s `off_at` is not the bug and is unchanged. A count or headway line's return run leaves where
its round trip leaves its trains (`trip_len` of run 0, plus the share of a headway line's spare time the spacing
stands at the far end). That is what lets a fleet of whole trains chain end to end: forced to 0 (as a rate line
has it), the headway lines at 10/10 draw 10 expresses for the 9 they own (30/10: 10 for 9; 10/30: 4 for 3), the
T-2607 shuttle problem back. The term's history: `trip_len % h` since the first commit ("an odd whole-line fleet
stays evenly spaced around the complete circuit"), the spare share since T-2607 (4815d83b, half at each end) and
T-2972 (9752cd03, the spacing chooses the share, for even turns at merges).

What it does do: the two directions of an express/local pair run at offsets the round trips fix relative to each
other, so they can't both be at the overtake plan's best (T-3346's "the two directions can't both have the best").
Two holes then showed:

1. **Nothing timed an express that reaches the stretch close behind a local.** `timing::passing_waits` only makes
   locals wait at passing stations; `timing::following` only times pairs with no passing station. A local can't
   step aside before the first passing station, so an express leaving A just behind one was drawn through it on
   A-B. The departure spacing put run 0 there because its pull to the best offset is quadratic over half a headway
   (a 75 s miss cost 0.06), and split the difference between the directions. Not only count lines: rate lines at
   unequal rates (6 and 6.3 an hour) drift through every offset and had 8 such passes in 4 h.
2. **The floor.** Where the trains' own waits in one direction run longer than its end stand allows,
   `timing::floor` puts that run back on the slot's averaged waits, and the locals leave passing stations before
   their express (headway 30/10: run 1, 16 bad). Which offsets do this is known to the overtake plan, not to the
   spacing.

## What changed (sim only; no figure, round trip, fleet, save or demand input moves)

- `timing::lead_in` (new, before the waits): for a faster run behind the one slower run on a stretch with passing
  stations, a faster train that would reach the first passing station less than `LEAD_IN_CLEAR_S` (10 s) after
  the slower one it caught on the way is timed behind it to there (`follow_one`, as `following` does), its loss on
  top of the slot's timetable and capped by its stand at the far end (`following`'s rule). Other trains keep the
  slot's timetable; a run with none to time gets no variants. No spacing is asked at the stretch's start, as in
  `Plan::sweep`. (The plan's `PASS_CLEAR_S` there would hold every express 18 s on rate lines at the best offset,
  for the share of its averaged loss the slot spreads past B.)
- `timing::passing_waits` reads each faster train as it runs (its lead-in variant), so the local waits for it.
- Overtake `Outcome` keeps what each of the 24 offsets tried costs (`costs`) and the longest any slower train waits
  there (`slow`); `Plan::at` is where the stretch starts in each run. `Capacity::follow_targets` returns them.
- `spacing`: a following pair with passing stations reads the plan's cost at the offset (0 to 1, a share of its
  worst, per train) instead of the squared distance from the best, measured where the stretch starts; an offset
  where the slower train that waits longest would miss its next departure (its excess over the averaged waits
  past the stand at its run's far end beyond `TURN_MIN_S`, with this line's spare share when it is the slower one)
  counts `MISSED` (2 x `MERGE_FIRST`, so it outranks a merge) per train.
- Saved games keep their placed phases (LPH1, LSP1) until an edit places the lines again.
