# T-020 notes: access logit and the other access leg

Before: each access subzone sent all its riders to its single best access/egress station pair, and
access was walk-only, so homes beyond 1.5 km of a station had no rail and station counts were
lumpy (entries 5.8%, exits 6.5% off the per-cell reference). Now: a logit over every access option
and egress station, an "other" access leg at the home end, and no walk bands by default. Same
recompute time as before, better accuracy, and 51% of commutes can use rail instead of 33%.
Code: `sim/src/demand/kernel.rs` (`build_subzones`, `station_exp`, `mode_choice`, `fast_ln`).

## The model

For a commute from origin subzone u to destination subzone v, every combination of an access
option a of u (walk to a station, or the other leg to a station) and an egress station t of v is
a rail alternative costing `access_a + R[a,t] + egress_t` perceived minutes (R = station-to-station
wait, ride and transfers). Riders split over them by a logit with scale θ per perceived minute,
and rail as a whole is priced at the logsum:

    rail cost = -ln( sum_a sum_t exp(-θ (access_a + R[a,t] + egress_t)) ) / θ + fare

which then goes into the rail-vs-road logit as before (β = 0.05, rail constant 1.5). This is a
nested logit with nest ratio β/θ = 0.25. Boarding and alighting at one station is not a rail
trip (the diagonal of exp(-θR) is zero).

**θ = 0.2 per perceived minute.** A station 5 perceived minutes worse (about 3.6 real minutes or
~200 m more walking) gets e^-1 = 37% of the better one's weight, a 73/27 split; 10 minutes worse,
88/12. Station-choice studies put the access-time effect at roughly this strength, and it has to
be well above β (0.05) for the nest to make sense: at θ = β a station 14 minutes further would
still get half the riders. The formulation keeps exp(-θ R) in f32, so θ times the longest
station-to-station cost must stay under ~85 (0.2 allows 7 hours); θ cannot be pushed towards
all-or-nothing, which is fine because all-or-nothing is what this replaces.

## Making it cheap

Done naively that is 6 x 10 alternatives per subzone pair instead of the old 6 comparisons. The
logsum factorises:

    S_uv = sum_t A_u[t] E_v[t],   A_u[t] = sum_a exp(-θ(access_a - m_u)) exp(-θ R[a,t]),
    E_v[t] = exp(-θ(egress_t - m_v)),   rail cost = m_u + m_v + fare - ln(S_uv) / θ

(m_u, m_v are the cheapest option at each end, so nothing underflows.) A_u is one row of S
floats per origin subzone, built once from at most 10 access options (S multiply-adds each), so
the pair loop costs one multiply-add per egress station, one ln and one exp, whatever the number
of access options. Adding the other leg costs nothing per pair. Rail trips are kept per (u,
egress station) as Y_u[t] = sum_v x_uv E_v[t] / S_uv and split onto access stations once per
origin subzone, od[a,t] += Y_u[t] w_a exp(-θR[a,t]), which conserves trips exactly (tested).
The loop runs origin subzone by origin subzone with two S-long scratch rows, so the old
S x subzones `OriginAcc` table (10 MB) is gone: peak heap fell from 19-36 MB to 11-18 MB.

Per pair this still costs ~35 ns native against ~21 ns for the old best-pair loop. Two things
measured along the way:

- `fast_ln` first used a branch to fold the mantissa into [0.71, 1.41); it mispredicted about
  half the time and the ln cost ~30% of mode choice. Now branch-free (subtract the bits of
  1/sqrt(2)) and a degree-6 polynomial with no division (least-squares fit, 2.6e-6 absolute, which
  is 1.3e-5 minutes in a logsum).
- Scattering each pair's trips onto egress stations inside the pair loop chains every pair to the
  previous one through the Y row; they are now kept per destination subzone and scattered after
  the loop. No measurable gain, kept since it costs nothing.

**The real saving is dropping the walk bands.** The logit smooths what the 5-minute bands were
there to fix (all-or-nothing choice at subzone boundaries), so subzones are now just the cells of
a zone that share a nearest station: 5.7M pairs instead of 26.5M with bands and the other leg, at
better accuracy than T-005's banded default. Bands stay available (`band_min`) for when a worker
pool gives headroom: 10-minute bands cut every error by about a fifth for 2.4x the pairs.

## The other leg

Drive and park, drop-off, or a feeder bus to a station; one generic leg, at the home end only.

    cost = 30 + 2.5 x (d x 1.3 / 25 km/h) perceived minutes,  to the 4 nearest stations within 20 km

- **30 perceived minutes constant**: about 7 for the platform walk from the car park or the bus
  wait (5 minutes at weight 1.37) plus ~23 for everything the generic leg hides: needing a car or
  a ride, the mode change, a bus that may not exist. It is the "heavily penalised" in SPEC 4.3:
  six transfer penalties.
- **25 km/h x 1.3 detour, weighted 2.5**: between a feeder bus and a suburban drive. 7.8 perceived
  minutes per km, twice what driving the whole way costs per km (2.8 real minutes x 1.33), so it
  gets worse with distance and only pays for long trips. 5 km to a station: 69 minutes; 10 km:
  108; 20 km: 186.
- Crossover with walking (25.1 perceived min/km) is at 1.7 km, past the walk cutoff, so inside
  walking distance the other leg only takes the logit's share: ~4% of riders boarding within 1 km
  of home, 13-17% at 1-1.5 km (bus to the subway).
- **4 nearest stations within 20 km**, from the subzone's centroid; also offered to walk-served
  subzones, so someone 1 km from a local stop can drive to the commuter station.
- **Home end only.** Park-and-ride and drop-off start at home; at the work end only walking.
  Jobs beyond walking distance of a station still get no rail (follow-up task).
- Homes with no station in walking distance form one subzone per 2 km zone (one per cell in the
  exact reference), costs from its weighted centroid.

Effect of the constants (New York, hand-made network, 5-minute bands, free flow; rail constant 1.5
throughout):

| constant, weight | rail trips | via other leg | homes beyond walk | other share at 0-0.5 / 0.5-1 / 1-1.5 km |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| walk only | 849,715 | 0 | 0 | |
| 20, 2.0 | 1,264,038 | 55.6% | 28.9% | 14% / 26% / 62% |
| 30, 2.0 | 1,176,103 | 35.5% | 26.2% | 2% / 5% / 21% |
| **30, 2.5** | 1,139,739 | 31.5% | 24.2% | 2% / 4% / 15% |
| 40, 2.0 | 1,112,075 | 25.5% | 22.9% | 0% / 1% / 4% |
| 40, 3.0 | 1,054,758 | 20.4% | 19.0% | 0% / 0% / 2% |

20/2.0 had 14% of people living 500 m from a station driving or bussing to one, which is wrong for
New York. 30/2.5 leaves walking alone inside 1 km and gives a third of rail to the other leg
overall, plausible for a region where commuter rail is mostly park-and-ride and many subway
riders arrive by bus. These are judgement, not fitted (follow-up task).

## Results (New York, defaults: no bands, logit, other leg)

Free-flow first estimate, morning peak:

- Commutes from a home with any access to a walk-served workplace: 51.2% (2.35M of 4.58M), against
  32.6% with walking only.
- Rail 1,129,744 = 24.7% of all commutes (walk-only logit 843,055 = 18.4%; T-005's all-or-nothing
  804,939). After one crowding round 1,001,673.
- 30.8% of rail trips reach their station by the other leg; 24.1% of rail trips come from homes
  with no station in walking distance.
- Rail trips by distance from home to the boarding station: 0-0.5 km 17.0% (5% of them by the
  other leg); 0.5-1 km 41.7% (3%); 1-1.5 km 14.6% (13%); 1.5-2 km 4.8%; 2-3 km 10.7%; 3-5 km 7.4%;
  5-10 km 3.1%; 10-20 km 0.7% (all other leg beyond 1.5 km). Distances are from subzone
  centroids, so without bands the first two bands blur (with 5-minute bands: 26% and 30%).

The rail constant 1.5 was picked in T-005 to give walk-only rail 15-18%; with the logsum and the
other leg rail is 25%, and crowding on the hand-made network is far worse (worst segment 265% of
crush before the first round, 132% after). Refitting the constant belongs with real targets
(T-007), not this hand-made network.

The logit roughly halves the station error at a given grouping (the T-005 diagnosis was right).
What is left is grouping: a subzone's walk time is the mean over its cells, and exp of a mean is
not the mean of exps. Pruning small zone pairs is no longer a good lever: it drops long trips,
which now include most park-and-ride, and segment loads suffer most.
