# T-552: a trip that rides backwards

Anita's Tokyo game, 2026-10-16: a trip from near Tabata 6-chome to Kanda-Hakusan had 31% of its
rail riders board Line 119 northbound to Oji 1, change, and ride Line 119 back south past
Tabata 6-chome; 33% rode straight there.

## Why

Line 119's southbound trains are full by the time they reach Tabata 6-chome, so riders there are
left behind and wait for a later train. The model prices that wait into the boarding (T-188,
"riders left behind"). One stop north, at Oji 1, the train still has room, so riding up there and
boarding before Tabata gets a place on the train. It is not the timetable, not the two directions
treated as two lines, and not T-200's board-and-walk.

Checked on a test line shaped like hers (Oji 1, Tabata, Ogubashi, Kanda-Hakusan and two stops
more, 2 minutes a stop, 900 riders a train at crush), with the reproduction test since deleted:

- **No crowding, any headway** (2, 5, 10, 20, 30 and 60 minutes, with or without the 5-minute
  change penalty): the back ride never joins. Sharing the two directions' trains halves the first
  wait but costs the ride out and back and a second wait.
- **Crowded ride only** (the seats-to-crush factor on each stretch, left-behind off): never. The
  back rider sits through the same crowded stretches from Tabata on, plus one more.
- **Riders left behind**: the back ride joins once the extra wait for a southbound train at
  Tabata passes 6.6 minutes at a 5-minute headway (7.3 at 12 minutes, 10.2 at 30). Without the
  change penalty (T-557) it is 3.0 minutes (3.7, 6.5). Whether Oji 1 is the end of the line or a
  stop on the way makes no difference to these.
- With 38,000 riders through Tabata in the morning peak against 32,400 places, the wait at Tabata
  reaches the 60-minute cap from the first crowding round.

## The fault, fixed

The strategy search offered lines to a station in order of their ride cost, and added the
left-behind wait only when weighing each. So the direct southbound, cheapest to ride, always
joined first, however long its wait, and could never be dropped. The northbound then joined beside
it, and riders split by frequency: half and half for the two directions of one line, which is the
31% and 33% in her explorer. Half the riders were costed as taking a train they mostly could not
get on. In the test, Tabata to Kanda-Hakusan cost 75 perceived minutes with both, 56 riding back
alone.

Now a line that leaves riders behind is offered at its cost with the wait, after the lines that
beat it (`kernel::skims_extend`, test `a_full_line_joins_at_its_cost_with_the_wait`). With a
60-minute wait the direct train drops out and every rider on that trip rides back; with a 5-minute
wait the direct train stays and nobody rides back. Without any riders left behind the search is
unchanged to the bit.

## Keep it?

Anita barred it (T-575, 2026-10-16): nobody changes onto the line they left, so these riders wait
at Tabata 6-chome (`kernel::Search`, change nodes; test
`riders_wait_for_a_full_line_rather_than_ride_back`, and `a_change_off_a_line_takes_the_stations_other_lines`
for a rider changing off a line at a station whose strategy rides it back). The proposal below
was not taken. On the real New York save the demand final goes from 10,433,452 to 10,433,195
rail trips a day; `realnet` on New York's real network (commutes, three rounds) 5,188,158 to
5,187,918 rail trips, and the stage time sum 24.0-24.7 s to 29.6-30.3 s (+22%: about 900 change
nodes a destination search, T-581; a first timing that showed no change ran one binary twice).
Still open to riders:
out on one line and back on another (another line from the stop upstream, or one parallel to the
full line), and walking to a station upstream at the home end.

Riding back is what Tokyo riders do on full lines (gyaku-nori: riding a stop or two the other way
to board ahead of the crush or for a seat). Proposal: keep it. Line 119 needs more trains or
longer ones at Tabata 6-chome.

Two things make it come sooner in Tokyo than it should:

- **Crush is a New York car's** (160 a 20 m car, `api::CRUSH_PER_CAR`) everywhere; Tokyo's cars
  carry more at crush, so its trains read as full earlier. T-560.
- **At the end of the line** the back rider is charged a fresh wait for the southbound, though in
  life they would stay on the train as it turns. Small next to the wait it avoids.

## Checks

`cargo test --features demand`: 194 passed (the new test fails on the old order: 41 perceived
minutes for 18). Smoke passed; on the real New York save the demand final goes from 9,775,704 to
9,777,718 rail trips a day (+0.02%), where some riders are left behind.
