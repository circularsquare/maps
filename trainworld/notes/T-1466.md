# T-1466: are game buses too busy?

Analysis only, 2026-10-27. No game code changed. Sweep script `checks/t1466-bus-sweep.mjs`, table script
`checks/t1466-bus-table.mjs`, raw results in `../../artifacts/trainworld/t1466/` (new.json, real.json,
newc.json and realc.json with the corridor legs, var-*.json for the experiments).

## Answer

Yes. In dense places a game bus carries 2 to 6 times the riders per route-km of the busiest real bus
routes, and 3 to 7 times the riders per bus-hour of real systems. Outside dense places it is in the
real range (a Babylon feeder 440 a km, a suburban crosstown 80 a km). Two things make it so: the model
gives a bus no cost of its own beyond its time (a bus is a train that is slow and cheap), and the game
has no other buses, so the first line takes everything a real city's bus network already carries.

Notes on reading it:
- Ridership follows capacity where capacity binds. The Brooklyn feeder at 30, 15, 8 and 4 minutes
  carries 6,500, 15,700, 30,800 and 55,700: demand is far above what 8-minute buses hold, loads sit at
  100 to 115% of crush and 1,000 to 3,000 riders a day are turned away. Midday is the fullest period (a

- The suburban cases are fine. Babylon (22 a bus-hour) and the Nassau crosstown (4) are within or under

- Manhattan 42nd St: adding the bus lowers rail trips by 21,000 and raises driving by 17,000. Not chased;
  the bus stops at ten subway stations and charges the fare curve on the whole trip's km. Worth its own look.

## Where the riders come from

Boardings less the network's rise in rail-plus-bus trips (bus-only trips count as rail) are riders who
were already on rail and now reach it by bus instead of walking or driving. The corridor legs
(`cellModes` for the cells within 400 m of the route) say where the new trips come from.

| Bus | riders | existing rail riders, now bus access | new trips | of them from walking | from driving | riders per local leg of corridor residents |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New game Queens 2 km | 10,850 | 9% | 9,900 | 54% | 46% | 0.041 |
| Manhattan avenue 6 km | 29,700 | 86% | 4,200 | 49% | 51% | 0.065 |
| Brooklyn feeder 3.6 km | 30,800 | 79% | 6,400 | 65% | 35% | 0.239 |
| Queens feeder 3.6 km | 17,900 | 78% | 3,900 | 40% | 60% | 0.124 |
| Mineola feeder 3.6 km | 8,650 | 85% | 1,300 | 24% | 76% | 0.116 |
| Babylon feeder 4.8 km | 2,140 | 56% | 950 | 15% | 85% | 0.098 |

- On the real save four in five boardings are people who already took a train. The bus takes their walk
  or drive to the station. Bus-only trips are at most 10% of boardings wherever a station is on the route
  (the station end of the bus has 85 to 160% of boardings plus alightings), and the whole of it only on
  routes with no station, which are small. So "bus-only trips count as rail" is not what inflates this.
- The Brooklyn corridor residents make about 129,000 local legs a day; the bus carries one boarding for every
  4.2 of them. New York's buses as a whole carry about one for every 20 (1.36M weekday boardings, 8.3M people at
  about 3.4 trips), so a single route in the game carries the share of a whole bus network. Rough, because it
  assumes a leg is a trip.
- The new game starts with no rail to speak of, so there almost all riders are new trips.

## What drives it

Experiments in a throwaway worktree (constants changed, rebuilt, same scenarios, reverted and the tree
removed). Riders a day, change from the base in brackets.

| Bus | base | corridor 300 m (was 400) | +5 perceived min to board a bus | flat 13 km/h (was 18) | all three, penalty 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New game Queens 6 km | 14,326 | 12,324 (-14%) | 11,777 (-18%) | 12,706 (-11%) | 7,398 (-48%) |
| Manhattan avenue 6 km | 29,731 | 27,041 (-9%) | 20,298 (-32%) | 25,349 (-15%) | 9,666 (-67%) |
| Brooklyn feeder 3.6 km | 30,769 | 28,853 (-6%) | 22,377 (-27%) | 28,695 (-7%) | 12,524 (-59%) |
| Mineola feeder 3.6 km | 8,652 | 8,655 (0%) | 5,561 (-36%) | 7,002 (-19%) | 2,406 (-72%) |

At 13 km/h the same headway needs a third more buses (6 to 8, 4 to 5), which is a cost effect the player
feels. The penalty was added to the boarding cost of every route node of a line with a bus-only stop
(`rn_span`), so it also applies to a rider changing from train to bus; a real build should decide that.

Candidates, in order of what they explain:
1. **Nothing makes a bus worse than a train.** Rail and bus share one cost: walk, half-headway wait, ride,
   2 minutes per change, fare, no liking or disliking. Real buses lose people to unreliability, crowding
   at the stop, and stops that are not worth the wait for a short hop. The penalty is the biggest lever
   measured (-18 to -36% alone). It is also the way a walk of 800 m to a station loses to a bus
   100 m from the door that goes 1 km.
2. **No other buses in the baseline.** Walking and "car" in the base stand for real buses too (docs say so),
   so the first line takes all of that. Not tunable on its own; it is why the check should also be run on a
   network of buses (below).
3. **Speed.** 18 km/h flat beats the model's own car in dense places (local streets fall to 16.5 km/h) and
   is 40% over real NYC (13) and London (14.8). Measured -7 to -19% only, because the feeder rides are short.
   Real suburban buses do run 20 to 25, so by place is better than a lower flat number.
4. **The 400 m corridor.** The walk is the straight distance plus 100 m at 13 minutes a km (path factor
   already in), so a rider 400 m away walks the equivalent of 650 m of road. 300 m gives a real 400 m walk.
   Measured 0 to -14%: small, because most riders live near the route's station end.
5. **Capacity does limit**, but at 100 to 150% of crush for the whole midday because the model lets a train
   carry up to the busiest-hour factor times crush in the mean. So a full bus looks "capacity-limited"
   and ridership is capacity, not demand. Dropping the allowance for buses would cut ridership further in
   dense cases and nothing elsewhere.
6. **Fare curve.** Not isolated. Bus kilometres count in the one fare, so a bus to a train adds fare on
   long trips and 42nd St probably shows it; no sign it raises ridership.
7. **Bus-only trips in the train share.** At most 10% of boardings; leave it.
8. **Running cost.** Not about ridership, but $3 a bus-km is $54 a bus-hour at 18 km/h against $166 to $238
   a revenue hour in the NTD (NICE, MTA Bus). At 380 boardings a bus-hour any fare near a dollar pays for
   a bus several times over (the game's actual bus income was not read).

## Proposal

Together (13 km/h, 300 m, 8 minutes to board) the four cases move to: New game Queens 1,230 a km and 48 a
bus-hour; Manhattan 6 km 1,590 a km and 59; Brooklyn 3.6 km 3,470 a km and 128; Mineola 660 a km and 25.
That is the busiest-real-route range (1,000 to 2,500 a km, 40 to 100 a bus-hour) for the Queens and
Manhattan lines, with Brooklyn still 1.4 times the M15 per km (a 3.6 km feeder to a hub, so it should be
near the top) and Mineola in the typical band. Peak loads fall to 0.2 to 0.8 of crush.

Order of change, cheapest first:
1. A fixed cost of boarding a bus, 5 to 8 perceived minutes, buses only, one constant in the kernel
   (`rn_span` or the walk-in). This is a "complicating factor" in Anita's terms, so it needs her yes. The
   car's ride to a station already has a fixed cost and trains have the 2 minute change, so a bus having
   one is the same shape.
2. Speed by place instead of flat 18 (T-1445's B3): the lesser of 18 and about 70% of the place's car street
   speed, which gives 11 to 12 km/h in a dense centre and 18 to 25 outside. Needs the density the car
   speeds already use.
3. Corridor 300 m, one constant (`BUS_REACH_M`), with the +100 m kept.
4. Running cost to about $9 a bus-km, which at 13 to 18 km/h is $120 to $160 a bus-hour. Money only.
5. Leave capacity (80 at crush), the fare curve and the train-share counting as they are.

Do not multiply riders by a factor to hit a number.

Follow-ups worth a task each: (a) check a whole bus network, say 30 feeders over New York, against the real
1.36M weekday boardings once the above is in; (b) 42nd St, why a bus cuts rail trips; (c) look at
whether walking 800 m to a station should lose to a bus ride of 1 km at all, since four in five boardings
are existing rail riders changing their access.

## How it was measured

`checks/t1466-bus-sweep.mjs real|new out.json [url] [ids]` on a Vite on its own port: adds a bus line with
`addBusLine` (straight, so road km is 1.2 times the drawn), constructs it, sets a headway, waits for the
demand final, records the view (boardings per stop, loads per period, capacity use, the split) and
`cellModes` for the cells within 400 m, removes the line and waits for the baseline. The baseline came back
to within 0.001% each time. The real-save baseline solve is 80 s, a bus about 90 s.
