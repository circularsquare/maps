# T-3542: four or five service periods (design, agent tiers, 2026-11-17)

A delta on notes/T-3346.md (Detailed mode, stages 3-4), whose calls stand. Read-only; nothing built, nothing measured
in a browser (five slots cannot be timed without the code; the figures below are estimates from T-3521 and T-3512).

## How it works today (short)

- A line has a count or a headway for High, Medium, Low (`LEVELS = 3`). `PERIOD_LEVEL = [H, H, H, M, L]` maps them onto
  demand's five periods (`PERIOD_HOURS` 6-10, 10-16, 16-20, 20-24, 0-6, in the line's local time), the same every day
  and every place. T-1536 (Anita, 10-27) joined midday to High on purpose, to add no periods.
- Track side, all per level: capacity and delays, holds and profiles (`Run::prof[3]`), round trips, the settle,
  spacing phases, the boarding account's departures, the renderer's profile index (stride 3). Departures are laid
  per stretch of one level, so High keeps one headway 6-20. Fleet is the most of any level (and of weekends, T-3521);
  running cost is each level's rate times its hours.
- Demand already solves **each of the five periods on its own**, reading its level's stop times and headway
  (`period_net`). An edit re-solves only the periods whose level changed (`same_periods`). NYC (T-3512): one period
  (low or medium) settles in 7-27 s; High's three periods, two on one worker, about 50 s.
- Saves: three counts per line, `LAU1` three headways, `LBS2` settled rates, `LWE1` (T-3521) weekend percent and three
  hand counts, all behind `lev < 3`.
- UI: the line panel and the schedule window both use `Schedule` (three rows); the window adds Weekdays and Weekends
  sections. The window opens on running rail lines only.

## The design

- **Model (T-3346's period slots).** The sim keeps everything per demand period: 5 slots in place of 3 levels, game
  wide. A line's choice of 3, 4 or 5 rows only says which slots share one count: 3 = High (morning, midday,
  afternoon), Medium, Low; 4 = Rush (morning and afternoon, two stretches, one count), Midday, Evening, Night; 5 =
  Morning, Midday, Afternoon, Evening, Night. Her names fit demand's periods exactly, so at default hours nothing is
  averaged and **demand gains no new factor**: same five solves, same memory.
- **Copy, not solve**: a slot whose every line has the same rates and times as an earlier slot is copied. A game where
  no line uses 4 or 5 rows costs what it does now and gives the same figures to the bit.
- **Tweakable hours** (her 11-17 add, the "start an hour earlier"): the five cut hours (6, 10, 16, 20, 24) are per line,
  whole hours, each row at least 2 h. A row that crosses into another period is averaged into that period's slot, as
  T-3346 agreed on 11-12: the slot runs the time-weighted rate, demand gets the rider-weighted mean headway. Demand's
  own period hours never move (that would be about 20% more solve time per boundary; still "Not proposed").
- **Line panel**: the line's 3, 4 or 5 rows, weekdays only, as now. **The window**: the period choice and the hours,
  then Weekdays and Weekends with the same rows. Nothing about periods shows outside the window.
- **Weekends**: one percent per line as T-3521 built, applied to every row; counts set by hand become one per row. Same
  rows and hours as weekdays.
- **Switching**: up copies (High to morning, midday, afternoon). Down asks, then gives each joined row the most of
  its rows' trains, so no period loses service quietly. Hours stay.
- **Rows tied across slots** (rush, or a 3-row line beside a 5-row one on shared track) can settle to different round
  trips. The row shows its longest headway; the fleet is the most any slot needs; departures break into stretches
  where the slots' patterns differ (so a 3-row line keeps one 6-20 headway unless a neighbour differs).
- **Game-wide readouts** that name a level (capacity markers "at high demand", the trouble list's waits, running-cost
  hours) name the period instead ("in the morning peak", the existing period names). The sky band stays.
- Bus and ferry lines stay at 3 rows (the window is rail only). A quad twin follows its line's choice.

## Choices for Anita (each with my pick)

1. **Per line or per game?** Per line: the choice sits in each line's window, as she said "in there", and T-3543's
   "make these the default" carries it to new lines. Per game would keep every panel alike but puts a game-wide switch
   inside one line's window. I'd pick per line.
2. **Hours per line, or one set for the game?** Per line (it is the same window). I'd pick per line.
3. **Shortest row**: 2 hours, and a row's headway at most its length. I'd pick 2 hours.
4. **Down-switch** takes the most of the joined rows, after a question. I'd pick the most.
5. **Row names**: hers (Afternoon for 16-20) in the rows; other places keep "Evening peak". I'd keep both.

## Cost (estimates; the real-save measurement is a done-when of stage 1)

- **Demand**: whole solve unchanged (five periods solved today). An edit to one row re-solves only its periods: a
  morning, midday or afternoon edit should settle like a medium one today (7-27 s on NYC) against about 50 s for a
  High edit. Worker dealing (`periodsOfWorker`) should split morning and afternoon across workers so a Rush edit is
  one period's wait. Memory: a few KB (stop times 6 to 10 floats a stop).
- **Track side**, only once some line uses 4 or 5 rows: per-slot work grows up to 5/3. From T-3521 a second
  three-level settle cost NYC about 3 ms and London about 7 ms an edit natively (5.3-6.4 to 8.4-9.5 ms; 13 to 21 ms),
  so five slots add about 2 ms (NYC) and 5 ms (London) per kind of day, roughly double with weekends: NYC about 6 to
  11-13 ms, London 13 to 25-30 ms natively, two to three times that in WASM. Memory: London's second service was
  19 MB; five slots add at most two thirds of the per-level part, under 13 MB a service. Load: NYC 0.1 s, London 2 s,
  up to 5/3 of the settle share.

## Stage 0: slots (built, agent periods0, tree tw/periods0, 2026-11-18)

No player-visible change, no strings, no changelog item. **No new save block and no save LEVEL bump**: every
save block still writes three rows, each from its row's slot, and a load spreads them back; saves come out byte
for byte as master's.

### What it is

- `params.rs`: `SLOTS = 5` (slot = demand period, `PERIOD_HOURS` order) for everything the track side keeps per
  period; `ROWS = 3` for what the player sets, a save keeps and the app reads (`LineData` tph/trains/auto,
  `Weekend`, the `Op`s, `line_trains`, `line_auto`). `PERIOD_ROW` (was `PERIOD_LEVEL`), `PERIOD_SLOT_3 =
  [0,0,0,3,4]`, `ROW_SLOT = [0,3,4]`, `SLOT_ROW`; `HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW` are now the rows' **slots** (0, 3, 4).
  `LEVELS` is gone. Setting `SLOTS = 3` built and gave master's figures to the bit (a check); since stage 1a it no longer builds (four and five rows need a slot per period).
- Per slot now: `line_tph`, `line_tph_rest`, `line_phase/split/pace`, `Run::holds/stop_holds/prof`,
  `LineSvc::round_trip/trains/delay`, capacity `rho/delay`, `LineHolds`, `Lattice` (with its `period_slot`),
  settle and pace state (`pace_in`, `swings`, `settle_edited`, `settle_kept`'s level), boarding `stop_dep`,
  `trip_lens` (`2 * SLOTS`, was a hidden `[f64; 6]` with `k / 3`).
- **Copy-not-solve is "share, not copy"**: `Network::period_slot` says which slot serves each period
  (`PERIOD_SLOT_3` while every line sets three rows). Only the slots some period uses are worked out and read
  (`net.slots()`, `slot_worked`; `Capacity::worked` for the capacity loops); the others are never written and
  keep their defaults (0, empty; split 0.5). A period reads its serving slot, so nothing is copied. A trip's
  `level` is its slot (0, 3 or 4 today); `Lattice::levels_on` joins periods by slot, so High keeps one 6-20
  stretch. **Which slots share is a fixed three-row map** (`PERIOD_SLOT_3`), not the brief's comparison of every
  line's rates and times; with every line on three rows the two agree. Stage 1 builds the map from the lines'
  rows (the five-slot test below is the evidence that equal inputs give equal results, so merging by equal
  inputs is safe).
- Conversions only at the edges: `Network::rows_in` / `rows_of` / `slot_row` / `row_slot` at `set_line`,
  `set_tph`, `line_data`, save read and write (LRT1, LPH1, LSP1, LPC1, LWE1 rates and kept levels), eras
  (`encode_eras`/`decode_eras`), and every WASM readout that takes or gives a level (rows: `stop_times`,
  `stop_departures`, `lines_info`, `line_summary`, `line_tph_on`, `line_tph_rest`, `line_held`, `line_delays`,
  `line_waits`, `markers`, `full_track`, `running_cost(_in)`, `line_upkeep`, junction moves). `train_at` takes a
  trip's slot.
- Row-valued decisions from slots go row by row at the row's slot (`auto_counts`, `trim_auto`,
  `weekend_counts_in`), never once per slot.
- The `MEDIUM` lead (`Service::lattice`) read `prof[1]`, a hidden literal; now `prof[MEDIUM]`.
- Demand stays per row in stage 0: demand/api.rs uses `ROWS`/`PERIOD_ROW`; the clock worker's snapshot reads
  `stop_times` by row (clock.worker.ts ~3714, demandClient.ts:700 stride `run * 3 + lev`, realsave.rs:651).
- Renderer: profile `(line * 2 + run) * SLOTS + slot`. App: new `app/src/map/profiles.ts` (`SLOTS`,
  `PROFILES_PER_LINE`, `META_PER_LINE`, `ROW_SLOT`, `profileOf/Line/Run/Slot`, tt2's names) used by network.ts,
  busLayer, logoNetwork, synth, terminalBays, stateRows, clock.worker, boarding.ts, trainGuard, trainFollow,
  pick, stationLife, networkTips, MareyChart, TrainInspector, TrainTable and the shaders
  (rendererShaders.ts interpolates `${SLOTS}`). `TrackApi.profile_slots()` is checked against it when the clock
  worker makes a model (console error on a mismatch). Stage 2's port: tt2's `((line*2+run)*3+level)*9+var` is
  `profileOf(...)` with `SLOTS` for the 3.

## Stage 1a: 3/4/5 rows in the sim and saves (built, agent periods1, tree tw/periods1, 2026-11-18)

No player-visible change, no strings, no changelog item. The window's controls, the line panel's 4/5 rows and
the readouts renamed by period are stage 1b.

### What it is

- **State**: `Network::rows_default` (3, 4 or 5, the game's) and `LineData::rows` / `line_rows` (0 follows the
  game). `Network::rows(l)`: own, else the game's; bus and ferry lines 3; a quad twin its line's.
- **No per-line slots.** The choices nest (4 splits midday out of high; 5 the afternoon too), so `period_slot`
  is the finest any live line sets: one of `PERIOD_SLOT_N` = `[0,0,0,3,4]`, `[0,1,0,3,4]`, `[0,1,2,3,4]`.
  `refresh_slots` works it out after every edit, undo and load (`commit_with`, `read_lpd1`).
- **Values in places, not wider arrays** (advisor's call): every `[T; 3]` (`tph`, `trains`, `auto`, `Weekend`)
  keeps its meaning for every line (high = rush or morning, medium = evening, low = night); `LineData::x: Extra`
  holds midday and afternoon (trains, headways, weekend counts and headways; rates live in `line_tph[1..2]`).
  `params::places`/`unplace` (5 places), `PERIOD_PLACE[n]` (the place each period reads), `ROW_PLACES[n]` (a
  line's rows in day order). Accessors `trains_of`/`auto_of`/`trains_at`/`auto_at`/`places_in`/`places_most`/
  `row_slots` replace `c[slot_row(l, s)]`; `slot_row` is gone.
- **Tied rows**: `auto_counts` takes the most any of a row's slots needs; `trim_auto` the fewest any leaves
  waiting; `weekend_counts_in` the most; `allocate_unallocated` the most (`places_most`).
- **Slot changes** (`TrackWorld::reslot`): `Network::seed_slots(old)` copies each newly worked slot from the slot
  that served its period (rates both kinds of day, phase, split, pace, kept lines) and puts slots no longer
  worked back to as made; then `reload` with rates and pacing taken as they stand and departures kept (the
  from-save flags), eras and shifts kept. Weekends keep their rates until the next edit settles them, as after a
  load (an immediate weekend settle cost London at 70% 9 s natively). The edit's result marks every line.
- **Switching** (`TrackWorld::rows_ops`): `Op::Rows { line: Option<u32>, rows }` plus, per line it moves, its
  counts (`TrainCounts`/`AutoTrains`), hand weekend counts (`Weekend`) and `Op::Extra`. A new row takes the most
  trains (the shortest nonzero headway) of the old places its periods had, so up copies and down joins by the
  most. The down-switch question is 1b's.
- **Departures joined** (`Service::joined`): a line's consecutive periods of one row (morning-midday,
  midday-afternoon) share a slot in its `Lattice::period_slot` where rates, offsets, phases (both kinds of day)
  and round trips are equal to the bit; never across rows. So a 3-row line in a game of five keeps one 6-20
  stretch while its slots run alike.
- **The 3-row setters on a 4/5-row line** (`set_train_counts`, `set_auto_headway`, weekend counts) set every
  high period: midday and afternoon take high's value (`high_rows`), so today's panel stays consistent.
- **Saves**: new trailing block `LPD1` after `LOS1`, written only when the game or a line sets other than three
  rows (layout in save.rs's header comment); LEVEL 22 to 23. The other blocks keep three rows, each from its
  row's slot; kept lines of slots 1-2 go in `LPD1` only. Eras: `ERA3` (every slot and each period's slot) only
  where some era runs other slots than three rows'; otherwise `ERA2` as before.
- **WASM**: `game_periods`, `line_period_count`, `line_periods_own`, `period_slots`, `set_game_periods(n)`,
  `set_line_periods(id, n)`, `line_row_trains`, `line_row_headways`, `set_line_row_trains(id, [..])`,
  `set_line_row_headways(id, [..])` (n values in day order), `line_tph_by_period`, `stop_times_in` and
  `stop_departures_in` (by period). App edit ops (protocol.ts): `setPeriods { line?, rows }`,
  `setRowTrains`, `setRowHeadways`, so play.mjs drives them through `tw.client.edit`.
- **Demand per period**: `demand::api::Service` keeps 10 times a stop (`(run * 5 + period) * n`) and 5 tph a
  line; `per_period` also takes the old 6/3 layout (fixtures, realsave.rs) and spreads it by `PERIOD_ROW`;
  `same_periods` compares each period's own times and rate. App: the clock worker's snapshot reads
  `stop_times_in`/`line_tph_by_period`; `packService`, demandClusters (`periodSig` per period), demand.worker
  strides use `TIMES_PER_STOP` (game/periods.ts). The demand cache keys a period array whose morning, midday and
  afternoon agree as the three rows (`asRows`), so a game of three rows should keep its cached results (not measured). The figures'
  `tph` stays three rows (morning, evening, night) for the money readouts until 1b.
- **Worker dealing**: two workers deal {morning, evening, night} and {midday, afternoon} (was {0,2,4}, {1,3}): the
  same work each, high's three still two on one worker, a rush edit one period on each. Three or more workers
  already part morning and afternoon.

### What it is

- **Window** (SchedulePanel.tsx `Periods`, rail lines only): under Set by, "Periods" 3 / 4 / 5 (the game's,
  `setPeriods` with no line); under it "Set for this line" (stores the line's own at the rows it has); once own, a
  "This line" 3 / 4 / 5 under the game's (one grid, the switches in one column) and "Same as game" where Set for
  this line was. A switch that joins rows (the game's down, this line's down, or Same as game below its own) asks
  first (`joinedRows`, `switchPeriods`, the `ask` dialog: red yes "Use 3 periods", no "Keep 5 periods"); a line's
  question names the most trains of the joined rows as shown (a headway line the shortest headway), the game's
  says "on every line without its own periods". Up switches ask nothing.
- **Table** (schedule.tsx): one component for 3, 4 or 5 rows, `lev` a row index. A line of three rows reads and
  edits exactly as before (every row function dispatches to the old ones: `setTrainCount`, `setHeadway`,

  `line_row_headways`, `line_row_weekend`) and edit by `setRowTrains`, `setRowHeadways` and the new `rowWeekend`

  (`rowOfPeriod`); crowding dots by the row's periods (`crowdIn`). Line panel: its rows, weekdays only.
- **Line panel** of a 4/5-row line: round trip, delay, average speed at the row now (its `rows` figures); delays and
  waits asked by period (`line_delays_in`, `line_waits_in`); held and limits per row (`line_row_held`, the lineHeld
  query's `rows: true`; the held rows named by row). Fleet over every row (`fleetOf`: trains bought, car steps).
