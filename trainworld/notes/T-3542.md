# T-3542: four or five service periods (design, agent tiers, 2026-11-17)

A delta on notes/T-3346.md (Detailed mode, stages 3-4), whose calls stand. Read-only; nothing built, nothing measured
in a browser (five slots cannot be timed without the code; the figures below are estimates from T-3521 and T-3512).

## How it works today (short)

- A line has a count or a headway for High, Medium, Low (`LEVELS = 3`). `PERIOD_LEVEL = [H, H, H, M, L]` maps them onto
  demand's five periods (`PERIOD_HOURS` 6-10, 10-16, 16-20, 20-24, 0-6, in the line's local time), the same every day
  and every place. T-1536 (Anita, 10-27) joined midday to High on purpose, to add no periods.
- Track side, all per level: capacity and delays, holds and profiles (`Run::prof[3]`), round trips, the settle,
  spacing phases, the boarding account's departures, the renderer's profile index (stride 3). Departures are laid
  per stretch of one level, so High keeps one headway 6-20. Fleet is the most of any level (and of weekends, T-3521);
  running cost is each level's rate times its hours.
- Demand already solves **each of the five periods on its own**, reading its level's stop times and headway
  (`period_net`). An edit re-solves only the periods whose level changed (`same_periods`). NYC (T-3512): one period
  (low or medium) settles in 7-27 s; High's three periods, two on one worker, about 50 s.
- Saves: three counts per line, `LAU1` three headways, `LBS2` settled rates, `LWE1` (T-3521) weekend percent and three
  hand counts, all behind `lev < 3`.
- UI: the line panel and the schedule window both use `Schedule` (three rows); the window adds Weekdays and Weekends
  sections. The window opens on running rail lines only.

## The design

- **Model (T-3346's period slots).** The sim keeps everything per demand period: 5 slots in place of 3 levels, game
  wide. A line's choice of 3, 4 or 5 rows only says which slots share one count: 3 = High (morning, midday,
  afternoon), Medium, Low; 4 = Rush (morning and afternoon, two stretches, one count), Midday, Evening, Night; 5 =
  Morning, Midday, Afternoon, Evening, Night. Her names fit demand's periods exactly, so at default hours nothing is
  averaged and **demand gains no new factor**: same five solves, same memory.
- **Copy, not solve**: a slot whose every line has the same rates and times as an earlier slot is copied. A game where
  no line uses 4 or 5 rows costs what it does now and gives the same figures to the bit.
- **Tweakable hours** (her 11-17 add, the "start an hour earlier"): the five cut hours (6, 10, 16, 20, 24) are per line,
  whole hours, each row at least 2 h. A row that crosses into another period is averaged into that period's slot, as
  T-3346 agreed on 11-12: the slot runs the time-weighted rate, demand gets the rider-weighted mean headway. Demand's
  own period hours never move (that would be about 20% more solve time per boundary; still "Not proposed").
- **Line panel**: the line's 3, 4 or 5 rows, weekdays only, as now. **The window**: the period choice and the hours,
  then Weekdays and Weekends with the same rows. Nothing about periods shows outside the window.
- **Weekends**: one percent per line as T-3521 built, applied to every row; counts set by hand become one per row. Same
  rows and hours as weekdays.
- **Switching**: up copies (High to morning, midday, afternoon). Down asks, then gives each joined row the most of
  its rows' trains, so no period loses service quietly. Hours stay.
- **Rows tied across slots** (rush, or a 3-row line beside a 5-row one on shared track) can settle to different round
  trips. The row shows its longest headway; the fleet is the most any slot needs; departures break into stretches
  where the slots' patterns differ (so a 3-row line keeps one 6-20 headway unless a neighbour differs).
- **Game-wide readouts** that name a level (capacity markers "at high demand", the trouble list's waits, running-cost
  hours) name the period instead ("in the morning peak", the existing period names). The sky band stays.
- Bus and ferry lines stay at 3 rows (the window is rail only). A quad twin follows its line's choice.

## Her answers (2026-11-17, via maps-60)

- Periods (3/4/5) and row hours: **the simplest control sets them for the whole game**; per line is an extra step
  ("you should have to do extra work to be able to say like oh this is specific to this line"). So the window's
  choice and hours are game-wide by default, with a per-line override.
- Shortest row 2 hours: yes.
- **No cap on a row's headway**: "sometimes you want a train line with like 6 hour headways. i think we shoul allow
  this and handle it how we're currently handling it." Drop "a row's headway at most its length".
- Switching down asks first: yes.
- 16-20 is "Afternoon": yes, as drafted.

## Choices for Anita (each with my pick)

1. **Per line or per game?** Per line: the choice sits in each line's window, as she said "in there", and T-3543's
   "make these the default" carries it to new lines. Per game would keep every panel alike but puts a game-wide switch
   inside one line's window. I'd pick per line.
2. **Hours per line, or one set for the game?** Per line (it is the same window). I'd pick per line.
3. **Shortest row**: 2 hours, and a row's headway at most its length. I'd pick 2 hours.
4. **Down-switch** takes the most of the joined rows, after a question. I'd pick the most.
5. **Row names**: hers (Afternoon for 16-20) in the rows; other places keep "Evening peak". I'd keep both.

## Cost (estimates; the real-save measurement is a done-when of stage 1)

- **Demand**: whole solve unchanged (five periods solved today). An edit to one row re-solves only its periods: a
  morning, midday or afternoon edit should settle like a medium one today (7-27 s on NYC) against about 50 s for a
  High edit. Worker dealing (`periodsOfWorker`) should split morning and afternoon across workers so a Rush edit is
  one period's wait. Memory: a few KB (stop times 6 to 10 floats a stop).
- **Track side**, only once some line uses 4 or 5 rows: per-slot work grows up to 5/3. From T-3521 a second
  three-level settle cost NYC about 3 ms and London about 7 ms an edit natively (5.3-6.4 to 8.4-9.5 ms; 13 to 21 ms),
  so five slots add about 2 ms (NYC) and 5 ms (London) per kind of day, roughly double with weekends: NYC about 6 to
  11-13 ms, London 13 to 25-30 ms natively, two to three times that in WASM. Memory: London's second service was
  19 MB; five slots add at most two thirds of the per-level part, under 13 MB a service. Load: NYC 0.1 s, London 2 s,
  up to 5/3 of the settle share.

## Stages (after T-3521 and T-3346 stage 2 land: both rewrite the same per-level arrays and the stride-3 index)

| Stage | What | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Slots: `LEVELS` 3 to 5 by period, copy-not-solve, renderer index, saves expand on load; real save and London to the bit, edit times unchanged | 5-8 |
| 1 | 3/4/5 rows per line: the window's choice, the line panel's rows, the save block, weekends per row, switching, readouts by period, worker dealing; measure NYC with every line at 5 rows | 6-9 |
| 2 | Tweakable hours: per-line cuts, rows averaged into slots, counts settled per row, demand's mean headway, the 2 h and headway caps, the hours boxes | 8-12 |

About 19-29 h in all, against T-3346's 18-25 for stages 3-4 (T-3521 built most of stage 4). Stage 2 can wait.

**What could break**: a stride or `[_; 3]` missed in stage 0 shows as trains drawn on the wrong profile (the to-the-bit
check catches it); T-3521's `LWE1` and `lev < 3` guards; old saves (load as 3 rows, unchanged); the lead and headway
caps assume a 4-hour shortest stretch (`LEAD_MAX_S`, `HEADWAY_MAX_S`), so stage 2 must lower them with the 2 h rows;
the T-3346 stage 2 renderer index `((line*2+run)*3+level)*9+var` and app `map/profiles.ts`.
