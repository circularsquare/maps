## Which pairs change most

Short and medium pairs against the car change most (+160% to +200%); pairs where the train already wins (Tokyo -
Sendai, London - Paris, Tokyo - Osaka) change little (+3% to +10%). The old rule charged every extra station within
a walk ln(n) / 0.10 perceived minutes, 7 to 18 minutes for 2 to 6 stations. On a 1-2 hour trip against a car that is
a large part of rail's cost and its share is small and steep, so removing it moves a lot. On long trips the mode
choice is damped by the trip's length (0.10 x sqrt(60 / T)) and the station choice softened the same way, so the
same minutes count for much less; and where rail already has most of the trips (London - Paris 94%, all of the
market's 23,984) there is little left to win. Edinburgh - Glasgow is the case players complained about: before the
fix its seven test feeders lowered rail (2,822 against 3,305 alone).

## Against real figures (after the fix)

- Edinburgh - Glasgow 0.8x real, about right.
- London - Birmingham about 2x over (17.6k against 7.4-10k). London's real network (171 lines) feeds Euston very
  well; Birmingham has 3 test lines.
- Beijing - Tianjin 0.26x. Both metros real; the market's whole corridor is 247,674 trips a day, half of them commute
  legs, and rail stays at 8%.
- Tokyo - Sendai 1.3x (30.5k against 22.9k); rail takes 88% of the market's 34,868.
- London - Paris about right or a little over, capped by the market's 23,984 trips (T-3426 said the same).
- Tokyo - Osaka 0.29x (28k against 97k) with the real networks, and below the line alone ("Looks wrong" 2); the
  market has 146,050 trips a day against MLIT's 129,736 by every mode, so the gap is rail's share, 19% against 75%.

## How it was measured

- Weekday, demand final, the same build script both sides (scratchpad feedsample/pairrun.mjs). Each run is a new
  sandbox game in its own headless Chrome; the inter-city line is built with the game's own edit ops.
- Real networks: the game's real-network saves (data/packs/real) for both cities, merged into one save by a small
  tool on the sim crate (feedsample/merge, `twmerge`): the first save's header and running side, the second's
  nodes, edges, stations, lines and links added with new ids. Merged: Beijing + Tianjin (48 lines), Tokyo + Sendai
  (150), London + Paris (268), Tokyo + Osaka (298). London - Birmingham uses London's save alone.
- Test feeders where there is no real network, about the real rail coverage: Edinburgh 3 (the tram, two cross-city
  lines standing in for the suburban routes), Glasgow 4 (the Subway as an open loop, North Clyde, Argyle and Cathcart
  lines), Birmingham 3 (Cross-City, the tram, the Snow Hill line). Each has a station within 300 m of the inter-city
  terminal except Glasgow's Argyle and Cathcart lines and Birmingham's Snow Hill line, which meet it through the others.
  Buses are not built anywhere.
- The inter-city line, near its real service: Edinburgh - Glasgow ordinary, 8 cars, every 15/15/30 minutes (high,
  medium, low demand), via Edinburgh Park, Linlithgow, Falkirk High; Beijing - Tianjin high speed, 8 cars, 7.5/10/30
  minutes, via Wuqing; London - Birmingham ordinary, 11 cars, 15/20/30, via Milton Keynes, Coventry, Birmingham
  International; Tokyo - Sendai high speed, 16 cars, 7.5/10/30, via Ueno, Omiya, Utsunomiya, Koriyama, Fukushima;
  London - Paris high speed, 16 cars, 40/60/120, via Ashford and Lille, ending beside Saint-Denis; Tokyo - Osaka high
  speed, 16 cars, 5/6.7/20, via Shin-Yokohama, Nagoya, Kyoto.
- Joining the line to a real network: each end is placed beside the nearest real station within 1.5 km and the two
  are linked (a passage). The offset needed to clear the real platforms: Beijing and Tianjin 150 m (inside the 300 m
  walk anyway), London Euston 150 m on an elevated level, Tokyo-Sendai 900 m, Tokyo-Osaka 300 m, London St Pancras
  and Saint-Denis 900 m. Paris Gare du Nord itself could not be reached under the RER at the deepest level.
- Real-network runs with a passage were measured after saving the built game and loading it again in a fresh page
  (see "Looks wrong" 1).

## Looks wrong (for follow-up, not this task)

1. **A passage added during play can be missed by long-distance demand.** On Tokyo - Sendai the same build gave
   either about 5.9k or about 30.5k, run to run, in both trees (5,913 twice and 5,896; 30,580 and 30,534; before:
   28,105, 27,984 and 5,210). The low figure is the line-alone figure, as if the passage to the metro were not there.
   It came whenever the solve after building was a quick update; a full solve (a reload of the same game) gave the
   high figure every time. Adding the passage before the line made no difference. A player who links an inter-city
   station to a metro might not see the effect until a reload.
2. **Adding a big real network can lower long-distance rail below the line alone.** Tokyo - Osaka: 27,857 before and
   28,361 after with both real networks, against 43,717 and 48,092 with the line alone, in both trees, so not the
   fix. A guess, not checked: the market keeps up to 10 access stations a zone, and with a dense metro the inter-city
   terminal drops out of many zones' sets, so their trips reach it only through metro rides and changes.
3. T-3426's London - Paris real figure (12.2M a year) is above ORR's 11M for every Eurostar route through the tunnel
   (2024-25); London - Paris is likely about 7-8M a year, 20k a day.
