# T-1644 GPU at zoom 15 on the Japan save (2026-10-29)

Panning at zoom 15 kept the GPU at 37% with the overlay at 6-7 ms a frame. Now 23-27% and 1.7-2.3 ms.

## Setup

As notes/T-1618.md: headless Chrome on the real GPU (ANGLE d3d11, RX 6600), canvas 1603x994 at pixel
ratio 1, globe and Terrain on, light shade, `t737-japan-trains.save` (807 lines, 9,529 stations, 5,170
trips), demand final (1,335 s with 1 worker), Tokyo station at zoom 15 unless the row says otherwise.
Pans are a `jumpTo` a frame east over 12 s. Raw logs: `artifacts/trainworld/t1644/` (`pan-before.log`,
`pan-gather.log`, `cpu-a.log`, `check.md`). Scripts: `notes/T-1644/` (`pan15.mjs` the ablation rows,
`cpu15.mjs` before and after by view, `cmp15.mjs` the pixel check, `shots.mjs`), on `hold.mjs`'s Chrome
from notes/T-1618/.

Measures: GPU % is the Chrome GPU process's 3D engine use (typeperf, the Task Manager figure);
overlay GPU ms is the timer query round `renderer.draw`. The two do not add up with MapLibre's own
timer (it reads 1 ms with a heavy overlay and 3-6 ms with a light one: a query spans the GPU waiting
and its clock changing). Trust GPU %: MapLibre alone is 20-22%, so the overlay's share was about 15
points and is now about 3-5. The 10.7 ms of T-1639's table is not reproduced here: the same view read
5.8-7.2 ms before any change (the save has been rebuilt since, and the machine's state differs).

## Where the time went (before; `pan15.mjs`, panning, clock paused)

Overlay GPU ms with a part of the draw's GL calls skipped (a program, or a pass by its uniforms):

| part | overlay GPU ms |
|---|---|
| everything | 5.8 |
| no track or line strokes | 1.9 (the strokes cost 3.9) |
| no stations or bridges | 4.9 (stations 0.9; alone 1.1) |
| no trains | 4.8 (trains 1.0; alone 0.8) |
| nothing drawn (clears, resolves, cache copies) | 0.15 |
| no casing passes / no fill passes / no through-the-boxes layer | 4.1 / 4.6 / 4.6 |
| cache samples 4 / 2 / 1 | 5.8 / 5.5 / 5.4 |
| 1x1 viewport (no fill work at all) | 4.5 |

So it was the strokes, and not their fill: MSAA hardly matters (4 to 1 samples saves 0.4 ms), and a 1x1
viewport keeps nearly all the time, so what costs is the vertex work and, it turned out, the calls.
Each of the three passes (casing, fill, the layer drawn through station boxes) drew 291k instances in
about 200 ranges, and every range rebinds nine attribute buffers: 15,000 GL calls a frame. Only
1,442 track and 1,442 line segments are on screen at zoom 15. Lanes are not drawn at this zoom, so
one mode a pass. The station boxes cost about 1 ms (28,587 instances in 3 calls, all stations whatever
is on screen), the trains about 1 ms; neither was the main cost.

Finer culling alone (16-instance chunks) took the instances from 291k to 47k but the overlay only to
5.1 ms: 250 ranges times three passes of rebinding is what was left (strokes alone 2.8 ms). The fix
that worked draws no ranges at all.

## What changed

- `map/strokeLod.ts`: chunk boxes are 16 instances, in levels of 8 boxes each (was 256 in groups of
  16); a box wholly in the view is emitted without testing what is in it; `minLevel` stops the testing
  at boxes of 128 or 1024 instances below zoom 12 (the globe's projection makes a box test cost about
  190 ns, and at zoom 11 a 16-instance level took 4-7 ms a frame); `mergeRanges` joins ranges.
- `map/strokeGather.ts` (new): when at most 40,000 instances show, they are copied from the arrays the
  strokes were made from (the network's, or a zoom band's simplified copy, plus the demand widths and
  traffic marks) into one buffer set of nine attributes, and each pass is one draw. More than that are
  drawn by ranges as before, gaps under 512 instances drawn through.
- `renderer.ts`: `pickStrokes` does the above; bands keep their strokes and widths; `strokeGather`
  flag (`?perfOff=strokeGather`, in the cache's key); `dropLod` frees the gather buffers.
- docs/architecture.md and code-map.md.

## What is left at zoom 15

Panning is now 23% of which MapLibre is 20-22. The overlay's 1.7 ms is the stations (1.1 ms: all 9,529
drawn three times whatever is on screen), the trains (0.8 ms: 5,170 trips of 96 vertices each, also
all of them) and 0.15 ms of clears and copies; strokes are about 0.4. With the clock running and the
map still, zoom 15 is 18-19% GPU for the same reason (stations and trains drawn 56 times a second).
Culling the stations and trains to the view would take most of the rest, but needs a spatial index
of stations and trips (the strokes' trick of boxes over a spatial order does not apply to either's
buffer order). Not done: it would save about 1.5 ms a frame and the station and train shaders are
heavy to change.
