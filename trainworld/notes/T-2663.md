# T-2663: attractions worldwide, and are there enough

Agent attract, 2026-11-06. Measure and answer only: no pack, demand or file changes.

## For Anita

**How it works.** Attractions are one world list, `app/src/game/attractions.json` (12,641 museums,
temples, castles, theme parks, zoos and the like from Wikidata), loaded by the game when it starts.
Every pack takes the ones near its cells when it opens, so every region gets them the same way,
the new world-plan regions included: there is no per-country step, and no rebuild is needed when a
region is added. Each attraction draws visitors from the pack's homes and restaurants and hotels
(half each, the chance halving every 10 km), on top of everyday outings.

**Are there enough?** In Europe, Japan, Korea and most of the US, yes. In China, India, Southeast
Asia, Brazil, Mexico and Nigeria, no: about half of the people in the built world (52%) live in a
pack with no attraction at all. The list only takes places that 10 or more Wikipedia languages
write about, and that rule favours Europe. Italy has 16 attractions per million people, Britain 11,
Japan 3.3, the US 1.7, but China 0.2, India 0.27, Brazil 0.28 and the Philippines 0.18. Shenzhen
(17M people) has one, Kunming and Guilin none, Mumbai 14, while OpenStreetMap tags 484 places in
Shenzhen, 155 in Kunming and 65 in Guilin as attractions, museums, theme parks or zoos.

A few kinds of place are missing everywhere: waterfalls (Niagara Falls, Iguaçu), caves, hot springs
(Pamukkale), opera houses (the Sydney Opera House), big wheels (the London Eye) and casino resorts,
so Las Vegas has one attraction (a small museum; OSM tags 159 places there).

**Does a player notice?** Mostly on the map. Attraction trips are small: about 1% of leisure
outings worldwide, 3-9% in Europe. What shows is a famous tourist city with no attraction marks and
nothing under the Attractions filter, and on long holidays its zones not pulling visitors.

**What I'd do** (two follow-ups, both changing only how the list is built; no pack rebuild):
- T-2666: add those missing kinds of place (waterfalls, caves, hot springs, opera houses, big wheels,
  casinos), and let an attraction reach a cell up to 2 km away, since a mountain's point is its top
  (Mount Tai and Jiuzhaigou are just over the 600 m and draw nobody now). About an hour to rerun.
- T-2667: fill the thin countries from OpenStreetMap's `tourism=*` places where Wikidata has
  nothing, sized by the same budget the list uses now. This one adds trips in Asia, Latin America
  and Africa (small, on top of leisure outings), so it is your call.

Places not built yet (Mecca, Cairo and Giza, Jerusalem, Cape Town, Marrakesh, Nairobi, Buenos Aires,
Cusco and Machu Picchu, Dubai) are already in the list and will draw visitors when their regions are built.

## 1. How attractions work today

Rules: docs/demand-sources.md "Attractions", docs/demand-worldwide.md "Attractions", demand-market.md
"Touristy zones". In short:

- **Source.** `pipeline/attractions_json.py`: Wikidata through QLever, items of 24 classes and their
  subclasses (museum, zoo, aquarium, amusement/theme/water park, botanical garden, planetarium,
  temples, shrines, churches, cathedrals, mosques, synagogues, castles, palaces, fortifications,
  observation towers, monuments, statues, archaeological sites) plus anything typed "tourist
  attraction" directly, with coordinates and **10 or more sitelinks or a visitor figure**. Left out:
  settlements, squares, streets, bridges, parks and nature (parks have their own draw), stadiums,
  stations, airports, universities, libraries, hospitals, hotels, shops, offices, cemeteries.
- **Size.** Wikidata's visitors a year (P1174; 1,273 places) else a budget by Wikipedia page views:
  6 outings a year per person within 25 km shared among the listed places there, plus one world pool
  of tourists (World Bank arrivals plus one domestic trip per resident). x5 in mainland China, x2.5
  in Korea. Kept from 100,000 a year.
- **Into demand.** At a pack's opening the demand worker hands its everyday layer the attractions in
  its box (`attractionsIn`, demand.worker.ts); each goes to the nearest cell within 600 m. Visits are
  their own purpose, "attractions": each a trip out and back, origins half residents and half leisure
  jobs (hotels and restaurants standing in for where visitors stay), 14.4 km decay, on top of social
  outings. Weekends take social's factors (1.54, 1.67), a superholiday's away day 1.5x a Sunday.
  The regional market uses them for one thing: a zone's touristiness (visits per resident, 3 to 30)
  tilts superholiday going-out trips towards it. Ordinary long trips are not drawn to attractions.
- **Same everywhere?** Yes. All 3,546 packs carry the everyday layer (`errand_jobs`), so all take
  attractions; the file is not tied to which packs exist (built 2026-11-02, before many regions),
  and nothing per pack is stored. What differs by country is only what Wikidata holds.

## 4. Looking outward

- **Simutrans**: each attraction has a "level" (popularity); passengers to it follow its level and the
  population that can reach it, and each arrival makes a return trip. The same shape as ours.
  [wiki](https://simutrans-germany.com/wiki/wiki/en_TouristAttractions),
  [attraction list help](https://translator.simutrans.com/data/htmlex/en/curiositylist_filter.html)
- **Cities: Skylines**: tourists are their own agents arriving through outside connections, drawn by
  the city's attractiveness (unique buildings, parks); the connections cap how many come.
  [Parklife dev diary 6](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/cities-skylines-parklife-dev-diary-6-introduction-to-tourism.1099844/post-24270369)
  Ours has no visitors arriving from outside a pack; leisure jobs stand in for their hotels.
- **Regional travel models with a visitor model** (southern Nevada, Hawaii): visitor trips are
  generated from hotel rooms times occupancy, and attracted by resort, casino and retail jobs.
  [NDOT statewide model](https://dot.nv.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/4968/636193792324500000).
  Our half-from-leisure-jobs origin is the cheap version of the hotel-room origin; OSM
  `tourism=hotel` would be the global source if it ever needed sharpening.
- **Page views as a visitor proxy**: daily mobile page views tracked gate counts at six Orléans
  attractions ([Eustache and Favier 2026, preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.29991)); page views
  help forecast arrivals ([Alis et al. 2015](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/79613)). Supports T-1766's budget.

Global sources and what each gives:

| source | gives | for us |
|---|---|---|
| Wikidata classes + sitelinks (in use) | names, kinds, fame | strong in Europe, Japan, the US; thin in China, India, Southeast Asia, Africa |
| Wikidata P1174 visitors (in use) | real counts for 1,273 places | mostly US, Italy, France |
| Wikipedia page views (in use) | a size for the rest | low where Wikipedia is little read (factors for China and Korea already) |
| OSM `tourism=*`, `historic=*` | named places everywhere, many more in Asia and Latin America | no sizes; mixed quality (small viewpoints, art); a `wikidata=` tag links many to the list |
| Overture Places / Foursquare OS Places | global POIs with categories, permissive licences | no visit counts; overlaps OSM; likely weak in China |
| UN Tourism key statistics | arrivals, domestic trips, nights by country | country totals only; could replace "one domestic trip per resident" ([UN Tourism domestic](https://www.untourism.int/tourism-statistics/tourism-data-domestic-tourism), [OWID](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/trips-by-domestic-tourists-per-person)) |

## 5. Recommendation

Not enough outside Europe, Japan, Korea and the US. Attractions are a small part of demand (1% of
social outings in the world, at most 9% in a country), so the fix is for what a player sees: famous
tourist cities with no marks and nothing in the Attractions filter, and their zones not counting as
touristy on long holidays. Both changes are in `pipeline/attractions_json.py` (and for the reach, the
everyday layer's 600 m); attractions are loaded at runtime, so **no pack rebuild** either way.

- **T-2666 [P2] Attractions: missing kinds of place and a longer reach.** Add waterfall, cave and show
  cave, hot spring, opera house, Ferris wheel and casino (counted even when also a hotel) to the
  classes; let an attraction take the nearest cell within 2 km instead of 600 m (it draws from the
  same pack either way). Done when: Niagara Falls, Iguaçu Falls, Pamukkale, the Sydney Opera House
  and the London Eye are in the file at 0.5M a year or more; the Las Vegas pack has at least 5
  attractions and 3 visits a year per resident; places within 2 km now drawing visitors include Mount
  Tai and Jiuzhaigou; the budget against Wikidata's figures stays at 70% or more within 3x; the file
  grows by under 10%; and nothing a player would see as wrong (a big wheel out-drawing its city's
  museums). Cost: `attractions_json.py --fetch` (QLever queries, sitelinks, the page-view dump read
  as it downloads, about 25 minutes; under an hour in all, one core, network-bound); the reach is a
  one-constant sim change and a WASM build. Adds visits (Las Vegas's resorts are tens of millions a
  year, in one pack), so flag to Anita with the numbers, though her standing word on attraction
  details (2026-11-02, T-1932) covers it.
- **T-2667 [P2] Attractions from OSM where Wikidata has none (Anita decides).** Named OSM
  `tourism=attraction|museum|theme_park|zoo|aquarium` (and `historic=castle|fort|monument|
  archaeological_site`) inside built packs, more than 100 m from a Wikidata row and not linked to one
  by its `wikidata=` tag, join the list sized by the same budget (page views of their linked
  article where they have one, else one edition's 300), kept from 100,000 a year as now. Done when:
  the share of built people in packs with no attraction falls from 52% to under 20%; China, India,
  Brazil, the Philippines, Vietnam and Nigeria each reach at least 1 attraction per million people
  and 0.5 visits a year per resident; Shenzhen, Kunming and Guilin each have 5 or more; Europe's and
  Japan's totals move under 10%; no OSM-only place over 2M a year. Cost: one pass over the built
  world's OSM (the Geofabrik extracts the routes use, or Overpass by region), a few hours on one or two
  cores; no pack rebuild. **Changes demand**: more attraction trips in Asia, Latin America and Africa,
  on top of social outings (in China and India from about 0.2-0.6% of social to perhaps 1-2%), and more
  zones there count as touristy on superholidays.

Not recommended: lowering the 10-sitelink gate (it multiplies Europe's places fourfold and barely
moves China's or India's); one more per-country factor (the lack is missing places, not low sizes).
