## Where the scale jumps come from (New York, `diag.mjs`)

The measure (T-2528): per station, the median of its hops' drawn over real lengths; per hop, the ratio of its two ends'.
Its 90th percentile, stage by stage:

| stage | master | targets from the field | + separation by shares |
|---|---|---|---|
| targets (`P.L`) | 1.48 | 1.33 | 1.33 |
| after B | 1.96 | 1.89 | 1.89 |
| after the snap | | 1.91 | 1.91 |
| after the routing (stations by projection) | | 1.93 | 1.93 |
| after the separation = drawn | 2.58 | 2.69 | 2.41 |

- **The targets were already fairly smooth** (p90 1.48). Smoothing them further barely moves the drawing.
- **B adds some** (to about 1.9): octilinear directions, spacing pushes.
- **The snap and the routing add almost nothing.** Placing stations along a piece by target shares in place of
  projection made no difference (2.57).
- **The separation makes most of it** (1.9 to 2.6). It meets only inequalities (spacing, order, gaps), by projections
  that move the drawing the least. A station's place was a distance from its leg's start, so when the spacing pushed a
  piece's end out, the whole stretch went into the last hop (Franklin Av - Park Pl drawn 58 pitches for a 6-pitch
  target). Placing stations as shares of their leg (parked) spreads that stretch evenly within a piece (2.69 to 2.41).
  What is left is between pieces: two pieces meeting at a key node are stretched by different amounts, and the node
  jumps. Fixing that inside this pipeline needs a soft term (each piece's drawn length per target near its
  neighbours'), so a least-squares stage in the separation.
- Drawn over target per hop runs from 0.50 (10th percentile) to 2.8 (90th) after the separation, against 0.64 to 1.71
  after B.

So a smooth scale has to be kept through the octilinear drawing stages, or come from a warp the drawing follows. Targets
alone do not survive.

## What was built and tried (all parked)

1. **A scale field** (`scaleField`): the targets over real lengths as a field on the ground. Each land hop is sampled
   every 500 m, and the field is a geometric mean weighted by real length and a Gaussian of R 1.5 km (a 750 m grid,
   read bilinearly). A hop's target is its own times the field's integral along it, geometric mean of the two. At the
   field's integral alone, Anita's line 8 graph drew line 8 round the wrong side of Fleetwood (t2160 l8-game).
2. **The sea's scale from the field.** The scale round a station is the field there (was a 10 km sum). A sea pair's
   scale is the geometric mean of its ends' field (was the targets along the way round by track, through Manhattan for
   the Sound).
3. **A far pull on sea pairs.** The pairs were only ever pushed apart, so on their own they cannot narrow a sound. In A,
   a pair drawn past 1.25 times its wanted length is now drawn back to it (weight 1).
4. **The sparse floor** (`liftSparse`) read bilinearly from the four 10 km squares round a hop's middle (was each
   square's own value, a step at every square edge).
5. **The separation by shares** (above). A rebuild keeps plain distances, so an edit moves little. With shares in a
   rebuild too, t2160's Tokyo one-added-station move went 0.054 to 0.117 hops.

## For the warp design

- A drawing that follows a warp keeps its scale only if the octilinear stages are tied to it. Here the separation alone
  turns a scale p90 of 1.9 into 2.6. Whatever the warp is, the stages after it (snapping, separation) need a term that
  holds pieces at the warp's local scale, or must be limited in how far they may stretch a piece.
- The Sound's width is set by the global geometry, not by local terms. Lines radiating from Manhattan at octilinear
  angles fan out, so a one-sided "at least" term cannot narrow it. A displacement cap or a two-sided term can.
- A field of R 1.5 km in metres is about right for New York's core. R 3 km smoothed no better and cut Yonkers' Hudson.
- The core rivers' room depends on where the drawing puts the river's crossings. That varies a lot between variants
  (9% to 38% thin), which suggests the water should come from the same warp as the stations rather than be fitted
  into what is left.

## Looked outward

- Wang and Chi, "Focus+context metro maps" (IEEE TVCG 17(12), 2011, doi 10.1109/TVCG.2011.205;
  https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/handle/11536/14562). The magnification is a smooth focus field. The layout solves
  octilinearity and regular station spacing together as least-squares energies, with edge lengths following the
  field. Their point that fisheye-style warps break octilinearity is why a warp needs the snapping afterwards, as
  Anita proposes.
- Nöllenburg, "A survey on automated metro map layout methods" (Schematic Mapping Workshop 2014), and Wu et al.'s
  survey (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7539984/). The methods that even out edge lengths do it per edge;
  I found none that takes the scale as a field across the plane.
