# T-142: non-work demand beyond New York

2026-10-12. First slice: every registered city now has New York's four non-work purposes
(shopping and errands, social and leisure, school, university) from national sources, under
Anita's "no city-specific data" rule. Second slice (same day, "T-142b" in file names): airport
ground trips and medical visits in all nine cities, below under "Second slice". What remains:
private schools, and other countries' adapters.

## What was true before

Only New York's pack carried the non-work arrays (`errand_jobs`, `leisure_jobs`,
`school_children`, `school_enrollment`, `university_adults`, `university_enrollment`), and
`demand.worker.ts` opened the everyday layer only for `pack.id === "nyc"`. The other eight cities
were commute-only: their bubbles, purpose pies and line purpose splits had Work alone.

## What changed

- **Pipeline.** `enrich_everyday.py <city>` (LODES CNS07+CNS19 and CNS17+CNS18 from the cached
  block CSV, the same land-area spread; now compacts the pack instead of appending),
  `enrich_schools.py <city>` (new `national(city)` path: NCES EDGE/CCD 2023-24 public schools in
  the pack's boundary, child homes from ACS B01001 as in New York; New York's DOE path unchanged),
  `enrich_universities.py <city>` (IPEDS 2023; plus the merger split below). `enrich_all.py
  [cities]` runs all three on finished packs (default: every registered city but New York);
  `build_city.py` now runs schools and universities for every city it builds. Cached inputs except
  the NCES queries (one bbox query per city, 1-3 pages) and IPEDS `ef2022a_dist.zip`.
- **Merged colleges** (`merged_sites`): an institution that absorbed others in the data year
  (`ACT` "C", `NEWID` pointing at it) has its total split over its own and the absorbed campuses'
  points by each one's 2022 eligible enrollment. In this region that is only Connecticut State
  Community College (2023: twelve colleges, 25.6k eligible students, all reported at Hartford).
  Hartford's pack keeps 5 campuses (58,234 students instead of 74,730), New Haven gains Gateway
  and Naugatuck Valley (48,425 instead of 40,954), New York gains Housatonic and Norwalk in
  Fairfield County (836,714 instead of 831,384; its other arrays bit-identical). Test:
  `test_university_sources.py`.
- **Runtime.** `demand.worker.ts` opens `EverydayApi` for any pack that has `errand_jobs`; a pack
  without the arrays (an old one) stays commute-only. The purpose filter (T-158), pie (T-143) and
  inspector needed no change; the inspector's scope notes say "across the metro area" instead of
  "across the New York metro".
- **Kernel** (`everyday.rs`, one small edit): the everyday pass searches strategies only towards
  stations in the access lists of its pairs' subzones, not every station, and returns zero loads
  without searching when it has none. Each destination's search is independent, so results are
  unchanged; a city opened beside the one being built in no longer costs a full search per period
  and round. New ignored test `every_pack_everyday` opens every shipped pack and prints the table
  below.
- Packs before this task: `data/work/pre-T142/` (all nine .json/.bin, 54 MB; delete when no longer
  needed). Old saves are unaffected (no save format change).

## Second slice: airports and medical visits (2026-10-12)

Two more everyday purposes, kinds 4 and 5 in `everyday.rs` (keys `airport`, `medical`; names
Airport and Medical; colours olive `#a8a032` and pink `#e98abd` in `purposes.ts`). Spec:
docs/demand.md 4.1 and 4.2; DECISIONS 2026-10-12.

### Sources

All downloads in `data/raw/t142b/`.

- **OurAirports** `airports.csv` (public domain, global): airports with `scheduled_service` yes
  and type large/medium/small inside the pack's counties.
- **FAA CY2024 commercial service enplanements** (`arp-cy2024-commercial-service-enplanements.xlsx`,
  national), joined on the FAA location id. Where the FAA has no row (Teterboro, Montauk,
  Hanscom), **Wikidata P3872** patronage / 2 (CC0, global fallback; all three under 30 outings).
- **BTS DB1B Market 2024 Q2** (national, free; 110 MB zip, 2.2 GB CSV): passengers starting
  their air journey at an airport against passengers connecting there (an airport in the
  middle of a market's `AirportGroup`). Domestic itineraries only; the share is applied to all
  boardings. Cached as `data/work/db1b_2024q2_od.json` (447 airports) so the zip can go.
  O&D share of boardings: BWI 0.66, IAD 0.74, PHL 0.82, DCA 0.83, JFK 0.90, EWR 0.90, LGA 0.90,
  BOS 0.98, PVD 0.99, BDL 0.99, the small ones 1.00. US as a whole 0.79.
- **Census 2020 county centres of population** (`CenPop2020_Mean_CO.txt`, national): the pack
  share, each US county within 300 km by population x exp(-d / 20 km).
- **OSM** via Overpass (`pipeline/overpass.py`, cached JSON per city): `aeroway=terminal`
  footprints (cargo, FBO, general aviation and military terminals left out by name; under
  1,500 m² left out); `amenity=hospital|clinic|doctors|dentist` and `healthcare=` the same;
  buildings inside hospital sites for floor area (`building:levels`, else `height` / 3.5 m, else
  3 levels). The public Overpass servers were overloaded all session (many 504s); a
  `map_to_area` building query over a whole pack never answered, so the buildings come from each
  hospital site's bounding box.

### Kernel

- `RATE`, `DECAY`, `OUTWARD`, `RETURN` have six rows. Airport: rate 1 (the input is already
  outings), 20 km decay, to the airport 30/35/22/6/7%, from it 12/33/28/24/3%. Medical: 0.04 per
  resident, 5 km, 30/55/12/2/1% and 8/60/25/5/2%. Judgement.
- Kinds 2 and 3 draw homes from the site (`site_anchored`); every other kind from the home end.
  The airport kind is sampled from every origin cell over the airports by a mass that is each
  terminal cell's trips divided by its draw (`airport_draw`: the decay-weighted origin weight
  around it), and an origin cell sends its weight times the sum over airports of mass x decay
  (`airport_scale` per zone). So every airport receives its own total and the split between
  airports follows distance. Origin weight: half residents, a quarter jobs, a quarter leisure
  jobs, each normalised; zero within 1.5 km of a terminal (JFK's block holds tens of thousands
  of airport jobs, who are not its passengers' ends).
- Purposes keep stable indices: a missing purpose before a present one is an empty slot (a pack
  with medical but no school still has six). A pack whose `airport_trips` are all zero
  (Springfield) has an empty airport purpose. A pack without the two arrays opens as before.
- **Samples per cell by purpose** (`SAMPLES`): 16 for the four older purposes (unchanged, so
  their destinations are bit-identical), 2 for airport and medical. With 16 the opening was
  70-90% slower (phl 365 -> 684 ms, wdc 528 -> 975 ms natively): the cost is per sample, and the
  older purposes' samples are mostly errands and social (school and university sample only from
  school and campus cells). With 4 and 4 it was 20-30% slower, with 2 and 2 about 5-15%. Two
  samples change no aggregate that matters: medical walk share moves at most 0.3 points and mean
  length 0.1 km against 16; each airport over 1,000 outings a day stays within 0.4% of its input
  except Westchester (4% short) and Long Island MacArthur (11% short), whose catchments reach
  past the 80 km cut.
- Tests: `airport_and_medical_purposes_conserve_and_stay_optional` (fixture: the airport keeps
  its trips, medical is the rate, timing, selections and cell sums agree, older purposes'
  destinations identical, empty slots, two airports split one home cell's trips by their totals);
  ignored `airport_medical_before_after` (every pack before against now: the four older
  purposes' destinations and info identical, opening times, per-airport totals);
  `every_pack_everyday` now expects six purposes.

### Limits

- Trips to an airport in another pack are dropped (Springfield to Bradley, Washington to BWI,
  New Haven and Providence to the big hubs), like every everyday purpose's cross-pack trips.
- DB1B covers domestic itineraries only; an international hub's connecting share may differ.
- The visitor end is jobs and leisure jobs, not hotels as such; no meeters and greeters, no
  airport staff (commuters already), no air cargo.
- Medical facility sizes are judgement, and OSM's doctors and dentists are mapped unevenly
  (Providence 43 doctors, New York 1,853); their visits then go to the nearest mapped places.
- Airport timing pairs a departing and an arriving passenger as one outing ("Return home" in
  the inspector is the trip from the airport); no weekly or seasonal pattern.

## Research from the first slice: sources that would serve every US city

Written before the second slice. Private schools are still only this; airports and medical
visits were built from it (FAA, DB1B, OurAirports, OSM; not CMS, whose hospital files are
US-only and whose visit volumes need cost reports), see "Second slice" above.

**Private schools.** NCES Private School Universe Survey (PSS), with EDGE geocodes for each school
and enrollment; national, every two years (https://nces.ed.gov/surveys/pss/,
https://nces.ed.gov/programs/edge/Geographic/SchoolLocations). Would slot into
`school_enrollment` beside the public schools, for New York too (about 10% of US K-12 pupils).

**Airports** (a new purpose: passengers to and from the terminal, local residents at one end,
visitors' hotels or homes at the other, broad timing):
- US: FAA CY passenger boardings (enplanements) per commercial airport, annual
  (https://www.faa.gov/airports/planning_capacity/passenger_allcargo_stats/passenger). Includes
  connecting passengers, so a hub's figure overstates its ground trips.
- US, the connecting share: BTS DB1B (10% ticket sample) gives each airport's origin-and-
  destination passengers against connections; BTS T-100 segment data gives monthly enplanements
  (https://www.transtats.bts.gov/). Both national and free.
- Global locations: OurAirports (public domain, every airport with type and IATA code) or OSM
  `aeroway=aerodrome` / `aeroway=terminal` for the terminal point.
- Global passenger counts: no free global register. Eurostat `avia_paoa` covers EU airports;
  national statistics elsewhere (Japan's MLIT airport statistics); Wikidata's patronage property
  (P3872) is a CC0 fallback with uneven dates.
- What a slice would need: a fifth purpose in `everyday.rs` (the purposes are fixed at four,
  `RATE`/`OUTWARD`/`RETURN`), its key and colour in `purposes.ts`, a terminal point per airport,
  daily ground trips = enplanements x 2 / 365 x the O&D share, and a split between resident and
  visitor ends. Not trivial, so not done here.

**Medical visits** (patients and visitors; staff are already commuters):
- US facilities with size: CMS Provider of Services file (every Medicare-certified hospital and
  many clinics, with address and bed counts; national, quarterly) and CMS Hospital General
  Information (locations, type). CMS hospital cost reports (HCRIS) carry inpatient days and, for
  many hospitals, outpatient visits: the closest national source of actual visit volumes.
- US budget: national visit rates per person from CDC NCHS's NAMCS (office visits) and NHAMCS
  (emergency and outpatient department visits), and the NHTS share of trips for medical purposes.
- Global facilities: OSM `amenity=hospital`, `amenity=clinic`, `amenity=doctors`, `healthcare=*`
  (and healthsites.io, built from OSM). No volumes; beds are tagged sparsely, so size would have
  to come from building floor area.
- LODES CNS16 (health care and social assistance) is too broad to stand for visit destinations
  (T-138): it includes home care and social services spread over residential areas.
