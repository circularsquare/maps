## For Anita

**What track is today, underneath.** The game only knows two kinds of track piece: single (one track, both ways or
set one way) and double (two tracks, one each way, which side follows keep right or keep left). Inside a double,
each track is already a one-way track, so your advanced idea is already how the game works. Quad is not a thing the
game remembers: the quad icon draws two doubles 8 m apart, and from then on they are just two doubles that happen to
be close. Passing tracks, terminal sidings and the second track drawn at a single-track station are station
upgrades drawn as track, not track.

**Where the words and tools disagree with that**
- Quad has an icon but no existence. Click quad and the panel says "Double track"; select, delete and stations take
  one pair; the Double up button says "this track has a pair beside it already".
- Where quad ends depends on who asks: the drawing lays pairs 8 m apart, track drawn beside goes 12 m off, the map
  draws pieces within 20 m as one band, and the two-pairs line switch counts stations up to 16 m apart.
- The 1 and 2 line icons mean three things. Track bar: how many tracks to draw (with a 4). Select bar: rebuild to
  that many (no 4; going to quad is a separate Double up button). Station bar: platform on one track or both, and
  on quad "both" means both tracks of the pair you clicked, not all four.
- On single track the station's 2 icon gives "both tracks" of a track that has one: it means a passing loop.
- Single track stations are drawn as two tracks by default, so single track looks double at every station.
- High-speed "can't be single track", yet flyover legs on high-speed track are single high-speed track (T-3352 is on
  it).

**Crossovers today (where trains change tracks)**
- *By itself, not drawn*: at every junction (three or more track ends meeting), a train may go from any track to
  any track carrying on beyond it. Where quad meets double, or quad ends on other track, that is a junction too, so
  lines change pairs there. Also where a line turns back partway along or at a bare track end.
- *By itself and drawn* (close in only): where a line ends at a double-track station (a scissors or one diagonal by
  the platform), and where single track that runs both ways joins one track of a double.
- *Never by itself*: along a plain stretch. Trains never swap between the two tracks of a double, or between the two
  pairs of quad, except at a junction. To swap pairs partway, you draw a piece of track from one pair to the other,
  which makes two junctions.
- *By hand*: there is no crossover tool. The game can still make crossovers (old saves keep theirs), but in October
  you decided players draw crossing track instead, so nothing in the game places one.

**Proposed: two levels**

*Simple: everything is double track.* The track bar's icons count tracks: 1 single, 2 double, 4 quad, later 6 and
8. Single is "half a double: trains take turns". Quad is "two double tracks side by side". Quad by direction (AA/BB)
is "one double inside another". Six tracks is three doubles. Quad becomes something the game remembers: it ends
where you ended it, where it meets other track, or where one of its doubles leaves. A click picks all four tracks
and the panel says "Quad track". Trains change tracks by themselves at junctions, at quad ends and where lines turn;
you never place a crossover.

*Advanced: every track has a way.* A second click on the same spot picks one double or one track. The panel shows a
small picture across the tracks, each with its arrow. You can set a single track's way, branch a single off one
track (as now), put a platform on chosen tracks, and place a crossover between two tracks side by side.

*How each place reads*
- Track bar: "Single track", "Double track", "Quad track" (hover: "Two double tracks side by side"); in the
  Advanced row for quad, "Side by side" or "By direction".
- Select bar: the same 1, 2, 4 icons, always meaning "make it this many tracks"; 4 replaces Double up.
- Station bar: 1, 2, 4 = how many tracks get a platform; the pointer picks which. On single track the 2 reads
  "Trains can pass here" and the 1 "Trains can't pass here".
- Track hover: "Quad track", "Double track", "Single track", "Single track, one way".

*Hex and more.* Six or eight tracks are more doubles, side by side or nested, so the simple level never changes.
Each track stays a separate piece underneath, so any count works without a new kind of track.

*The station editor.* A quad you can name gives a station something to stand against: tracks counted across, the
places between and beside them where a platform can go (T-3348's platform places). The editor later swaps those
places for platforms you draw; a platform serves the tracks it touches. Tracks routed into an odd-shaped station are
the advanced level's single tracks.

## Detail for agents

### Facts behind the summary (code as of 2026-11-12)

- `EdgeData.tracks` is 1 or 2 (`sim/src/track/net.rs`); there is no quad flag. Double track's two tracks are lanes
  (T-3137, `lanes.rs`), a path entry `edge << 1 | dir` names the lane, one direction per lane by the running side.
- Quad: the track bar's `Icon.tracks4` sets `quadTrack`, the route op lays a second double at 8 m (`express_verts`,
  T-255); doubling up (T-708) the same. Pairing afterwards is by geometry: `twin_at` (doubling up's `Alongside`,
  station naming, T-1687), `station_beside` 4-16 m plus the complex (T-3333/T-3356), `map/corridors.ts` 20 m ribbons
  (T-1799), drawing beside at 12 m (`BESIDE_SPACING`, T-1895).
- Track inspector title: `trackInspector.name` with kind crossover / hsr / double / single only
  (`TrackInspector.tsx`); quad shows "Double track" with the Double up button (refused, `Alongside`).
- Count icons: `TrackCountButtons` (widgets.tsx) in the track bar with the quad icon as a child, and in the Select
  bar (`Blueprint.tsx`, 1/2 only, T-3321); Double up is a separate `Icon.doubleUp`. Station bar and panel:
  `PlatformTracks` (ToolBar.tsx), "Platform on one track" / "Platforms on both tracks" (T-3332).
- Crossovers: move table per node (track-and-service.md 6.2 "Lanes"): any end on the other side, with lanes for the
  move. A plain two-ended node never changes lanes (no wrong-line running, DECISIONS T-3137 C1). Turning back only at
  a stop or a bare end (T-797). Drawn assumed crossovers: `turnbackStrokes` (map/network.ts, T-658d) at a line's first
  and last stop on double track only; `singleLeadStrokes` (T-3161). Edge crossovers: `TrackApi::add_crossover` /
  `preview_crossover` exist with a worker op (`crossover`), no UI sends it (T-733, T-1688).
- Station-only tracks: passing tracks (T-2233, drawn by `passingTracks`), terminal, and the single-track station
  split (T-3212, drawn by `singleSplitEnds`).

### Proposed storage for "quad as one thing" (item 1)

A bundle id per piece plus its slot across (0..N-1), saved in a trailing block; set by drawing with the 4 icon,
doubling up and AA/BB's preset; cleared on a piece that leaves (a pair branching at a junction ends the bundle
there). Old saves: bundle found once on load by today's `twin_at` rule. Readers that can switch from geometry to
the bundle: doubling up's `Alongside`, `station_beside` (keep the complex rule as fallback), AA/BB's end groups,
the station bar's pointer pick, Select's pick. Keep lanes at two per piece (T-3348 option (b) stays rejected); N
tracks are N/2 doubles or one-way singles in one bundle. Map ribbons (`corridors.ts`) stay geometric for lines.

### Outside (6 searches)

- Subway Builder: single, double and quad are track types with cost multipliers (0.75, 1, 1.5); single is one-way
  only (a spur can't shuttle), quad is by direction (tracks 1-2 one way, 3-4 the other). A reviewer notes the game
  doesn't show a single track's way. wiki.subwaybuilder.com/index.php/Construction; a Lagcom review post.
- NIMBY Rails: every track is its own piece, one way or both; the wiki's track layout guide teaches crossovers and
  path conflicts as an advanced topic. wiki.nimbyrails.com/Track_layout_guide, /How_to_build_track.
- Transport Fever 2: tracks are laid one at a time; parallel snapping guides came in the 2024 autumn update (and
  Transport Fever 3); crossovers are drawn by hand, with players asking for a crossover tool (a mod exists).
  steamcommunity.com app 1066780 discussions; transportfever3.com dev blog 3.
- OpenTTD: every track is one piece; one-way running comes from one-way signals, and passing loops are made of
  one-way tracks. wiki.openttd.org/en/Manual/Signals, /Tutorial/Two Tracks.
- A-Train: nothing found in the searches; not described.

Takeaway: the games either show every track (NIMBY, OpenTTD, TF2: powerful, crossovers are the player's job) or
offer fixed track types (Subway Builder: simple, but its hidden single-track way confuses players). The proposal is
the second on top, the first underneath.
