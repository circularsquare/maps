# T-2604: a quick profit estimate after a schedule change (design)

Agent profitpeek, 2026-11-06. Anita (TODO.md): "would it be possibel to get semi instant predictions of profit
change after a train schedule demand change? fine if not easy". Design only; no game code changed.

## How profit is worked out and shown now

- **Network profit a day** = fares (`incomeByDay`: demand's trips and passenger-km per line and period,
  `game/money.ts`) minus running cost (trains an hour x 2 x line km x ($4 + $2 a car) x economy, per level's
  hours) minus upkeep. A schedule edit leaves the network's upkeep alone; it only moves the lines' shares.
- **Running cost is exact the moment an edit lands** (the clock worker has the new trains an hour). The line
  panel's Profit row and the Lines tab's Money hold it at the service the shown demand was solved with
  (T-1492, `todayService`), so both halves move together when demand lands.
- **Fares wait for demand**: the edited line's figures come with its cluster's local solve (T-1733; about
  25 s on Anita's North America save, T-2532), the network's with the whole solve (95-230 s there). The line
  panel shows the old figures with "loading..." meanwhile.
- **The edit note** (T-2125, `game/editNote.ts`) shows the network's change in train trips and profit a day,
  only once demand has settled after the whole solve.
- **A precedent**: the Money tab's fare estimate (T-1300, `game/fareEstimate.ts`) shows riders and fares while
  a fare edit is unsolved, from `DemandView.fareSense`: the mode choice's own sensitivity, rail trips lost per
  perceived minute added to every rail trip, summed over every pair of the last solve. It came within 0.5-3%
  of the re-solved fares.

## Measured: seven edits on the real New York save

`artifacts/trainworld/t2604/edits.mjs` (headless, own Vite, sandbox, `?lod=fine`, 1 demand worker, the weekday):
each edit made, settled, then put back and settled, so 14 changes; `edits.json` and `edits.log` beside it,
`analyse2.mjs` the estimators. Money is game dollars a day ($M). Every put-back undid its edit exactly.

| edit | train trips | fares | running | profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1, high trains 26 to 39 (1.27M riders) | +18,421 | +4.25 | +9.56 | -5.31 |
| D, high 21 to 14 | -42,010 | -9.72 | -8.12 | -1.60 |
| A, high 15 to 23 | +17,431 | +5.16 | +10.75 | -5.59 |
| Babylon Branch, high 3 to 2 | -8,751 | -5.57 | -3.85 | -1.72 |
| Long Beach Branch, high 1 to 2 | +3,946 | +1.61 | +2.89 | -1.28 |
| 7, 9 cars to 7 | -1,235 | -0.35 | -4.67 | +4.32 |
| F, a middle stop dropped | -6,957 | -1.37 | +0.45 | -1.82 |

On 1 worker the edited line's local result came 75-110 s after the edit and the final 185-270 s. The fare
change is often the same size as the running cost change, so leaving it out is not an estimate: cutting
Babylon's trains saves $3.85M of running and loses $5.57M of fares.

Estimators, each from what the page holds at the moment of the edit (the line's boardings per period, its old
and new trains an hour, `fareSense`), against the re-solve's profit change over the 14:

| estimator | mean error | worst | sign right |
|---|---|---|---|
| running cost alone (fares unchanged) | $4.00M | $9.72M | 10/14 |
| **+ trips from the wait change, network fare per trip** | **$1.35M** | **$3.31M** | **11/14** |
| + the same with a fare per trip from the line's own fares | $1.81M | $4.59M | 10/14 |
| + a crowding term (loads held, crowd factors redone) | $4.29M | $13.9M | 11/14 |

The wait term: each boarding's wait is half the headway, so riders a period change by
`boardings x (exp(e x dWait) - 1)`, e being `fareSense.local` per perceived minute over rail trips
(-0.021 a minute here), and each trip gained or lost pays the network's mean fare a trip. Trips came out
within 15% for the 1 and the A (frequent subway), half of the D's (crowding: riders the cut turns away and
the crowding on a line the D shares, which the term leaves out), and 1.3-4x too many on the two Long Island
branches (long trips, hourly trains: their riders weigh a minute less than the network's mean rider). The
crowding term made it worse in every form tried: with loads held, a cut piles riders onto fewer trains who in
the solve move to a parallel line. Cars and stop edits move the wait term by nothing, so it gives running cost
alone for them (right for cars, where fares barely move; the stop drop's -$1.37M is missed).

The three wrong signs are all edits near break-even (true profit change $1.3-1.7M against $3-8M of running).
That is where a player most wants the answer, and where a first-order estimate is least sure.

## Options

**A. Page estimate from the last solve's sensitivity (recommended first).** Running cost exact; fares from the
wait term above. Pure TypeScript beside `fareEstimate.ts`, no worker or Rust change: under a millisecond.
Accuracy as measured: about $1.4M mean error on changes of $1-10M a day, right in size for frequent lines,
too large for sparse long lines, sign unsure near break-even. If wrong, the player sees the figure move when
demand settles (an arrow can flip on a near-even edit).

**B. A per-station wait sensitivity from the kernel (the second stage).** In each mode choice the kernel
already works out each pair's share again a perceived minute dearer (T-1300, about 1% of a solve's CPU); also
adding it per first boarding station of the pair's station-pair terms (one more add per term, beside the
station-pair trips it already adds) gives the page each station's riders' own sensitivity. A line's term is
then its stops' sensitivities by its share of each stop's boardings, transfers at the network mean. This fixes
the sparse and long lines (their pairs' own shares and costs), not crowding. Rust, worker and protocol: its
own tree. Expected: the branches' 1.3-4x down to about the frequent lines' 15%; not measurable before it is
built (re-run `edits.mjs` then).

**C. A quick partial solve.** One pass of one period at the last crowding for the touched populations: on New
York about 7 s a period-pass on 1 worker (local solve 75-110 s for 3 periods of about 4 rounds), Tokyo's
15-20 s (T-1733). Not within a second or two without a new kernel path that searches only the pairs that rode
the line; without its crowding rounds it shares A's crowding gap. Large Rust work for a figure the local solve
gives anyway in 25-110 s. Not recommended.

**D. Solve the next step ahead.** When a line panel opens, spare workers local-solve its +1 and -1 train at the
level in force, so those two clicks are answered exactly at once. Two local solves (50-220 s of workers) per
line opened, in the way of the whole solves that must run to their end (T-1734), and nothing for any other
edit. Not recommended.

**E. Running cost alone.** Free, but off by the whole fare change (mean $4.0M, worst $9.7M): every train added
looks like a pure loss. Not worth showing.

## Recommendation

A first (one stage, about one Sonnet agent: `game/profitEstimate.ts`, the edit note's estimating state, a check
built on `edits.mjs`), then B if the near-even cases matter in play (one Opus stage in its own tree: kernel,
worker, `demandSums`, the estimator switched to it, the 14 edits re-measured).

**Shown in the edit note, nowhere new.** The note today appears only once demand settles. With the estimate it
appears at once on a schedule edit (trains, headway, cars; stop edits and new lines keep today's wait) with the
profit row only, muted (no green or red, the arrow grey) and a "~" before the figure; it stays while demand
settles, then eases from the estimate to the solved figure, takes its colour and the trips row, and goes 8 s
later as now. Several edits in a row add their estimates up, as the note adds the edits. The line panel and the
Lines tab stay as now ("loading..." on the old figures): the estimate is the network's, and a line's own profit
moves mostly by riders taken from parallel lines (doubling 大江戸線 put +123,516 riders on it and took 76,679
off 54 others, T-2180), which this cannot know.

  about +$4.3M per weekday"
