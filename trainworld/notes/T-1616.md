# T-1616: why every city loses money on the all-Japan save (analysis, 2026-10-28)

Anita: "noticed that in the all japan save, every city loses money. is that like... whats that a
symptom of? what are the main causes of the discrepancy with real life, if there is a discrepancy?
flat crossings? bends in track causing slow trains? unrealistic track maintenance costs for long
track? seems like its mostly from the few very long lines."

## Summary

- **Mostly the save, not the game's rules.** The all-Japan save was built as a stress test: each
  MLIT line name became one end-to-end service, run at the frequency and train length of that
  line's busy median section. So the Tohoku Line runs 15-car trains every 6 minutes all day from
  Omiya to Morioka (539 km, 128 stops), carrying 3 riders a car. That one line loses US$615M a game
  day, a third of the whole network's loss. The worst 26 lines make half of all losses.
- **The save's fares are an old curve, half today's on long trips.** It charges $0.053 a km in
  Japan past 20 km, where a new game charges $0.131 and JR charges about $0.11 (ordinary) to $0.16
  (Shinkansen). With today's default curve and riders held fixed, the network would go from
  -US$1.92B to about +US$0.13B a game day.
- **Running trains is 80% of the cost; upkeep is 20%.** With no upkeep at all the network still
  loses US$0.59B a day. Upkeep per km is below real Japanese figures, not above them.
- **Flat crossings and curves are not it.** The save has no curve slower than the 160 km/h cap and
  no crossover limits; capacity delay matters only on a dozen short city lines. Long lines are slow
  (about 70 km/h end to end) because they stop every 4-5 km, and the Shinkansen run as ordinary
  160 km/h trains, not the high-speed kind.
- **Real rural JR lines lose money too, at about the game's rate.** JR West's San'in Line from
  Izumoshi to Masuda spends 446 yen for every 100 it earns; the game's San'in Line spends 455.
  What is off is the busy side: the Tokaido Shinkansen earns about a third of its real fares per
  route-km, so the profitable lines cannot carry the rest as JR's do. On a steady weekday Tokyo,
  Osaka, Sapporo and Okinawa make money and the other 41 zones lose it.

## How it was measured

`t737-japan-trains.save` (709 lines, 10,320 stations, sandbox) imported into a new game on my own
Vite, one demand worker (the core ledger granted 2), demand final after about 50 minutes. The page
dump (`../artifacts/trainworld/t1616/lines.json`) has each line's stats, demand figures and the
income the game computes (`incomeByDay`, weekday). Scripts here, all `node trainworld/notes/<x>`:

- `t1616_money.mjs` per-line money a steady weekday (writes `t1616/summary.json`): fares as the game
  pays them, running cost as `runningToday` works it out (trains an hour each way x 2 x km x
  (US$4 + US$2 a car) x 100, high level 14 h, medium 4, low 6), upkeep as the line's share
  (`LineStats.upkeep`).
- `t1616_tables.mjs` groups by length, worst and best lines; `t1616_speed.mjs` speed limits and
  delay; `t1616_whatif.mjs` static what-ifs; `t1616_cities.mjs` per zone.
- The clock's region ledger after the run (`t1616/regions.json`) covered only about three game hours
  of its first day, so the per-city table splits the lines' steady figures by route km and
  boardings instead (close to `regionMoney.ts`, not identical).

All money below is the game's, US$ a game day (100 real days), unless it says "real".

## Against real figures

- **Riders on the Tokaido Shinkansen are right** (299,000 past a point against 274,000; 61 riders a
  car-km against about 59 real [EST: 55.2 billion pkm over about 130,000 trains a year of 16 cars
  and some 450 km each]). Its fares are 30% of real, its costs 45%, so its margin is 20% where
  JR Central's is 43%.
- **Japan as a whole carries about half its real rail volume** in the game: 555M pkm a day against
  about 1,100M (MLIT's monthly rail statistics, 30-35 billion pkm a month in 2023), and 22M rail
  trips a day against about 56M passengers (operators count a change between them twice). The
  save's trains were sized to real passing counts, so they run emptier: 26 riders a car-km overall
  (16% of crush).
- **Fares on long trips** (Japan scale 6.1, real dollars): the save's curve is a straight line,
  $0.79 + $0.053 a km. Tokyo-Shin-Osaka (552 km) costs $30 in it; the default curve a new game gets
  (T-1521) charges $72; JR charges ¥8,910 ($59) for the ordinary ticket and ¥14,720 ($98) by
  Nozomi. At 100 km the save charges $6.1, the default $12.4, JR about ¥1,980 ($13).
- **Upkeep**: the game charges US$300 a route-km a real day (US$110k a year). JR Central spent
  ¥185.4bn on maintenance in FY2024 (track, structures and trains) over about 1,970 route-km, about
  US$630k a km a year; a thin JR West line costs about US$310k a km a year in all, trains included.
  So upkeep for long track is low against real, if anything. Route-km are not doubled much either:
  the save has 27,269 km of double track against Japan's roughly 27,600 km of railway, though
  each line has its own track.

## Anita's suspects, checked

| Suspect | Finding |
|---|---|
| Flat crossings | Capacity delay is over 5% of the round trip on 11 lines, all short city lines (Hanshin Namba, Osaka Loop, JR Tozai, Fukutoshin, 10-17%). No long line has any. Not a cause. |
| Curves slowing trains | No stretch in the save is limited below 160 km/h by curves or crossovers (the save's track is straight pieces between N02 points). Not a cause here, though it would be in a player's network. |
| Slow long trains | Yes, but from stops, not track: lines over 300 km stop every 4.6 km and average 75 km/h. The Shinkansen run as ordinary trains, 136-142 km/h end to end against about 225 km/h for a real Nozomi. Real riders take the Shinkansen for these trips; the game's long lines carry few long riders. |
| Upkeep for long track | 20% of all costs; at zero the network still loses 587M. Below real figures per km. Not a cause. |
| A few very long lines | Yes. Lines over 100 km lose 1,566M of 1,921M; the Tohoku Line alone 615M. |

## Causes, ranked

1. **The save runs commuter service end to end on long lines** (about -1,000M to -1,500M). Each
   MLIT line name is one service at its median section's frequency and length (realnet_n02.py:
   `peak = 0.06 D / seats`, `cars = D / 12000`), so the busy Tokyo end sets the trains for the whole
   line. Static what-if: trains cut to their riders (40 a car-km where they carry fewer) takes
   running from 5,355M to 3,282M and the network to +151M. Real JR splits these lines into commuter
   services and 2-6 car rural trains.
2. **The save's old fare curve** (about -2,000M at fixed riders). Half today's default per km past
   20 km; a third of JR's on the Shinkansen. With the default curve, riders fixed: +134M. Riders
   would fall some (demand's fare sense: 36.5M fewer rail trips a day per curve dollar on every
   fare, weighted to short trips), so the true figure is lower.
3. **The Shinkansen run as ordinary trains** (part of 1 and 2). 160 km/h cap, no high-speed track
   or trains, so long trips are slow and fewer ride them; and the game has no Shinkansen fare
   premium, so even full trains earn $0.048 a pkm against JR's $0.161.
4. **Demand fills about half of Japan's real rail volume** while the save's service is sized to the
   real volume. Partly the save's poor service (all-stops long lines, slow Shinkansen, no shared
   trunks or through running), partly the demand model. Not measurable apart until the save runs
   sensible services.
5. **Peak frequency runs 14 hours a day** (the high level covers 6-20, midday included). The save's
   high, medium and low counts do not follow that: the Chuo Line's are 2.4, 6.9 and 2.7 an hour, the
   Tohoku Line's 10.4, 3.7 and 4.9 (night above evening). A save problem more than a rule one.
6. Upkeep, flat crossings, curves: not causes on this save.

## Proposed fixes

1. **Rebuild the all-Japan save with sensible services** (T-1625; no rule change): split each N02
   line where its passing count changes a lot, sizing each piece's trains and cars to its own
   section's count; Shinkansen on high-speed track with high-speed trains; counts mapped onto the
   game's three levels; today's default fares. It would make the save a fair test of the game's
   money and of Japan's demand.
2. **Anita can test today without a rebuild**: in the Money tab, drag the fare curve's 100 km
   point up to about twice its height (a new game's curve: in Japan $0.79 at 0 km, $1.84 at 20 km,
   $12.36 at 100 km). Most zones should then lose far less, once demand re-solves.
3. **A fare premium for high-speed trains** (T-1626; **a money rule, Anita decides**): a ride on a
   high-speed train pays the curve times a fixed factor, about 1.6 (JR's Nozomi over its ordinary
   ticket, ¥14,720 / ¥8,910). It would let high-speed lines earn like real ones and give the dearer
   track and trains a return. Demand would need the same factor in route search, so it adds a
   factor demand sees.
4. **No change to upkeep, crossings or curves** on this evidence. If anything upkeep is low.
5. Not proposed: changing demand from this save. Its service is too far from real to judge
   Japan's demand by; T-1625's done-when includes the check.
