# T-2496: the demand worker's WASM heap, structure by structure

Agent wasmmem, 2026-11-16, tree `.tw-trees/wasmmem`. Follows notes/T-2485.md (memhunt), T-3160, T-3472.

## Short version

- **Counted** every structure a solving worker holds (`sim/src/demand/mem.rs`, `mem_report` on `DemandApi`,
  `EverydayApi`, `IntercityApi`, `MarketPacks`) on the real New York network natively
  (`cargo run --release --features demand --bin demand_mem -- --parts`: zones nyc, nhv, hfd, lck fine with their
  everyday layers, the market of nine packs round New York, the real network, five periods and two crowding rounds).
- **The biggest single holder was the market's corridor figures: 94 MB a solving worker** on every save with a
  market open (every pair of the world's places with longer trips, 8.18M corridors, an index u32 and trips f64 each;
  it depends on the world's places alone, not on which zones are open).
- **Cut**: a bit per corridor of the world instead of the index (2.2 MB for 31), trips as f32 (31 MB for 62).
  **-59 MB of heap a solving worker, live and peak; WASM memory -30 MB a worker on the real save, -62 MB a worker at
  a desktop game's first line.** No rail, purpose or line figure moved; the corridor table's longer trips summed
  over its 8.18M rows moved by 2e-10 of the total (262,445,273.70 -> .75).
- Phones gain nothing at the first line: their one solving worker has no market open there (41 + 49 MB WASM, as before).

## The change

`sim/src/demand/intercity.rs` `Corridors`: `trips_at: Vec<u32>` -> `has_trips: Vec<u64>` (a bit per corridor,
`np (np + 1) / 2` bits), `trips: Vec<f64>` -> `Vec<f32>`, read through `Corridors::with_trips` (the corridors with
trips in order and their trips as f64). Every reader walked them in order already (`corridor_trips`,
`corridor_summary`, `table_rows`). `daily` stays the f64 sum. Built in `market.rs` `corridors_of`.
The table key the workers hash (`tableColumns`) changes once, as the trips' bits do; a cached result keyed on the
old key is not shown again, as after any network change.

## What is left, by size (estimates of agent time)

1. **The solve's high water and the allocator's slack** (82 + 64 MB a worker on the real save): what the periods
   allocate at their peak (the strategy tables, two kept; mode choice's outputs) and whether freeing or sizing them
   lowers the peak; a smaller grow step on desktop for the slack. Needs a peak profile first: 2-3 hours, maybe
   30-60 MB a worker.
2. **Per-period copies, about 46 MB a worker on the real save**: the commutes' station OD (`SubOut::od`, dense,
   2.9 MB a period on New York, mostly zeros) as sparse; the everyday mode chances per pair (`probability`, two f32
   a pair, 3.5 MB a period) as u16; the market's station patterns (`Period::st`, 2.8 MB a period). About an hour
   each; u16 chances are an approximation to measure.
3. **Each solving worker's JS copy of the table's row ids** (`corridorTable` keeps `p.corridor_rows()`, 8.18M
   u32 = 33 MB of JS a worker, only for `rowOf`): the WASM could give rows directly (a rank over a row bitmap,
   1 MB). About an hour; JS, not WASM, but per worker.
4. **The corridor trips themselves** (31 MB a worker as f32): only the worker that sends the table needs them all;
   the others read them for selections and the summary. T-2136 territory (the page's table).
5. New York's everyday samples (25 MB), the market's per-site arrays (`cell_zone` as u32 saves nothing in the WASM).
