# T-118: Longer trips visible

(The bubble style below, one amber pale-and-solid disc per place with name tags at a fixed 44 px,
was replaced by T-120: one commuter-style disc per far zone, notes/T-120.md.)

Anita: select a Philadelphia commuter bubble, pan to New York, and the focus followed the nearer
city and cancelled the selection; the only view of the T-117 market was a corridor table in the
City tab. Now a bubble selection and a station each show their longer trips by place, in the
inspector and on the map.

## What was built

Rust (`sim/src/demand/intercity.rs`, `api.rs`):
- `Period` keeps rail trips per directed pair (`pair_rail`, averaged over crowding rounds with
  `msa`) and, per station, rail trips starting and ending there by far market plus those counts
  times the far zone's x and y (`st`, width `ST_W` 18). Both come out of `pass`, the same loop that
  loads the lines, so they equal the line loads' entries and exits. Solve cost on the real-pack
  profile went from 3.7 to 7.9 ms (small network; one 667k float array and six adds per term).
- `IntercityApi::selection_flows(market, xy, share)`: see below. `station_flows(station)` (the
  snapshot's station index, mapped through `station_map`). `solved_mask()`.
- `DemandApi::cell_commuters()`: commuters living then working per cell (the pack's commute
  weights), for the selection's share of a square.
- Tests: `intercity_selection_places_conserve_and_rail_is_within_all` (places sum to the zone's
  departures and arrivals, rail <= all in each place and direction, a half share gives half,
  a point off every site matches nothing, far centroid is where it should be) and
  `intercity_station_places_equal_entries_and_exits` (per station and in total, before and after
  crowding rounds; unknown station answers empty). 84 native tests pass.

Mapping a selection of local cells to the market (the decision to check):
- The market's 95,710 sites are 600 m squares built by `pipeline/intercity.py` from the same
  pack cells (population and jobs of NYC and Philadelphia cells), in the New York frame. So a
  selected cell falls in exactly one site: its square, `floor(x / 600)` of its world position.
- A square only partly selected counts the selected share of its commuters (homes for a homes
  selection, jobs for a jobs one; the pack's commute weights stand in for population). A whole
  bubble is therefore exact, the edge of a box selection is proportional.
- A site's share of its zone's departures is its population over the zone's; of arrivals its
  attraction (`pop + 2 jobs`) over the zone's. The worker computes the squares and shares
  (`demand.worker.ts`, `selectionSquares`), Rust looks the squares up and sums the zone pairs.
- Starts use `by_a` pairs, ends use `by_b` pairs, by far market. Selection totals therefore equal
  the market's own pair totals: nothing is estimated again. `matched` reports the share of selected
  squares found among the sites (1.0 in both real runs).
- Places are the market's three, not zones: the far end of each is shown at the mean position of
  the trips' far zones (`all`-weighted), so "Elsewhere in the New York region" sits where the long
  trips from the selection actually go, not at the region's centre.

App:
- `demandProtocol.ts`/`demand.worker.ts`/`demandClient.ts`: `icSel` and `icStation` queries,
  versioned and round-aware like `flows`/`station`: a worker answers null unless it is at that
  version and round for all its periods, the client adds the workers' answers, needs every period
  (mask) and the same version still shown, and the focus epoch rejects late answers.
- `game/demand.ts`: `IntercitySelection`, `IntercityStation`, `intercitySelection()`,
  `intercityStation()`, place names. `map/intercityView.ts`: asks when the selection, the demand
  result or the focus changes, keeps the last answer meanwhile, draws bubbles and labels.
- Bubbles: a pale disc (all modes) with a solid one inside (by train), area proportional to trips,
  biggest 44 px across the radius at any zoom (`DemandOverlay.interScale`), under the commuter
  discs. The far places are tens of km apart and from the selection, so a geographic size made
  them either specks or screen-filling.
- Inspector: `ui/inspector/LongerTrips.tsx`, in the commuters inspector (after the commute
  places) and the station inspector (after the commuters). Settings: a "Longer trips" check
  (`display.intercity`, default on) hides the bubbles and labels, not the numbers.

## A bug found on the way

`DemandClient` reset its cached cells on an effect on `activeCity`, and effects run in no useful
order. With commuter bubbles already on, clicking Philadelphia ran the bubble view's effect first,
which asked `cityCells()` and got New York's cached cells, so a Philadelphia selection drew at New
York positions and named New York stations. `syncFocus()` now runs in every query and cell/mode
request as well as in the effect. (T-116's own check missed it because it turned the bubbles on
after changing city.)

## Checks

- `cargo test --release --features demand`: 84 passed, 5 ignored.
- `npm run build --prefix app` (type check and production build).
- `node trainworld/notes/t118-browser.mjs` (needs python serve.py on 8800; playwright from the
  npx cache via PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE; isolated browser profile): imports the real NYC network plus a
  Philadelphia line, selects 3 km around Times Square and around City Hall, asserts places sum to
  the totals and rail <= all, selects the station with the most longer-trip rail riders and takes
  screenshots (`../../artifacts/trainworld/T-118-selection-nyc.png`, `../../artifacts/trainworld/T-118-selection-phl.png`,
  `../../artifacts/trainworld/T-118-selection-phl-region.png`, `../../artifacts/trainworld/T-118-station.png`, evidence in `T-118-browser.json`).
  Results: Times Square selection 8.9k longer trips start there (62 by train), 16.4k end there
  (179 by train); City Hall 4.9k and 6.7k, none by train (that run has no line to another city).
  Bridgeport (the busiest station for this) has 61 riders a day starting and 57 ending.
- The rail share of longer trips is tiny on this network because it has no intercity line; that
  is the T-117 market meeting the real New York graph, not a display problem.
