World save, New York in view, Amsterdam selected at 0.5 s: New York's figures 1.8 s; Amsterdam's figures 10.8 s and
graph 11.5 s, with the final at 11.4 s. Selecting a zone after the final (Osaka, coarse): graph in 0.3-0.4 s.

1. **The graph waits for a final for every zone, the zone in view too.** `tripLengths` asks the workers about the
   round shown with no wait; during a solve with the barrier every worker is already past it, so the answer is
   empty and the graph comes only with the zone's cluster's final or the solve's. It never asks the focus pool
   either. Asked of the next round with `wait` (as T-327's view questions are) it is answered at 32.7 s for Seoul
   and, from the focus pool, 18.1 s for Tokyo: 39 s and 54 s sooner on 2 workers.
2. **The selected zone's split itself waits for its cluster's final** (71 s): while the focus pool shows the zone
   in view, the cluster solved ahead (T-402) is put in only at its final, so its other zones show nothing
   from its rounds.
3. **Order** (which cluster goes ahead) matters only when the selected zone is in another cluster than the zone in
   view (the world save: Amsterdam in the second group, 11.5 s). On the Tokyo save the selected zone shares the
   zone in view's cluster, so no cluster order changes anything.

## Proposed build

1. `tripLengths` through `target(zone)` (T-327's next round, with `wait`; the focus pool where it shows the zone).
2. The cluster solved ahead puts its rounds in for the selected zone too, so its split shows from round 0.
3. The worker's cluster order and the open queue take the selected zone after the zone in view (`next`), and the
   cluster solved ahead is the first ranked one, so a selected zone in another cluster goes ahead when the zone in
   view has none.
4. Maybe: the focus pool solves the selected zone after the zone in view; measure against 2 first.

maps-17 approved 1-3; 4 only if the selected zone still lagged its cluster's round 0 by more than ~10 s.

## Built (1-3), and what it needed beyond the plan

- 1 alone did nothing on the Tokyo save. During a load the view shown is the *game before's* final (the new
  game's empty network, v1) with the load's figures put in, so its version and round never change until the load's
  final. Two things followed: `target`'s next-round case is now for any zone with no settled figures that a solve
  with the barrier covers, whatever the view is of; and the graph, which asks once per shown version and round, is
  asked again on `demandAskTurn` (bumped when a barrier solve is sent and at each round of a cluster solved ahead or
  of the focus pool put in without the view's round changing).
- The city view mounts the graph only once the zone has figures, which is round 0's publish, and by then every
  worker is past round 0. So the page asks for the city view's zone itself at each round's start (`lengthsAhead`)
  and hands the graph the round shown's answer, as `viaRound` does for the views.
- Seoul was open bare in the aux worker while the load's solve ran, so its question went to the aux (no answer);
  it now goes to the solve whenever the solve covers the zone.
- A question that waits for a cluster's last round came back empty at the solve's final, and shared a cache key
  with the plain question of that round; the key now tells them apart, and the cluster's final bumps the turn.
- `?perfOff=selected` turns all of it off (the before rows below).

## Left as they are

- On the Tokyo save Seoul is solved coarse in the load's solve (19 of its 20 zones are) and only refined by a
  second whole solve (v4) after the load's final, about 6 s later, though it was selected 8 s before the load's
  solve was sent. It happens without this change too.
- A selected zone in another cluster than the zone in view's waits for the second group during a load (the world
  save above). Solving it ahead too, when the focus pool already has the zone in view, would need the hold to count
  the focus pool's final; not measured.
- A zone selected while a solve runs reorders the opens at once, but the solve only from the next one.
