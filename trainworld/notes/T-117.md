# T-117: Initial intercity demand

First Northeast prototype: local networks feeding New York/Philadelphia intercity lines,
with towns such as Trenton along the corridor. Implement a separate long-distance trip layer
whose attribution and time profile are visible and can be tuned. Existing M2 design in SPEC
sections 3 and 5 and backlog T-011/T-053 remain dependencies; do not silently call commute
gravity between distant cities an implemented long-distance model.

## Implemented demand model (2026-10-10)

`pipeline/intercity.py` builds `data/packs/intercity.json` from actual NYC/Philadelphia
population/jobs cells and Mercer County's 2020 Census block internal points plus LODES jobs.
There are 95,710 weighted 600 m endpoint sites inside 1,211 coarse 6 km zones. All positions
use New York's real-metre origin, including Philadelphia's different longitude scale.
Mercer is a town market with no fabricated local commute pack.

The separate market represents non-commute long-distance trips: visits, personal travel,
shopping and business. Each resident generates 0.015 departures/day, distributed across the
represented destinations by attraction `population + 2 * jobs`, distance power 1.2, and a
180 km exponential tail. A smooth 40–80 km onset also applies within cities, so a city
boundary does not introduce a rail or trip-generation cliff. Opposite zone-pair totals are
averaged to give symmetric daily travel. Undirected averaged pairs below 0.01 trips per
direction are pruned together; retained pairs always have an exactly equal reverse. Row
normalisers keep the generator's temporary storage linear in the number of zones, rather
than retaining a reverse-pair map. These constants are transparent judgement values,
**not fitted corridor OD**.
The resulting represented market is roughly 424,000 daily departures across all corridors,
including long trips within the New York region; it is not a claim about NYC–PHL rail riders.

Population-weighted origin access and population/jobs-weighted destination access are
separate, drawn from the endpoint sites. Each site offers the existing six nearest stations
within the same soft fast-walk catchment as local demand. Zone weights retain the penalty
for neighbourhoods without access; at most eight weighted station options per end remain.
Rail mode choice (beta 0.025/minute) competes with a judgement road alternative: 15 minutes
plus 1.25 times straight-line distance at 85 km/h. The shared strategy graph includes every
local and intercity line, so feeders, transfers, service frequencies, segment fares and
crowding all affect the choice and receive actual segment loads/boardings. No express-only
shortcut or station-city affiliation bypasses local networks. Air, coach, driving access,
induced demand and measured OD calibration remain future work.

Period trip shares are 20/35/25/15/5 percent, giving personal travel a stronger midday role.
Crowding uses the exact existing period busiest-hour factors and the same frozen aggregate
loads as local populations. Corridor attribution includes six market pairs (three cross-place
and three within-place pairs), and total/rail trips are exposed separately from commuter data.

## Primary research and limits

- [BTS long-distance definition](https://www.bts.gov/archive/publications/america_on_the_go/us_business_travel/box_a)
  defines a long-distance tour using a furthest destination 50 miles from home. The prototype's
  40–80 km smooth onset approximates a transition rather than claiming that exact tour definition.
- [BTS summer long-distance travel facts](https://www.bts.gov/statistical-products/surveys/national-household-travel-survey-summer-travel-quick-facts)
  reports personal/leisure travel as the majority of long-distance travel. This motivates a
  distinct non-commute layer; these older national survey figures do not determine our OD rates.
- [NextGen NHTS OD portal](https://nhts.ornl.gov/od/vis/chord) is the intended future total-market
  calibration source. It has not been imported in this prototype.
- [NEC Commission FY24 ridership](https://nec-commission.com/weekend-travel-and-expanded-service-boosts-ridership-on-the-northeast-corridor-in-fy24/)
  reports about 44,000 Amtrak weekday trips across the entire NEC. This is a rail plausibility
  check only, not an NYC–PHL all-mode market or a fitting target for every represented corridor.

## Checks

Five focused native tests check symmetric daily totals/time shares, asymmetric attraction
and low-flow pruning (both directions retained or dropped together), no-network and invalid
inputs, actual feeder boardings plus entry/exit conservation, corridor attribution conservation,
fare/service/slower-time sensitivity, shared external crowding, and different origin-home vs
destination-job station preferences. An ignored release real-pack profile opens the market
in about 223 ms and solves a three-station corridor in about 3.7 ms after the symmetric-pair
fix (424,125 daily departures, 667,706 retained directed pairs). That small-network timing
is not a claim about a full metro graph. App integration/build/browser checks are recorded by root.
