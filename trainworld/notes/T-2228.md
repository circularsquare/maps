## 1. Short answer

Passing tracks become a fourth station upgrade. Each direction gets a second track with its own
platform: trains that stop stand on the outer one, trains that do not stop run through on the inner
one. Where a slower line stops at such a station, the timetable has its train wait there for every
faster train that would otherwise catch it before its next passing station. The express keeps its
speed and the local pays a few minutes per overtake, as a Kodama does for Nozomi. A button on the line
panel adds them at every station where the line's trains get passed, in one edit, the way "Expand all"
lengthens every short platform. Price: the platform plus 400 m of the station's track at its level
(US$22.5M for a 200 m ground station, US$45M for a 400 m high-speed one).

## 2. What the game does today

A faster line on double track it shares with a slower one loses time behind it (T-1924): the average
following loss `D - (H - s)/2` over each shared stretch. When both run the same path end to end (the
Tokaido case, every Nozomi and Kodama running Tokyo to Shin-Osaka) order on the line never changes, so
the express takes the local's whole time at any headway (T-1965, `FOLLOW_LOCKED`). The line panel
splits that out as "Behind slower trains", naming the line ahead; on screen the express is drawn
trailing the local (`trainGuard.ts`). Quad track's pairs are separate edges, so an express on its own
pair shares nothing and keeps its speed: today quad is the only fix.

Measured in a sandbox on the dev server (`T-2228-measure.mjs`), dwell 30 s, turnaround 3 min:

**Shinkansen-style line**: 180 km of high-speed track, 7 stations 30 km apart (A to G), high-speed trains;
local at all, express at A, D and G.

| Headways (local / express) | Local round trip | Express round trip | Express "Behind slower trains" |
|---|---|---|---|
| alone (or the express on its own track, as on quad) | 1h45m (49.3 min one way) | 1h23m (38.5 min one way) | |
| 10 / 10 min | 1h47m | 1h47m (50.2 min one way) | 23m24s |
| 30 / 10 | 1h46m | 1h45m | 22m18s |
| 20 / 5 | 1h51m | 1h50m | 27m24s |
| 10 / 10, express from B (not end to end) | 1h48m | 1h25m against 1h12m free | 13m30s |
| 30 / 10, express from B | 1h46m | 1h16m against 1h12m free | 4m00s |

So on shared double track the Nozomi is a Kodama: 50 minutes for 180 km against 38.5 on its own
track, and it needs 11 trains for a 10 minute service instead of 9. Only an express that starts or
ends somewhere else gets the averaged loss, smaller at sparser locals.

**Commuter-style line**: 24 km of ordinary track, 13 stations 2 km apart, express at every fourth.
Local 25.1 min one way, express 12.7 min alone; together every 10 minutes the express takes 57.0 min
a round trip against 31.4 (25m36s behind the local), the same at 5/10 and 15/15.

## 3. How real lines and other games do it

Full report with links: [research/passing-tracks.md](../research/passing-tracks.md). What matters here:

- **Real lines pass at stations.** On the Tokaido Shinkansen 10 of 17 stations have through tracks
  beside platform loops (Odawara, Mishima, Shin-Fuji, Shizuoka and so on); none between Tokyo and
  Odawara. A Kodama takes 3h54 Tokyo to Shin-Osaka against 3h12 with no waits, about 4-5 minutes a
  wait. Tokyu Toyoko has four overtaking stations, about every third stop, and added one at Yutenji in
  2017 because expresses caught locals on the 7 km without one. Japanese 2-island, 4-track stations
  give a cross-platform change between local and express. Mid-line loops are mostly for single track
  and freight (Swiss freight overtaking tracks, about 750 m).
- **No game offers a station upgrade for it.** NIMBY Rails, OpenTTD and Transport Fever make the
  player build extra tracks and path signals by hand, and who waits falls out of signalling: a train
  goes if its path is free. TF2's own guides say expresses still get stuck and recommend quad track.
  NIMBY's wiki describes a "timed overtake" (the slow train waits on a short loop) and laying out the
  timetable so the fast train leaves first. Railway Empire has line sidings that hold trains but slow
  the route. So a station upgrade with the timetable doing the waiting would be new, and fits this
  game's no-signals rule (SPEC 2.1).

## 4. Options

### A. Passing tracks as a station upgrade (recommended)

- **Builds**: an icon in the station panel's upgrade row, like the terminal; or the line panel's button
  for all the stations at once (section 5). No track drawing.
- **Sees**: zoomed in, the station's two tracks bow out to four round the platforms; a local stands on
  the outer track while the express runs past on the inner one. The express line panel loses most of
  its "Behind slower trains"; the local's gains a "Waiting for faster trains" row naming where.
- **Sim**: no new graph. The station stays one node on the same two edges, its extra tracks implied, as
  the terminal's sidings are; line paths, routing and the track drawing are untouched. The overtake
  plan (section 5) replaces the following loss on any shared stretch with a passing station inside it.
- **Cost rule**: the platform plus 400 m (turnouts and run-out either side) of double track at the
  station's level, times 1.5 on high-speed track and 2 over water: US$22.5M for a 200 m ground
  station, US$45M for a 400 m high-speed ground station, US$120M for a 200 m station at -2. No upkeep,
  as with the other upgrades. Alternative: the terminal's flat 1 km.
- **Interface and save**: one more upgrade bit (8, saved as bit 7 of the node flags, which today's
  reader passes through and masks off, so an older build drops it silently); the `upgradeCost` answer
  gets a fourth price; one new query for the line button's stations (additive). No save format change
  otherwise; old saves have no passing tracks and run exactly as now.

### B. Passing loops between stations

A loop anywhere on plain track, where the slower train stands to be passed. Real for single track and
freight, rare for passenger double track. Needs a stopping place that is no stop (a new node kind or a
station with no platform), a tool to place it, and riders on a local standing outside a station. It
would also give single track its passing points back (T-1967). Not now; a someday task (T-2234).

### C. Building it yourself from quad track (works today, partly)

A player can already draw a short stretch of quad through a station so the express's path runs on the
other pair there. The follow stretch is then cut at each such station (the pairs are separate edges),
so the express loses only the averaged loss between them, and the local loses nothing: overtakes come
free, the problem T-1281 ruled option D out for. It also takes several tools per station and relies on
the route search putting each line on the right pair. Not a fix, but it is what a player can do now.

### D. A block simulation

Every train through track blocks, overtakes falling out of it (T-265, N2 in notes/T-1900.md). The true
answer, weeks of work, and against SPEC 2's performance idea. Not now.

## 5. The recommended design

### The station

Passing tracks give each direction two platform tracks: the outer one for trains that stop, the inner
one for trains that pass and for a second train stopping (an express calling there stands beside the
local, a cross-platform change). Capacity: a stopping train holds one of two platform tracks (dwell +
60 s pooled over two, as the terminal pools its sidings); a train not stopping holds no platform (no
40 s for passing through).

### How the timetable picks where the local waits

Per demand level, for each shared stretch with at least one passing station inside it where the slower
line stops (`track/overtake.rs`, beside `build_follow`):

1. Rank the lines on the stretch by their free time over it, slowest first.
2. Lay out one cycle of departures from each line's phase: the lowest common multiple of their
   headways, at most 2 hours.
3. Walk the stretch's passing points in order (where the slower line stops and the station has
   passing tracks). At each, a slower train waits for every faster train that would otherwise come
   within the spacing `s` of it before its next passing point (`s` the section occupancy at the faster
   train's speed, 218 s at 320 km/h, 144 s at 160), and leaves `PASS_CLEAR_S` (60 s) after the last of
   them passes. A wait longer than its own headway less `s` is not taken (it would block the next
   local); the faster train then follows it to the next passing point.
4. Between passing points every train keeps `s` behind the one ahead, so a faster train that catches
   a slower one there follows it (T-1924's loss, worked out train by train).
5. Average over the cycle: each slower line's wait at each passing station becomes a hold at that stop
   (`stop_holds`, so its trains stand there on screen), and each faster line's remaining loss stays
   its Follow resource (kind 11, "Behind slower trains"). The local's waits are a new resource kind 13
   with no utilisation and no dot, like kind 11.
6. Phases: for a pair with passing stations, the faster line's phase is the one of 24 a headway with
   the least total of waits and loss (T-1925 tries 24 already); the locked placement of T-1965 applies
   only to stretches with no passing station.

When the headways are multiples of each other (10/10, 30/10) every local trip waits at the same
stations, so the averaged holds are exact and the drawn trains match. Otherwise the holds are averages
and a drawn express can still catch a local between passing stations; the display guard keeps it
behind until the next one. Stretches without passing stations are computed as now, to the bit.

### What it gives (`T-2228-overtake.py`, best phase / average over phases)

**Shinkansen line**, one way. Free: local 49.4 min, express 38.6.

| Headways | Passing tracks at | Express | Local | Local's waits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 / 10 | none (today: express trails) | about 50 | 49.4 | |
| 10 / 10 | D only | 40.6 / 43.2 | 54.0 / 54.3 | 1 of 2.8 min |
| 10 / 10 | C and E | 38.6 / 39.8 | 58.6 / 60.0 | 2 of 3.8 min |
| 10 / 10 | B to F | 38.6 / 38.8 | 56.1 / 59.1 | 2 of 2.9 min |
| 30 / 10 | D only | 41.3 / 39.8 | 66.8 / 71.6 | 1 of 15.6 min |
| 30 / 10 | B to F | 38.6 / 38.7 | 56.1 / 60.7 | 2 of 2.9 min |
| 20 / 5 | B to F | 39.5 / 39.4 | 97.3 / 111.5 | 5 of 8.5 min |

**Commuter line**, one way. Free: local 25.2 min, express 12.8.

| Headways | Passing tracks at | Express | Local |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 / 10 | the express's stops (E, I) | 12.8 / 14.5 | 34.5 / 35.4 |
| 10 / 10 | every third stop | 13.0 / 13.8 | 31.7 / 33.3 |
| 10 / 10 | all 11 | 12.8 / 12.9 | 31.8 / 33.5 |
| 5 / 10 | every third stop | 16.1 / 17.8 | 28.4 / 28.1 |
| 5 / 10 | all 11 | 12.8 / 13.1 | 29.4 / 30.5 |

- The express gets back to within a minute of its own track in every case with passing tracks at
  every station, and so of what quad gives.
- The local pays: 7-10 minutes on the 180 km line at 10/10, two waits of about 3 minutes, close to a
  real Kodama's 4-5 minute waits. With an express every 5 minutes it waits a lot (half an hour more):
  the expresses alone fill 73% of the track, so the local has little room. That is the real cost of
  mixing speeds, and the place quad still wins.
- A single passing station in the middle helps the express less and makes the local wait long there
  for several expresses in a row (15.6 minutes at D, 30/10).
- **Every station, or only some?** Every station is never worse. Every second or third station gets
  nearly all of it while locals run no more often than expresses; with locals twice as often (5/10),
  every station still saves the express 3-5 minutes over every third. So every station is a sensible
  default: simple to explain, the best result, and cheap beside quad (the 24 km commuter line: 11
  stations for US$248M against US$900M for quad's second pair at grade; the 180 km Shinkansen: US$225M against
  US$10B for a second high-speed pair). A button that picked only the stations where overtakes happen
  now would change its answer whenever a headway changes, and the player would be back clicking
  stations; the station panel's icon remains for anyone who wants fewer.

### The line button

Mirrors "Expand all" under Train length (ui-inspectors.md, `LongerPlatforms`): on a line panel with a
"Behind slower trains" row, or on the slower line's panel while a faster line is behind it, a button
under the round trip: "Add passing tracks at 5 stations for US$225.0M" (no price where the stations are
still blueprint). Its stations are the slower line's stops strictly inside each shared stretch, less
those that have passing tracks already (`TrackApi::passing_spots(line)`, new and additive). Its hover
names them ("At B, C, D, E and F"). One `stationUpgrade` edit with all of their nodes, as the complex
view already sends; built stations pay at once and clear the undo history, blueprint ones go into the
blueprint. Cash short: the button shakes and knocks as Expand all does. Gone once none is missing.

### What the player sees

Mockup: `artifacts/trainworld/t2228/mockup.png` (station panel icon and hover, the map close in, the
express panel today and after, the local panel after).

- Map, zoom 14 on: the station's box holds four tracks, the outer pair bowing out before the platforms
  and back after, as the terminal draws its sidings. A train that stops is drawn on the outer track, a
  train that passes on the inner one (`laneShiftM`), and the display guard lets a train that does not
  stop there pass one standing at a passing station: the one place order on a track may change.
- Station panel: the fourth upgrade icon, two tracks bowing round a platform.
- Express panel: "Behind slower trains" shrinks or goes, the round trip and train count drop.
- Local panel: a "Waiting for faster trains" row with its places ("At E, for Express trains").
