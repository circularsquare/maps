# T-4041: riders take the train that arrives first (agent firsttrain2, 2026-11-22)

Background: notes/T-4019.md (the rule, the Newark to Trenton and Long Island runs, the worked-out shares).

## 1. The cause in the code (tree tw/firsttrain, same as master here)

**Choice between parallel lines at one station** is the textbook "optimal strategy" for riders who turn up
without the timetable (`sim/src/demand/kernel/search.rs`):

- `join_line` (about line 2125): a line with cost from boarding `d` and `f` trains an hour joins the station's set
  when `(weighted + f d) / (frequency + f) + board_cost(60 / (frequency + f))` comes out below the current cost.
  `board_cost` is half the pooled headway times the station's wait factor, plus the change cost. For two lines
  this reads: the slower one joins when it is slower (minutes plus fare) by less than half the faster one's
  headway. Called from `offer_line` (station not settled yet), the settle step (about line 1872, the set rebuilt
  cheapest first) and the change nodes (about line 2066).
- What is stored per destination is one bit per route node (`DestStrategy::board`, `Skims::board[slot]`), and
  every reader splits riders over the accepted lines **by trains an hour only** (`accepted`, line 549, returns
  `rn_tph`; `assign.rs` `departures`, line 318, used by the loading walk `DestWalk::run`, the everyday
  `LaneWalk`, fare zones, `trip_km`, `trip_ends`, and the trip explorer's reads in search.rs). No reader knows
  the lines' costs, so a faster line in the set gets no more than its share of trains.
- So Newark to Trenton (local 41 min every 23, express 31 min every 36, same fare): 10 min slower is under the
  express's 18 min half headway, both join, the split is 2.6 : 1.7 trains an hour = 61% local.

**Long trips, costed one way, loaded another**: `Search::entry_costs` (about line 1651) works out each station's
cost as an intercity trip's start at the planned first wait (`planned_board_cost`: half the headway, but at most
5 min plus a sixth of it, and at most 30). That cost goes to the mode choice only
(`DestStrategy::entry`); the loading reads the plain strategy's board bits, built at half the headway. With
hourly trains the cost sees the express as worth taking alone once it saves 10 min, but the loading splits the
riders by trains an hour until it saves 30.

**The tram's 2 min boarding cost does not enter the choice between lines at a shared station.**
`Graph::rn_in` / `rn_out` (`train_in_min`, `train_out_min`, 2 + 2 min on trains and trams, none on buses, T-3910)
are added after the search (`trip_ends`): "route choice between lines does not see them". Where a tram and a bus
share a stop they split by trains an hour alone. Where they have their own stops (the usual case: a bus line's
stops are bus-only stations), the choice is made earlier, in the walk to a station: each cell's stations are
weighed by a logit over walk plus the station's cost (`subzones.rs`, about line 499), and there the tram pays its
4 min while the bus does not. The bus also has the walk on its side: a home within 1000 m of a bus route walks to
the route itself plus 100 m (`access.rs` `BUS_REACH_M`, `BUS_WALK_EXTRA_M`; buses stop every 400 m in the model),
while a tram rider walks to the tram's own stop.

(Correction to the line above, after reading `subzones.rs` 470-514: that logsum is only the walk from a subzone's
members to each station. The 4 min enter the station pair's route cost column, `search.rs` about line 1246,
`b + self.ends[src]`, which the trip's choice among its stations and modes reads.)

## 2. The fix, and why it is not built in this session

The tree was clean (`git status` empty, head ed955687) when I started; the earlier agent left no edits.

A threshold change in `join_line` can't do it: the split stays by trains an hour, so Newark to Trenton can only
flip between 39% express (both lines in) and 100% (local out), never about 59%.

Riders who know the timetable ("information at stops"), done consistently, needs three parts:

- **A. Shares stored per destination.** Each line's share at a station = the chance it gets the rider there
  first, with each line's wait uniform over its headway (times the station's wait factor) and the lines
  independent. The board bits can't carry a share, so a short list (station or change node, route node, share)
  goes beside them in `DestStrategy` and `Skims` slots, as `start` does; only stations accepting two or more lines
  towards the destination need an entry, so it stays small.
- **B. Every reader takes the shares.** `departures` and `accepted` (about ten call sites: the loading walk, the
  everyday `LaneWalk`, both fare-zone walks, `trip_km`, `trip_base_mul`, `trip_ends`, the trip explorer's reads
  and its wait minutes) and the change nodes' bits (which use `rn_tph` directly, `assign.rs` line 338).
- **C. The search cost to match.** The station's cost becomes the expected arrival over the lines it takes
  (`E[min(wait_i + cost_i)]`), so `join_line`'s two sums (`frequency`, `weighted`, per station and per change
  node) become a short list of lines, and `entry_costs` the same with the planned first wait. That also closes the
  long-trip mismatch, if the loading reads shares built on the planned wait for a trip's first boarding. It moves
  mode choice, so the ceiling tests (notes/T-636.md) rerun and `docs/demand-worldwide.md` changes.

A and B alone (shares, today's sets and costs) would get Newark to Trenton to about 59% and is about 2-3 h, but
it leaves the costs saying one thing and the loading another, the same kind of mismatch the task is closing.
All three: about 6-9 h with the kernel tests that pin today's shares (`kernel/tests.rs` near lines 558 and 924)
and the ceiling runs, in line with T-4019's 3-4 h prototype plus 4-6 h landing. Over this session's 2 h, so
stopped here as the brief says.

**This rule does not help a tram that is no faster than the bus.** With regular headways and riders taking
whichever comes first, the less frequent line wins less than its share of trains: a tram every 7.5 min beside a
bus every 3.75 min, same ride, gets 25% (today's rule gives it 8 / 24 = 33%). The tram gains only where it is
faster: a tram every 10 min at 10 min ride beside a bus every 5 at 13 min goes from 33% today to about 55%. So
for Anita's tram and bus complaint, the timetable rule helps where trams are faster than buses on the street; the
pull from frequency at equal speed would grow a little.

## 3. The street tram's boarding cost against the bus's frequency (measured, nothing changed)

The cost is 2 min boarding plus 2 min leaving (`train_in_min`, `train_out_min`), so 4 min a trip, at street stops
too since T-3910 (`STAIRS_AT_GRADE`); buses pay none.

Native scratch test (taken out of the tree afterwards): the kernel tests' small city and two metro lines, plus a
tram of 8 stops 1 km apart along y = 1500 m (8 an hour, 90 s a hop) and a bus of 8 stops along y = 1800 m, same
hops; boardings on each, period 0. "Reach" is the bus as the game builds it (bus-only stops, homes within 1000 m
of the route walk to it); "shared" puts the bus on the tram's own stops. The tram is 300 m nearer the centre, so
the absolute shares lean tram; the differences are what count.

| layout | bus an hour | tram share with the 2 + 2 min | without | the cost's part |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| own stops, bus reach | 8 | 51.0% | 71.8% | 21 points |
| own stops, bus reach | 16 | 36.9% | 58.6% | 22 points |
| own stops, no reach | 8 | 27.9% | 51.1% | 23 points |
| own stops, no reach | 16 | 16.9% | 36.1% | 19 points |
| shared stops | 8 | 50.0% | 50.0% | none |
| shared stops | 16 | 33.3% | 33.3% | none |

So where tram and bus have their own stops (the usual build), the 4 min take about 21-22 points of the tram's
share, more than doubling the bus's frequency does (14 points with the cost on, 13 without). Where they share
stops the cost does nothing and the split is trains an hour exactly; that is the case the timetable rule (part 2)
changes, and only where the tram is the faster.
