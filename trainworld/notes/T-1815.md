# T-1815: what blocks the clock worker during a demand solve (2026-10-29, agent t1815)

## How it was measured

A temporary timer module in the clock worker (since removed) timed every message handler and
every timer callback over 8 ms, plus a 5 ms heartbeat that logged gaps over 45 ms with the last
task started; `checks/old/t1810-transfers.mjs`'s rounds drove it (headless, one demand worker, the
ghost moved every frame), with each preview ask's wall time printed beside the worker's log.

## Cause

The panels' account reads. Each time the account changes during a solve (each kind of day's
riders, the transfers, the last boarding step), three reads follow from the main thread:
`today` from `game/today.ts`, `today` again from `TroubleList`, and `linesPeriods` from the Lines
tab. `today` is `TrackApi.past_day` over every line's departures for 24 hours, **35-60 ms** on
shared New York (`settle` and the JS packing are 0-2 ms; it is all Rust); `linesPeriods` is 11-17
ms. They queue together, so a preview asked meanwhile waited for 60-130 ms of them, and a burst
came every few seconds of a solve (3-7 `today` asks in a second). Boarding steps (10-85 ms) and
the transfers (30-60 ms) add to some of the worst waits.

Not this task's: the edit itself (setTrainCount) holds the worker 320-570 ms at the round's start
(first preview after it waits 95-316 ms), and once on Tokyo a preview's own mask install took
119 ms.

## Fix

1. `clockClient.query`: the panel reads (`today`, `linesPeriods`, `linePeriods`, `lineAccount`)
   asked while track previews or station checks were asked in the last 250 ms wait until previews
   stop for 250 ms, the same ones (by JSON) asked once. The panels' counts lag while the ghost is
   moving (DECISIONS).
2. `today()` in the clock worker keeps its last answer, keyed by a counter every non-preview,
   non-query message (before and after), boarding step, `sendTrips` and load moves on, plus the
   time and the part-paid hour; the duplicate asks get a copy. Checked: every hit compared equal to
   a fresh answer bit for bit over two NYC rounds (~25 hits).
3. `Boarding::tally` works out each departure's `cum` once instead of twice (the same calls in the
   same order, so bit-equal); saved only ~5 ms of the ~45.

## Left

- `past_day` itself is ~40 ms and runs on every distinct account change and 4 times a second while
  the clock runs (`today.ts` asks on each clock change), whether or not a preview is near: a
  per-line cache in `Boarding` (redone lines only) would cut it to the changed lines.
- The edit's 320-570 ms hold before its solve.
