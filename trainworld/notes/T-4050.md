## Design (advisor consulted before coding)

The timetable already spaces lines that share track (docs/track-and-service.md "Spaced from other lines",
T-1925: two lines on one track at one headway leave half a headway apart; merges first), so the demand rule
follows it where riders cannot tell the lines apart.

- **Coupled hop** (`build_graph`, `Graph::rn_even`): two route nodes are coupled when both leave the same
  platform (same station, same `rn_dx`/`rn_dy`) and reach the same platform at their next stop, their raw
  ride minutes within 0.5 min (`FOLLOW_TIMED_MIN_S`, 30 s, the track side's "one pattern"), both board
  there with trains, neither a bus nor a ferry (they can pass). A skipped stop changes the next stop, so the
  4 and 6 are never coupled; with no stop between, there is nowhere to pass. Per route node its class (the
  least route node of the class), `NONE` for none; empty when nothing couples.
- **Served alike** (`Search::alight`, filled when a route node settles): where a rider boarding there gets
  off, (station, stops ridden). Coupled departures with the same alight are one evenly spaced service:
  in the pool (`Search::pool_lines`, a pre-pass before `pool_cost`) they become one entry, cost from boarding the
  trains-an-hour mean of theirs, trains an hour summed; its share splits by trains an hour. Coupled lines
  whose riders get off at different places (a destination on one branch) stay independent, as now.
  Within one service the lines join as riders take evenly spaced trains (the old common-lines
  rule): from the cheapest, each joins while its cost from boarding is below the service's so far
  (trains-an-hour mean plus half the combined headway); one that does not join gets share 0 (its
  trains come between the service's, so waiting for the cheaper one always pays). The cost is
  continuous (a line at the edge adds nothing) but a share is not: dearer by 3 with the other every
  10 keeps its half, by 6 gets none, the step at half the other's headway, so crowding can move a line
  of a pair in and out between rounds (the pre-T-4041 rule's behaviour; see "drift" below). One
  line's two runs (a balloon down its trunk) are never a class: not spaced.
  Not by equal cost from boarding: each route node has its own crowding factor, so branch trains reach
  the trunk with different costs and a tolerance would flip between crowding rounds.
- `pool_cost` and `pool_two` are unchanged: a network with nothing coupled gives the same numbers.
- The cut (which lines may join) stays per line, so it is looser than the merged pool's: a line past the
  merged entry's latest arrival joins with share 0. Same work as master, never wrong.

  though its train runs just ahead of the one it changes to.

  count as one service when their rides match within 0.5 min. tw/track (stations as anchors, one-track
  stations) should keep giving lines on one track one snapshot station per platform; recheck there.

- In game only the positive case is shown (one line's stops reused by a second); quad twins (two
  stations a pair apart merged into one demand station) are untested there, covered by the kernel
  test's own platforms only.
