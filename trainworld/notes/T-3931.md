## ownwaits: round trips on the trains' own waits (headway lines)

**Rule.** After each retime, per headway line, run and slot: every timed train of the reference weekday is ready
at its run's end (`trip_len_var`), and the departure there that the slot's train takes is found in the other
run's departures. Where a train is ready more than 0.5 s after it (`END_TOL_S`, under the hand-over's 1 s), the
whole run stands `k` headways more at that end, `k` the most any of its trains misses by
(`LineSvc::end_wait[slot][run]`, `timing::own_ends`). Departures whose next is in another stretch of the day (or
another day) are left out: the fleet changes there anyway, and at 10/10 one local at 19:48, the high stretch's
last, would otherwise have cost a train all day.
- `end_wait` is in the round trip (`LineSvc::round_trip_at`, used by `update_scoped` and `probe_trips`) and in
  each trip's length (`trip_len_var`, so `run_deps` ends, `reach`, `profile_meta`'s trip seconds and the era
  trip lengths), never in `trip_len`, `spare` or the offsets: a whole headway at an end moves no departure, so the
  waits it was sized on stand and nothing iterates. `stand_at_end`, the floor and the spacing read none of it.
- `TrackWorld::retime` (world.rs; the only world.rs function touched, plus the constant `OWN_END_ROUNDS`): after
  `timing::retime`, `own_ends`; where a line's `end_wait` moved, `settle_unpaced(false)` (the auto counts buy the
  train from the round trip, as any headway line's), the moved lines noted changed, and retime again; at most 2
  rounds. Not the pacing or the weekends' settle: their rates read no headway line's count while it has the
  trains its headway needs.
- Count lines keep their round trips (a longer one lowers their rate and moves every departure, so the waits
  move and it would have to iterate). Loops and one-way lines too.
- No save change: `end_wait` is derived; a load settles without it, retimes, then settles the train in (the
  test checks a saved and loaded 10/30 game has the same counts, round trip and trips).

## movedhour: a moved hour runs its row's timetable

**Cause.** With the morning from 5, the hour 5-6 is a piece of the night's slot (demand's periods never move) run at
the morning's own rate. Its departures carried the night slot's level (`day_stretches`: `levels[piece.q]`), so they
ran the night slot's timetable (its averaged waits, against the night's 1 an hour), and its return offset, spare
time and phase were the night slot's too (`hours_of`: `off_at(s, ..)`, `phase_in(&p, s, ..)`). Timed train by train
against the morning's expresses, its locals waited 470-525 s past the night timetable's stand at the end, `floor`
put the whole night run back on the night's timetable, and the expresses were drawn through them (6 bad passes
E-F, 5:00-5:22, on headway lines).

**Fix (sim only).** `Hours::row_piece` (service.rs): a piece in a slot its hours share between rows runs the
timetable of the nearest piece of its own row, through the day (the night crosses midnight), that has a slot to
itself; itself where its row has none (the midday 12-14 inside the midday period keeps today's behaviour).
`day_stretches` lays the piece out at that slot's level and `hours_of` works its offset, spare time and phase on
that slot, keeping the piece's own rate (`place_tph`, the settle's). Derived from the pieces, so no save change
(ERA4 stores pieces and `own_on`); the timing and span keys read the new offsets through `Hours::words`.
- The 5-6 piece of a game moved to the morning from 5 now runs exactly as the morning does; where its rate
  equals the morning's to the bit, 5-10 is one stretch.
- Figures: round trips, fleets, rates and demand's trains an hour read none of it (they keep the night slot's).
  What moves for a line with moved hours: its departures in the moved hour (offset and phase now the morning's),
  their stop times (the morning's timetable), so demand's span (`span::line_span`, the share of time with a train
  due) and the boarding account follow the trains as they run. Lines at the default hours: nothing.
- Test `a_moved_morning_hour_is_timed_too` on headway lines and on count lines (local 12/4/2 trains, express
  9/9/2), with night trains too: every 5-6 local runs the morning's slot timed train by train, 0 bad passes 1-7
  (58 good on headway lines), and on headway lines no local 1-7 leaves service. On the base it fails (5-6 locals on
  the night slot, floored, 6 bad). `shinkansen_rates` (the fixed-rate fixture kept only for this test) is gone, so
  T-1183 no longer needs it.
- Count lines' locals still leave service there (2:32, 4:53, then about one every 23 min 5:16-6:38), as count
  lines' locals do in the morning itself (T-3929's "lost" column): the round trip sized on averaged waits.

## T-3929 follow-up: what was wrong

The batch check (notes/batch-2026-11-21.md, batch 7) ran t2233-passing-map on count lines. Its count numbers come
back exactly from the game's own network in Rust (the save exported after the build, loaded with
`save::decode`): same trips, same versions, same bad passes. Two causes, both in how a run's many per-train waits
are folded into `MAX_VARS` versions (`timing::cluster`):

1. **Merged versions inflate the waits, and the floor then drops the whole run.** Count lines drift against each
   other, so a run has 50-80 different wait patterns a slot. Merging only neighbours in sort order into 8
   versions added about 50 s a trip. At 12/8 (the game's network) the own waits are 72 s over the averaged ones
   (stand at the far end 105 s with 64 versions), the 8 merged versions 132 s over (stand 48 s, under the 59 s the
   floor asks), so `floor` put local run 0 back on the slot's averaged waits: expresses drawn through locals and
   locals leaving 1 s after them. T-3929's lead-in made this cross the line (expresses held to B make the locals
   wait longer there), which is the 100 s -> 1 s.
2. **A merged version waits longer than a train needs and leaves just in front of the next express.** "Each wait
   no smaller" is not safe: at 12/9 local run 1 (game trip 17) ran a version that waited at E for another train's
   express and pulled out 1 s after the next one passed. This one was there before T-3929 too (base 1 s).

## What changed

- `timing::cluster`: merges the cheapest pair of any two versions, not only sort-order neighbours (each key's
  cheapest partner cached, so a merge costs one pass over the keys). A merge takes an `ok(key, train)` test:
  merges every train of may run are taken first, others only when none is left. Each train then runs the
  cheapest version that fits over its own waits and that it may run.
- `overtake::conflicts` (new): the stations where a local with given waits leaves with an express less than its
  lead behind that catches it before the next passing point, as a bit set. At passing stations a train may run a
  version only where that set is inside its own waits' set (its own has one only where the wait it needs is past
  the T-3238 cap). The following and lead-in versions keep merging freely (larger stand and loss only puts the
  faster train further behind).
- `MAX_VARS` 8 -> 16 (`PROFILE_VARS` 17, app `map/profiles.ts` with it). At 12 the 12/8 run stands 76 s at its
  far end, at 16 86 s, at 24 108 s.
- Cost: the profile table is lines x 2 x 5 slots x 17 entries of 4 floats (the real save, 109 lines: 296 KB from
  157 KB); profiles are built only for versions a run has. The real save has no timed run at all (no passing or
  following pairs), so its retime is unchanged; the 12/8 pair's retime is about 5 ms on the agent profile.
- t2233-passing-map takes `--count` (the two numbers are train counts) and prints which versions each run's trips
  ran (`vars`; `[0]` alone is a floored run). `SAVE_TO=<file>` saves the game as built, for a Rust probe.
- Test: `counted_expresses_never_run_through_locals` now asserts no bad pass at all (the 60 s leave included) at
  headway 10/10 and counts 12/9, 12/7, 13/9, 9/6, 10/12 (all had bad passes at 23c1bf72: 2, 25, 58, 31, 52 in 4 h).

## Left

  and draws expresses through locals. The way out is T-3346's open fix: size the round trip on the trains' own
  waits (figures move). Until then the trade is a whole run drawn badly against some trains leaving service.
  My pick: keep the floor for now and do T-3346's round-trip fix as its own task, since dropping the floor brings
  back 30/10's locals standing a headway at the end, and the fixture cells it covers are rarer counts than the
  ones the game check uses.
- Small losses against the base on the must list: 4/9 timetable soonest 105 -> 93 s and its longest drawn lag
