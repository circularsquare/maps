# T-1858: why the viewed city takes minutes to show demand on the all-Japan save

Anita, 2026-10-30: "I'm back to testing the huge all Japan save. I'm noticing it still takes like
multiple minutes to show demand data for the currently zoomed in city. Why is this? What exactly is
being worked on during this wait?"

Read-only: notes and code read, nothing run (no browser while another agent's world run holds the
cores), nothing changed. Times are from earlier measurements (T-1607, T-1594, T-1533, T-1847,
T-782); none was taken for this note. "Final" means the whole solve's last round.

## 1. What the wait is

All of Japan is one connected cluster: 45 zones, 10,320 stations, high speed joins them. A load
(or any edit to the network) solves that cluster whole, in this order:

1. Network from the clock worker, then zone packs downloaded and opened in every solving worker
   (about 7 s a worker for Japan's 45, T-898). The viewed zone's pack goes first (T-1533).
2. Each worker builds the world's corridor table once (about 3 s, T-1847) and composes the market
   from the market packs.
3. A rough solve of the viewed zone alone, no market, one period solved and the others estimated
   (T-1533, T-1538, T-1543). It runs only while packs are still opening. It is shown as an estimate.
4. The whole cluster, five periods dealt over 2 or 3 workers, four rounds each (free flow, then three
   crowded rounds). Every round re-solves the 88 city populations (30-40 s together) and the market
   (the longer trips and commutes between zones; about 75% of a round, about 8,200 destination
   searches over the whole graph per pass, 105-120 s for a daytime period in the browser). Rounds
   took 250-295 s each on 3 workers (T-1607), so the whole load is about 950-1,300 s (954 s T-1594,
   1,012 s T-1607, 1,311 s on 2 workers T-782).
5. The cluster's final lands, the card and the clock cap lift, and the cache is written.

Tokyo's two populations alone take 50-65 s a period (T-1733), so even the viewed zone's own share
of a round is a minute or more, and the market it crowds on is the rest.

## 2. Why the viewed city cannot be final sooner

- **The zone in view is already first, but first inside one cluster.** The worker puts the viewed
  zone's cluster at the front. For Japan that cluster is the country, so "viewed zone first" saves
  nothing once packs are open. `viewFinal` (game/demand.ts) is true only when the shown result is
  final, or the viewed zone's own cluster has its final, so it waits for round 4 of everything.
  `viewRecomputing` (T-1851) reads the same thing, so the tag "Trips being recomputed" and the
  clock's 4x cap stay up for the same stretch.
- **Tokyo's riders depend on the rest.** Line loads (crowding) are shared across the network, and the
  market (half of Tokyo's loads in T-1733's measure) is solved over the whole graph. The three
  crowding rounds are not settled (Tokyo's loads still move 12-15% in round 3), so no shortcut
  gives bit-exact figures before the last round.
- **A cache exists, but the workers do not use it as a starting point.** Each final result is stored
  in IndexedDB beside the save slot (T-328, T-1539: `workers/demandCache.ts`, key
  `demand:<slot>:<network hash>`). On load, if the key matches exactly, the figures show 3-5 s in
  (T-1533, measured on New York). The key is the network as demand reads it, every pack's hash, the
  app build, the worker count, the perfOff flags and which zones are coarse. Any difference means
  no cache. Then the workers still solve the whole thing from scratch and replace it. The cache holds
  only what the page draws (lines, stations, zone splits, market trips, the viewed zone's bubbles),
  not worker state (the loads each round was priced on), and "workers answer no question about a
  cached view": a click or another purpose's bubbles wait for the fresh final.
- **When the cache misses (I think likely in testing, not verified).** Every dev-server start or
  build changes `BUILD`; any line edited since the last final changes the network hash; a rebuilt
  pack or a different fine/coarse set changes the key. On a miss the viewed city shows only the
  rough estimate, then the whole solve's per-round estimates, then the final. The miss reason is in
  `window.twDemand.client.cacheLog.miss` with `?debug=1` ("build", "none", "zones", "pack <id>",
  "late", ...); that is the first thing to read in her session.

## Recommendation

First read the cache's miss reason in her session (a one-line check, no code). If it is "build" or
a changed network, do (b) for the build case now: small, gives seconds on every reload of an
untouched save, and carries the "Trips being recomputed" label we already have. Do not build (d)
for load: its gain over (a)+(b) is only on saved-then-edited networks and it costs T-1734's size
for 1-2 minutes, not seconds. For edits on Japan, T-1733 already shows the touched zones in 2
minutes. If she wants the viewed city within seconds on a changed network, nothing existing gets
there: it needs a different (coarser, no market) answer, (c), and its figures would be marked
rough for the whole solve.

measures a Japan cache hit; the 3-5 s figure is New York's), and how long the rough solve takes
