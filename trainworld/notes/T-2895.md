## How it works today

- A line is an ordered list of stops plus the exact path between them. Trains run the list forward and
  back (or round, for a loop). The path is found hop by hop, fastest first (`Network::route`).
- The player never sets the order. A map click on a station tries it in every position and keeps the one
  whose whole route costs least: length, plus twice the length (at least 5 km) for each place the train
  turns back (`best_slot`, `order_cost`). The other stops stay in their order.
- After every stop edit of an open line whose route turns back, the game puts all the stops in again one
  at a time and takes that order if it turns back less and costs less (`straighten`). DECISIONS says it
  outright: "A player's own order is not kept against it: no control sets stop order by hand."
- The sim itself takes any order: `set_stops(line, stops)` routes whatever list it is given. So the
  missing piece is a control, and a way to stop `straighten` undoing it.
- The stop list (line panel) shows rows in line order, north or west end on top. While editing stops:
  red crosses, a click on a dot takes a stop out keeping the path, "+ 2 stations passed" rows.
  Nothing reorders. Ferry lines already drag port rows to reorder (`FerryPorts`).
- Buses: the route is the road the player drew, so the order is fixed by the road. Stops place
  themselves, or follow a spacing (T-2885). There is no order to choose.

## What "order" means for a train

Stops in order fix a sequence of hops; the track fixes whether each hop exists. Three things follow.

1. **Where order differs from the map's order.** Only where the track gives a choice: a ring or any
   cycle (which way round), a triangle or bypass (through the terminal or past it), a spur or stem the
   train runs out and back over, a station the route passes twice. On a plain line with no cycles there
   is one sensible order and the game already finds it.
2. **Turning back.** A train may turn back at a stop or at a bare end of track, nowhere else: never at a
   station it passes, never at a junction (T-797). Turning back at a stop is always allowed, so an odd
   order is rarely impossible; it is slow. Each turn stands the line's turnaround, riders through it pay
   that in their ride, and a station side takes 12 reversing trains an hour (30 with a terminal).
   Running twice over the same track is allowed and loads that track twice.
3. **Truly impossible orders** are two kinds: no track joins two stops next to each other in the list at
   all, or the only way from one to the next turns back at a junction. Example: trunk station T, a Y
   junction, branch stations B and C. "T, B, C" is impossible (B to C would turn at the junction);
   "B, T, C" works, turning back at T.

Terminals, passing tracks, through-running: a dead-end station in the middle of the order is a
reversal like any other (pooled terminus capacity, the line's turnaround). Passing-track overtake plans
follow the path and are worked out again after any stop edit, as now. Through-running is just a line
whose order carries on past a station that other lines end at; nothing new. Line groups never read the
order.

## Who needs it: the cases

1. **A terminal on the way, with a bypass.** Frankfurt, Leipzig, Stuttgart, Zurich: the player wants
   X, Terminal, Y with the train reversing in the terminal, and a chord lets trains pass it. X, Y,
   Terminal has no reversal, so the click puts the terminal at the end of the line, and if the player
   could force the middle, `straighten` would move it back.
2. **Which way round a ring.** A C-shaped line on a ring, stops N, E, S, W, meant to run N-E-S-W. If W
   to N is shorter than S to W, the click puts W before N and the line covers the wrong side. Today the
   only fix is to add a stop on the stretch that was meant.
3. **Stopping twice at one station.** Out and back over a stem past a junction station (A, Y, J, Y, B).
   The sim allows it; no click can make it (`best_slot` and `passed_stations` refuse a station that is
   already a stop).
4. **"I clicked them in my order."** A player from Transport Fever, OpenTTD or NIMBY Rails (from memory:
   all three keep the stops as an ordered list the player edits) expects the list to be theirs. Here the
   game sorts silently, and when its guess is not theirs there is no way to say so.

What an order can not do, and a requester may really have meant: choose which of two tracks to take
between the same two stops (that is the path; today, add a stop on the way), a one-way loop (T-798 L3),
different stops in each direction. None of these is in this design.

## The design: drag rows in the stop list; a dragged order is kept

One position: **no mode toggle and no typed numbers. While editing stops, the player drags a stop's row
to a new place in the line panel's stop list. From then on that line keeps its order until the player
presses Sort stops.**

Step by step:

1. Open a line, Edit stops (or New line, Copy line). The pointer over a stop row is a grab hand, as on a
   ferry line's ports. No hover text, no grip icon.
2. Press on a row and drag. A line shows the gap it would drop into (the ferry list's `drop-before` look).
   Escape puts it back.
3. Drop. One edit, one undo. The route is found again for the new order. The map, the stop times and the
   reversal icons (map and list, T-797) follow at once, so a turn the player did not mean shows straight away.
   No confirmation, however odd the order: an odd order is the feature, and undo is one key.
4. The line now has a **hand order**. `straighten` no longer runs on it. A Sort stops button appears in the
   Stops heading while editing; it is the only sign of the state, and it is absent on every other line.
5. Map clicks work as today on a hand-ordered line: the new stop goes in the cheapest place among the
   stops as they stand, the others do not move. If that place is not the one wanted, drag it.
6. Sort stops clears the hand order: the stops (each station once) are put in again one at a time, as
   `straighten` does, and that order is taken. One undo.

Rules at the edges:

- **Loops.** The first stop and the closing stop are one station and do not drag; nothing drops before
  the first or after the closing row. The rows between drag freely (which way round, in what order).
- **End stops** of an open line drag like any other; the line then ends somewhere else.
- **The flipped list** (north on top means the list may be drawn last stop first): the drop index is
  mapped back to line order.
- **Related lines view**: no dragging while it is on in stage 1; the rows there are not a plain list.
- **Touch**: none in stage 1 (the ferry drag skips touch too). Stage 4.
- **The group view** edits (take out keeping the path, end the line here) do not touch the order.

### Refusals

An impossible order is refused on drop: the row goes back, and the message at the map's lower left (where
stop refusals go now) names the hop, which today's "No track joins these stations in this order." does not.

- no track between them: `No track joins {a} and {b}.` and the missing stretch flashes on the map
  (T-1896's gap, between those two stations)
- only by turning at a junction: `Trains can't get from {a} to {b} without turning back at a junction.`

The sim names the first hop that fails (`route` stops at it today and returns nothing; it returns the
index instead) and which kind (`reach_from` says whether the two share any track).

Stage 2 says it before the drop: the gaps a drop would be refused in show no drop line, and a gap that
adds a reversal shows the small reversal icon on the drop line. One query when the drag starts, the row
tried in every gap with the hop memo, the same work as one station of `stop_tiers`.

### Stopping twice (stage 3)

No new gesture. While editing, the "+ N stations passed" row lists stations the path runs through between
two stops. Let it also list a station that is already a stop elsewhere on the line when the path passes it
again; Add makes it a stop there too, path untouched (`stopped_at`). That covers case 3 exactly when a
second stop is physically there to make. A line that should end at a station it stopped at earlier
(a "9" shape that is not a loop) stays out; nobody has asked.

### Existing lines and saves

- A hand order is one bit on the line (`LineData::hand`), off for every existing line, so nothing changes
  until a player drags. It rides in the line's data: undo, redo and Copy line carry it.
- Saves: a trailing block listing the hand-ordered lines (as `LBP1` does for bus spacing). A save without
  it loads every line as now.
- Track edits later: unchanged. A deleted piece reroutes by the fastest path in the same order, or the
  line is broken if that adds a reversal; a removed station leaves the others in order.

### What it costs in the sim and in demand

Nothing new, which is the reason to do it this way. The order only feeds `route`. A worse order is a
longer path, more turnaround stands, so a longer round trip, more trains for the same headway, slower
rides and fewer riders; reversing trains meet the existing limit per station side; track run twice is
loaded twice. Demand already takes the line as ordered stops with a time between each pair, and a
station visited twice as two places to board (T-797). A drag wakes demand like any stop edit. No rule
changes, so demand-worldwide.md needs no entry.

## The bus idea (T-2891)

It does not belong in this task, and should not share the mechanism. A bus's order is its road; what
T-2891 asks is which stations along the road are stops. What the two should share is the shape: no
separate mode switch, one line-level state the player enters by doing the thing and leaves with one
control. For the bus that control already exists: make `By hand` a step of the Stop spacing stepper
(Auto, By hand, 400 m, ...). Choosing it clears the placed stops except the two ends, and clicks on
stations along the route add or remove them, as she described. Left to T-2891.

## Considered and rejected

- **A manual mode toggle where clicks append in click order** (Anita's first idea). It makes the common
  click worse (a station in the middle of a line lands at the end and the line zigzags until dragged),
  gives a click two meanings, and adds a switch that shows nothing most of the time. Dragging gets the
  same result from the list the player is already looking at.
- **Typing a number per stop.** Slower than a drag, every other number has to shift, and it invites
  orders nobody meant.
- **Dragging on the map** (Mini Metro style). The map drag is already the pan and the node drag, and a
  position on a 2-D map does not say where in the order it goes.
- **Up and down buttons on each row.** Two more icons on every row; a fallback for touch at most.
- **A warning or confirmation for an order that reverses.** The icons already show it, and it would
  repeat on every drag.
- **Dropping `straighten` for all lines.** It fixed lines that went back and forth on 17 of 120 click
  orders (T-1899); it stays for lines nobody ordered by hand.
- **Via points** (pass this way without stopping). The real answer to "which track"; a separate task.

## The hardest problem

An order is not a route. The player sets the list; the game still picks the track between each pair,
fastest first, and the side a train arrives on decides whether the next hop turns back. So the same hand
order can give a route the player did not mean, can start reversing after a track edit elsewhere, and the
refusals are about arrival sides and junctions, which the player never sees as such. The design leans on
what already shows this (reversal icons at once, the gap flash, one undo) and on naming the two stations
in a refusal. If playtests show people fighting the route more than the order, the next step is via
points, not more order controls.
