# T-3833 Schematic coastline with no sharp corners (agent coastround, 2026-11-20)

Anita, 11-20: spots along the coastline have sharp corners, "sometimes it looks like where a round water has taken a
bite out of land and left a tapering corner ... it would be cool if we got the entire coastline, the entire surface
between land and water, to not have any perfect corners". Kept: the 0/45/90 look (T-3358 option E).

## Where the corners came from

Census of Hong Kong's fitted sea rings (scratch `census.mjs`, 89 rings, 38,000 pitches, 3,708 runs):

- 1,061 runs under 2 pitches between two turns (486 steps, 575 nibs or notches). `rounded` gives a corner at most 0.49
  of each run, so a one-pitch jog rounds to about half a pitch: at the zoomed-out view (5 px a pitch) a sharp step.
- 135-degree turns (the "tapering corners"): `rounded` draws one quadratic with its control at the corner, whose tip
  radius is only 0.16 of its tangent length, so even an unclamped tip came to a point. 282 tips under 0.6 pitch.
- 52 spikes (a run going out and straight back, zero width).
- Where a band (T-3358) meets the coast or another band: two separately painted shapes, a square corner of land
  between them that no rounding reaches. Also a band's inside at a bend tighter than half its width (Harlem River, 5.1
  pitches wide, bends at 2.5), and the square step where its width changes.

## What is in the game now

- `coastRound.ts` `cleanRing` (on each fitted coast ring, before rounding): a run under 3 pitches is taken out (its
  neighbours run on to meet; at a step the shorter side moves onto the other's line; a nib or notch under 3 deep, or
  under 1.2 wide and 8 deep, is cut off at its base). Each move at most 3 pitches, never sweeping over a station's
  centre or bringing the coast within 1.2 of one (unless it already was). Spikes and straight-on vertices go; a ring
  left under 6 square pitches with no station near goes.
- `borders.ts` `filleted` (the coast and the bands' middles; borders keep `rounded`): every corner a circular arc of up
  to the radius (coast 3 pitches, as before), drawn as quadratics of at most 45 degrees each, so a 135-degree tip is as
  round as a 90-degree corner; a run shared by two corners split by what each asks (equal radii), not 0.49 each; runs
  in line joined first (a straight-on vertex used to cut a run in two and clamp its corners).
- Bands (`riverRoute.ts`): middle corners at max(2.5, half the band + 1) pitches, so the inside edge's radius is at
  least a pitch; a width step's wider run gets a round end.
- `coastRound.ts` `bandFillets`: where a band's side or round end crosses the coast, another band or its own other
  side, the corner of land is filled with water along an arc of 1.5 pitches touching both (less where either side
  runs straight for less, or within 0.75 of a station's centre), a separate patch (`DiagramWater.fillets`, filled
  nonzero) reaching 0.04 into the water either side so antialiasing leaves no seam. Line against line, or line against
  a round end (the arc touches the end's circle).

The radius: the coast's 3 pitches (unchanged), fillets 1.5 (between the band ends' 1.17-6 and the coast's 3), band
insides at least 1. No three-version sheet: the call was "rounded, same look", and the two shapes considered (fixed
radius by morphology, this) differ in cost and in what they delete, not in look.

### Ruled out: one radius for the whole water by raster closing and opening

The general fix (rasterise sea and bands, close then open by a disk, trace) costs about 90 ns a cell for three exact
distance transforms (scratch `bench.mjs`); Hong Kong's coast at an eighth of a pitch is about 9 million cells near the
coast, so 1-3 s. And a radius big enough to read at the zoomed-out view (a pitch or more) deletes the 1.5-pitch thin
straits and piers that `sheetThin.ts` draws on purpose; 0.6 keeps them but reads as sharp at 5 px a pitch. Advisor
pointed the same way (stay on the fitted polylines).

## Left

- A corner between features 2-5 pitches apart still gets only the radius its runs allow (about 1 pitch); at 3 px a
  pitch these read as small corners. More would mean taking out longer runs (more coast moved).
- Fillets where a sea curve (a rounded corner) meets a band are as long as the curve's flattened piece is straight,
  so small; none were seen sharp in the shots.
- Zone borders end where the coast was traced; the coast now moves up to 3 pitches where small features go, so a
  border's end can stop short of or past the coast by that much (faint, not seen in the shots).
