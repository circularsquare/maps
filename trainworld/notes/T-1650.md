# T-1650: cutting a big connected network for demand (design, 2026-10-28)

Design only; nothing in the app or sim changed. Measured natively on the captured networks of the
all-Japan save (`t780jp`: 10,320 stations, 709 lines, the same network as T-1607's browser runs)
and the all-China save (`t1545cn`: 2,363 stations, 123 lines, from `t1156-all-china.save`). Scripts
in `T-1650/` (the dump is a scratch test, `T-1650/t1650.rs`, included into a copy of the crate; not
in `sim/`), outputs and logs in `../artifacts/trainworld/t1650/`. "M" is measured, "I" inferred,
"G" a guess.

## For Anita

**Short answer: geographic cuts are a poor fit; freezing by population is the win.** The thing an
edit pays for on Japan is the regional market (the longer trips and the commutes between zones): it
is about 75-80% of every crowding round (T-782, T-1607), but it carries only about 2.4% of Japan's
boardings (M, free flow, so if anything high). And its riders are long-distance, so Japan has no
weak links: even cut into 3-6 big regions, 26-39% of the market's rail trips and 47-64% of its
rail-km cross a cut (M). A region re-solved on its own catches only 22-61% of what an edit changes
in the market (M), and costs about 15% of a whole market pass (M).

What works instead is T-1609's mechanism used for edits: after an edit, re-solve only the city
populations the edit touches, against everyone else's loads frozen (the market included), show
that, and let the whole solve follow and replace it. On the edits measured, freezing the whole
market moved no line over 10,000 riders a day by more than 0.9% for a train-count edit, and 13 of
358 lines past 2% (worst 10.5%) for a new 675 km express (M, first order). The one visible lie:
**a new intercity line shows its local riders at once and its longer-trip riders only when the
whole solve lands** (the express's market riders, about 90,000 a day at free flow, would show as 0
for that time). An optional stage fixes most of that.

China: the market cuts cleanly (2-4% of its trips cross at regions of 10-20% of the stations), but
it costs 0.6 s a pass natively, so cutting it buys nothing. China's edit time is the cities, and
the population freeze alone gets it.

## 1. Where the cuts would fall

Method: one period (the midday-heavy period 1, 35% of the day) of the market at free flow on the
whole network, every zone pair's rail trips dumped; places (zone packs) merged greedily by how much
of the smaller side's rail crosses to the other, with no part allowed past a cap of the network's
stations (`T-1650/cuts2.py`). Greedy is crude (it put Okhotsk with Kanto, which crosses nothing),
but the totals are what matter. City populations need no cut: each already solves on its own
30 km reach and shares only crowding with the others.

**Japan** (M; market rail 113,665 a period: longer trips 54,206, commutes between zones 59,459):

| largest part | parts | market trips crossing | rail-km crossing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10% of stations | 13 | 66% | 87% |
| 20% | 8 | 51% | 77% |
| 30% | 6 | 39% | 64% |
| 45% | 5 | 26% | 47% |

At 30%: Kanto with Tohoku's south, Niigata and Yamanashi (2,506 stations, half its market rail
crossing); Osaka with Nagoya, Hiroshima, Shiga and Wakayama (2,363, 61% crossing); Kyushu, Shikoku
and the San'in (2,247, 70%); Hokkaido, the north of Tohoku and the Hokuriku (1,515, 41%). Tokyo
alone: 83% of its market rail leaves the zone. No cut is weak: the shinkansen's long trips and the
commutes over zone lines in Kanto and Kansai are what the market is.

**China** (M; market rail 344,947 a period, mostly everyday outings and commutes over zone lines;
longer trips 21,102):

| largest part | parts | market trips crossing | rail-km crossing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5% of stations | 158 | 64% | 70% |
| 10% | 147 | 3.5% | 42% |
| 20% | 140 | 2.2% | 21% |

The natural parts are the metro regions: the Pearl River Delta in two (Guangzhou with Foshan;
Shenzhen with Dongguan and Hong Kong), each crossing 3.5-6% of its trips; Beijing with its
neighbours, the Yangtze delta, and dozens of small groups that cross nothing. The high-speed lines'
long trips are most of the crossing rail-km.

**How much of each line is market** (M, period 1 scaled to a day, against the browser's full
solve on the same network): Japan 2.4% of all boardings; of 358 lines over 10,000 riders a day,
307 are under 5% market and 20 over 10% (the most: 18-32% on the three lines with the most longer
trips). China 2.5%; of 117 big lines, 95 under 5% and 16 over 10%.

## 2. The scheme

**Local solve after an edit** (builds on T-1609, which solves one zone against the cluster's loads
frozen from the last solve and splices it into the shown result):
1. The touched set: every city population whose reach holds a station or line stop the edit added,
   removed or changed (`reachView` already finds these). Usually one to three zones; a high-speed
   train-count edit touches every zone along it.
2. Those populations solve through their crowding rounds against the frozen loads of everything
   else (the other cities and the whole market, from the shown result): T-1609's mechanism with a
   set instead of one zone.
3. Their figures and their lines' new loads go into the shown result; the market's figures stay
   as they were. The constructing label (T-1665) clears for a line when the local solve covering
   its zones lands, so it clears zone by zone, as asked.
4. The whole solve starts as now and replaces everything when it lands. It is the exchange: there
   is no cheaper one, since the crossing riders' pass is a search per station over the whole graph,
   which is the whole market's cost.

**When the frozen boundary is stale.** From the edit until the whole solve lands: the market and
untouched cities do not answer the edit (the market's riders keep their old routes, untouched
cities keep the old crowding on shared lines). Nothing else goes stale.

**Edits while a whole solve runs.** Today a new edit cancels the running solve, so on Japan
(about 15 minutes, below) a player building steadily would never see a whole solve land. Instead
the whole solve runs on to its end on the network it started with, and on landing the zones edited
since are solved locally again on top of it (seconds). So every landing counts, and the figures
are never more than one whole solve behind.

**Steady figures.** The shown figures move twice per edit: the local solve (the edit's own zones,
at once), then the whole solve (everything, by the market's response: under 1% on lines for the
train-count edits measured). Both changes are one step, as a refine is; money and points already
spread a change over the game hours (T-684). The status tag keeps saying the figures are being
worked out until the whole solve lands, as now.

**Optional, for the one visible lie** (stage T-1735): re-solve with the edit also the market pairs
with an end in a zone the edited stops serve, searching only those zones' stations (T-1545's
`pass_searched` for a window of a few zones). A pair that both starts and ends elsewhere stays
frozen. Not measured: the nearest measured stand-in, the market pairs inside Kanto alone, caught
61% of the new express's market change and 59% of its own market riders; pairs with one end in
the edited zones should catch more (G: 80-90%), at a cost of a few percent of a pass (G).

**What is not recommended:** geographic parts with the crossing frozen. They catch 22-61% of the
market's change for 15% of a pass each round, when freezing the market whole already keeps lines
within 1% on ordinary edits.

## 3. Speed and error

**Today, an edit on all of Japan** (I: no edit was timed; from the load's rounds, 250-297 s a
round at 3 workers, T-1607, and the market 4/5 of a period): a new station re-solves all five
periods, about 1,000-1,100 s; a train-count edit at high demand three periods, one a worker, about
500-600 s.

**Native market pass** (M, period 1, free flow, one core): whole network 50-52 s; Kanto's pairs on
Kanto's clipped network (3,327 stations, 248 lines) 8.0 s; Kansai's (3,042, 239) 5.7 s; Tokyo,
Gunma and Yamanashi (2,398, 196) 3.9 s; Kanto's pairs on the whole graph 14-17 s.

**A local solve, market frozen** (G, from the cities' 30-40 s a round-period in the browser, T-782,
and Tokyo about 11-13% of Japan's city packs by size): a Tokyo edit's touched set about 15-30% of
the city work, so 5-12 s a round-period, about 40-100 s on the slowest worker against about
1,000 s: **10-25 times faster**. A small zone: under 20 s (G). China (169 zones, edit 29-31 s
measured before T-1545/T-1594, I: less now): a metro edit touches one zone and its neighbours, a
few seconds (G). The first figures (T-1543's preview) come sooner either way.

**Error against a whole solve** (M, market only, free flow, first order: the market's change on
each line that a frozen market would hide, as a share of the line's riders a day; lines over
10,000):

| edit | market change hidden | lines past 2% | past 10% | worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| a Tokyo line's trains doubled (line 363, 1.44M riders) | 3,300 boardings a day | 0 | 0 | 0.2% |
| the line with the most market riders, trains doubled (468) | 21,500 | 0 | 0 | 0.9% |
| a new 675 km express over line 378, 7 stops, 6 trains an hour | 136,000 | 13 | 1 | 10.5% (a 10,000-rider line) |
| the same, Kanto's market pairs re-solved | 51,600 | 4 | 0 | 9.9% |

Not measured: crowding. The market's own crowding moves its loads a lot (T-782), but here the
frozen market keeps crowding the lines it rode, which is right until it reroutes; the untouched
cities' answer to the touched zone's new crowding is the same lie as T-1609's, which T-1223's whole
re-solves of a refine put under 0.1% of rail.

So the error is well inside the 10-20% Anita accepts on lines, and the zone in view is exact for
its own riders (its city populations are re-solved in full). The new line itself is the
exception: its longer-trip riders are missing until the whole solve.

## 4. What the player could see, stages, questions

**The lie.** After an edit, for the minutes until the whole solve lands (about 15 on all of Japan,
under a minute on China): the edited zones' figures are right, other lines are within about 1%,
and **a new or faster intercity line shows only its local riders**: on the measured express, about
90,000 longer-trip riders a day appear only when the whole solve lands (fewer with T-1735).

**Stages** (ids reserved, not filed):
- **T-1733** Local solve after an edit: the touched set, T-1609's frozen-loads solve over it, the
  result spliced in, the constructing label clearing per zone. Done when: on the all-Japan save a
  new station in Tokyo shows its zone's figures within 2 minutes of the edit, and once the whole
  solve lands no line over 10,000 riders a day moves more than 5% except lines the edit added or
  changed service on; China's metro edit within 10 s. After T-1609.
- **T-1734** The whole solve no longer cancelled by an edit: it runs to its end on its network and
  the zones edited since are solved locally on top. Done when: building one line every minute on
  the all-Japan save, a whole solve lands at least every 20 minutes and its figures equal a fresh
  solve's of the same network to the bit.
- **T-1735** (optional, after Anita has played T-1733) The market pairs with an end in the edited
  zones re-solved with the local solve. Done when: on the measured express a local solve shows at
  least 80% of the market riders the whole solve gives it, for under 20% of a market pass.
- T-1736, T-1737 unused.
