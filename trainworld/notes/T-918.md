# T-918: a softer mode-choice curve with longer tails (measurements, 2026-10-22)

Anita, 2026-10-22: "whats something like an s curve but more spread out? a squared s curve, in a
way? i suppose thats sort of like introducing a built-in liking for either mode. i feel like the
threshold should be softer, with longer tails. this also reflects variation in car ownership, i
guess." Anita picked the ratio curve at power 3 the same day, on the condition that it stays
symmetric about equal costs, with a fade between 3 and 4 times; it is built (see "Build" at the
end). The measurements below are as they were when she decided.

## Reading

- **The ceiling tests leave little room for a longer tail on a curve in minutes.** As T-854
  found, the riders who board a bad train are the same ones who drive past a good one, so every
  curve in minutes that fattens the tail costs test A. The arctan (82.9 / 82.7) and the softer
  logistic 0.07 (81.1 / 80.5) fail A. The mix of two logistics passes only with 10-15% of riders
  in the flat part (A 85.6 / 85.3 at 10%, 85.2 / 85.0 at 15%; 20% is 84.9 / 84.7), and at those
  sizes its tail is about what today's 5% floor already gives (slow line from the suburbs 6.6-7.3
  against today's 6.9).
- **The log-ratio curve is the one that gets longer tails and still passes, with room.** Rail's
  share of rail + car = 1 / (1 + (rail / car)^3). A: 91.8 / 92.1, B: 80.9 / 81.6. A train twice as
  dear as driving keeps 11%, three times 3.6%; the slow suburban line gets 10.2 / 12.9 (today 6.9
  / 10.4); a line 60 minutes worse than a 30 minute drive keeps 3.6% (today 0.25%). It spreads with
  trip length by itself, so it replaces today's damping past 60 car minutes as well as the bounds.
  Real networks gain 3-5% rail, New York's commuter rail 38%.
- **The cost is farming.** With no cut-off, a useless line three times worse than driving takes
  3.6% of the trips it touches and 1.5% at four times (today 0.5% and 0.1%; the bounds give
  nothing past 3x). The arctan is worse still (5.3% and 3.5%).

## Recommendation

**The log-ratio curve at power 3, without the 5% bounds and without the damping in the mode
choice**: rail's share of rail + car = 1 / (1 + (rail / car)^3). It is the only shape measured
that has visibly longer tails (a slow suburban line +3 points, a line an hour worse than a half
hour drive 3.6% instead of 0.25%) and passes both ceilings with room (A 92, B 81). It is also
simpler to say than today's three rules (a scale in minutes, the damping past an hour, the
bounds): "a train twice as dear as driving gets 1 in 9 riders, three times 1 in 28". The bounds
are not needed: the curve gives slow lines more than the floor did, and nobody is held off a train
that is much better.

Its costs: farming (3.6% of the trips a line three times worse than the car touches, 1.5% at four
times, against 0.5% and 0.1% today), and the curve no longer matches today in the middle on short
trips (steeper) or long ones (flatter); real networks gain 3-5%. If farming matters, a fade to
nothing between 3 and 4 times the car's cost would cap it (not measured).

The gentle alternative is the **mix at 15%** (85% of riders at scale 0.115, 15% at a quarter of
it), also without the bounds: closest to Anita's "variation in car ownership" in words, real
networks within 1%, farming 2.0% at 3x. But it passes A by nothing in London (85.0), and its tail
is about what today's floor gives, so it would hardly be seen in play.

Arctan, the softer logistic and the mix at 20% or more fail test A.

Not done: the trip explorer (`trip.rs`) and the regional layer were not changed in the copy;
walking keeps today's logit and damping in every variant.

## Build (2026-10-22)

Anita's choice: the ratio curve at power 3, symmetric about equal costs, the 5% bounds and the
mode choice's damping gone, and a fade to nothing between 3 and 4 times the car's cost and its
mirror for the car.

**Symmetry.** Rail's share is s(q) = 1 / (1 + q^3) for q = rail / car, so s(1 / q) = q^3 / (q^3 +
1) = 1 - s(q): the car's share at q is rail's at 1 / q, and equal costs give exactly half. The fade
keeps it: rail's share is times (4 - q) clamped to 0-1, the car's times (4 - 1 / q), so the two
fades mirror each other too. A test checks the mirror at q from 0.2 to 3.2 and three trip lengths.

**Code.** `Params::ratio_share(rail, car)` (kernel.rs) holds the curve and the fade
(`ratio_fade_lo` 3, `ratio_fade_hi` 4, settable by `--set`); `Params::car_choice` now takes rail's
cost and the free driver's car cost and returns rail's share of all trips: walking's share of
today's three-way logit (damped as before) per driver, and rail of the rest by `ratio_share` on
that driver's own car cost (free, or plus the space's price), mixed by the free share.
`rail_bounds`, `rail_share_parked`, `rail_floor` and `rail_floor_ratio` are gone. The commute
lanes (`mode_choice`) do the same in F8, a cube and two clamps per lane, no exp; the everyday
pass and both trip-explorer functions (`trip::pair_choice`, `everyday_choice`) call `car_choice`.
The regional layer: its commutes between zones use `ratio_share` (they choose as local commutes
do; no walking there), and their unused rail constant `CPair::asc` is gone; the regional market
(rail against a logsum of car and plane between regions) keeps its damped logit, since the curve
is a two-way rule and the plane is a third mode with its own pack figures.
