## What the dot is

The schedule's crowding dot (`CrowdDots`, LineInspector.tsx; `Boarding::crowding` fields 9 and 10)
is the average wait for a train with room **of the riders at platforms where trains come full**
(average wait there at least 60 s), a rider turned away counting 30 minutes, pooled over the
level's periods by those riders. Riders at platforms with room do not count. So the dot measures
how bad it is where it is bad, not how many riders it is bad for.

## Cause

1. **The dot's pooling, mostly.** On both lines every level-wide measure says High is worse:

   | Line | Level | Dot | Avg extra wait, all riders | Share turned away |
   |---|---|---:|---:|---:|
   | NJCL #82 | High | 8.2 | 2.60 min | 8.7% |
   | NJCL #82 | Medium | 15.0 | 1.95 min | 6.5% |
   | BNTNM #94 | High | 9.4 | 4.21 min | 14.0% |
   | BNTNM #94 | Medium | 10.9 | 2.34 min | 7.8% |

   The dot is close to 30 min x (turned away / riders at busy platforms). In the evening peak the
   overload is spread over many platforms (46-62% of riders), in the midday it piles up at a few
   (18-28%), so fewer riders turned away still give a higher dot. The morning peak has no busy
   platforms, so it neither helps nor dilutes High: High's dot is the evening peak's alone.
2. **Midday really is busy on lines with many everyday riders.** Errands (55% out, 49% back),
   social (35%, 25%) and school returns (82%) leave in 10-16. On 41 of 102 lines midday riders per
   hour match or beat the morning peak's. Its flows run both ways and are shorter, so its busiest
   stretch beats the morning peak's on only 4 lines and both peaks' on none. On NJCL and BNTNM the
   midday is overloaded (load/cap 1.05 and 1.15, 4/10 and 10/23 trains full): the trains really
   are short then, only a bit less short than in the evening peak.
3. **Not the busiest-hour rule**: Medium's factors (1.15, 1.3) are lower than the peaks' 1.4, so
   that rule favours Medium. **Not the train dealing**: same trains/h in both, departures by the
   same rule; midday has more trains in total only because it is 6 hours.
4. **The evening 20-24 is never the problem**: no same-train line has a busy platform there.
5. No bug: the pooling is T-1255's design (DECISIONS 2026-10-25) and works as written.

## Options and their effect on these figures

- **A. Shift the boundaries** (say High 6-10 and 15-20). Midday's crowding is at its middle hours
  (the tent peaks at 13:00), not its shoulders, so moving 15-16 into High leaves the midday's
  busiest hour where it is; the dots would barely move (estimated, not run: it needs every share
  table in demand-timing.md re-derived for new hours and a re-solve). A fourth level for midday
  alone would let the player set it apart but changes nothing at the same trains. Not worth it.
- **B. Grade the dot over all the level's riders** (the average extra wait of every rider, or the
  share turned away). NJCL: High 2.60 / Medium 1.95 min (8.7% / 6.5% turned away); BNTNM: 4.21 /
  2.34 min (14.0% / 7.8%); High worse on both, and no change in order on the other 23. But this
  is the line-wide average T-1255 dropped on 2026-10-25 because it diluted a 7 cut to a third to
  5 minutes (DECISIONS), and the share turned away is the "riders against room" figure Anita
  ruled out on 2026-10-24. So B reopens two of her calls, for two lines out of 25.
- **C. Keep the dot, explain it better**: the hover lists each of the level's periods with a
  train-count fact ("10-16: 4 of 10 trains full, 2,479 riders went another way"), so the player
  sees Medium's red is the midday's and that the midday really is short of trains. Figures
  unchanged.

Pick: C. The Medium red on these lines is real (the midday turns away 2,479 and 6,506 riders a
weekday); what misleads is only the comparison with High, whose evening peak is worse but spread
over more platforms. Adding Medium trains is the right move for the player either way. A small UI
task (LineInspector.tsx `CrowdDots` hover), not built here: Anita's go first.
