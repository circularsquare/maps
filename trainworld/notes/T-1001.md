## Defensive fixes built

- `setTrackType` (game/ui.ts) in one `batch`: the tool never previews a mix of old and new type.
- tools.ts `segmentClick` and `takeBackSegment`: `placing` is cleared in a `finally`, so a failed
  edit never leaves later clicks ignored.
- clockClient.ts: a preview callback that throws still sends the preview waiting behind it.
- realRoute.worker.ts: a route search that throws answers "off", so a click waiting on it becomes an
  ordinary segment instead of waiting for ever.

## T-1008: the main thread after a big save loads

Measured with `checks/t1008-load-lag.mjs` (dev server, headless Chrome, 2 demand workers): the save
imported into a new game, long tasks over 50 ms counted from the import (count / longest / total
ms), and each demand-client message's main-thread ms. Outputs: `artifacts/trainworld/t1008/`.

What it was. Every period's result carried the market's corridors in full: 1,149,870 place pairs
(1,516 zones, all of them with longer trips or commutes), 37 MB of corridors plus 18 MB of commute
legs and 9 MB of mode shares a message. The client summed them per kind of day (`corridorSums`,
about 130 ms a day, three days on the first result), copied four whole columns per day on every
later result, and the city bubbles built a Z x Z matrix (55 MB) on each. On top of that, the save's
first world state ran zone lookups that scanned every zone for every station (`cityNear`,
`zonesTouching`, `marketZonesNear`, `zonesNearStations`: realms, holidays, the demand client's
reach), which in Japan (10,171 stations) was a single 3.7-4.5 s task.

What changed.
- The corridors are a table no network or period changes (pairs, longer trips and commute legs a
  day, plane and car shares; `CorridorTable`), made once per market part in the worker from new
  `IntercityApi` functions (`corridor_pairs`, `corridor_trips`, `commute_base`, `corridor_shares`,
  `corridor_rail`) and sent to the main thread once per key. A result carries the key, two period
  factors and the pairs with rail. A day's `Corridors` is the table, two scales and the pairs with
  rail; nothing on the main thread walks every pair per result. This is T-790's fix as well.
- The city bubbles keep per-zone totals from the table (once per table) plus the railed pairs, and
  work out a selected zone's row only when one is selected. points.ts reads the railed pairs only.
- `cityNear`, `zonesTouching`, `marketZonesNear` and `zonesNearStations` look zones up through a
  whole-degree grid of centres and boxes (cities.ts `zonesNearBox`); same answers as the scans at
  23,032 places (random, on and near every zone centre), 50 times quicker near zones.

The figures are unchanged: the NYC save's final on all three kinds of day (rail share 2.624%,
11,654,110 rail trips, 20,395,580 riders, every corridor total) the same to three decimals before
and after. Japan's demand gives no result within two minutes, so its numbers are the load alone.

What is left (not demand's corridors):
- Japan's load: the places of every station and track edge against zone outlines (zones.ts
  `placeAt` / `nearestZone` / cityReach.ts `distanceTo`, about 0.85 s of the longest task) and
  boundary geometry as boundaries arrive. T-1021.
- A track edit in Japan costs one 190-260 ms task, the same before and after: the renderer
  rebuilding the whole network's strokes (`setNetwork`, about 155 ms), demand views (50 ms) and
  money (30 ms) on the clock's state. That is the likeliest "1 fps" in a big game: a freeze per
  click, not a slow frame rate. T-1020.
- NYC's first result still takes one 90-140 ms task (three days' views, the bubbles' table totals
  once, garbage collection).
