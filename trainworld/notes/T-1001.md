## Defensive fixes built

- `setTrackType` (game/ui.ts) in one `batch`: the tool never previews a mix of old and new type.
- tools.ts `segmentClick` and `takeBackSegment`: `placing` is cleared in a `finally`, so a failed
  edit never leaves later clicks ignored.
- clockClient.ts: a preview callback that throws still sends the preview waiting behind it.
- realRoute.worker.ts: a route search that throws answers "off", so a click waiting on it becomes an
  ordinary segment instead of waiting for ever.

## T-1008: the main thread after a big save loads

Measured with `checks/t1008-load-lag.mjs` (dev server, headless Chrome, 2 demand workers): the save
imported into a new game, long tasks over 50 ms counted from the import (count / longest / total
ms), and each demand-client message's main-thread ms. Outputs: `artifacts/trainworld/t1008/`.

What it was. Every period's result carried the market's corridors in full: 1,149,870 place pairs
(1,516 zones, all of them with longer trips or commutes), 37 MB of corridors plus 18 MB of commute
legs and 9 MB of mode shares a message. The client summed them per kind of day (`corridorSums`,
about 130 ms a day, three days on the first result), copied four whole columns per day on every
later result, and the city bubbles built a Z x Z matrix (55 MB) on each. On top of that, the save's
first world state ran zone lookups that scanned every zone for every station (`cityNear`,
`zonesTouching`, `marketZonesNear`, `zonesNearStations`: realms, holidays, the demand client's
reach), which in Japan (10,171 stations) was a single 3.7-4.5 s task.

What changed.
- The corridors are a table no network or period changes (pairs, longer trips and commute legs a
  day, plane and car shares; `CorridorTable`), made once per market part in the worker from new
  `IntercityApi` functions (`corridor_pairs`, `corridor_trips`, `commute_base`, `corridor_shares`,
  `corridor_rail`) and sent to the main thread once per key. A result carries the key, two period
  factors and the pairs with rail. A day's `Corridors` is the table, two scales and the pairs with
  rail; nothing on the main thread walks every pair per result. This is T-790's fix as well.
- The city bubbles keep per-zone totals from the table (once per table) plus the railed pairs, and
  work out a selected zone's row only when one is selected. points.ts reads the railed pairs only.
- `cityNear`, `zonesTouching`, `marketZonesNear` and `zonesNearStations` look zones up through a
  whole-degree grid of centres and boxes (cities.ts `zonesNearBox`); same answers as the scans at
  23,032 places (random, on and near every zone centre), 50 times quicker near zones.

The figures are unchanged: the NYC save's final on all three kinds of day (rail share 2.624%,

What is left (not demand's corridors):
- Japan's load: the places of every station and track edge against zone outlines (zones.ts
  `placeAt` / `nearestZone` / cityReach.ts `distanceTo`, about 0.85 s of the longest task) and
  boundary geometry as boundaries arrive. T-1021.
- A track edit in Japan costs one 190-260 ms task, the same before and after: the renderer
  rebuilding the whole network's strokes (`setNetwork`, about 155 ms), demand views (50 ms) and
  money (30 ms) on the clock's state. That is the likeliest "1 fps" in a big game: a freeze per
  click, not a slow frame rate. T-1020.
- NYC's first result still takes one 90-140 ms task (three days' views, the bubbles' table totals
  once, garbage collection).

## T-1020: track placement unresponsive

Measured with `checks/t1020-click-latency.mjs` (dev server, headless Chrome, 2 demand workers): the
track tool with keep placing on, a start then pieces each reached by 20 pointer moves; per click the
press to the first frame drawn after the world changed, per move the time to the first frame showing
a ghost asked at or after it, frame gaps, long tasks, and where the time went (worker steps, state
arrival). Outputs `artifacts/trainworld/t1020/`.

**Anita's repro (fresh game, London, then Shanghai, then London): the cause.** With keep placing on
the run stays open, so the first click in Shanghai, and every pointer move before it, asked for a
piece of track from London to Shanghai: 10,256 km, 102,559 points, 11.1 s in the clock worker the
first time (water masks along the way) and 2.8 s after. Previews are one in flight, so the worker
spent all its time on these; nothing placed (the edit queued behind them) and the ghost froze. Back in
London the run's start was then in Shanghai, so the same again. The headless check hung for over 8
minutes on it. Not the network's size, demand or the masks: London previews stayed 1-2 ms after the
long one. Programmatic edits in both cities were 1-3 ms in the worker.

Fix: a free-drawn piece reaches 500 km at most (straight line between its ends, `MAX_PIECE_KM` in
tools.ts): past that the tool asks for no ghost, its tag says why, a click is refused at once. Real
routes (snap, T-727) are not capped. Also the run's start is kept in degrees, not local units, so its
marker and the click-on-start test stay right when the draw anchor moves to another city.

| repro (after) | click to drawn p50/max | ghost p50/p90 | far click |
|---|---|---|---|
| London, 4 pieces | 29/33 ms | 26/34 ms | |
| Shanghai, 2 pieces | 31/31 | 27/34 | refused, no edit, no long task |
| London again, 4 pieces | 36/37 | 26/34 | refused, no edit, no long task |

Before, on the same tree: London 36/96 ms, then the check hung in Shanghai.

**Bigger networks (mostly not changed yet, T-1028 to T-1031).** Same check, before:

| setup | click to drawn p50/max | ghost p50/p90/max | frames over 50 ms | long tasks a click |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| new game, 5 lines | 29/37 ms | 28/35/625 | 1 | 0 |
| NYC real save | 120/157 | 29/53/717 | 1 | 0.13, 6 ms |
| Japan real network (860k segments) | 692/850 | 342/642/1853 | 39 | 1.25, 175 ms |

NYC's click: the worker 75-82 ms (apply 4, state 12, buffers 20-24, the boarding hour for two epochs
32-34; the state waits for the first epoch's trips, T-648), the main thread 22-26 ms taking the state,
then a frame. Japan's: worker 389 ms (apply 65, state 108, buffers 158), state arriving 535 ms after
the press, 112 ms of main thread on it (setNetwork 62: strokes 30, textures 22; deserialising the
state 43; effects on the world 33; money 8; toWorld 7), and on each pointer move the pick and snap grids
rebuilt for every new network (`CellIndex`, about 90-130 ms each over 860k segments, built per stroke
set and level; until built, every query scans everything) plus every station's shape scanned until
the station reach table was built in idle time (about 1 s of a 20 s run).

Built for the big networks: the station reach table (renderer `stationReachNow`) and the trains'
line blocks (pick.ts `lineBlocks`) are worked out when first asked for instead of in idle time, a few
ms each, so no move scans every station or trip meanwhile. After: new game 30/35 ms, ghost 26/34;
NYC 106/131 ms, ghost 28/35, no frame over 50 ms; Japan 708/946 ms, ghost 340/680/1731, 31 frames
over 50 ms (39 before), 1.13 long tasks a click, 165 ms: a big network is still slow, see T-1028 to
T-1030.

Tried and dropped: indexing the pick grids by runs of chained segments (a 9,600-polyline,
860,000-segment test: 124 to 91 ms at the finest level, 75 to 40 at the next; the run pass alone is
~50 ms), not enough to change Japan's ghost (p50 342 to 375 ms in one run). A per-edit grid has to be
patched, not rebuilt: T-1030. The worker's `networkFromState` can say which stroke range changed (the
pieces it reuses are the same objects, so the common prefix and suffix are found by identity, the
track speeds compared by value); a first version of that was written and taken out again unused, the
renderer side is T-1028.

Drawn output: no drawing code changed. The check's `SHOTS=1` (the real save after seven edits, four
views, then the same network uploaded whole) differs by 155-307 pixels of 1.26 million between the two
captures with no change at all, the baseline for T-1028.
