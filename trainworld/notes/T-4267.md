# T-4267 One design so no step of an edit scales with the save (agent editdesign, 2026-11-25)

**Summary for Anita**
- Have we been doing this? Partly. Each stage since T-4144 made one step cheaper, but an edit still walks the whole save about twenty times (in the track model, the worker and the page) just to find what changed, so the steps left still add up to about a second on your Asia save.
- Why the first station lands and the next don't: the second click waits for the first station's answer, and the first station's whole-map rebuild starts right then as one unbroken block of 0.15-0.6 s that the second station's own edit waits behind. Every click after waits the same way, 0.5-1 s each. Our benchmarks timed one edit on an idle game, so they never saw it. Deleting track is the same: its question waits behind that block, and an edit that costs anything is answered only after the whole rebuild.
- The design: the track model keeps one list of what each edit changed, and every layer after it (rows, the drawn map, the page, the renderer, trips) keeps its work by record and redoes only what is on that list. The edit and its answer come first (aim: under 50 ms on your Asia save), then the drawn map in small pieces that any click can go between, then trips and demand after the map shows.
- Stages: about 22 of 1-5 h, about 38 h in all, up to about 55 with four that go ahead only if measuring calls for them. Part 1, later clicks land like the first (10 h). Part 2, every edit answers in a frame or two, passages and panel edits included (12-14 h). Part 3, the built look in about 0.2 s (12-13 h). Part 4, trips after the map (3 h).
- New line (0.6-2.2 s today) and Delete line (about 0.8 s): after parts 1 and 2 both show in a frame or two. A new line becomes one change to one line record; a deleted line's row goes at its answer. The bands beside a deleted line close up after part 3.
- What you'd see differently: no figure changes; paid edits move the cash at once. If measuring shows the game's crash copy (a copy of the whole save after every edit) costs real time, it moves to pauses: after a crash the edits since your last pause are replayed, and lost only if that fails.
- Your calls: the design. Later, only if measuring after part 1 calls for them: train counts on lines two or more lines away from an edit settling up to a second later, and cost totals kept as running sums, off by under a millionth of a dollar until the next save (I'd say yes to both then).
- Open stages: T-4149 stage 2, T-4249 and T-4153 stage 6 land as they are and become its first steps; T-4150 stage 2 and T-4262 become stages of it; T-4214 D and E are no longer needed; T-4220 stays as it is (trip figures, not the map).
- PR 29: it speeds the scan behind the Edit stops markers, which runs after each stop click. Real on Belgrade's long lines (2.4 s to 0.4 s), small on your save (0.15 to 0.08 s), and not on the station, delete or passage path. Same idea as this design (keep a search, redo less), but kept only within one scan. Keep its cap at 8 MB; 16 would save about 40 ms per scan on your 40-stop lines.
- Memory: this design about 3-5 MB more; with T-4153's and PR 29's, about 20 of your 30 MB.
- Gemini's review found real gaps (the crash copy, more whole-save steps in money and demand, a way the first stage could lose an edit, low hours): all taken in, so the hours went up from 30 to about 38.

## 1. Why the first station lands and the later ones don't (from the code)

Click 1, worker idle: `stationClick` (map/stationTool.ts 135-188) draws the box from the ghost at the click (`ahead`), sends
`addStation` 15-26 ms after the press, and stays "being carried out" (`Tools.carrying`, `clicksOut`) until its answer.

Click 2: `waitClick` (map/toolWait.ts 70-88) finds `mapBehind` true (`clicksOut > 0`, or click 1's patch not laid), so it
waits with the spinner after 0.3 s and nothing drawn. It is carried out once click 1's answer and patch are in.

Click 1's answer comes right after its apply and patch (clock.worker.ts 1074-1075). In the same task the worker schedules
click 1's follow-up (`later`, clockFollowUp.ts 64-86): its first piece is `buildState` plus `complexOfNodes`
(clockMain.ts 509, 539), one unbroken task of 150-280 ms on her save with T-4149 stage 2 (500-1,000 before). Click 2's edit
reaches the worker a few tens of ms later, while that piece runs, and waits for it; then it goes ahead (`goesAhead`,
`standAside`), is applied (0.1-0.45 s, below), answered, and `later` throws away the follow-up's work and starts it again
(clockFollowUp.ts 67). Click 3 waits for click 2's answer, which waited for click 1's rebuild. So in a run of stations each
click costs about one rebuild piece plus one apply, 0.5-1 s on her save, and nothing is drawn for it meanwhile.

When click 2 is carried out its box is usually drawn (the page's layout guess is made at once in `place`, toolMarks.ts 197,
stationTool.ts 500-535), but not where the spot has 7 or more candidate tracks (`HOLD_AT`, a held guess) or the guess
refuses: there it shows only with its network, 1-2 s later. Tokyo's bundles have such spots.

The benches never saw this: edit_bench times one edit from an idle worker, and its "second station" is logged, not
measured (T-4240). Every stage was judged on single edits.

## 4. The design

**The rule.** No step of an edit costs time that grows with the save. Copying references (a list of pointers) or a tight
Rust loop over the 242 lines, each under 1 ms at her save's size, is fine; a step that calls into the model, builds an
object, hashes or compares per edge, node or station of the whole save is not. The whole build stays for a load, an anchor move, the side flip, and `?patch=check`, which builds it beside the
kept one and compares (the pattern that kept T-4149, T-4150 and T-4153 exact).

**Data.**
- **One change log in the track model.** Each edit appends its sequence number and what it touched: edges and nodes made,
  changed or gone, lines whose row, path, run or service moved (the settle's lines included), resources, stations whose
  complex changed, buildings. Written where the model changes, not in `commit_with`: what `Touch`, `Derived`, `ServiceDirty` and the settle's counts
  hold, plus name changes, links and apart, buildings, fares and repricing, which go around `commit_with`; a kind not logged
  answers "everything". Undo and redo log what they put back; a trial cuts its entries when it rolls back. Every reader keeps the number it last read to and asks for "changes since n" (rows, the patch, the drawn network,
  the page, trips, demand). This replaces `peek_touched`/`take_touched`, `take_changed_lines` and `take_moved_lines` with
  one thing, and a dropped or restarted build simply asks again from its own number. Past a cap (as `PATCH_MAX`, 2,000
  records) or after a load, the answer is "everything", which takes the whole path.
- **Ids.** As now. A reader reads whatever an id names when it reads, so reuse is safe for it. (Holding freed ids back,
  as the first draft had, would grow the arrays under hover trials and fights undo's claiming ids back; it was only for
  stale clicks, which wait for the patch.)
- **Indexes in the model**: station to the lines stopping there; the set of lines with a way back of their own, kept as
  lines and one-way flags change (with a count of one-way edges); line to the resources it uses; the bus routes and kept
  stops by grid cell (T-4249's `bus::lines_touched`); retime's clusters by line, each with its key; running cost totals
  only if A7 shows the sums cost (a running total has to follow every coupled price: stations by their edges and water,
  junctions, crossings, passages, shared BRT lanes, buildings).
- **Before-values on first write.** What the apply compares before and after (`line_rates_all`, `had`, `keys`,
  `demand_view`) is recorded for a line the first time the edit writes it (in the services update and the settle), so a
  line the edit never writes costs nothing and cannot count as moved.
- **The worker keeps rows by id** and reads only the logged ids: flags per id instead of the whole flagged lists; the
  network-wide reads (markers, conflicts, singles, crossings, full track) kept by resource or edge and replaced for those
  touched; per-line tables (terminal ends, first stops, countries) for touched lines. T-4149's `touched` on every state
  is the right shape for the page and stays; it is filled from the log instead of from `rowsDelta`'s walk.
- **Per-line slots** in the sample, phase, line and bend arrays, with some room to grow, written only for lines whose runs
  moved; the drawn network posted as the changed strokes, slots, edge points and stations; the renderer uploads only those
  ranges (T-4262).
- **One world on the page**, patched from each edit's word by id; its readers take the word's ids (T-4150's `PerRecord`
  readers, `worldDiff` from ids instead of a walk). The tools' separate world (`clickWorld`) and the copied edge map go
  once the readers are cheap.

**Order of work.**
1. At the click (page, one frame): what is drawn ahead now, as today.
2. The edit's first task in the worker (aim: under 50 ms on her save): the apply with the indexes, the word with the
   touched rows and the money's split, and the answer, for every edit (map, panel, paid or free). The next click goes.
3. Then pieces of at most about 20 ms, any edit, check or question going between them: the drawn network by touched
   records, the state's trips worked out, then its post with the trips straight after (T-648: nothing between them). A
   read begun when an edit comes carries what it took into the next read.
4. The page lays the network delta and draws.
5. After that frame: boarding for the lines whose runs moved, demand's snapshot (debounced, as now), the panels' day
   figures, achievements, label plans, undo's warm-up, and the crash copy in a pause if K is built.

**What it makes approximate (for DECISIONS when built; drafts):**
- (T-4267, only if A3 is built) Cost totals are running sums kept by the records an edit touched and their neighbours.
  Lie: the totals shown can differ from a whole recount by float rounding, under a millionth of a dollar, made whole again
  at each load and save.
- (T-4267, only if A6 is built) The settle at an edit steps the lines one shared resource from it; lines sharing track
  only through those are settled in the next background piece, before the next edit, any save or crash copy, and inside
  the edit's undo step. Lie: on big shared-track networks, for up to about a second, their trains, their upkeep and the
  trigger for calculating trips run on the old counts.
- (T-4267, only if K is built) The crash copy is taken in a pause (no edit for a second), the edits since kept and
  replayed after a crash. Lie: if the replay fails, the edits since that pause are lost, and the recovery notice says so.
- (T-4267) No step of an edit walks the whole save except to copy references; the whole build stays for loads, anchor
  moves, the side flip and `?patch=check`.
Everything else stays exact, checked against the whole build as now.

**What she would see differently.** Panels, lists and the cash change in a frame or two after any edit, paid ones
included; the built look follows in about 0.2 s; trains take their new runs a moment after, as today but sooner. No figure
changes. With K, a crash goes back to the last pause and replays the edits since.

## 5. Her edits under it

- **Stations in a row**: part 1 (pieces, a fast apply) makes each later click wait one apply and at most one piece, under
  about 0.1 s; part 2 answers at the word; part 3 draws the built station in about 0.2 s.
- **Deleting track**: the question (`removal`) answers within one piece (under 50 ms) instead of waiting out a rebuild; the
  delete is answered at its word whatever it charges; the track is hidden at the click as now.
- **Removing a passageway** (`separate`, from the panel): answered at its word with the rows (panel and complex), the
  bridge goes with the network delta, demand's snapshot as now (debounced, patched by record only if D2 is built).
- **New line**: one commit (A5) touching one line and no track or station, so no bus line or way back is rebuilt; its row in
  the word puts it in the Lines tab and opens its panel; nothing to draw. About a frame of worker time.
- **Delete line**: hidden at the click as now; its row goes at the word (list, panel); the lines beside it re-settle (they
  are touched: the capacity it freed) and their bands close up with the network delta.
- **The rest of T-4144's list**: a track segment, blueprint drags and the select tool, Construct (paid: answered at its word
  now), colour and line settings (rows first already: now at the word), station panel edits, undo and redo (an edit like
  any other in the log), stop clicks (already rows at once; their held network gets cheaper), train counts and headways
  (their light state stops walking every record), fares and loans (already small), buildings and passages (today a whole
  state at once: now the word, then the network delta).

## 8. PR 29 (T-4255, stop-marker scans)

- **Where it sits.** `stop_tiers` answers the Edit stops markers (map/stopMarkers.ts 284-309): asked when Edit stops opens
  on a line and again after each stop click's new world. The markers show the page's estimate at once and the worker's
  tiers replace it; meanwhile the scan holds the worker, so the next stop click's answer can wait behind it. It is on no
  station, delete or passage path.
- **Is it faster?** Yes where lines are long and the network mid-sized: Belgrade's 58-stop line 2.4 s to 0.37 s, which a
  player feels in Edit stops. On her save 0.15 to 0.08 s per long line, barely felt. Results identical.
- **What to learn.** Its idea (a search kept and reused instead of done again) is this design's rule. But it keeps the
  trees only within one scan (`HopMemo::for_stop_scan` per call), so each stop click still searches the whole network
  again. Kept across edits, a stop's tree would be dropped only when an edit touches an edge it reached: a later stage
  for stop clicks if they feel slow, not needed for her current waits.
- **The cap.** A tree on her save is about 176 KB ((2 x 8,746 + 3 x 9,215) x 4 bytes), so 8 MiB holds about 40, fewer
  than a 40-stop line's keys: that is why her save gets 2x and Belgrade 6x. 16 MiB would cover those lines, about 40 ms
  less per scan, for 8 MB more on small devices (WASM memory never shrinks). Keep 8 MiB.
