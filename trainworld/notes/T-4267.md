## 1. Why the first station lands and the later ones don't (from the code)

Click 1, worker idle: `stationClick` (map/stationTool.ts 135-188) draws the box from the ghost at the click (`ahead`), sends
`addStation` 15-26 ms after the press, and stays "being carried out" (`Tools.carrying`, `clicksOut`) until its answer.

Click 2: `waitClick` (map/toolWait.ts 70-88) finds `mapBehind` true (`clicksOut > 0`, or click 1's patch not laid), so it
waits with the spinner after 0.3 s and nothing drawn. It is carried out once click 1's answer and patch are in.

Click 1's answer comes right after its apply and patch (clock.worker.ts 1074-1075). In the same task the worker schedules
click 1's follow-up (`later`, clockFollowUp.ts 64-86): its first piece is `buildState` plus `complexOfNodes`
(clockMain.ts 509, 539), one unbroken task of 150-280 ms on her save with T-4149 stage 2 (500-1,000 before). Click 2's edit
reaches the worker a few tens of ms later, while that piece runs, and waits for it; then it goes ahead (`goesAhead`,
`standAside`), is applied (0.1-0.45 s, below), answered, and `later` throws away the follow-up's work and starts it again
(clockFollowUp.ts 67). Click 3 waits for click 2's answer, which waited for click 1's rebuild. So in a run of stations each
click costs about one rebuild piece plus one apply: 0.6-1.45 s on her save on master (a piece of 0.5-1 s), 0.25-0.73 s once
T-4149 stage 2 lands. Nothing is drawn for it meanwhile.

When click 2 is carried out its box is usually drawn (the page's layout guess is made at once in `place`, toolMarks.ts 197,
stationTool.ts 500-535), but not where the spot has 7 or more candidate tracks (`HOLD_AT`, a held guess) or the guess
refuses: there it shows only with its network, 1-2 s later. Tokyo's bundles have such spots.

The benches never saw this: edit_bench times one edit from an idle worker, and its "second station" is logged, not
measured (T-4240). Every stage was judged on single edits.

Two more things land between her clicks (Fable's review). The page's take of each state (130-180 ms), the renderer's
upload (65-180 ms) and the heavy frame after it (50-220 ms) block the page itself, so a press made then waits too. And any
other message the worker gets while a follow-up waits or runs (the page's money figures, demand's riders and transfers,
a panel query) runs every piece left of it in one block (clock.worker.ts 731-734). This chain is
read from code: stage H measures it, with the hold switched off for today's rule.

## 4. The design

**The rule.** No step of an edit costs time that grows with the save. Copying references (a list of pointers) or a tight
Rust loop over the 242 lines, each under 1 ms at her save's size, is fine; a step that calls into the model, builds an
object, hashes or compares per edge, node, station or running train of the whole save is not. M measures which steps
break the rule; past profiles say the per-line loops mostly don't. The whole build stays for a load, an anchor move, the
side flip, an edit that really moves every line (rows that split or join the slots, the game's hours), an edit over the
cap, and `?patch=check`, which builds it beside the kept one and compares (the pattern that kept T-4149, T-4150 and
T-4153 exact).

**Data.**
- **One change log in the track model.** Each edit appends its sequence number and what it touched: edges and nodes made,
  changed or gone, lines whose row, path, run or service moved (the settle's lines included), resources, stations whose
  complex changed, buildings. Written where the model changes, not in `commit_with`: what `Touch`, `Derived`, `ServiceDirty` and the settle's counts
  hold, plus name changes, links and apart, buildings, fares and repricing, which go around `commit_with`; a kind not logged
  answers "everything". Undo and redo log what they put back; a trial cuts its entries when it rolls back. Every reader keeps the number it last read to and asks for "changes since n" (rows, the patch, the drawn network,
  the page, trips, demand). This replaces `peek_touched`/`take_touched`, `take_changed_lines` and `take_moved_lines` with
  one thing, and a dropped or restarted build simply asks again from its own number. Past a cap (as `PATCH_MAX`, 2,000
  records) or after a load, the answer is "everything", which takes the whole path.
- **Lines noted, lines moved.** The model over-notes lines (a settle notes every line it scheduled, most unmoved, T-4133),
  so the log holds the lines noted and the line stamps (wasm/touched.rs 89-107) stay the filter from noted to moved, read
  once per read for the noted lines only. Without it every noted line's row, samples, bands and trips would be redone.
- **Reprices are changes.** Ground, roads, masks or street speeds arriving (clockGround.ts) log the edges and nodes they
  repriced and the bus lines they timed again, so they take the edit's path instead of sending a whole state.
- **Ids.** As now. A reader reads whatever an id names when it reads, so reuse is safe for it. (Holding freed ids back,
  as the first draft had, would grow the arrays under hover trials and fights undo's claiming ids back; it was only for
  stale clicks, which wait for the patch.)
- **Indexes in the model**, each built only if M shows the walk it replaces: station to the lines stopping there; the set
  of lines with a way back of their own, kept as lines and one-way flags change (with a count of one-way edges); line to
  the resources it uses; the bus routes and kept stops by grid cell (T-4249's `bus::lines_touched`); retime's clusters by
  line, each with its key; running cost totals (a running total has to follow every coupled price: stations by their
  edges and water, junctions, crossings, passages, shared BRT lanes, buildings, and every reprice).
- **Before-values** (`line_rates_all` twice, `had`, `keys`, `demand_view`): loops over the lines, kept whole unless M finds
  them over the mark. If built, a line counts as written only where a write changes its value: `place_weekend` rebuilds
  every line's weekend departures on every edit, `pin_twin_phases` every twin's, and the settle resets every line's
  `pace_in`, so "first write" alone would record nearly every line, and a missed write would leave a line's trains wrong
  after an undo (undo's `found` checks only the lines its record names).
- **Fan-out per layer.** Each layer turns the logged records into its own work by its own rule: rows go from a moved path
  to its edges and their end nodes, and a line that starts or stops running reads its track again (stateRows.ts
  406-420); bands, the drawing order and the complexes have theirs. Each stage writes its layer's rule down and `?patch=check` checks it. On shared track one edit
  can name dozens of lines (`was_sharing`, `sharing_closure`, the settle's `recomputed`): that work scales with the
  bundle, not the save, which fits her goal; D3 times it on Tokyo's bundles.
- **The worker keeps rows by id** and reads only the logged ids: flags per id instead of the whole flagged lists; the
  network-wide reads (markers, conflicts, singles, crossings, full track) kept by resource or edge and replaced for those
  touched; per-line tables (terminal ends, first stops, countries) for touched lines. T-4149's `touched` on every state
  is the right shape for the page and stays; it is filled from the log instead of from `rowsDelta`'s walk.
- **Per-line slots** in the sample, phase, line and bend arrays, with some room to grow, written only for lines whose runs
  moved; the drawn network posted as the changed strokes, slots, edge points and stations; the renderer uploads only those
  ranges (T-4262).
- **One world on the page**, patched from each edit's word by id; its readers take the word's ids (T-4150's `PerRecord`
  readers, `worldDiff` from ids instead of a walk). The tools' separate world (`clickWorld`) and the copied edge map go
  once the readers are cheap.

**Messages while a follow-up waits.** Today any message but a preview, the delete question or an edit that goes ahead
runs the whole follow-up first (clock.worker.ts 731-734), so nothing but previews reads the model ahead of its posted
state (T-1407, T-3803). Each message kind gets one of three answers: it passes (reads nothing the follow-up changes, or
is an edit that joins it), it waits (kept aside and taken right after the follow-up's state, the newest of each kind and
day: demand's riders and transfers, which nothing waits on and which may not reach the model ahead of its state), or
it ends the wait, as now. H
writes the list for its hold; B4, where every edit is answered ahead of its map, writes it for every kind.

**Order of work.**
1. At the click (page, one frame): what is drawn ahead now, as today.
2. The edit's first task in the worker (aim: under 50 ms on her save): the apply, the word with the touched rows and the
   money's split, and the answer, for every edit (map, panel, paid or free). The next click goes.
3. While she keeps clicking stations or deleting, the follow-up is held until she pauses (H). Then pieces of at most
   about 20 ms once parts 2 and 3 have made each piece small, any edit, check or question going between them: the drawn
   network by touched records, the state's trips worked out, then its post with the trips straight after (T-648:
   nothing between them). A read dropped when an edit comes asks the log again from its own number.
4. The page lays the network delta and draws.
5. After that frame: boarding for the lines whose runs moved, demand's snapshot (debounced, as now), the panels' day
   figures, achievements, label plans, undo's warm-up, and the crash copy in a pause if K is built.

**What it makes approximate (for DECISIONS when built; drafts):**
- (T-4267, stage H) Stations and deletes clicked less than half a second apart, or while the station ghost is still
  being asked, share one follow-up: their built look, the trains' new runs and the trigger for calculating trips wait
  until she pauses, at most 5 s into a run. Lie: meanwhile the map shows the at-click drawings only (none at spots with
  7 or more tracks), and a crash during the run goes back to before it (at most 5 s of edits; already so today for
  clicks faster than a rebuild). The crash part goes if M finds the crash copy cheap enough to take at each answer.
- (T-4267, only if A3 is built) Cost totals are running sums kept by the records an edit touched and their neighbours.
  Lie: the totals shown can differ from a whole recount by float rounding, under a millionth of a dollar, made whole again
  at each load and save.
- (T-4267, only if A6 is built) The settle at an edit steps the lines one shared resource from it; lines sharing track
  only through those are settled in the next background piece, before the next edit, any save or crash copy, and inside
  the edit's undo step. Lie: on big shared-track networks, for up to about a second, their trains, their upkeep and the
  trigger for calculating trips run on the old counts.
- (T-4267, only if K is built) The crash copy is taken in a pause (no edit for a second), the edits since kept and
  replayed after a crash. Lie: if the replay fails, the edits since that pause are lost, and the recovery notice says so.
- (T-4267) No step of an edit walks the whole save except to copy references; the whole build stays for loads, anchor
  moves, the side flip, edits that move every line and `?patch=check`.
Everything else stays exact, checked against the whole build as now.

**What she would see differently.** From H: in a run of stations or deletes each click lands like the first, and the
built look comes when she pauses. After part 2: panels, lists and the cash change in a frame or two after any edit, paid
ones included. After part 3: the built look follows in about 0.2 s; trains take their new runs a moment after, as today
but sooner. No figure changes. With K, a crash goes back to the last pause and replays the edits since.

## 5. Her edits under it

- **Stations in a row**: H holds the follow-up while she keeps clicking (no click and no station ghost for 500 ms ends
  it), so no rebuild, trips or page take comes between clicks, and each later click waits only for the click before's
  answer: its apply, 0.1-0.45 s on her save now, less after T-4249 and the A stages. That is more than half of each
  later click's wait on master (the rebuild piece, 0.5-1 of 0.6-1.45 s) and 40-60% with T-4149 stage 2 (0.15-0.28 of
  0.25-0.73 s), read from code; H measures it. The page's 0.25-0.5 s of work per state no longer lands between clicks.
  Clicks further apart with no ghost asked between (moving over open ground) still meet the rebuild's first piece, as
  today; H's slower row shows how often. The built look comes at the pause plus one
  rebuild (0.5-1 s on her save until parts 2 and 3, then about 0.2 s); a station at a spot with 7 or more tracks is
  drawn only then. A paid station still waits for its state until B4. A click right after a pause, and the one 5 s into

## 8. PR 29 (T-4255, stop-marker scans)

- **Where it sits.** `stop_tiers` answers the Edit stops markers (map/stopMarkers.ts 284-309): asked when Edit stops opens
  on a line and again after each stop click's new world. The markers show the page's estimate at once and the worker's
  tiers replace it; meanwhile the scan holds the worker, so the next stop click's answer can wait behind it. It is on no
  station, delete or passage path.
- **Is it faster?** Yes where lines are long and the network mid-sized: Belgrade's 58-stop line 2.4 s to 0.37 s, which a
  player feels in Edit stops. On her save 0.15 to 0.08 s per long line, barely felt. Results identical.
- **What to learn.** Its idea (a search kept and reused instead of done again) is this design's rule. But it keeps the
  trees only within one scan (`HopMemo::for_stop_scan` per call), so each stop click still searches the whole network
  again. Kept across edits, a stop's tree would be dropped only when an edit touches an edge it reached: a later stage
  for stop clicks if they feel slow, not needed for her current waits.
- **The cap.** A tree on her save is about 176 KB ((2 x 8,746 + 3 x 9,215) x 4 bytes), so 8 MiB holds about 40, fewer
  than a 40-stop line's keys: that is why her save gets 2x and Belgrade 6x. 16 MiB would cover those lines, about 40 ms
  less per scan, for 8 MB more on small devices (WASM memory never shrinks). Keep 8 MiB.
