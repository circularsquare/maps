## 3. The design

**The rule.** No step of an edit costs time that grows with the save. A pointer copy, or a Rust loop over the 242 lines
reading a number each, under 1 ms at her save's size, is fine; a step that calls into the model, builds an object,
compares or hashes per edge, node, station, resource or running train of the whole save is not. What an edit really
reaches is fine whatever its size, with one exception named in section 5 (the bundle). The whole build stays for
the cases at the end of section 1, and for `?patch=check`, which builds it beside the kept one and compares: the
pattern that kept T-4149 to T-4314 exact.

**What each layer reads.** Each layer already takes its changes from the one before (row 12 from the model's touched
lists, 13 from the rows, 14 from the rows log, 18 from the word's ids, 19 from the network's touched sets). What is left is:
- **The train work by what changed.** T-4441 timed it on her trunk click: the settle's rounds are 4 ms of 152, though
  they go over all 239 lines with trains; the cost is the work for the 3 lines the click reached, some of it done twice
  and wrapped in whole-save work: the capacity update for weekdays and again for the weekend service (62, 40 of it
  `Capacity::update`'s loops over every edge, node and resource), retime's redone cluster (48: `passing_waits` 35 and
  `lead_in` 10, about 12 ms a line), the weekend copy and placing (`weekend_own` over every line three times an edit,
  every line's counts swapped in and out, every line's phases copied), and the trains noted (12). The stages go at those.
  The settle's rounds stay over every line (a few numbers a line, 4 ms on her save). If a wide bundle (edge 1818) ever
  makes them count, scoping them needs what Gemini found (section 8, finding 7): `auto_counts` and `trim_auto` reach
  past the scope, `settle_kept` is cleared whole, `cap.fill` can cut a line outside it; the lines a settle leaves not at
  rest would have to join the next one's seeds.
- **Indexes the model lacks**: line to the resources it holds (T-4309), each resource by the edges and nodes it stands on
  (for `Capacity::update`), station to the lines stopping there, `edge_lines` patched by the lines whose paths moved
  rather than rebuilt, the bus routes by grid cell, the kept set of lines with a way back of their own and the track each
  one's search went over, the twins' parents by their stops and route, the running lines on each edge (already
  `lines_on_edge`, for `moves_no_train`), and the cost sums kept by record (T-4299 has the parts). In the worker, each
  end platform group's lines (terminalBays.ts), with group numbers that stay put from edit to edit.
- **Owed together.** Clicks that move running trains join the owed train work as clicks that move none already do, so a
  run of them settles once, after the answers. Its running trains are noted then, as one click's are now (`pay_owed`),
  so nothing whole comes back onto a click's confirm.
- **Every edit answered at its word**: the word, the patch and the answer right after the edit's own model work. A click
  owes its train work until after the answer (T-4272, A1). Delete line, New line, Construct and a passageway keep theirs
  inside the commit, which the C stages make cost the bundle, and are sent as clicks so they are answered at their
  patch; a patch for a line gone or new, and for a line broken while its train work is owed; a paid edit's split from
  the word's own records against the same records before it.
- **Trips by line**: the hour's trips sent and laid per line whose run moved, with every line sharing an end platform
  group with one (the platforms there are shared out among them by arrival).
- **The demand snapshot patched by record**, due by the stations the edit touched.

**Order of work at an edit.** At the press: the drawing ahead, as now. In the worker, under about 20 ms: the checks,
the word, the patch, the answer. Then pieces of a few ms each, any click or question going between them: the owed
train work by scope, the rows, the network, the post with the trips of the moved lines. Then the page takes it in a
frame or two. After that: boarding, the snapshot (debounced), demand's local solve; the crash copy at a quiet moment.

## 5. The lies

New, for DECISIONS when each is built (drafts):
- (A1, corrected 11-27 after Gemini's review) Clicks on running track that come before the train work of the one before
  has run are worked out as one edit, when that work runs after their answers. Lie: on shared track a settle depends on
  where it starts, so after a quick run a line sharing that track can have a train more or fewer, and the cash those
  cars' price more or less, than the same clicks made slowly. Undoing the whole run puts back exactly what was there
  before; undoing only some of its clicks settles each as an edit, so such a line can be a train up or down there too.
  The total for the run is what the whole path gives for the run as one edit.
- (C7, only if built; corrected 11-27) The settle at an edit steps the lines one shared resource from it; the whole scope
  steps again in the next background piece, before any save, crash copy, undo, redo or whole commit (a click owed
  together takes it into its own work). Lie: on big shared networks, for up to about a second, the far lines' trains,
  upkeep and the trigger for calculating trips run on the old counts; and since a settle depends on where it starts, a
  line on that track can end a train up or down from what one settle gives, and stays so until an edit reaches it again.
- Everything else here is exact: C2-C4 give the same rates and timetables to the bit, C3's fallback too once its piece
  has run (until then, for one piece of tens of ms after the map shows up, the line panel's weekend figures are from
  before the edit, and where the edit switched a line between its own weekend rates and the weekdays', its timetable
  variants for that piece). C5 gives the same timetables or says the shift to her (a profile put together in another
  order can differ in its last bits). A2-A8 and D1-D2 read the same records (D1 with every line sharing an end platform
  group), D4 makes the same model again. A6's cost sums reuse T-4299's lie (fixed-point sums against a float sum over
  every record, a part in 1e9 at most).
- Ruled out (11-27): skipping the weekend copy and placing on edits that change no weekend row (about 49 ms of a trunk
  click's 152, T-4441). Lie: a line that runs its own weekend rates would keep, on weekends, the rates, holds and
  departures worked out before the edit until a weekend setting changed or the game loaded. With a stop added, its
  weekend trains would run more often than its weekend fleet allows (the old rate over a longer run), a reroute or
  extension far more, the lines sharing its track would not follow it on weekends, and its weekend departures would
  keep their old places, so they can bunch with other lines'. C3 takes the same time out exactly.

What is not possible without a lie, plainly:
- **The bundle.** A line's trains depend on every line it shares track with, and on theirs: the settle's scope is that
  whole chain. On her Tokaido trunk one station's scope reached about 150 lines (449 line updates over three service
  passes, T-4267-measure.md). C2-C5 make the work cost that chain instead of the save; on her save the chain can still
  be most of the save. Bounding it is C7, with its lie, which lasts (above).
- **Long trips.** The regional market solves over the whole joined-up cluster, since its longer trips ride across it
  (docs/demand-runtime.md 330). An edit that changes them (a stop on a run of 20 minutes or more, a line switched on or
  off, a line's run time moved 3 minutes or more) takes a whole solve, 100-250 s on Asia; the market's window (T-1735) and
  T-4220's side calculation already shorten what she waits for. Making it local would change what demand is, which is
  her call, not this design's.

Existing lies these stages keep: T-4272 (trains bought after the answer, cash may go below zero), T-4281 (a train up to 5
cm off along its track past a cut), T-4299 (kept money trusts the namers; fixed-point sums), T-4220 (an edit's side
calculation against the figures on screen).
