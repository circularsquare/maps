## What a player can start in

The new game sheet (`ui/NewGame.tsx`): five suggested starts (`START_CITIES`: New York, London,
Paris, Tokyo, Toronto), the player's location, any registry entry found by name (3,545: 405 metros
built as cities and 3,140 zones), or the world map. So every registry entry needs a label.

## The measure

A start's score is the **weekday trip legs a km² with both ends within 10 km of its centre**, no
rail: commutes (the pack's gravity, `DemandApi::flows_day` from the circle's homes, its far cells
inside the circle) and every everyday purpose (`EverydayApi::legs_within`, new: each sampled
outing whose two ends are inside), both legs, walkers included. Attraction visits are left out
(small, and they need the world attraction list). Each pack the circle reaches counts its own
trips, so trips between two packs are missing: 11 of the 408 well-known starts have over a
quarter of the circle's people in another pack (Johor Bahru 72% in Singapore's, Macau 69%), the
rest under that.

Code: `sim/src/bin/start_difficulty.rs` (one pack at a time, one JSON line each) and
`pipeline/start_difficulty.py` (runs it, adds the packs up, cuts the tiers). A full run is 18-24
minutes on one core; `--tiers` re-cuts in seconds. Check: with a 1,000 km circle the bin gives New
York 68.28M legs and Houston 22.42M, against the registry's `localTrips` 68.45M and 22.5M (the
1% `FLOW_CUT` of far commute cells).

**The centre.** For a metro, the OSM city or town in its own place list named as it is, nearest
the registry centre within 40 km; for a zone, the registry centre. The registry centres (where the
camera starts) are off for many metros: 112 move over 5 km, among them Oklahoma City 31 km (it
was at Norman), Perth 28 km (at Joondalup), Atlanta 23 km, Liège 21 km, Rotterdam 22 km (at The
Hague), Brasilia 15 km, Rio 15 km, Beijing 13 km, Singapore 13 km (the island's middle). OSM also
has metro label nodes, so the nearest same-named place is taken, not the most populous (Sydney's
label node is 38 km west of the city). Proposed follow-up task: move those registry centres, the
start camera, onto the same points.

**Radius.** 10 km: a 20 km circle fits the first view (zoom 11.2, about 50 x 27 km on a 1080p
screen) and is about the length of a first line or two. 5 km scores compact centres (Paris, Hong
Kong, New York's Manhattan); 15 km scores whole metros (Moscow, Beijing). Among the 408 well-known
starts the rank correlation (Spearman) is 0.89 between 5 and 10 km and 0.95 between 10 and 15 km;
the tier stays the same for 305 of 408 at 5 km and 339 of 408 at 15 km.

**Walking.** Counting only legs not walked (the ones rail can win) changes little: rank
correlation 0.98, the same tier for 370 of 408. So all legs, the plain version.

**The open question: money.** Fares (and train running costs) follow income to the 0.5 (T-2670),
while construction, upkeep and trains are world prices. So a rider in India pays about 0.17 of a
US rider's fare, and Bengaluru at 67,104 legs a km² brings in about a third of New York's fare
money a km². Weighting the score by the start country's fare scale over the US's (a "money"
version, same thirds) keeps the known-easy cities easy but moves the US up and India down: Houston,
Phoenix, Atlanta and Dallas become medium (failing the brief's check), Toronto, Boston, Chicago,
Washington and San Francisco easy;
India's 75 metros go from 37 easy and 12 hard to 10 easy and 54 hard; Lagos easy to hard. Which is
"hard" depends on whether a first save fails for want of riders (the train-share goals, which don't
care about income) or for want of money. Recommendation: the plain density, as Anita proposed: it
gets the brief's checks right and says what a player sees (busy streets or empty ones); the money
side belongs to the fares, which a player can change. The money version is one line in
`tiers()` if she prefers it.

## Cut points

The well-known starts: the five suggested ones, every metro built as a city (405) and zones with
over 1.5M people inside the circle (3 more: Lianchi, Kuiwen, Banyu Asin), 408 in all. Cut into
equal thirds: **easy from 12,761 legs a km², medium from 7,558, hard below**.

| Tier | Well-known starts | All 3,545 starts |
|---|---|---|
| easy | 136 | 136 (3.8%) |
| medium | 136 | 199 (5.6%) |
| hard | 136 | 3,210 (90.6%) |

Anita guessed about 6% easy, 24% medium and 70% hard of all regions. The thirds of the well-known
starts give 3.8%, 5.6% and 90.6%, because most zones are countryside and small towns (over 1.5M
people in a 10 km circle is a big city), and only 66 zones reach the medium cut. If she wants
nearer her guess, the cuts can be set by share of all starts instead: 6% and 30% of all give easy
from 9,724 and medium from 3,353, and the well-known starts split 202 easy, 177 medium and 29 hard
(Houston, Phoenix, Atlanta, Dallas and Chicago medium; Los Angeles and Boston easy). That loses the
even thirds she asked for, so the thirds are the proposal.
