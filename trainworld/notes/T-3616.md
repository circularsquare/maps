## Why the hold was there (T-1734)

T-1734 (notes/T-1734.md, DECISIONS 2026-11-04) made an edit's solve run to its end: a newer network waits on the page
until the final, so building steadily on all of Japan lands a whole solve every 19-26 minutes where before a newer
edit cancelled the running one and nothing landed. The one opening is the 8 s gap after the local result
(`EDIT_SETTLE_MS`). A network held since *before* the local result waited for the final, because all of Japan's local
solve takes about 2 minutes: building a line a minute always has one held, and letting it in at each local result
would restart the local solve for ever and never start a whole solve.

On Kaohsiung the local solve takes 2-7 s, so any edit made during it (edits a few seconds apart) fell on that rule
and waited the whole solve, 163-170 s (T-3600 runD, kcheck). The cause is that last clause of `firm()`, not the 8 s
wait. runD's off-phase solve log shows it: v3 held at 312.18 s, its local result at 312.39 s, `whole` sent, and the
next edit's solve (v8) sent at 482.64 s, 11 ms after v3's final; the same for v9 (held 657.32, local 657.82).

## The change (`workers/demandClient.ts`, page side only)

- A network held during an edit's local solve now goes at the local result, as one arriving in the 8 s gap does,
  while the first edit since a whole solve last started was sent at most 60 s before (`LOCAL_CHAIN_MS`; `chainFrom`,
  carried from a superseded edit's solve that had not sent `whole`). Past that it waits for the final as before, so
  a local solve slower than 60 s (Japan's) holds as T-1734 built it, and a whole solve still starts once edits pause.
- Nothing changes once `whole` is sent, or when no worker solves locally (NYC's line-to-0 edits, solved whole at
  once): those holds wait for the final as before.
- For the first 10 s of the local solve (`HOLD_QUIET_MS`) a held network is taken to go at the local result: the
  page neither starts a zone solved aside (T-2605's focus worker, which would be ended seconds later) nor counts a
  second solve in the time left (T-3599's `editHeld`, so the hover doesn't double for a wait of seconds). A timer
  at 10 s (or at the 60 s, if sooner) makes it an ordinary hold, so all of Japan's aside starts at most 10 s later
  than before. Once every worker's plan is in, a held network is looked at again, so a solve whole at once (no local
  stage) gets its aside and doubled time left at once, as before.
- `?perfOff=heldLocal` restores the old rule.

The worker sees exactly what an edit in the gap gave it before (a new solve after a local result, before `whole`),
which runE of T-3600 already exercised in chains of local solves.

Not changed: the 8 s `EDIT_SETTLE_MS`. Edits further apart than the local solve plus 8 s (about 13 s here) still
come after `whole` went and wait for that whole solve, 150-170 s on this save. Lengthening the gap would put every
edit's final later by as much, on any game whose edits take the local path (NYC's real save solves the edits tried
here whole at once, so it has no gap).

## An edit made during the whole solve (not built)

Anita's likely use is one line at a time with a pause to look, often more than the local solve plus 8 s (about 13 s
on Kaohsiung) apart. Such an edit comes after `whole` went and still waits for the whole solve, 150-170 s, before its
local result. What it would take to give it its own quick result:

**What stands in the way.** The local solve needs every population of the cluster and the market part as the last
landing left them (T-1733): the touched zones are re-solved on everyone else's loads as that landing priced them
(`trail`), and the market window on the frozen market part. Once the whole solve starts, every population of the
cluster (here 120 zones, 241 populations) and the market part are being overwritten round by round, so that state
is gone until the next landing.

1. **Interrupt** (stop the whole solve, start the new network's). There is then no landed state to solve locally
   on, so the new edit gets no quick figures either: it goes straight to a whole solve. It only brings the final
   sooner (T-3600 option 1), and reopens what T-1734 fixed: steady editing never lands a final. Not useful here.
2. **Pause, solve locally, resume, in the main pool** (T-1734's "local solve in between the whole solve's rounds").
   At the next population pass the workers stash the touched populations' Rust state for the periods in hand, put
   them on the newest network, solve locally against the last landing's trail and market loads, show that, restore
   and resume. Needs: a Rust stash/restore of a population's solve state, exact to the bit, so the final still equals
   a fresh solve (a new WASM function pair, for city, everyday and market-window populations); keeping the last
   landing's `trail` and the market part's landed loads apart (JS arrays, about 10 MB a worker); and the figures of a
   local result composed from the landed result, since the populations no longer hold it (`figures` reads them all).
   Then on the page: the local figures are of a network newer than the whole solve in hand, so when that older final
   lands it must not put the edited lines back to their old riders (kept over it as T-2605's aside is, `withZone`),
   and the local-on-top after it replaces them. Cost while it runs: each pause adds the local solve (2-5 s here) plus
   the stash and restore to the whole solve. What it reopens of T-1734: the whole solve no longer runs untouched, so
   steady edits push its final later; it needs a cap (pause only while the local solve is quick, at most a few pauses
   or about 20% of the whole solve's time per solve), and a resume that isn't exact would bring back T-1736-like
   drift. Memory: the stash is the touched populations' period state (Kaohsiung's 3 zones are small; Tokyo's would
   be hundreds of MB, so big touched cities keep the hold). Rough size: Rust 4-6 h, worker 4-6 h, page 2-3 h, checks
   2-3 h: **about 12-18 hours** (not checked against a landed task of this kind; none is close), and its exactness
   risk is real.
3. **Beside, in another worker.** A worker holding a copy of the cluster's landed state would need every pack of the
   cluster and the market open and solved (on this save 22-28 s of setup per worker and most of a worker's memory),
   kept current after each landing. T-2605's aside worker already solves the zone in view alone on the newest
   network; it could show the edited lines' figures too, but those are that zone's riders alone (no market, no other
   zones: Salt Lake City's lines read 50,400 against 67,800, a long line near zero, T-2793), and the intercity lines,
   which are what she asked to see, can't be done there at all. A line switched to no trains would read 0 correctly.
   About 3-4 hours for that much, but the figures are wrong for most edits.
4. **A longer wait before the whole solve where it pays.** Not this question's answer, but the cheap route: the gap
   (`EDIT_SETTLE_MS`, 8 s) set from the whole solve's expected time, for example a seventh of it within 8-30 s
   (T-3599's estimate has it), and only where the local solve was quick (under about 10 s), so all of Japan keeps
   8 s. On Kaohsiung (whole solve 150-170 s) about 20-25 s: edits up to about 25-30 s apart each get their local
   result in seconds, and a final comes about 15 s later (under 10%). What it reopens of T-1734: the gap is the one
   window where a new edit restarts the local stage, so steady editing at that pace would never start a whole solve;
   it needs a latest start for `whole`, counted from `chainFrom` (say 60 s, as `LOCAL_CHAIN_MS`). Meanwhile the
   figures are the local results only (far zones and the market's far pairs as before the edits; T-3600's runE: the
   switched-off feeders read 787-3,356 until the final), and the 4x clock cap after an edit lasts the longer wait too.
   NYC's edits tried here are solved whole at once and have no gap. About 2-3 hours with a run on her save.
