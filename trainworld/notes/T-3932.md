# T-3932: a cores slider with its memory and speed

Built 2026-11-27 by coreslider2 (tree `.tw-trees/coreslider2` from master d7a891da), from the half-built work of
coreslider (11-22, paused for usage; its patch had the setting and part of the estimate, no UI). Her words: 11-21
"rework 'use more cores' to have an actual slider where people can select how many cores and it estimates the
resulting memory usage right below and the estimated speed gain or loss"; 11-27 "so people can customize exactly how
much they want to spend (also indication that it wont take effect until restart)".

## What it is

Settings, Performance, first row (and the start menu's gear panel): **Cores**, a slider 1 to `coresMax`, the count
beside it, and a small **Auto** button, lit by default. Under it, in a game, refreshed every 2 s, muted lines for the
count shown:

- "Uses about 2.3 GB": the Memory row's own reading (`liveBytes`) at that count, the largest solving worker standing
  in for any added (a phone's shared aux worker comes back, 80 MB, once there is a second). Red past 85% of the limit
  where the browser reports memory, as the Memory row; then the hover "The game runs on fewer cores when it would pass
  8 GB".
- "Loads in about 1m10s": this game's last load that calculated its trips (`lastWait`, now with the workers it ended
  on), while the network is within a fifth of that load's size.
- "Trips are calculated in about 15s after an edit": this page's last edit solve, from the first edit its chain of
  restarts followed on from to its final (not the quiet refresh, not a load).
- Once the slider differs from the pick the pool started with: "Applies after a reload" and **Reload** (saves, then
  `?continue=1` back into the same game, `reloadIntoGame`, which "Play without intercity trips" now uses too). No
  button while viewing a shared network (a reload would leave it). Otherwise, when the pool runs fewer than the count
  shown (the 8 GB limit, or the tab's out of memory cap): "Running on 2 cores to fit in memory".

The start menu shows the row alone: no pool runs there, so the pick applies to the game opened.

## Bounds, default, and what a pick does

- **Most**: half the browser's threads (one per core on two-thread cores), at most 5 (one per period), 1 in a 32-bit
  browser; the row hides where that is 1 (2-3 threads, 32-bit). Ruled out: threads less 2 (5 on an 8-thread laptop
  would put solving workers on the hyperthreads that the page, the clock, the aux and the focus workers need, and the
  speed estimate cannot see that).
- **Auto** (`cores: null`, the default) is today's rule untouched: 3 on 12+ threads, 2 on 6-11, 1 below or on a phone
  or 4 GB device, at most 2 where memory is not reported, then stage A's 17.5% share cuts a big game. While a game
  runs on Auto the slider sits at the count it runs; Auto pressed after a pick shows what Auto would give this game
  (`workersFor` on its workers' need).
- **A pick** replaces the rule and drops stage A's share (as "Use more cores" did); the 8 GB limit (`capAhead`,
  `capPool`, `growTo`) still cuts it. Moving the slider clears the tab's out of memory cap (T-3072/T-3933), so the
  next load runs the pick; one that runs out again opens with half as before.
- **Migration**: stored "Use more cores" ticked became its count, half the threads less one (1 to 5), a pick;
  unticked is Auto. Both are always within the slider. She plays with it off: Auto, unchanged for her.
- **Latched**: the pool keeps the pick it started with (`DemandClient.picked`) until the next load. Before, the old
  checkbox was read live by `capPool`/`growTo`/`solvingRoom`, so ticking it mid-game lifted the share at once and a
  quiet `memCheck` could grow the pool and solve everything again. Saves' memory estimates (`estimateGB`) read the
  live setting, as they describe the next load.

## The speed model (as fitted 11-22 by coreslider; not refitted)

T(n) = T(k) x r(n) / r(k), k the workers the anchor ran on, r(m) = f + (1 - f) x the busiest of m workers' share of
`PERIOD_COST` (with `marketsOfWorkers`' market moves for a load, a worker's own periods for an edit). A wait under 10 s
(`PACE_SOLVE_MS`) is shown as it was: mostly set-up and downloads that more workers do not share.

- Busiest share, a load: 1, .561, .387, .346, .226 for 1-5 workers. r at f 0.4: 1, .737, .632, .608, .536.
- Loads f = 0.40, fitted in sample on T-3373 A's measured loads to the final: New York at 1/2/3 within -5..+8%,
  all-China within -9..+7% (notes/T-3373.md).
- Edits f = 0.17, from one point: North America 296 s on 1 worker, 188 on 2 (T-3868), a whole solve. Lie: since
  T-2188 a city edit ends at its local result, which nobody has timed at two worker counts.
- Under `?split=1` (T-3373 B) there are no estimates: B's per-worker memory and dealing want their own (coreslider's
  `Holding` version: git history of notes/archive/trees/coreslider-2026-11-24.patch, removed with this task).

Memory is the Memory row's fit (notes/T-3757.md, within about 20% of the browser's total). T-3373 A's two-count rows
suggest a further worker adds about its WASM plus 0.15 GB (New York +443/+502 MB against 536; all-China +1.43/+1.50 GB
against 1.45), where `LIVE_PER_EXTRA` (1.56 x WASM) says about 0.5 GB more a worker on all-China: the slider overstates
big games' extra workers, and agrees with what the Memory row will say after the reload.
