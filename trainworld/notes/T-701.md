# T-701: how drive times are set, and what real road speeds would cost

For Anita, 2026-10-19. No code changed for this.

## How it works now

**Inside a city's pack.** A drive is a length and a speed, set separately.
- Length: the straight line times a routed factor from OpenStreetMap roads (T-275: shortest road
  route round water and over bridges), times 1.074 so New York keeps its fitted level. A pack without
  routes uses 1.3.
- Speed: not taken from the roads at all. It comes from how dense the two ends are (people living and
  working per km2). Each end's first 2 km run on local streets and the next 8 km on main roads; any
  middle beyond that runs at 100 km/h. In open country local streets are 40 km/h and main roads 80.
  Both fall towards 15 km/h as density rises, half way at 5,000 per km2. These numbers were fitted
  to New York's census car commute times (T-090), so they already mean "a typical commute at peak".
- The five periods all use the same speeds. There is no time of day in the drive.
- The 1.33 is not a speed. It is the weight the drive gets in effective time; the real minutes
  are not changed by it.

**Between packs** (the long-distance market): 15 minutes plus 1.25 times the great circle distance at
85 km/h (docs/regional.md 5.1). Routed drives there are T-279, still open.

**"The speeds of the places at each end"** was the density rule above, written as one phrase. The
explorer's Drive hover now gives the average speed of the drive instead: 17 km/h from SoHo to Times
Square, 21 km/h from Brooklyn to Midtown, 37 km/h from Hempstead to Midtown.

## What road speeds would cost

**Road class speeds** (motorway, trunk, primary and so on, each with a speed):
- Data: the cached road files already keep each road's class, so no new download. The speed limit tag
  is not kept, and it is patchy in the US anyway, so classes only.
- Pack size: no change. The drive table would hold minutes per straight km instead of road length
  per straight km, in the same bytes (New York's drive table is 3.2 MB of its 17.8 MB).
- Build time: about the same. The route search runs on minutes instead of metres (New York 455 s).
  Every pack's routes stage would be rerun, 10 s to 8 min each.
- Solve time: no change worth measuring. The pair loop already computes a drive time from a few
  numbers; it would read one from the table instead.
- The real cost is calibration. Class speeds are free flow, and the density speeds stand in for
  congestion, so the density slowdown stays and the two have to be fitted together on New York
  again. Rail shares move in every city, and the two ceiling tests with them. I would guess a day of
  agent work for the pipeline and a second one for the refit.

**Time of day.** Each period already has its own parameter set, so one drive multiplier per period
costs nothing in pack size or solve time. What is missing is numbers: the current speeds are a
peak commute, so midday and night would be faster, and nothing in our sources gives by how much.
Something like peak 1.0, midday 0.8, evening 0.9, night 0.6 would be a guess. It would change which
mode wins by period (cars win more at night), so it needs the same refit.

I would not add either yet. The average speeds the explorer now shows (17 to 37 km/h) are plausible
for peak, and neither change fixes a visible problem. If you want one, time of day is the cheap
one.
