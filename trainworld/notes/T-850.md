Worker MB includes the empty worker's 1.2 MB; a solving worker holding the pack and the small line
reads the same (38.8 for New York). Chrome: aux and solving workers 7.9 (iwt), 11.5 (aad), 20.9
(osa), 37.4 (nyc) MB, as in Node.

- **Download**: 78 bytes a cell raw, about 40 gzipped (aad 47, nyc 38, iwt 31: from the gzipped
  `.bin` with and without the dropped cells).
- **Memory**: 256-282 bytes a cell in every worker that holds the pack (fitted from the three
  versions of each pack; T-044 found 250). A reached zone is held by every solving worker, so about
  1.3 KB a cell across a five-worker pool. The rest follows the 2 km zones, not cells: 1.4-1.6 KB a
  zone in the countryside (aad 4,109 zones about 6.5 MB of its 11.5), 2-3 KB in cities. A mean
  European countryside zone (6,200 cells, 1,220 zones) is about 1.7 MB of cells and 2.0 MB of zones
  a worker: **cells are under half of a countryside zone's memory**.
- **Opening** follows zones, not cells: aad with 37,798 cells opened in 799 ms and with 13,293 in
  766 ms; New York's 146k extra res-9 cells cost 0.6 s, about 4 microseconds a cell. Removing 4,500
  cells from a countryside zone saves 10-30 ms, under the run-to-run spread.
- **Solving** (T-325 bare, or with a line): about 2 microseconds a cell for three periods, so tens of
  ms on a countryside zone. A real network's solve follows subzone pairs and stations (T-044 section 1);
  cells this sparse sit in few subzones.
- The market packs (T-776 rows, `<id>.market.json`) are built from 600 m squares and 6 km zones, not
  cells; nothing here changes them except the site count (T-044: fewer sites, same totals).

(c) at 2,500 trip ends: 27% of cells, 5.5% of trip ends moved; at 10,000: 37%, 15.9%.

**Which cost matters at hundreds of zones.** Not time: opening and solving follow zones and stations.
Not the download: a countryside zone is 1-2 MB gzipped, fetched only when viewed. Memory is the one
that grows, because every reached zone sits in every solving worker: a network over 100 countryside
zones holds about 100 x 4 MB x 5 workers = 2 GB, of which (b) saves about 0.25 GB. The larger part is the
2 km zones (cells per zone are only 5.1 in European countryside; 17% of 2 km zones there hold only
cells under 5 people), and beyond that the world market a worker holds (T-801: New York's market
125 MB a worker against its pack's 37). A coarser zone grid for countryside packs (say 4 km) would
save more than any cell rule, but changes the gravity and the routes; it would need its own
measurement.

## 4. Recommendation

Don't drop by people: under 5 people takes away 3% of jobs and empties a sixth of the zones. If
anything is done, use **(b), a trip-end floor of 50, merged within the zone part**, as a fourth rule
in `pipeline/merge_cells.py` after T-510's lone-cell join: any cell under `FLOOR = 50` trip ends
joins the nearest cell of its zone part at or over the floor (a merged group's trip-end-weighted
centre, the busier cell's index, the same square and water tests); a zone part made only of such
cells keeps one cell. No people or jobs are lost, zone sums are unchanged, so the gravity and the
routes stay valid and it remains a last step. Gain: about 30% of cells, 18% of pack bytes, about
10% of a countryside zone's worker memory, no time. Moderate value; low risk. It fits best into the
next time every pack is remerged for another reason, rather than a remerge of its own.

Building it: about 60 lines in `merge_cells.py` (a KD-tree per zone part, as T-510's join), a
`--stats --floor` switch to report it first, then `merge_cells.py --all` and `market_zones.py` for
each pack (fewer 600 m sites, same totals; the Northeast's joint gravity rewrite as in
adding-a-city.md 7), then `pack_gate.py`. Timed here: the merge is about 0.5 s a pack and
`read_pack`'s check 1.4 s, so with market_zones about 5 s a pack, **about an hour on one core for
the 719 packs**. Run it only between Europe batches, since the batch runner writes the same packs,
and every pack needs its res-9 copy in `data/work/res9`. Checks: smoke, the real save's rail total
(T-044 moved it 0.04%), `t325-bare-check.mjs`.

## 5. T-850b: the floor rule, built and measured (2026-10-21)

Anita, 2026-10-21: "yeah i think we should probably merge more of these small cells". Built in
`pipeline/merge_cells.py` (`_floor`, `FLOOR`, `--floor N`), **off** (`FLOOR = None`) until the run in
5.3: wave 4 of T-788 calls `merge_cells` per pack and must not change rules mid-batch. With the floor
off the module gives the live packs' cells to the byte (checked on all eight sample packs).

**The rule as built**, after T-510's join: every unit (a cell with whatever merged into it) under
`FLOOR` trip ends a weekday joins the nearest unit of its zone part at or over the floor, by centre; in a
zone part with none, every unit joins the busiest. One change from section 4's design: the unit joined
**keeps its own index and place** rather than moving to the group's weighted centre. Moving it would
shift the busier unit too (more trip-end metres in all: a joiner of w at d from a unit of W adds w·d kept
in place against 2dwW/(W+w) at the centre, never less when W ≥ w), could stand it on water, and would
break read_pack's 900 m / 700 m bounds on a merged cell's distance from its index. So no water or square
test is needed and read_pack is unchanged. `--stats --floor N` reports any floor; writing with a floor
other than `FLOOR` is refused, since `nocar.py` and `everyday_rates.py` remerge by the default and
assert the result equals the live pack.

### 5.2 The floor: 50 trip ends a weekday

50 is the largest floor whose effect stays small by the brief's measure: the countryside packs move
0.5-1.6% of trip ends (median 0.8%; trj's 3% is a 579-cell pack) about 0.9 km, no station of 50+ entries
on any test line moves more than 1.7% (3.8% on vek's rural line, of 100 entries), and rail moves under
0.2% on every town line. At 75 the moved share is already 0.9-2.8% and stations of 60-80 entries start
moving 4-5%; at 100 the first stations pass 5% (crn, trj). It takes 24-34% of a countryside pack's cells
(-12% New York), 12-19% of its bytes and about 10% of its worker memory, as section 4 estimated.
