# T-2802: phone memory, the first line and the load peak

Picks up from notes/T-2794.md ("T-2802: the first line", "What is not done"). Measured 2026-11-11, agent phonemem.

## Method

`checks/t2802-memory.mjs` on a scratch production build: `vite build` with the app's config minus its pack copying,
served with the packs straight from `data/` (Range, no-store), emulated Pixel 7 (DPR 3, touch, Android UA), the
t2794 walk (menu, Start in New York, demand final, five camera jumps, a three-station line with the clock running
45 s). Every second it samples Chrome's private bytes by process type and, over a raw CDP connection, each JS
context's V8 heap (used) and ArrayBuffer backing stores (`Runtime.getHeapUsage`). With forced collections off
(`NOGC=1`) the figures are what a lazy collector leaves; without it, each context's WASM is read by `queryObjects`
on `WebAssembly.Memory` (a GC per context). Attribution runs used memory-infra dumps before and after collecting
every context, heap snapshots per context, and the sampling heap profiler started in each worker at its creation
(auto-attach with wait-for-debugger). `performance.measureUserAgentSpecificMemory` needs cross-origin isolation,
which the game does not have, so it was not used.

**A measurement artifact in the old check.** `t2794-mobile-memory.mjs` jumped the camera with
`page.evaluate(() => map.jumpTo(...))`, which returns the map, and Playwright serialises its whole object graph:
about 1 GB allocated on the page over the five jumps, 70-110 MB of it standing as page heap at the camera stage.
Part of the earlier "peak" was this. Fixed in t2794-mobile-memory, t1205-memory, t2840-lite-memory and the new check.
About 70 other evaluates in 50 checks do the same (T-3160); they only slow those checks.

The load peak (about 900-950) is the same plus what lazy collectors leave: at demand final, before collecting,
V8 heaps held 273 MB committed against 74 after (maplibre's worker 93 committed, 31 used, from parsing vector tiles;
the page 64), ArrayBuffers 137 against 76, Blink buffers 124 against 112. Each context that read `outlines.json`
(the page, the clock worker, and on a phone a fresh shade worker per cut) took the 11.4 MB text plus about 24 MB of
parsed arrays plus its Int32 copies.

## What changed

- **WASM grows 8 MB at a time on a small device** (`set_heap_step` in sim/src/heap.rs, exported by both modules;
  `heapStep()` in workers/deviceMemory.ts sends it in the clock's and the demand workers' init). The 32 MB step
  stays on desktop. -64 MB at the first line; the clock worker needs only 9.
- **Only the page fetches and parses `outlines.json`**, on every device. It packs the zones' rings once
  (`PackedOutlines`: one Int32Array of deltas, ring and zone offsets, 10 MB) and posts a copy to the clock worker
  (`{ kind: "outlines" }`, handled outside its queue; init no longer carries a URL) and to each shade worker.
  The small device's `CompactOutlines` is views on it. The desktop page no longer keeps the file's text for the shade
  worker. Takes the clock's parse out of the load and a fetch plus parse out of every phone shade cut.
  `packedRings` decodes to exactly the file's rings (all 3,982 zones checked), and the clock's place lookup from the
  copy names the same zones as the page's for 12 sample cities.

## What is left (T-3160)

- MapLibre's worker: up to about 90 MB of committed heap after the basemap's tiles parse, collected late.
- The Blink buffer partition's ~75 MB that is not script source: nothing in JS points at it. Next step would be
  Chrome's native heap profiler (`--memlog`) with symbols.
- The page's 41-58 MB of ArrayBuffers (snapshot retainers would name them).
- GPU memory in the renderer, about 110 (three WebGL contexts).
