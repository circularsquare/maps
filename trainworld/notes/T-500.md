# T-500: commutes matched by industry (design stage)

2026-10-16. Design and measurement only: no pack, pipeline, sim or doc file was changed.
Anita (2026-10-16): yes to industry matching, and asked whether the pull towards near jobs is
something we can tune.

Scripts in `notes/t500/`: `parse.py` (census 2020 tables 8 and 10 to long CSVs), `seg.py` (the
Tokyo prototype: gravity split by segment, fitted per segment, against tables 4 and 10),
`shares.py` (office shares and census rates by municipality), `nyseg.py` (New York on LODES OD's
sector groups). The Tokyo zone cache is `notes/t465_prep.py` rerun on the current res-9 pack (with
T-465's at-home fix). Logs: `../artifacts/trainworld/T-500-tokyo.log`, `T-500-nearend-nyc.log`.

## Short answer

- **Matching by industry barely moves Tokyo.** With the pack's curve for every segment, trips
  misplaced between municipalities go from 16.5% to 16.2-16.4% (4 groups), 16.0% at the limit of
  19 separate industries. West Tokyo to Chiyoda, Chuo and Minato stays at 14.6-14.8% (census
  17.1%); the centre still fills from within 5 km (54-55% of that ring's commuters go there,
  census 45%).
- **Why:** office workers do not live apart. Office industries are 37% of Musashino's commuting
  residents, 43% of Chiyoda's, 27% of Adachi's, 23% of Hachioji's. The centre's residents are as
  office-heavy as the west, so giving the centre office jobs to office workers keeps them there.
  From any one place office workers go to the three central wards about 2.3x as often as the rest
  (Musashino 35% against 16%, Adachi 36% against 15%), but the mix differs too little between
  places for that to shift much.
- **A curve per segment helps a little.** Fitted on census flows by industry (table 10), office
  workers take a flatter curve (d^-1.6 exp(-d/30), mean 13 km) and everyone else a steeper one
  (d^-2.4 exp(-d/300), 8 km). Two segments: misplaced 15.8%, own municipality 34.0% (census
  34.9%, kept, unlike a flatter single curve). The centre's numbers do not move (west to the three
  central wards 14.8%). Four segments are no better (15.9%, own 32.6%).
- **New York (LODES SI01-03):** 7.4% misplaced between counties with one gravity, 7.3% split by
  the three groups. All three groups fit the same curve (d^-1.2 exp(-d/60)), so the further gain to
  6.0% is a refit, not the split.
- **Anita's question, near-job pull:** yes, that is the curve, and it is fitted on each pack's
  census flows, not hand set. Tokyo's d^-2.2 exp(-d/80) is the best single curve on table 4.
  Flatter curves (T-465) and a softened near end (here) both lose the own-municipality share fast
  and never lift the west's share of the centre above 14.8%. Splitting by industry lets office
  workers have a flatter curve, which is real (office trips run about half again as long), but it does not touch
  the centre problem either.
- **Recommendation: do not build it now.** The gain (0.7 points in Tokyo) is about T-498's, at
  more cost and with no worldwide source for flows by office industry (LODES groups office with
  health and retail). Every test so far (curves, near end, travel time, industry) leaves the
  centre over-filled from within 5 km and short from 10-30 km by the same amount: that is a
  different kind of term (8). Anita decides.

## 1. Segments and sources

Census 2020 major industries (JSIC A-T) grouped:

| Group | Industries | Share of Tokyo's commuting jobs |
|---|---|---|
| Office | G information, J finance, K real estate, L professional, R other business services (agency staff, building services) | 25% |
| Retail and personal services | I wholesale and retail, M accommodation and food, N personal services and leisure | 26% |
| Industry and logistics | A-F (farming to utilities), H transport and post | 27% |
| Public, health, education | O education, P health and welfare, Q cooperatives, S government | 22% |

Two groups (office with S government, the rest) did as well as four. Unclassified (T) is left out
and the shares renormalised. Wholesale sits with retail though Tokyo's trading houses are offices;
the census's major level does not split them.

What each country publishes (both ends needed; flows by industry only for a curve per segment):

| Country | Residents by industry | Jobs by industry | Flows by industry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Japan | census 2020 table 8, every municipality (`e08-01.xlsx`, one national file) | table 8 by place of work, every municipality; or T-453's economic census mesh (no government) | table 10 (`e10-PP.xlsx` per prefecture of work): every municipality of residence to prefectures, designated cities and their wards, prefectural capitals and cities of 200k+ (92 workplaces in Tokyo's pack) |
| US | LODES RAC CNS01-20, block | LODES WAC CNS01-20, block | LODES OD SI01-03 only: goods; trade, transport, utilities; all other services. No office split |
| England | census 2021 TS060, OA | BRES by LSOA (T-453) | not found in the open 2021 origin-destination set (sex, age, travel method are); not checked further |
| Canada | census 2021, DA | 98-10-0492-01, CT of work (T-453) | not checked |
| Elsewhere | rarely at small area | rarely | none: today's single gravity |

So Japan has everything; the US has both ends but flows only in groups that do not separate office
work; England and Canada have both ends and would borrow curves.

## 2. Model

One doubly constrained gravity per segment s, residents of s to jobs of s, summed:

    T = sum_s  row_s(i) col_s(j) f_s(d_ij)

- Home end: a zone's commuters x its municipality's (or unit's) share of commuting residents in s
  (table 8: employed residents less those working at home). Work end: commuting jobs x the
  municipality's share by place of work. Each segment's homes are scaled to its jobs, as the pack
  scales the total now.
- f_s: fitted per segment on flows by industry where they exist (Japan's table 10, on the listed
  workplaces), else the pack's curve for every segment.
- Cost: S solves in the pipeline (Tokyo 4,792 zones: seconds each natively); the pack carries
  `zone_row` and `zone_col` per segment (8 bytes x zones x S) and each cell's commuters per
  segment at both ends (or one share byte per segment); opening a city rebuilds S zone tables
  (~2 ms each in WASM now). The bubbles and catchments sum the segments.

## 5. How much the near-job pull can be tuned (Anita's question)

The bias towards near jobs is the curve's steepness, d^-a exp(-d/L): a larger a keeps people
nearer. It is not set by hand: each pack with a flow table fits a and L on it (Tokyo on census
table 4, the US on LODES, London on the 2011 census, Toronto on its census), and packs without one
borrow a fitted curve. Tokyo's fit is d^-2.2 exp(-d/80), mean commute 9.7 km.

What loosening it does in Tokyo (T-465 and here):

| Curve | Misplaced | Own municipality | West to 3 central | Ring 0-5 km to 3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| d^-2.2 exp(-d/80) (fitted) | 16.5% | 33.9% | 14.6% | 55.4% |
| d^-1.6 exp(-d/40) | 20.8% | 22.8% | 14.2% | |
| d^-1.0 exp(-d/40) | 34.5% | 10.5% | 13.9% | |
| near end softened: (d+2 km)^-2.6 exp(-d/80) | 19.8% | 24.9% | 14.3% | 55.6% |
| near end softened: (d+4 km)^-2.2 exp(-d/80) | 27.8% | 14.2% | 14.1% | 47.7% |
| census | | 34.9% | 17.1% | 45.3% |

A looser curve pushes trips outward evenly in every direction: what it frees goes to the 30-60 km
ring (Chiba, Saitama, Ibaraki), the own-municipality share collapses, and the west's share of the
centre does not rise. Only a much softened near end empties the centre's own ring, and it costs
20 points of own-municipality share. So the fitted curve is already the right single setting.
A split by industry does allow a flatter curve where the data says so (office workers), and that
is the part of T-500 that measurably helps (15.8%, own share kept).

## 6. Recommendation

Not now. Two groups with fitted curves would take Tokyo from 16.5% to 15.8% misplaced and keep
the own-municipality share, about what T-498's travel time gives (15.9%), but:

- it needs flows by industry to fit the office curve, which only Japan has openly; the US groups
  do not separate office work and gave nothing beyond a refit; England and Canada would borrow
  Tokyo's ratio of curves, an untested assumption;
- it doubles the gravity in the pack and at runtime, and adds per-segment cell arrays;
- it does not touch what Anita saw (the west under-sent to the centre, the centre over-filled
  from within 5 km).

If Anita wants it anyway, the build is two groups (office with government; the rest), Japan
first.

**The rule for `docs/demand-worldwide.md` (Commute flows), only if built:**

> **Matched by industry (T-500).** Where a census gives employed residents and jobs by industry
> at municipality level or finer, commutes are two gravities: office industries (information,
> finance, real estate, professional and business services, government) to office jobs, the rest
> to the rest, each doubly constrained. Office workers' curve is flatter (Tokyo d^-1.6 exp(-d/30)
> against d^-2.4 exp(-d/300), fitted on census table 10). In Tokyo trips misplaced between
> municipalities fall from 16.5% to 15.8% and the own-municipality share holds. Why it may not
> carry over: few countries publish flows by industry, so most packs borrow Tokyo's pair of
> curves; where residents' industry mix varies little across a city (Tokyo: 23-44% office) the
> split changes little. Check: one flow table by industry per country where one exists; compare
> each group's mean trip with Tokyo's (office 13 km, others 8 km).

## 7. Build plan (if approved; after nothing else is rebuilding packs)

1. **Pipeline** (P2, one agent, ~4 h): `japan.py` group shares by municipality from table 8 at
   both ends (one national file, 31 MB) and the group curves from table 10 (47 files, ~130 MB,
   fetched per prefecture as table 4's are); `tier0.py` per-group commute arrays and a Furness solve
   per group; `us` packs from RAC and WAC CNS (office = CNS09-14 and CNS20) with Tokyo's curve
   ratio; England (TS060, BRES) and Canada the same, borrowed curves; every other pack one group.
2. **Sim** (P2, ~3 h): pack format with `zone_row` and `zone_col` per group, the gravity rebuild
   and the joint gravity (`joint_gravity.rs`, Japan and the US Northeast) per group, cells'
   commuters per group, tests; measure the opening cost.
3. **Rebuild** (P2, ~3 h agent, machine time a few hours): every Japan pack and zone, the nine US
   packs, London and Toronto; the Japan joint solve twice; merge, `read_pack.py`, `register.py
   --market --trips`. Others keep one group and need no rebuild.

## 8. What is left for the centre

Four tests now leave the same gap: no curve (T-465), no travel-time weighting (T-498), no industry
split (here) lifts the west's share of the three central wards above 14.8% (census 17.1%) or
brings the inner ring below 54% (45%). The census shows places at 12 and 20 km sending the same
share to the centre, which a distance-only pull cannot give. The usual remedy is a destination
term: big job centres draw from further than their size alone says (a competing-destinations or
intervening-opportunities model, T-185's nearest-job rank in the US work). Not tested; a possible
next task if Anita wants the centre fixed, not filed.
