# T-3113: Catalan (ca)

Language code `ca`, Central Catalan (the IEC standard): "estació", "cotxe", "ordinador"; no Valencian or Balearic forms
("estació" not "estacio", "cotxe" not "carro"). Intl's `ca` numbers and lists are right as they are (1.234,5; compact
"763 m", "1,2 M"; lists "a, b i c"; percent "85 %" with a no-break space). Nunito's Latin subset (U+0000-00FF) covers à è é í
ï ò ó ú ü ç and the middle dot U+00B7, and latin-ext covers Ŀ ŀ, so no font work. Catalan runs about as long as Spanish
(10 to 20 percent over English); narrow places use the short forms below, and a screenshot pass checks them. `OFFERED` gets
`ca` only after all units have three passes. Browser tags `ca`, `ca-ES`, `ca-AD` and so on already pick `ca` through
`langOfTag` once it is in `OFFERED`.

## Voice

- Full sentences address the player as `tu`, in the imperative with no pronoun ("Tria una ciutat", "Construeix una línia").
  Never `vostè` or `vós`. Why `tu`: Catalan software and games (Softcatalà's Firefox, Minecraft in Catalan, indie games)
  speak to the user as `tu`; `vostè` reads like a bank. Never mix.
- Buttons, toggles and menu items: the `tu` imperative, as Catalan software words them ("Desa", "Cancel·la", "Elimina la
  línia", "Mostra els colors de les línies", "Construeix"). Tabs, labels and column heads are plain nouns ("Línies",
  "Diners"). A hover that is a phrase has no verb of its own unless the English has one.
- Plain, short, a little cute, like the English. No "usuari", no "realitzar" or "efectuar" where "fer" will do, no
  "mitjançant", no "tanmateix" or other stiff connectives, no "proceder".
- Quotes: «text» with no spaces inside. No em dashes (house rule): a colon, a comma or a period.
- Periods only on full sentences; phrases, labels and hovers end bare.
- Apostrophe: always the typographic `’` (U+2019), never a straight `'`. This keeps ICU from reading `'{` as a quoted
  brace. The middle dot of "l·l" is U+00B7 ("Cancel·la", "col·lectiu", "intel·ligent"), never a full stop or "l.l".
- No spaces before `: ; ? !`. Percent: Intl writes "85 %" with U+00A0; a message with a literal percent sign does too.
- Sentence case. Days, months, languages and nationalities are lower case ("dilluns", "març", "català").
- Kept as written: km, m, mi, ft, km/h, mph, m2 and the money symbol.

## Plurals

`one` and `other`, as English: `{count, plural, one {# estació} other {# estacions}}`. Catalan `one` is exactly 1; a fraction
takes `other`. Intl may report `many` for round millions; it falls back to `other`, so write only `one` and `other`. Where the
noun does not change ("# min"), both branches read the same.

## Elision, contraction, gender and placeholders

A placeholder holds a name or a thing from the data, so the sentence cannot know its first letter or gender. Catalan elides
before a vowel or h ("de Osaka" -> "d’Osaka", "la" -> "l’", "el" -> "l’") and contracts (de+el = del, a+el = al, per+el =
pel), and articles and adjectives agree with the noun. So never put `de`, `el`, `la`, `l’`, `d’`, `del`, `al`, `pel`, `que`,
`ho` or an adjective that must agree directly in front of a placeholder. Reword so the value stands alone:
- A label and a colon: "Estació: {name}", "Ciutat: {city}", "Línia: {line}".
- A fixed noun, then the value: "línia {line}", "estació {name}", "tren {train}" ("a la línia {line}", "a l’estació
  {name}"): the noun carries the article, the name takes none.
- A preposition that never elides: `a`, `cap a`, `entre`, `en`, `per`, `amb`, `sense`, `sobre`, `sota`,
  `fins a`. NOTE: `des de` and `de` DO elide ("des d’Osaka", "d’1 min"), also before numbers. "a {city}", "entre {a} i {b}", "per {line}". Prefer a noun in front of the value to a bare `de`:
  "línia {line}" over "de {line}".
- Where English says "Remove {what}?" with an unknown thing: "Eliminar {what}?" has no article; do not write "el/la {what}".
- A message whose subject is a placeholder avoids a participle or adjective that agrees: "Línia {line}: construïda" is wrong if
  the noun is not fixed; use a label and colon, or an invariable word ("{x}: fet").
- A count before a noun ("{n} estacions"), never "un/una {n}".
- A placeholder holding a purpose, period, level or day-kind name (lower-cased by the code with `toLowerCase`, which
  Catalan words survive) is a bare noun or short noun phrase that reads after `per a` or `:`: "feina", "hora punta", "túnel",
  "dia laborable". It must make sense where the English places it with no article, so pick wording that works in a pattern
  like "desplaçaments: {purpose}" or "per a {purpose}" rather than "els {purpose}". Beware `per a` + vowel: "per a" never
  elides.
- Two nouns in a row where English stacks them ("{city} riders"): "passatgers de {city}" has the elision problem; write
  "passatgers: {city}" or "passatgers a {city}".
