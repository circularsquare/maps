# T-3253: how far option A reaches (T-3239)

Agent aimpact, 2026-11-12. Anita: "option A does have pretty wide-ranging impacts right? what are the
ones you'd expect to be noticeable? could we test them and maybe a couple other important cases and see
how much of an impact this change makes? does this severely mess anything up? ... would you say that
this is a conceptually safe move in that it makes things strictly simpler?"

## What A is (one paragraph)

The regional market (longer trips between places, and the commutes and everyday outings that cross a
zone line, which share its route search) picks routes with a fare weight. Before A that weight was a
local trip's (6.96 perceived minutes a dollar in a Japan start, 4.38 in a US start); A makes it 0.4 of
that, the weight the longer trips already use in mode choice (T-1521). The code change is two lines in
`app/src/game/income.ts`: `marketFareWeight` x 0.4, and `fareFactors` / 0.4 so every trip's mode choice
lands back on exactly the weight it had. Local (within-pack) trips never see it.

## Expected before measuring

A can only move a route choice where two routes charge different fares for the same trip. The fare is by
the whole trip's km on the curve plus a high-speed (or ferry, bus) multiplier on that line's km; the
transfer fare is 0 by default; ferries and buses are 1.0x by default. So:

1. **Games with no high-speed line (and default ferry/bus multipliers)**: almost nothing. Two routes
   differ in fare only by their km, and a route that is longer in km is usually slower too, so the
   lighter fare weight rarely flips anything. Expect well under 1% anywhere.
2. **Games with a high-speed line at 1.5x beside a slower line**: longer trips and commutes between
   zones move from the ordinary line onto the high-speed line wherever the high-speed line saves more
   than 0.18 perceived minutes a km (at 0.4: 0.5 x 0.88 x 0.4) instead of 0.44. A player would see:
   (a) the high-speed line's riders up, its longer-trip riders most; (b) the parallel ordinary line's
   riders down by the same riders; (c) far fewer "ride the Shinkansen, change to a local line for the
   last leg" routes; (d) fare income up a little, since riders who move pay the 1.5x on those km.
3. **Rail's total**: mode choice weighs money as before, but now prices the route the search picked.
   For longer trips the new route is cheaper in their own eyes, so rail trips rise a little where (2)
   happens. For commutes between zones the route is picked at 0.4 but priced at their local weight in
   mode choice, so a commuter moved onto the high-speed line sees a dearer rail trip, and their rail
   share can fall a few percent (T-3239 saw Utsunomiya's 568 a day go to 538).
4. **Local trips, the ceiling tests, the fare panel's estimate**: unchanged. The T-636 ceiling tests
   (A and B) are the local layers with a made-up train and no market at all, so A cannot touch them.
   The fare sensitivity figure (`fareSense.market`) is per curve dollar and is converted with the same
   weight it is measured in, so it should match to float noise.

## Method

Two worktrees from master 81e128db (`.tw-trees/aimpact-before`, `.tw-trees/aimpact-after`), sharing
the main tree's WASM and packs; "before" has `income.ts` as at d5911dec (the units fix only), so the
two builds differ by option A alone. Each save is imported into a `?new=1&opened=all&paused=1` page in
each build side by side (2 cores, 2 demand workers each), waited to the weekday's final, and read:
rail trips (`railTripsPerDay`), every line's riders (all, longer trips, work), fare income a weekday
(`incomeByDay[0]`), the local layers' split per city, the market over corridors with rail, and the
trip explorer's routes (`marketTrip`, both ways) for the 10 corridors with most rail riders and the 6
with most commuters between zones by rail. Scripts in the agent's scratchpad (`measure.mjs`,
`pair.mjs`, `euro.mjs`, `jpnhsr.mjs`, `compare.py`). A page is read only once its final has stayed
the newest for 30 s: a save with added lines solves twice on load, and a first pass read the first
solve's final as if it were the last (it showed the Tokyo high-speed game moving +176%, all of it the
difference between two solves, not A; rerun).

**No real save has a high-speed line.** The real saves (New York, Tokyo, Osaka, Sendai, jpn7) have
no line marked high speed: jpn7 carries the Tokaido and Sanyo Shinkansen, but as ordinary lines (1.0x
fare, ordinary trains' top speed), because `sim/src/bin/realsave.rs`'s graph path sets every line
`fast: false` (only its traced path, line 415, reads the `hsr` flag). So two games were made:

- **Paris - Lyon** (`euro.mjs`): a sandbox game, Paris start. A high-speed line Paris - Lyon, no stop
  between (79 minutes), every 30 minutes at high (40, 60), and an ordinary line Paris - Dijon - Lyon
  at its own 160 km/h (168 minutes), every 30 minutes too; 1.5x.
- **Tokyo with Shinkansen** (`jpnhsr.mjs`): the real Tokyo save, switched to sandbox, with a Tohoku
  line Tokyo - Omiya - Utsunomiya - Koriyama - Sendai (every 15, 20, 30 minutes) and a Joetsu line
  Tokyo - Omiya - Takasaki (20, 30, 60) in deep tunnel at 320 km/h, stations about 110 m east of the
  real ones; 1.5x. The real conventional lines (Tohoku main line, Takasaki line, Ueno-Tokyo line,
  Shonan-Shinjuku line) run beside them at their real stops and speeds.

Only the Paris - Lyon game moved more than ~5% anywhere: its high-speed line went from no riders to
3,994 a day, the ordinary line lost 58% of its riders, Paris - Lyon rail trips rose 3,487 -> 4,018
(+15%, from the plane mostly: plane 611 -> 532), and fare income rose 38% (the high-speed line's
riders pay 1.5x on its km). Paris - Dijon and Dijon - Lyon (ordinary line only) did not move. No page
had a console error in either build.

**Why Paris - Lyon moves and Tokyo does not.** At 1.5x the high-speed line costs 0.44 perceived
minutes a km more at a local trip's weight, 0.18 at A's. Paris - Lyon's ordinary line is a player's
straight 160 km/h line: the high-speed line saves about 0.22 minutes a km, between the two, so the
riders flipped whole. Tokyo's real conventional lines stop often (Utsunomiya 1 h 40 by rapid), so the
Shinkansen saves around 0.5-1 minute a km and won before A as well: the Tohoku line carried 31,902
longer trips before and 31,951 after. What A did change there: the longer trips on the Ueno-Tokyo line
fell 7,851 -> 5,724 and on the Shonan-Shinjuku line 12,063 -> 11,208 (riders who left the
Shinkansen for a conventional line into Tokyo now stay on), and the Joetsu line's fare income rose 5%
(Tohoku 1.2%). That is the player's report fixed, and a player would see it only in those lines'
panels.

**Commutes between zones** (the part A gets wrong on purpose): no measurable change in any save,
-0.1% at most; on the Tokyo game the Tohoku and Joetsu lines' work riders rose 6,500 -> 6,588 and
8,156 -> 8,186. They move only where a high-speed line is a close call against a fast ordinary line
at commuting distance, as T-3239's Utsunomiya case with a 160 km/h line beside it (0% -> 34% of
them on the high-speed line, commuters by rail 568 -> 538, -5%).

**Fare sensitivity** (`fareSense.market`, the fare panel's estimate of what a dearer fare does to the
market) reads 1.2-1.6% smaller in every save with no route change: the market's step is a fixed number
of perceived minutes, which is now 2.5x more dollars, so the slope is measured over a wider step. Not
visible; where routes move (Paris - Lyon) it moves with them.

**T-636's ceiling tests** are the local layers with a made-up train and no market, so A cannot touch
them; not rerun.

## The concept

What A is: a longer trip decides train or car (or plane) weighing a dollar at 0.4 of what a local trip
does (T-1521, US DOT's values of time); before A it then chose its train route as if a dollar weighed
the full local amount. A uses the 0.4 it already had in the second place too. No new number, no new
factor: one multiplication in `marketFareWeight` and the matching division in `fareFactors`.

Simpler or just more consistent: more consistent, not simpler. The code is the same size, and it trades
one mismatch for another. Before, longer trips priced money one way for "train or car" and another for
"which train"; after, they price it one way for both. But commutes and outings that cross a zone line
share the market's one route search, so they now pick routes at 0.4 while their own mode choice stays
at the local weight: the mismatch moved from the longer trips (most of the market's riders on any
high-speed line: Tokyo game 31,951 longer trips against 6,588 commuters on the Tohoku line) to them.
Two route searches would remove it at about twice the market's search time.

What would argue for undoing it: (1) if commuters riding a high-speed line between zones looked too many
(none here; T-3239's Utsunomiya case is the place it shows); (2) A makes a high-speed line's
multiplier bite less for long trips, so a player can charge more on it before riders leave, which was
already true of the longer trips' mode choice; (3) the default 1.5x now sits just under the long trips'
break-even against a 160 km/h line (1.53x), so a corridor flips whole on small changes (T-3239's
"cliff"): that was already so before, at 1.21x. None of these is a reason by the numbers here.
