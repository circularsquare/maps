# T-1737: why far lines' trains an hour move when a line is built, 2026-10-29

Question (from notes/T-1734.md): on all of Japan the local solve on top of a landing touched all 44
zones, said to be because 12 lines built in the meantime moved trains an hour by over 5% on lines
across Japan. Is that a real knock-on, and should the touch rule (T-1733, `sameView` in
`workers/demand.worker.ts`) ignore it?

## Answer

Trains an hour on far lines barely move. What touches far zones is the **service span** (`span`, the
share of riders who find a train within their wait tolerance, per stop and period), not trains an
hour. Three separate things, only the first real:

1. **Local knock-on on shared track (real).** A new line that crosses existing track at grade takes
   capacity there (flat crossing, track-and-service.md 6.2), and `Capacity::fill` cuts every line on
   that track by the same proportion. Tokaido (Tamachi-Shinagawa) 11.5 to 4.6 an hour, Odakyu 1.8 to
   0.9, the Hibiya line 9.5 to 3.8, in the Tokyo area, within 35 km. A line's trains an hour is
   one number for its whole length, so a long line cut at its Tokyo end is cut everywhere: the
   Tohoku Shinkansen (line 408, Shin-Aomori to Tokyo, 595 km, 23 stops) went 1.92 to 1.33 an hour at
   the high level when the 26th line was built across it near Ueno (the Tohoku zones of T-1734's v7,
   12 touched). Real, and a player who builds across a Shinkansen would expect it to slow down. But the
   zones along it only have one stop each on it, so their own trips cannot ride it between two
   of their stations; the market carries those trips, and the market is not re-solved locally.
2. **Span steps (noise).** `stop_phi` rounds to hundredths and a line's day has a whole number of
   trains, so (my reading, the departures were not dumped) a few seconds on a run time, or a held
   line's trains an hour creeping 4%, add or drop one train in the evening or night, and the span of
   every stop of that line in that period jumps by exactly 0.05 (0.92 to 0.97, 0.37 to 0.32). `viewDiff` allows `0.05 * max(1, |p|)` = 0.05
   on span, and a float32 step of 0.05 is 0.05000001, which is over. So every zone on the line is
   touched, with trains an hour inside 5%. Seen on all of Japan: the Tohoku Shinkansen's Tokyo end
   (aom, iwt, akt, fks, tcg, gnm, nig) and a held Osaka line (osa, shg).
3. **A held line's creep (noise, one off).** Lines on a full resource are never iterated by the
   settle (`settle_counts` counts a line as active only when it is short and not held), but every
   edit that makes a level active steps all lines at that level. A held line's rate is
   `want x scale` from progressive filling, so it moves a little each pass until the shares
   converge: Osaka Loop (line 636, medium level) 3.249 to 3.116 (4.1%) over the first three edits
   after a load, anywhere in Japan (a Sapporo edit does the same), then still. track-and-service.md
   says a held level "counts as settled, so an edit elsewhere leaves it to the bit"; it does not
   until it has converged once. That is 3 lines in this save, none past 5%.

## Rule change proposed (T-1764)

In `viewDiff`, the touch rule:

1. **Span: one step does not count.** Compare `span` with an absolute tolerance of 0.06, so a
   single 0.05 step (one train more or less in a day) is the same view. A real service change moves
   trains an hour or several steps and still touches. In this run it leaves tyo, yns and szo
   touched, the other 9 not (12 to 3), and the far span jumps go away.
2. **Lines with one stop of their own in the zone do not count for trains an hour or run times.**
   A view's line with at most 3 stops (`lineN`: one inside the reach plus the pads either side)
   cannot carry the zone's own trips between two of its stations, so a change in its service only
   matters to the market. That drops the Tohoku zones when the Shinkansen itself is cut (edit 25),
   which rule 1 does not. Needs a check on the pads' transfers (a rider alighting at a pad to change
   to a line whose span starts there) before it ships, and a measurement on Japan: not done here.
3. Not proposed: ignoring the Tokyo-area tph knock-ons (1), they are real and near the edit.

Nothing in the settle needs to change for this task. The creep (3) could be closed by iterating held
lines to convergence at load (`reload`), so the first edit does not move them; it moves 3 lines by
under 5% here, so it is not worth a task on its own.

## The failing check (checks/t263-attractions.mjs)

The check's view, not a game bug. `acrossLine` puts its middle station at the centre it is given, and
the check gave Ueno (139.7764, 35.7188), 17 m from the museum's mark (139.7766, 35.7188). The new
station is named after the museum and drawn with a station circle; a hover gives way to the selected
station and the station circles (`hoverAbove`, T-189 and T-1448), so `twViews.hover` returned null
there and the screenshot showed the station's tag, with the museum only in the resting list (T-732).
That is the intended order: a station on a mark wins the hover. Fixed in the check by building the
line 0.003 degrees (330 m) north; the museum's hover reads "Tokyo National Museum, About 6k visits
per weekday, 2% by train" and the whole check passes (7 of 7, 4 minutes on Vite 6126).
