**Recommendation in short.** A full-map view, swapped with the map by one button (key M), in the
look of the game's own line diagrams carried to the whole network: each line one continuous ribbon
with rounded bends, lines side by side with the map's 1 px gap, every stretch exactly 0, 45 or 90
degrees (where geography wants another angle, an orthogonal leg then a diagonal leg), stations as
big white pills with an ink outline across the lines that stop there. Laid out in a worker of its
own, rebuilt in the background a few seconds after edits settle while the last diagram stays up.
Clicks select on it as on the map. Later: subtle boxes round each city once a diagram spans
several, then water drawn schematically. Seven stages, T-2159 to T-2163, T-2168, T-2169.
Best pictures: `artifacts/trainworld/t2146/r2-tyo-centre.png` and `r2-nyc-centre.png`.

## 1. What the game has to draw from

- **The track model** (Rust, `sim/src/track`, read through `TrackApi`): nodes (x, y, level, platform
  length, heading; a node with a platform is a station), edges (two nodes, tracks, length), lines
  (name, symbol, colour, `line_stops` as station nodes, `line_path` as `edge << 1 | direction`,
  trains an hour, `line_group` and its name). `passed_stations` lists the stations a line runs
  through without stopping, but only those on its own path, never one on a track beside it.
- **Complexes** (`game/complexes.ts` `buildComplexes`): stations within 100 m, or with platforms
  within 50 m, or linked by the player, are one complex. The main thread keeps them current
  (`complexView.ts`, `ComplexBuilder`); the clock worker has its own copy. A complex is the
  diagram's station: one dot however many platforms it has.
- **What the renderer derives, in the clock worker** (`map/network.ts`): `lineBands.ts` orders the
  lines on each edge left to right by the pairwise crossing-minimisation rule (follow two lines to
  where they part, the one turning left goes left) and `corridors.ts` samples every edge every 25 m
  with the edges running beside it within 20 m. That second table is exactly what the diagram
  needs: **on the real New York save, lines that share a trunk are mostly on their own edges about
  4 m apart, and expresses on their own tracks** (lineBands' comments), so lines sharing edges
  says little. An express is put on the local's diagram edges by the stations whose platforms lie
  beside its track.
- **Diagrams already in the game**, each of one line or one group, not to scale: the line panel's
  stops strip (north up, T-1894), related lines (`relatedLayout.ts`, Rust `line_related`, T-1168)
  and the group view (`groupLayout.ts`, T-1791, T-1809). Their look (bullets at line ends, pills
  for a complex, ticks for stops) is the look to keep.
- **Groups** (`game/lineGroups.ts`): a Lines tab grouping the service never reads; useful to the
  diagram only as a hint for merging strokes when zoomed far out.

The graph the mockups built (scratch script `graph.py`, below): nodes are complexes; each line
becomes the chain of complexes it runs through (its stops, the stations on its path, and stations
whose platform is within 35 m of its track and parallel to it, within about 25 degrees); an edge
joins consecutive complexes of a chain and carries its lines.

| Save | Stations | Complexes on lines | Lines | Track edges | Diagram edges |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New York (T-007-real-nyc.save) | 1,756 | 855 | 109 | 1,647 | 968 |
| Tokyo (data/packs/real/tyo.save) | 1,908 | 1,447 | 133 | 2,134 | 1,654 |
| All of Japan (data/work/realnet/jpn_real.save) | 10,320 | 9,106 | 709 | 9,611 | 9,561 |

grows about linearly with the number of complexes.

## 2. References

Layout research:
- Nöllenburg and Wolff, the mixed-integer program (IEEE TVCG 2011),
  https://i11www.iti.kit.edu/extra/publications/nw-dlhqm-10.pdf . The best-looking automatic maps;
  hours on a city (about 10 hours with labels, quoted secondhand in
  https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/files/pub/smw19-paper-5.pdf ). Not for a game.
- Wang and Chi, "Focus+Context Metro Maps" (IEEE TVCG 2011),
  https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/bitstream/11536/14562/1/000296241900086.pdf : octilinearity, even
  station spacing and closeness to geography as energy terms solved by least squares at
  interactive speed. Option B below is this family. Their follow-up "Interactive Metro Map
  Editing" (Wang and Peng, 2016), https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/handle/11536/129484 , re-solves
  after edits.
- Stott, Rodgers et al., multicriteria hill climbing (IEEE TVCG 2011),
  https://www.cs.kent.ac.uk/pubs/2011/2963 ; Hong et al., spring layout (2006),
  https://www.cs.kent.ac.uk/pubs/2005/2355/content.pdf .
- Bast, Brosi and Storandt, "Metro Maps on Octilinear Grid Graphs" (EuroVis 2020),
  https://ad-publications.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/EuroVis%20octi-maps.pdf , and "Metro Maps on
  Flexible Base Grids" (SSTD 2021),
  https://ad-publications.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/SSTD_Metro%20Maps%20on%20Flexible%20Base%20Grids.pdf :
  stations on grid nodes, edges routed as shortest paths on the grid, a fast greedy version
  reported at well under a second for city networks. Option C below.
- LOOM line ordering, "Efficient Generation of Geographically Accurate Transit Maps" (2018/2019),
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.02226 : crossing-minimal line order on shared edges; New York 0.7 s
  with an ILP solver. Base problem: Benkert, Nöllenburg, Uno and Wolff (2006),
  https://i11www.iti.kit.edu/extra/publications/bnuw-miecw-07.pdf . The game already has a
  pairwise heuristic for this in `lineBands.ts`.
- Surveys: Wu, Niedermann, Takahashi, Roberts and Nöllenburg (CGF 2020),
  https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/files/pub/metromapsurvey-2020.pdf ; the 2022 follow-up on running
  time and interactivity, https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07301 , which says globally optimal methods
  are not for instant layouts; Nöllenburg's 2014 survey,
  https://i11www.iti.kit.edu/extra/publications/n-asamm-14.pdf .
- Newer directions: shape-guided mixed layouts (Batik et al. 2022), k-linear maps (any set of
  directions, TU Wien, https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/k-linear-metro-maps/ ). Readability: Max
  Roberts' studies found a curvy Paris map planned faster than the octilinear one and preference
  not following speed (https://repository.essex.ac.uk/26174/1/Roberts_et_al_IDJ_2016.pdf ), so
  octilinear is a look players recognise, not a proven best. Cameron Booth's Transit Maps blog
  (https://transitmap.net/about ) for design rules: legibility first.

Open code:
- ad-freiburg/loom, https://github.com/ad-freiburg/loom (C++; tools topo, loom, octi,
  transitmap): **GPL-3.0**, so nothing can be copied into the game; demos at
  https://loom.cs.uni-freiburg.de/ and https://octi.cs.uni-freiburg.de . Useful to compare our

- **A. The tidied map** (Mini Metro look): real directions with evened lengths, every hop a
  straight run and a 45 degree run. Cheapest, but every hop bends, so lines read as staircases
  (`nyc-A-centre.png`). Kept only as the start for B.
- **B. Octilinear least squares** (Wang and Chi's family): round 1 drew B's positions with straight
  hops. 82% of edges came within 3 degrees of octilinear and the rest were visibly off, which is
  what Anita saw. B is still the layout, but round 2 adds a routing stage after it that makes every
  stretch exact.
- **C. The grid** (LOOM's octi): stations on an octilinear grid, hops as shortest grid paths.
  Exact by construction. It is more work to write (we can't take LOOM's GPL code), it needs a huge
  grid at country scale, and one new hop can reroute its neighbours. Round 2's routing gets exact
  angles from B without it. C stays a fallback if B plus routing can't be made to look right at
  the busiest interchanges.

### The round 2 pipeline (recommended)

1. **Graph** (clock worker): complexes, and each line's chain of complexes: its stops, stations on
   its path, and stations whose platforms lie beside its track, within 35 m and parallel. So an
   express on its own tracks shares the local's edges: on the real save the 2 and 3 pass 79 St and
   86 St on the 1's edges. Built from `corridors.ts`' beside table, which is already kept between
   builds.
2. **Positions** (diagram worker): first A, real directions with each hop's length
   `1000 m x (length / 1000 m)^0.35`, so the centre opens up and the suburbs close in. Then 30
   rounds of B. Each edge takes the nearest free octilinear direction at both ends, with terms for
   length, straightness through two-edge stations, closeness to the last round and spacing. All of
   them are linear, so a round is one sparse solve.
   **Room for bundles**: a stroke pitch is the median hop over 12. Every edge is at least 1.6 times
   its two ends' bundle half-widths, and unconnected stations are kept apart by their half-widths
   plus half a hop. Without this, a ten-line trunk was wider than its hops (round 2's first try).
3. **Segments**: chains between key nodes (a branch, a line ending, the set of lines changing). A
   segment whose stations neither run straight nor fit one bend is split at its widest station,
   recursively.
4. **Snap**: one small solve on the segment ends alone. Every segment within 14 degrees of an
   octilinear direction is made exact, as a heavy term against staying near B.
5. **Route**: each segment is drawn straight when within 0.5 degrees. Otherwise it gets an
   orthogonal leg then a diagonal leg, or the reverse, whichever passes nearer its stations.
   Stations are placed along it by their share of its length. New York: 370 pieces, 316 straight
   and 54 with one bend.
6. **Order**: on each piece, one stroke per colour, ordered left to right by `lineBands.ts`' rule.
   For two lines, follow both ahead to where they part, and the one turning left goes left; if
   they never part ahead, look back.
7. **Draw**: each line is one path. Its slot's offset is kept along a piece; where two pieces meet
   at a corner, the offset lines meet at their crossing point. A change of slot on a straight run
   is a 45 degree jog. Every corner is rounded with radius `6 W + bundle half width ± offset`, so
   the bends of a bundle are concentric. Lines are W wide, each with a casing in the background
   colour, so neighbours sit a gap apart as on the map.
8. **Stations**: a line's stop points at a complex within 1.6 pitches of each other form one
   white pill, a capsule along their main axis with the ink outline, as in the line panel.
   Separate pills of one complex are joined by a passage (a narrower white bar with the outline).
9. **Names**: eight places round the pill at two distances, kept off lines, pills and other names,
   interchanges first, then line ends, then the rest. The name is cut at the first "/" ("Times
   Sq-42 St").

What round 2 shows. Tokyo round Shinjuku reads as a transit map: long straight runs, 45 degree
turns, round bends, pills, names placed. New York's outer lines and the Brooklyn and Queens
trunks are clean, but these faults are left for the stages:
- **Interchanges where many lines turn** (Times Sq to Penn, 14 St, 59 St) come out as fat pills,
  because the lines' stop points scatter round the corner. Splitting such pills by bundle made
  clouds and was dropped. The fix is in the layout: route a big interchange as a straight crossing
  of its bundles (a key node whose pieces pass straight through it), so its pill lies across
  straight lines. T-2161 owns this.
- **Names**: about 70 of the stations round Times Sq are named. A few sit at the second distance
  where it's unclear which pill they belong to, and repeats like "23 St" look alike. T-2162 should
  keep names on one side along a straight run and prefer the side away from other stations.
- **Wide bundles** where every commuter branch has its own colour (New Jersey Transit into Penn,
  the Long Island Rail Road's main line). That is the save's colours; far out, merging by colour
  and group (level of detail) helps.

### Rebuilding

The diagram is rebuilt in the background a few seconds after edits settle, and the last one stays
on screen until the new one is ready ("doesnt have to be super fast to rebuild", Anita). Each
rebuild starts from the previous positions, with the closeness-to-last-round term against them,
so an edit doesn't reshuffle the map; a new complex starts at its neighbours' positions plus its
real offset. No local re-solve. On load, the first build shows "Calculating diagram" in the busy
tag.

### Multi-city diagrams: city boxes

Once a diagram spans several cities (zones), each city's stations sit in a subtle box, like fare
zones on some maps: a rounded rectangle round its complexes' diagram positions, padded by about a
hop. It gets a faint tint of the ink at about 4% and a 1 px outline at about 20%, with the city's
name in a corner in the panels' small size (the zone's shown name, `zoneNames.ts`; which zone a
station is in, `cityWithin`). Lines between cities run between the boxes. Layout: after B, each
city's nodes are moved as one block so boxes don't overlap (blocks pushed apart along the line
between their centres), then one more B round with the block positions as the new start.
Intercity hops are already short, from the evened lengths. Islands and unlinked networks are
packed the same way, shrinking the empty sea between them. All of Japan as one diagram (section
3, round 1 `jpn-B-centre.png` for Kansai) needs this and level of detail.

### Water, schematically (reach goal)

Rivers drawn as constant-width bands at 0, 45 and 90 degrees, and coasts as octilinear outlines,
as on the New York subway map:
- **Source**: coasts from the coarse sea outlines the game already loads (`packs/outlines.json`,
  `coarseOutlines.ts` `coarseWater`). Rivers and lakes from the water polygons the map's tiles
  already carry, or the track model's water mask (`cost.rs`). The stage picks one, and it must be
  a source every country has.
- **Into diagram space**: a piecewise-affine warp over a triangulation of the complexes' real
  positions to their diagram positions. Water outside the network's hull is clipped.
- **Shape**: a river's centre line is simplified and routed with the same orthogonal-then-diagonal
  router as the lines, then drawn as one light blue ribbon of fixed width under everything. A coast
  is simplified and each edge routed the same way, as a filled shape.
- **Check**: every line must cross a river in the diagram as often as it does on the ground. A
  mismatch (the warp folding a bank across a line) shifts the river to the side its stations are
  on, and failing that it is left out rather than drawn wrong.

## 4. Where it lives, and the plan

Decided here:
- **A full-map view, not a tab or a panel.** One button at the map's upper left, above the view
  rows, and key **M** (free). The map canvas gives way to the diagram in the same place; the dock,
  top bar, inspector and selection stay. A tab or the inspector is far too small for a network,
  and a floating window breaks the dock's no-floating-windows rule (ui-panels.md).
- **The camera carries over**: going to the diagram centres it on the selection, or on the complex
  nearest the map's centre; going back does the same the other way. Pan and zoom use the map's
  gestures.
- **Clicks are the map's clicks**: a station selects its complex, a line selects the line, and
  hovers are the map's station and line tags. A selected line draws full and the rest faint, as
  the group view's hover does.
- **View only.** Build keys (1 to 4, N, S, U) switch back to the map first. Editing on the diagram
  would be a Mini Metro style game of its own; not proposed.
- **Look**: the game's line diagrams carried to the network. The map's land colour as background
  in both themes, line colours, the panel's white pills with ink outlines, rounded continuous
  ribbons, names in Nunito / Zen Maru Gothic at the panels' sizes, bullets at line ends as in the
  group view.
- **Drawing**: a 2D canvas with its own culling, which also gives the picture to save. The WebGL
  renderer could draw it later (the start menu's logo already feeds it buffers without the sim,
  `map/logoNetwork.ts`), which would bring trains onto the diagram.

Stages (TASKS.md, Interface, under T-2146):
1. **T-2159 The diagram graph** (half a day). As section 3 step 1, in the clock worker beside
   `lineBands`, posted with network states. Done when a Node check on the real New York save,
   tyo.save and jpn_real.save gives counts near section 1's table, the 2 and 3 share the 1's edges
   from 96 St to Times Sq, and New York builds in under 50 ms.
2. **T-2160 The layout worker** (a day). Steps 2 to 6: A then B with room for bundles, segments
   and splits, the snap, the routing, the line order. A rebuild starts from the last positions.
   Done when a Node check on New York and Tokyo has every drawn stretch within 0.5 degrees of
   0/45/90, no two stations closer than their half-widths plus half a hop, New York in under 5 s
   and Tokyo in under 8 s, and one added station moving the median complex less than a tenth of a
   hop.
3. **T-2161 The view and its drawing** (a day). The button and M, the canvas with pan and zoom,
   steps 7 and 8 (ribbons, jogs, concentric bends, pills and passages), big interchanges as
   straight crossings, level of detail by zoom, both themes, the background rebuild with the last
   diagram kept up. Done when screenshots round Times Sq and Shinjuku look at least as good as
   `r2-nyc-centre.png` and `r2-tyo-centre.png`, Times Sq, 14 St and 59 St have no pill wider than
   two pitches across, and New York pans at 60 fps.
4. **T-2162 Names** (half a day to a day). Step 9 with `labelLayout.ts`' `BoxGrid`, names on one
   side along a straight run, more names as the zoom grows. Done when no name round Times Sq
   overlaps a line, pill or name, every interchange there is named, and no name sits nearer
   another station's pill than its own.
5. **T-2163 Clicks, the map and a picture** (half a day). Clicks and hovers, the faded rest, the
   camera carried between map and diagram, build keys back to the map, "Save picture" (a PNG of
   the whole diagram). Done when smoke passes, a click on Times Sq opens its inspector, M returns
   to the map centred there, and New York's picture opens with its names readable.
6. **T-2168 City boxes** (half a day to a day). Section 3, multi-city: boxes, blocks pushed apart,
   islands packed. Done when T-125-northeast.save draws each city in its own box with no two boxes
   overlapping, and all of Japan draws Okinawa within a box's width of Kyushu.
7. **T-2169 Water** (reach goal, a day or two). Section 3, water. Done when New York shows the
   Hudson, the East River and the harbour as fixed-width octilinear bands, every line crosses
   them as often as on the ground, and Tokyo Bay's coast is octilinear.
