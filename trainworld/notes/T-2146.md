# T-2146: a diagram of the network (design)

Anita, 2026-11-03: "diagram of network is something we should do yeah. though seems pretty hard yes."
Why players want it: research/player-voices.md section 14 (Subway Builder's route map, Mini Metro's
look, a picture to keep).

**Recommendation in short.** A full-map view, swapped with the map by one button (key M): the
player's network drawn octilinear (0, 45, 90 degrees), stations at their complexes, lines side by
side, laid out by a least-squares solve in a worker of its own, started from geography so the
diagram still looks like the city, and kept stable across edits by re-solving only near the change.
Clicks select on it exactly as on the map. Mockups from the real saves show the idea holds up for
New York, Tokyo and, at regional zoom, all of Japan from one solve. Five stages, T-2159 to T-2163.

## 1. What the game has to draw from

- **The track model** (Rust, `sim/src/track`, read through `TrackApi`): nodes (x, y, level, platform
  length, heading; a node with a platform is a station), edges (two nodes, tracks, length), lines
  (name, symbol, colour, `line_stops` as station nodes, `line_path` as `edge << 1 | direction`,
  trains an hour, `line_group` and its name). `passed_stations` lists the stations a line runs
  through without stopping, but only those on its own path, never one on a track beside it.
- **Complexes** (`game/complexes.ts` `buildComplexes`): stations within 100 m, or with platforms
  within 50 m, or linked by the player, are one complex. The main thread keeps them current
  (`complexView.ts`, `ComplexBuilder`); the clock worker has its own copy. A complex is the
  diagram's station: one dot however many platforms it has.
- **What the renderer derives, in the clock worker** (`map/network.ts`): `lineBands.ts` orders the
  lines on each edge left to right by the pairwise crossing-minimisation rule (follow two lines to
  where they part, the one turning left goes left) and `corridors.ts` samples every edge every 25 m
  with the edges running beside it within 20 m. That second table is exactly what the diagram
  needs: **on the real New York save, lines that share a trunk are mostly on their own edges about
  4 m apart, and expresses on their own tracks** (lineBands' comments), so lines sharing edges
  says little. An express is put on the local's diagram edges by the stations whose platforms lie
  beside its track.
- **Diagrams already in the game**, each of one line or one group, not to scale: the line panel's
  stops strip (north up, T-1894), related lines (`relatedLayout.ts`, Rust `line_related`, T-1168)
  and the group view (`groupLayout.ts`, T-1791, T-1809). Their look (bullets at line ends, pills
  for a complex, ticks for stops) is the look to keep.
- **Groups** (`game/lineGroups.ts`): a Lines tab grouping the service never reads; useful to the
  diagram only as a hint for merging strokes when zoomed far out.

The graph the mockups built (scratch script `graph.py`, below): nodes are complexes; each line
becomes the chain of complexes it runs through (its stops, the stations on its path, and stations
whose platform is within 35 m of its track and parallel to it, within about 25 degrees); an edge
joins consecutive complexes of a chain and carries its lines.

| Save | Stations | Complexes on lines | Lines | Track edges | Diagram edges |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New York (T-007-real-nyc.save) | 1,756 | 855 | 109 | 1,647 | 968 |
| Tokyo (data/packs/real/tyo.save) | 1,908 | 1,447 | 133 | 2,134 | 1,654 |
| All of Japan (data/work/realnet/jpn_real.save) | 10,320 | 9,106 | 709 | 9,611 | 9,561 |

All of China would be larger again, but a player's network that size is rare; every cost below
grows about linearly with the number of complexes.

## 2. References

Layout research:
- Nöllenburg and Wolff, the mixed-integer program (IEEE TVCG 2011),
  https://i11www.iti.kit.edu/extra/publications/nw-dlhqm-10.pdf . The best-looking automatic maps;
  hours on a city (about 10 hours with labels, quoted secondhand in
  https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/files/pub/smw19-paper-5.pdf ). Not for a game.
- Wang and Chi, "Focus+Context Metro Maps" (IEEE TVCG 2011),
  https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/bitstream/11536/14562/1/000296241900086.pdf : octilinearity, even
  station spacing and closeness to geography as energy terms solved by least squares at
  interactive speed. Option B below is this family. Their follow-up "Interactive Metro Map
  Editing" (Wang and Peng, 2016), https://ir.lib.nycu.edu.tw/handle/11536/129484 , re-solves
  after edits.
- Stott, Rodgers et al., multicriteria hill climbing (IEEE TVCG 2011),
  https://www.cs.kent.ac.uk/pubs/2011/2963 ; Hong et al., spring layout (2006),
  https://www.cs.kent.ac.uk/pubs/2005/2355/content.pdf .
- Bast, Brosi and Storandt, "Metro Maps on Octilinear Grid Graphs" (EuroVis 2020),
  https://ad-publications.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/EuroVis%20octi-maps.pdf , and "Metro Maps on
  Flexible Base Grids" (SSTD 2021),
  https://ad-publications.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/SSTD_Metro%20Maps%20on%20Flexible%20Base%20Grids.pdf :
  stations on grid nodes, edges routed as shortest paths on the grid, a fast greedy version
  reported at well under a second for city networks. Option C below.
- LOOM line ordering, "Efficient Generation of Geographically Accurate Transit Maps" (2018/2019),
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.02226 : crossing-minimal line order on shared edges; New York 0.7 s
  with an ILP solver. Base problem: Benkert, Nöllenburg, Uno and Wolff (2006),
  https://i11www.iti.kit.edu/extra/publications/bnuw-miecw-07.pdf . The game already has a
  pairwise heuristic for this in `lineBands.ts`.
- Surveys: Wu, Niedermann, Takahashi, Roberts and Nöllenburg (CGF 2020),
  https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/files/pub/metromapsurvey-2020.pdf ; the 2022 follow-up on running
  time and interactivity, https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07301 , which says globally optimal methods
  are not for instant layouts; Nöllenburg's 2014 survey,
  https://i11www.iti.kit.edu/extra/publications/n-asamm-14.pdf .
- Newer directions: shape-guided mixed layouts (Batik et al. 2022), k-linear maps (any set of
  directions, TU Wien, https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/k-linear-metro-maps/ ). Readability: Max
  Roberts' studies found a curvy Paris map planned faster than the octilinear one and preference
  not following speed (https://repository.essex.ac.uk/26174/1/Roberts_et_al_IDJ_2016.pdf ), so
  octilinear is a look players recognise, not a proven best. Cameron Booth's Transit Maps blog
  (https://transitmap.net/about ) for design rules: legibility first.

Open code:

## 3. Options

Mockups: `C:\Users\anita\projects\maps\artifacts\trainworld\t2146\`, each save drawn whole
(`<save>-<option>.png`, SVG beside) and round one station (`-centre.png`: Times Sq for New York,
Shinjuku for Tokyo, Osaka for Japan). `-geo` is the real track, `-topo` the diagram graph at real
positions with straight hops. Strokes are one per colour on an edge in a naive hue order, names
placed greedily, so crossings and overlapping names are the mockup's, not the method's.

Times are the Python prototype's (numpy, scipy's sparse least squares, loops in Python), one core:

| | New York | Tokyo | All of Japan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graph (chains, beside test) | 1.7 s | 1.8 s | 4.2 s |
| A: lengths evened | 0.5 s | 0.7 s | 9 s |
| B: octilinear, 30 rounds from A | 3.4 s | 8.3 s | 39 s |
| Edges within 3 degrees of octilinear after B | 82% | 83% | 83% |

Most of the time is Python building the system each round; a typed-array port in a worker should
be several times faster (to be measured in T-2160; its done-when sets the bar).

### A. The tidied map (Mini Metro look)

What the player sees: the network at roughly its real shape, the dense centre spread out and the
long suburban hops shortened, every hop drawn as a straight run and a 45 degree run.
Layout: one least-squares solve keeping each hop's real direction with its length
`1000 m x (length / 1000 m)^0.35` (a 250 m hop draws 615 m, a 20 km hop 2.9 km), pairs closer
than half the typical hop pushed apart. Cheapest and most stable: an edit moves almost nothing.
Look: `nyc-A-centre.png`. Every hop bends, so long lines read as staircases and parallel lines
jog together; it reads as busier than the map it replaces. Good as a first frame for B, weak on
its own.

### B. The octilinear diagram (recommended)

What the player sees: a transit map. Straight runs through stations, turns at 45 degrees, the
centre given room, the overall shape still the city's (`nyc-B-centre.png`, `tyo-B-centre.png`).
Layout: A's solve, then rounds of least squares where each edge takes the nearest free octilinear
direction at both its ends (two edges at a station never share one), with terms for length, a
straight line through each two-edge station, closeness to the last round (so it never jumps) and
spacing. All terms are linear, so each round is one sparse solve; 30 rounds from A.
- **Where**: a diagram worker of its own, started the first time the player opens the diagram.
  The graph (complexes, chains, beside stations) is built in the clock worker next to `lineBands`
  from `corridors.ts`' beside table and posted with each network state, as `NetPatch`-style
  changes. The clock worker never runs the layout: a solve of seconds there would stop the trains.
- **On edit**: keep every existing node's position; a new complex starts from its neighbours'
  diagram positions plus its real offset; then a few rounds with only nodes within about three
  hops of the change free and the rest held. The diagram stays recognisable across edits (Mini
  Metro never moves a station; a diagram that reshuffles on every edit would be worse than none).
  A full re-solve only on load, and saved positions are a later nicety, not needed.
- **Country scale**: one solve works (the Kansai crop `jpn-B-centre.png` is from the all-Japan
  solve and reads as a regional map), but the whole country on one screen is a skeleton
  (`jpn-B.png`): thin lines, no names, islands such as Okinawa far off in a corner. So the view
  needs level of detail: far out, strokes merged per colour (and per group), stations of two edges
  hidden, names for the busiest interchanges only; separate pieces of network (islands, unlinked
  cities) laid out alone and packed by geography with the empty sea between them shrunk.
Look at New York: Lexington, Eighth Avenue and the Brooklyn lines come out as recognisable
straight runs; the faults in the mockup are line order (needless crossings round Grand Central),
very wide bands where every commuter branch has its own colour (New Jersey Transit into Penn), a
few edges left a few degrees off, and names over lines. Each has a stage below.

### C. The grid diagram (LOOM's octi)

Stations snapped to an octilinear grid, each hop routed as a shortest path on the grid. Cleanest
result (exact angles, no overlapping edges, room for names can be priced in), reported at under a
second for cities with the greedy version; more work to write (we cannot take LOOM's GPL code), a
grid of millions of cells at country scale unless cut into regions, and less stable on edit (one
new hop can reroute its neighbours). No mockup made. Kept as a later upgrade: B's positions are a
good start for it, so it could replace B's last stage without changing anything around it.

## 4. Recommendation and plan

Build B. Where it lives and how it behaves, decided here:
- **A full-map view, not a tab or a panel.** One button at the map's upper left, above the view
  rows, with its key **M**: the map canvas gives way to the diagram in the same place, the dock,
  top bar, inspector and selection unchanged. A tab or the inspector is far too small for a
  network, and a floating window breaks the dock's no-floating-windows rule (ui-panels.md).
- **The camera carries over**: going to the diagram centres it on the diagram position of the
  complex nearest the map's centre, or on the selection if there is one; going back does the same
  the other way. The diagram has its own pan and zoom (same gestures as the map).
- **Clicks are the map's clicks**: a station selects its complex (the station inspector opens), a
  line selects the line, hovers are the map's station and line tags. A selected line draws full
  and the rest faint, as the group view's hover does. Locate in the inspector moves the diagram's
  camera while it is up.
- **View only.** Build keys (1 to 4, N, S, U) switch back to the map first. Editing on the diagram
  would be a Mini Metro style game of its own; not proposed.
- **Look**: the map's land colour as background in both themes, line colours, white stations with
  the ink outline at interchanges, names in Nunito / Zen Maru Gothic at the panels' sizes, bullets
  at line ends like the group view.
- **Drawing**: a 2D canvas with its own culling, like the station circles' canvas; it exports to a
  picture directly. The WebGL network renderer could draw it later (the start menu's logo already
  feeds it buffers without the sim, `map/logoNetwork.ts`), which would bring trains onto the
  diagram; not needed for the first version.

Stages (filed in TASKS.md, Interface, under T-2146):
1. **T-2159 The diagram graph** (half a day). Complexes, each line's chain with beside stations
   from `corridors.ts`, edges with their lines, built in the clock worker beside `lineBands` and
   posted with network states. Done when a Node check on the real New York save, tyo.save and
   jpn_real.save prints counts close to the table in section 1, the 2 and 3 share the 1's edges
   between 96 St and Times Sq, and the build takes under 50 ms on New York.
2. **T-2160 The layout worker** (a day). Option B with A as its start, separate pieces laid out
   alone and packed, a last pass that snaps near-octilinear edges exact, the local re-solve on
   edit. Done when a Node check on the three saves has at least 95% of edges within 1 degree of
   octilinear and no two stations closer than half a hop, New York solves in under 1 s and all of
   Japan in under 10 s, and adding a station to one line moves no complex more than three hops
   away.
3. **T-2161 The diagram view** (a day). The button and M, the canvas with pan and zoom, strokes
   in `lineBands`' pairwise order, one stroke per colour on an edge, stations, level of detail
   by zoom, both themes, "Calculating diagram" in the busy tag while the first solve runs. Done
   when screenshots of New York and Tokyo look at least as good as `nyc-B-centre.png` and
   `tyo-B-centre.png` with fewer crossings, and panning New York keeps 60 fps.
4. **T-2162 Names** (half a day to a day). Station names placed beside their dots in one of eight
   places, kept off lines and each other (`labelLayout.ts` `BoxGrid`), interchanges and termini
   first, more as the zoom grows; line bullets at the ends. Done when a New York screenshot at the
   Times Sq crop has no name over another or over a line, and every interchange there is named.
5. **T-2163 Clicks, the map and a picture** (half a day). Station and line clicks and hovers, the
   faded rest, the camera carried between map and diagram, build keys back to the map, and a
   "Save picture" button that saves the whole diagram as a PNG. Done when the smoke check passes,
   a click on Times Sq in the diagram opens its inspector, M returns to the map centred there, and
   the saved picture of New York opens with its names readable.

Later, unfiled: trains moving on the diagram, the Traffic display's widths on it, option C as the
last stage, SVG export, positions kept in the save.

## Player-facing words proposed

- The button's hover: "Show network diagram (M)", and while the diagram is up "Show map (M)".
- The busy tag during the first solve: "Calculating diagram".
- The button in the diagram view: "Save picture".
- The saved file's name: "<game name> diagram.png".
- Station and line hovers reuse the map's tags; nothing new.
