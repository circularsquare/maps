# T-264 superholidays: design (2026-10-26)

A design for Anita's yes or no. No code yet. It narrows T-263's stage 4 sketch (notes/T-263.md,
"Stage 4: long holidays and going home") to what she asked for on 2026-10-24: Chinese New Year,
Golden Week, Obon, Chuseok and Thanksgiving as long-distance days with a direction, trips to
touristy places, and telling the player what is coming. Builds on T-262 (Saturday and Sunday,
holidays run as Sundays), T-1157 (China's official schedule) and T-263 stage 2 (attractions).

## What a superholiday is

A run of days off (weekend days, holidays, and a single working day caught between them, as
T-263's sketch has it) that holds a **going-home holiday**. Found in `pipeline/holidays_json.py`
from the calendar the game already uses (the `holidays` package for 250 countries, China's
official table from T-1157), so any country the package covers gets them with no work. Two
small tables do the rest:

- **Names** that make a run a superholiday wherever they occur: Spring Festival, Lunar or Chinese
  New Year, Korean New Year, Tet, Chuseok, Thanksgiving (not Japan's Labor Thanksgiving Day),
  Christmas Day, Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha (and the bayram names), Diwali, Nowruz, Songkran,
  Hari Raya; China's National Day and Japan's Constitution Memorial Day (the two Golden Weeks).
- **Dates** for the few the package lacks: Obon in Japan, August 13-16.

Long weekends and every other holiday stay as they are: a Sunday.

Each superholiday has three parts, all whole local days:

| Part | Which days | Thanksgiving 2026 | Spring Festival 2026 (official) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Going out** | the day before the run; the two days before a run of 5 days or more | Wed 25 | Fri 13, Sat 14 (a make-up working day) |
| **Away** | the run's days, less the last (the last two for 5 days or more) | Thu 26 to Sat 28 | Sun 15 to Sat 21 |
| **Coming back** | the run's last day; its last two for 5 days or more | Sun 29 | Sun 22, Mon 23 |

## What it does to demand

Factors on what the weekday solve already has, one direction rule and nothing else. All new
numbers, so all for Anita's yes:

| Day | Local trips | Attractions | Longer trips between cities | Commutes between zones |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Going out | a weekday's | a weekday's | **x 2**, weighted outward | a weekday's |
| Away | **0.7 x** a Sunday's | **1.5 x** a Sunday's | a Sunday's | a Sunday's |
| Coming back | a Sunday's | a Sunday's | **x 2**, weighted homeward | a Sunday's |

- **Trips home, and where they go.** The longer trips keep their destinations (the market's
  gravity) and gain a direction: on a going-out day a pair's trips from place i to place j are
  weighted `1 + 0.4 x (P_i - P_j) / (P_i + P_j)`, P a place's people, and j to i the opposite,
  so a big city against a small town runs about 70/30 outward and two big cities 50/50. A
  coming-back day takes the minus sign. The pair's total is unchanged by the weight; the x 2 is
  the extra travel. No source per country: the rule is population alone. Anchors for the x 2,
  from memory and to check when built: China's rail over the 40 days of Spring Festival travel
  averages about 1.2 times normal with peaks near 2; JR's Obon and New Year peaks are 1.3-1.6
  times normal on the Shinkansen; US air on the Sunday after Thanksgiving about 1.25 (road more).
- **The inverse of migration** would send Shenzhen's trips to Hunan and Guangxi rather than to its
  neighbours. No world source exists; a census table of where people were born against where they
  live, by province or state, does in many countries (China's 2020 census by place of household
  registration, the US ACS state of birth, India's migration tables). That is "a method any census
  can feed", but a table per country. Not proposed now (question 2).
- **Attractions** run 1.5 times a Sunday while people are away: locals out and visitors in town
  (half of attraction visits already start where visitors stay). Trips from far away to touristy
  places (Kyoto, Orlando) are question 3.
- **Commutes cut**: the away days are days off, so commuting is already a Sunday's (0.24 of a
  weekday); the 0.7 on local trips is people gone home. Their trips where they visit are not
  modelled, so home towns are not busier locally.
- The extra riders do not see their own crowding in mode choice (the weekend's approximation,
  T-260). The boarding account fills trains to crush and leaves the rest on the platform, so a
  line short of trains has full platforms on a rush day. That is the game: capacity for the rush.

## How it runs

Three new kinds of day beside weekday, Saturday and Sunday (`DAY_KINDS` already carries kinds
through the factors, views, boarding account and money).

- **Away is a scaled Sunday**: its own row of factors in `days.rs`, worked out from the weekday
  solve like Saturday and Sunday. No solve.
- **Going out and coming back cannot be scaled**: the market adds up both directions of a pair
  when it loads the lines, so a direction needs the trips weighted pair by pair. The market's
  last loading pass, which already visits every pair it solves, also fills two more columns per
  period (loads, boardings and alightings weighted out, and weighted back), on the same routes
  and modes. No new route search and no new mode choice. Queries that read directed zone pairs or
  a station's far zones apply the weight when asked, since both ends' places are known.

Cost, rough, from T-262's and T-1206's measurements:

| | Real save (New York) | China network (169 zones) |
|---|---|---|
| Solve | nothing to see: New York's longer trips are about 1,700 rail legs a day | two multiply-adds per pair in the last pass: under 1 s on the 29-31 s edit solve (est.) |
| Memory a solving worker | under 1 MB | a few MB on 2.4-2.6 GB (est.) |
| Main thread, each publish | about 11 ms a kind (T-262), so 33 ms | about the same per kind, more with more lines |
| Clock worker, first hour after a result | three kinds took 0.6-0.7 s (1.3 s busy, T-1230); six would double it | larger by the number of lines; not measured |

To keep that last row down, the new kinds' views and accounts are made only while a
superholiday is within the next 7 days in a country the player has built in, and only for that
country's lines. The rest of the year costs nothing.

## What the player sees

- **A heads-up in the top bar**, from 7 days ahead, only for countries the player has built in:
  "Golden Week in 3 days", then "Golden Week" while it lasts. Its hover gives the days: "Out Wed 28
  and Thu 29. Back Sun 3 and Mon 4." Clicking it shows demand for the next going-out day (the
  Day choice in Settings gains Out and Back while one is near), so the player sees which lines
  will fill before the rush comes.
- **The sky**: going-out and coming-back days in a paler pink than the holiday, with a small arrow
  (out or back) at their start; the holiday's hover gains "Long trips out of big cities" or "Long
  trips home".
- **Crowding**: the trains and platforms that already show crowds; nothing new.
- **Money**: a tick under each holiday day in the Money history, its hover the name and country,
  so a fares spike on a rush day has its reason (T-263 sketched it; not built yet).

## Questions for Anita

1. **The numbers.** Longer trips x 2 on going-out and coming-back days, up to 70/30 by direction;
   local trips 0.7 of a Sunday while away; attractions 1.5 times a Sunday. Long weekends stay
   Sundays. *My pick: yes as written; one strength for every superholiday keeps it simple.*
2. **Where home trips go.** Population alone (big places send, small places receive), or each
   country's census table of birthplace against residence where one exists? *My pick: population
   alone now. A census table can come later for China, if Spring Festival looks wrong in play.*
3. **Far trips to touristy places.** On going-out days, weight trips into places with many
   attraction visits per resident (from attractions.json) up to twice as heavy, so Kyoto and
   Orlando pull in visitors from far away. One more number, and the same pass's columns. *My pick:
   yes, but after T-1373, as T-1374.*
4. **The heads-up.** The top-bar line from 7 days ahead, as above, or a calendar icon with the next
   21 days' holidays (T-263's sketch)? *My pick: the top-bar line. It shows only when something is
   coming, and clicking it shows the rush's loads.*

## Stage 1 as built (T-1372, 2026-10-26)

Calls beside the design (DECISIONS.md, T-1372): runs of at least 3 days; work and airport keep a
Sunday's on away days; the calendar icon's click shows the away day until T-1373 gives going-out
days their own demand; the away day's account only within 8 days, its fares and points within 21.

`checks/t1372-superholidays.mjs` on the real save: weekday, Saturday, Sunday and average figures equal
8800's (the last commit) to the bit; the away day is Sunday times 0.7 (errands, leisure, school,
medical), 1.5 (attractions), 1.0 (work, airport, longer), rail 7.67M against Sunday's 10.33M; the
calendar changes kind only on 6,800 country-days away and 7 Obon days over 7 years, 236 countries
with superholidays. The day views took 273 ms with the away day against 198 without (sums worker,
median of five; 198 against 332 in a second run); the solve is unchanged code (final solve 135 s here against 92 s on 8800, then 116 s against 108 s, with other
agents' builds running).

## Stage 2 as built (T-1373, 2026-10-26)

Calls beside the design (DECISIONS.md, T-1373): the x 2 is against a weekday on both rush days
(coming back keeps a Sunday's timing); Anita's boost towards rural areas, small towns and smaller
cities is in the lean's sizes (people times the square root of people per 6 km zone), so a pair's
trips in all are kept and the lean stays at most 70/30; one assignment column (longer trips times
their lean, out = own + it, back = own - it) rather than two, skipped past 256 MB of S x S table
(T-1503); the rush views made only within 21 days, the account within 8; the Day choice's three
superholiday days on a row of their own as icons; the calendar's click shows the next going-out day.

Every other purpose is a weekday's going out and a Sunday's coming back, to 1e-6. New York's places
are all big and dense, so its lines barely lean; its longer trips are about 2,000 rail trips of
12 million, which is why the day barely moves there. The China network moves as little: its
longer trips ride rail for about 3,300 line boardings a weekday against 39 million rail trips, on
ten high-speed lines between 24 metros. That is the market's figure before this task, worth a
look of its own (T-1504). The save's own date is within 21 days of Golden Week, so its rush views
were made at load (demand final 335 s after the import).
