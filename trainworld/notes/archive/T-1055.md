# T-1055 and T-1056: rail trips that ride no train, and rail trips that walk further than walking

## T-1055: a boarding rides at least one stop

Anita (2026-10-23): "i noticed a trip where a passenger travels to a transfer station, transfers,
and then gets off without having taken any train. they pay a fare."

**The case.** The demand graph has, at each stop of each line and direction, a route node. A
station boards a route node (its wait, plus the 2 minute change cost the first boarding gets
back), a route node rides to the next stop, and a route node leaves the train: alighting at its
own station, or walking to another station within 300 m or in the same complex (T-161). The
strategy search runs back from the destination and offered a route node to its station at the
route node's own cost, which is the cheapest of riding on and leaving. Where leaving was cheaper
(the destination is a short walk from that platform), the station boarded the route node and its
riders, following the route node's best edge, walked off at once. So a trip could walk to station
A, "board" a line there, walk to station B of A's complex (or within 300 m) and end there: rail
with no train. The same hole is T-200 (seen in T-195: Jersey City to Wall St, riders boarding the E
at World Trade Center to walk to Fulton St), and a third form: board a line only to alight at the
same station and board another.

**Why it got riders.** It does not have to beat walking: walking takes its share first by minutes,
and rail and the car split the rest by how many times cheaper each is. A wait of a few minutes plus
a walk of a few hundred metres is cheap next to a short drive with parking, so for trips between
cells near two stations of one complex it took a share from the car, and paid the base fare
(`fare_min`, added once to every rail option in the mode choice) like any rail trip.

**The fix, in the search** (`kernel::Search::run`). Each route node keeps its cost by its ride edge
(`Search::ride`, known once its next stop is settled). A station is offered a route node at that
cost, not at the route node's own: where riders on board do best to stay on, as before; where they
do better to leave, the boarding waits its turn in the queue at the ride's cost (the T-552 queue
slot, now also used for this). Everything that follows a strategy sends a boarding along that ride
when the route node's own best edge leaves the train (`Graph::rn_next`): the kernel's assignment
(`DestWalk`), the everyday lanes (`LaneWalk`), the fare's expected km (`Search::trip_km`) and the
trip explorer (`trip::walk`). Riders already on the train still leave it wherever leaving is best.
No filter afterwards, so nobody is left without a route: a pair whose only "rail" was the walk now
has no rail option and its trips go to walking and the car.

Test: `kernel::tests::a_boarding_rides_at_least_one_stop` (a walk 200 m away and a 1 km complex
member; from the station the trip rides two lines, riders already on board from upstream still walk
over, loads and the trip explorer agree).

**The real save** (T-007, a weekday, in the game on my Vite):

|---|---|---|
| Rail trips a day | 11,654,110 | 11,645,298 (-8,812, -0.08%) |
| New York: train / walk / drive of all trips | 16.83 / 24.86 / 58.31% | 16.82 / 24.87 / 58.31% |
| Boardings a day (all lines) | 20,395,584 | 20,426,726 (+31,142) |

Boardings rise while trips fall: riders who boarded to walk off now ride a stop and change.

The stations that lose most are where a line's stop sits within 300 m of another station: New
York's Flushing-Main St 76.4k to 71.4k entries, Grand Central 39.6k to 37.5k, Trenton 4.1k to 2.7k;

## T-1056: rail trips that walk further than walking there (measured, not built)

Anita: "maybe should disallow trips where like the total walking distance if you take the train is
longer than the total walking distance if you walk directly."

**Method.** A scratch copy of the crate with the T-1055 fix (`T-1056/measure.patch` (script removed 2026-10-24, in git history), `t1056.rs`,
`t1056_ceiling.rs`; scripts `run_real.py`, `run_ceil.py`, `sum.py`, paths set for the scratchpad
they ran in). Walking by train is the straight metres of the walk to the boarding station (the
subzone's mean, as access uses; nothing for a drive to the station), the expected walks between
stations and platforms along the strategy (a new station-to-station table, worked out from each
destination's strategy like the fare's km), and the walk from the last station. Walking there is
the straight line between the two ends (commutes: subzone centres; everyday: the pair's distance).
`T1056=1` counts, per trip, the share of its rail riders on station pairs that walk further;
`T1056=2` takes those station pairs out of the choice (the logsum and the loads), so trips with no
pair left get no rail and go to walking and the car.

**How many walk further today** (share of rail trips, one way):

|---|---|---|---|
| New York | 1.2% | 4.6% | under 0.5 km 90%, 0.5-1 km 48%, 1-2 km 12%, 2-5 km 2%, over 5 km 0% |
| Tokyo | 3.6% | 4.8% | under 0.5 km 100%, 0.5-1 km 71%, 1-2 km 18%, 2-5 km 2.5%, over 5 km 0% |

**What the rule would move:**

| Tokyo | 15.717M to 15.149M (-3.6%) | 31.65 to 30.74% | 29.32 to 29.87% |

Station entries that change most: New York Woodhaven Blvd 43.1k to 40.5k, East 105 St 17.4k to
15.0k, 169 St 19.5k to 17.1k, Parkchester 30.1k to 27.8k; Tokyo Kasai 35.3k to 32.4k, Chigasaki
17.8k to 15.3k, Nishi-Kawaguchi 38.4k to 36.1k, Shiki 20.2k to 17.9k. Lines lose 1-5% (New York's
Q, 2, 7 about 2%; Tokyo's Tobu Tojo line 5%). These are short hops between neighbouring stops of
one line: suburban and outer-borough stations, not the hubs.

**Anita's ceilings** (rail of rail + car; the T-938 harness with the rule: no train where the two
3 minute walks are longer than walking the straight line, under 460 m; only everyday trips are
that short):

| | New York | London | Paris | Tokyo | Houston |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (target 85) | 92.9 to 91.9 | 93.0 to 91.8 | 94.0 to 92.7 | 94.1 to 93.2 | 87.9 to 86.4 |
| B (target 50) | 82.4 to 81.5 | 83.1 to 82.1 | 84.9 to 83.8 | 85.2 to 84.3 | 72.6 to 71.4 |

**Reading.** Small and in the direction Anita meant: 3-4% of rail trips, nearly all under 2 km, go
to walking and the car; both ceilings drop about a point and still pass (Houston's A is the
closest, 86.4).

**Why it was not built here.** The rule cannot live in the route search as T-1055 does: the search
goes station to station and does not know where a trip starts or ends, and the walks to and from
the stations are the larger part of the comparison. It belongs in the choice of station pairs for
each trip, which the mode choice does in four places (the commutes' eight-lane loop in
`kernel::mode_choice`, which never forms station pairs one by one but multiplies a row per origin
into each destination's egress weights; `trip::pair_choice`, which must match it to the bit; the
everyday pass; the trip explorer), plus a new station-to-station table of walking metres from the
search. The commutes' loop is the costly part: a pair the rule can touch needs its station pairs
one by one and its loads put straight onto them. A per-origin row of the most walking to each
egress station would find those pairs cheaply, since only pairs under about 5 km can be touched.
The scratch version doubled to tripled solve time (New York 36 s to 107 s), with no attention to
speed. Filed as T-1059.
