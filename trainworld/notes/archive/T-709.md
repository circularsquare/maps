# T-709: why the explorer's route carries so few of the train users

2026-10-19. Measured with the real New York save and a six-station Toronto test line on
Yonge Street (the busiest period of each trip; `DemandApi.trip` answers, grouped by lines and end
station).

How the shown route is picked: the model spreads a trip's train users over station pairs near each
end (an access logit), then at each station over the useful trains by frequency. `routes()` lists
every path of that tree with at least 0.5% of the users, biggest first, at most 8. The panel shows
the first. It is therefore already the route most users take, and it was also the biggest group when
routes with the same lines to the same station name are added (checked on all 7 NYC trips).

| trip | station pairs | busiest pair | main route | 8 listed routes together | in routes under 0.5% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SoHo to Times Sq | 36 | 12% | 5.5% | 27% | 73% |
| Union Sq to 23 St | 33 | 28% | 12.7% | 53% | 47% |
| Brooklyn (Nostrand) to Rockefeller | 60 | 15% | 4.9% | 21% | 79% |
| Hempstead to Rockefeller | 48 | 20% | 6.9% | 20% | 80% |
| Queens (75 Av) to Wall St | 54 | 19% | 7.9% | 37% | 63% |
| Bronx (Jackson Av) to 49 St | 60 | 22% | 8.3% | 38% | 62% |
| Harlem to Union Sq | 48 | 25% | 5.4% | 31% | 69% |
| Toronto Bloor to Union (one line) | 13 | 67% | 67% | 99.5% | 0.5% |
| Toronto Eglinton to Queen | 12 | 71% | 71% | 99% | 1% |

So the thin spread is the model's: New York has 30 to 60 near-equal station pairs and several lines
at each. It is also why "choice of routes" is large (the logsum over that many options). Nothing in
how demand assigns riders was touched.

Panel change: a muted "Smaller routes" row under "Other routes" with what the main route and the
listed rows leave (72% in the Brooklyn trip), and a hover on it and on the share sentence saying how
many pairs of stations the users spread over and what the busiest pair takes. Showing more routes
would not help (60% to 80% sit in routes under 0.5%).
