# T-033: globe as an option beside the flat map

Done 2026-10-24 in one sitting. The design is in docs/ui-map.md "Globe" and docs/architecture.md 7
("Flat map or globe"); the calls in DECISIONS.md (T-033).

## How it is built

- `map/gl.ts`: `Camera` (`cameraFrom` reads a custom layer's `defaultProjectionData`: on the globe
  `mainMatrix` is the unit sphere to clip space, `fallbackMatrix` the flat map's, plus
  `projectionTransition` and `clippingPlane`); `localCamera` per anchor; `PROJECT_GLSL` with
  `project(vec2)`; `setCamera`; `mercToCss` (CPU twin); `behindHorizon`.
- Shaders using `project()`: stroke, station, bridge, train (`rendererShaders.ts`), crossing marks
  (`gradeMarks.ts`), transfer bubbles, demand discs and lines (`demandOverlay.ts`), the realm shade
  (`shadeLayer.ts`, a MapLibre custom layer, positions relative to the view centre in Mercator units:
  `u_globe_at` = centre, scale 1).
- `overlay.toScreen`/`toScreenInto` go through `mercToCss` on the globe; `screenToLocal` (MapLibre's
  unproject, checked by projecting back) feeds `pick.ts` `pointerLocal`, which takes the affine
  camera round the pointer's own place on the globe.
- DOM marks placed by `map.project` hide behind the horizon: airport badges, realm circles, zone
  loading bars, longer-trip labels and the box selection of their bubbles.
- `mapView.ts` `followProjection`: `map.setProjection` on `style.load` and when the setting changes.

## Not done, or approximate

- Sizes in px per metre keep the flat map's scale at the view's zoom (decided; DECISIONS).
- The demand hover's reach in local units (`demandHover.ts`, `unitPerPxAt`) is the flat map's; on
  the globe zoomed far out it is off by the Mercator factor between the pointer's latitude and the
  view's (about 15% across the US at zoom 3.6), so a small bubble's hover reach is a little off.
- Long straight segments are drawn as one straight screen line between their projected ends (no
  subdivision), so a segment hundreds of km long bows slightly from the sphere at world zoom; track
  segments are short enough that this was not visible.
- DOM marks whose place is behind the horizon would get NaN positions from `overlay.toScreen`
  (tool handles, stop markers); they appear only at zooms where the horizon is far off screen.
