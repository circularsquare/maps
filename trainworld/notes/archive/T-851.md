# T-851: does a ride on the player's network count as access to a long-distance station?

Two questions for Anita (2026-10-21): (1) does the market treat "metro to St Pancras, then the
high-speed train" as a rail option, or only the walk and the 15 km ride to a station; (2) the 25x
drop in New York's regional rail on 2026-10-17 (notes/T-846.md), against real figures.

## 1. Yes: a ride on the player's own lines is part of the rail trip

**Code.** The market's access is the walk or the 15 km ride from each site to a station (docs/regional.md
5.1 and 5.3), but that is only the first leg. From there the trip is routed over the whole network,
every line and every walking transfer within 300 m (`xfer_walk_m`), and priced door to door
(regional.md 5.2: "Local feeder lines, transfers and the intercity service receive actual loads").
A zone pair is solved when both zones reach some station and their stations are in one component of
the rail graph (`station_components` in `sim/src/demand/intercity.rs`; "track component" in the
comments means the graph of lines and transfer walks, demand-market.md). So a zone near a metro
station that connects to St Pancras is served, and its riders take the metro, change, and ride on.

**Test.** New sandbox game in London on my own Vite (6176, `demandWorkers=2`), headless Chrome.
T-828's 4-stop high-speed line St Pancras - Ashford - Lille - Paris Nord (high-speed track at -1,
400 m platforms, fast trains, 10 cars, 4/3/1 an hour). Then metro lines, each with its own track and
its own station 150-200 m from the terminal (so the change is a transfer walk), every 5 minutes
(10 at night):

- London A: Wimbledon - Clapham Junction - Victoria - Kings Cross - Finsbury Park - Wood Green
- London B: Ealing Broadway - Shepherds Bush - Paddington - Kings Cross - Liverpool Street -
  Stratford - Ilford
- London C: Croydon - Brixton - Elephant - Kings Cross - Highbury - Tottenham
- Paris 1: La Defense - Saint-Lazare - Nord - Montreuil
- Paris 2: Saint-Denis - Nord - Chatelet - Montparnasse - Massy

| | London - Paris by rail, a day | share of the corridor (29,889) | air | high-speed line riders | boardings St Pancras / Paris Nord |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| high-speed line alone | 13,441 | 45.0% | 16,123 | 29,015 | 8,532 / 12,213 |
| + three London metro lines | 17,378 | 58.1% | 12,264 | 70,827 | 47,390 / 14,184 |
| + two Paris metro lines | 20,281 | 67.9% | 9,418 | 82,586 | 48,691 / 23,229 |

The line alone matches T-828 (13,460). Feeders at the London end add 29%, at both ends 51%, taking
rail to 68% of the corridor, near the published 70-80% of rail and air. London - Lille rose 2,811 to
3,771 and Paris - Lille 10,397 to 13,109 the same way. (The St Pancras jump in boardings is mostly
London locals riding to Ashford through the metro; not looked into.)

**For the player:** feeding the terminals is the lever T-828 named, and it works as it should:
Eurostar riders come from wherever the player's metro reaches, not only from within a walk or a
15 km drive of St Pancras. With no feeders London - Paris rail stays at 45% (below real life, as
Anita expected); with a modest metro at each end it is near real. No change proposed.

## 2. New York's 25x drop

**The save has no intercity line.** Its 109 lines are the subway, PATH, LIRR, Metro-North and NJ
Transit. None leaves the New York zone except Metro-North's Waterbury branch to Derby-Shelton (New
Haven County, 0.5 trains an hour). The New Haven Line stops at Stratford (still Fairfield County), NJ
Transit's Northeast Corridor at Princeton Junction (Mercer), and there is no SEPTA, River Line or
Amtrak. The zone is 26 counties: the New York MSA's 22 plus Fairfield CT, Dutchess, Orange NY and
Mercer NJ (`pipeline/build_city.py`).

**Most of the 25x was trips inside the New York zone, not leaving it.** The market's "longer trips"
include trips of 25 km and more with both ends in the zone (Long Island to Manhattan, say). By
corridor (both ways, weekday):

| | before T-636 (T-373's real network, 2026-10-15) | now (2026-10-21, browser) |
|---|---:|---:|
| longer trips, both ends in the New York zone | 106,139 | 3,783 |
| longer trips New York - New Haven | 1,456 | 983 |
| longer trips New York - Litchfield | 68 | 48 |
| longer trips New York - Philadelphia | 0 | 0 |
| longer trips leaving the zone, all | 1,524 | 1,031 |
| commute legs between zones (all of the save's) | 1,538 | 1,268 |
| **rail trips leaving the zone, about** | **3,060** | **2,300** |

So rail leaving the zone fell about a third, not 25x. The 25x is the inside-the-zone part, where
T-636's sharper choice (0.05 to 0.10 a perceived minute, rail constants gone) hit long trips on
slow commuter lines hardest. The rest of those trips are partly in the local layers anyway, which
taper over 40-80 km (regional.md 5.1), so 3,783 is not New York's whole long non-work rail travel.

**Real rail trips across the zone's edge, a day (both ways):**

| | figure | source | trips crossing the edge, a day |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Amtrak at the zone's stations, 2024 | 15.05M boardings and alightings a year: New York Penn 12.05M, other NY stations 0.62M, NJ stations 1.84M (Newark 0.80M, Trenton 0.39M, Metropark 0.34M), Stamford 0.39M, Bridgeport about 0.15M | Rail Passengers Association fact sheets (NY, NJ, Stamford, 2024; Bridgeport 2022 scaled) | about 35,000 (41,200 less pairs inside the zone such as Trenton - New York) |
| Metro-North, New Haven County stations | New Haven Union 5,285 boardings a day (2025), Milford 1,508, West Haven 1,117 (2018), State Street and the Waterbury branch a few hundred each | Wikipedia station pages, from Metro-North | about 16,000 |
| SEPTA Trenton Line at Trenton | 1,241 boardings a day (2017) | Wikipedia | about 2,500 |
| NJ Transit River Line at Trenton | 990 weekday boardings (2025) | Wikipedia | about 2,000 |
| **all** | | | **about 55,000** |

The model's 2,300 (or 3,060 before T-636) is 4-6% of that. Both are far below real, and for the
same reason: the save has none of the lines that carry those trips. Amtrak is about two thirds of
them, and the commuter third runs on Metro-North east of Stratford, SEPTA and the River Line, none of
which is in the save. Neither figure is "closer to real" in any useful sense; the save is a network
of local and commuter lines that stop at or inside the zone's edge.

## 3. New York - Philadelphia against London - Paris

On the save there is **no rail path** New York - Philadelphia at all, so there is no time to set
against the car: NJ Transit ends at Princeton Junction and nothing in the save reaches Pennsylvania.
That alone gives 0 rail on the corridor (782,519 trips a day in the corridor row, all modes).

**Test: give the save a line.** The real save on my Vite, made a sandbox, plus a new line on its own
high-speed track (level -2): Midtown (34th-38th St, about 650 m from Penn, so no transfer to the
subway; platforms beside Penn clashed with its own) - Newark - Trenton - Philadelphia 30th St, 139 km,
4/3/1 an hour, 10 cars.

| | end to end | New York - Philadelphia longer trips by rail | commute legs between zones by rail (all) | line riders |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| the save | no path | 0 | 1,268 | |
| fast trains | 34 min | 29,865 | 8,571 | 55,049 |
| ordinary trains, same track | 56 min | 16,353 | 6,965 | 37,653 |

For scale: by the market's rule the car takes 133 minutes centre to centre (15 + 1.25 x 134 km at
85 km/h) plus fuel and parking; Amtrak's Northeast Regional takes about 80 minutes and Acela about
70. Real rail New York - Philadelphia is perhaps 5-7k a day (Philadelphia 30th St had 5.04M Amtrak
boardings and alightings in 2024, 13,800 a day, New York its top pair; plus a little by NJ Transit and
SEPTA through Trenton).

So the market does treat New York - Philadelphia like London - Paris once a line exists: 0 to
16-30k riders a day, already above real in absolute terms. The share stays low (about 4-8% of the
pair's roughly 380k longer trips) for two reasons that are not speed:

- **The competitor is the car, not the plane.** At 134 km the car is 133 minutes door to door; at
  450 km London - Paris by car is about 6 hours with the Channel crossing, and the plane carries its
  90 minutes at airports. Rail wins London - Paris wherever it reaches; against the car rail must
  also beat the drive to and from its stations.
- **New York - Philadelphia's trips are spread out.** Its ground trips are weighted to the nearest
  zone pairs across the seam (Mercer and Middlesex to Bucks and Burlington; T-377: a quarter under
  80 km), and both zones are large (26 and 11 counties). London - Paris's 29,889 are the long-tail
  rule's, concentrated on the two cities. Four stations reach much less of New York - Philadelphia.

Speed matters as expected under the sharper choice: 22 minutes slower halves rail (29.9k to 16.4k).
The save's real chain (NJ Transit local to Trenton, then SEPTA, about 2h40 station to station if
both reached Trenton) would be slower than the car before access and get close to nothing.

## Judgement

- Leaving the zone, the model is 4-6% of real both before and after T-636, because the save has no
  intercity line and its commuter lines stop at or inside the zone's edge. That is the network, not
  the demand rule.
- The 25x is inside the zone (106k to 3.8k long trips on commuter lines). Which of those is right
  is a separate question, and the local layers carry part of those trips too; not settled here.
- With a fast line New York - Philadelphia behaves as London - Paris does: the line fills (30k a
  day) and feeders and more stations raise it. Nothing qualitatively different; no demand change
  proposed.
