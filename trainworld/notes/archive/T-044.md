# T-044: merging sparse cells into their H3 parent

2026-10-15: design and measurement (sections 1-5), then built (section 6). Anita: "cost scaling with
area seems like it suggests to me that we should do more merging." Measured after T-395, T-397,
T-398 and T-399. Scratch work (not kept): a copy of the sim crate with a probe, `merge.py` (the
merged packs), `compare.py`, `person.py`.

**Method.** A res-8 parent (7 res-9 children, 0.74 km²) merges when its present children's trip
ends a weekday (commutes and everyday outings at both ends, the perf survey's estimate) are under
the threshold. Merged here only **within a 2 km zone** (a parent cut by a zone edge gives one
merged cell per zone), so zone sums, the shipped commute gravity and the routes stay exactly as
they are. The merged cell sums every weight, sits at the trip-end-weighted centre of its children
and takes the parent's H3 index. One solving worker natively (counting allocator), commute engine
and everyday layer, no market, periods 0, 1, 4, free flow and three crowding rounds; New York on
the real track (1,756 stations), Tokyo, Toronto and Windham on T-420's synthetic grid (16 + 16
lines 3 km apart, a stop every 1.5 km, 12 radials). Native times +-30% (shared machine).

## 1. What cells cost now

| Pack | cells | `.bin` raw / gz | worker heap after solve (pack bytes included) / peak | everyday opening | solve |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New York (real track) | 207,766 | 18.3 / 8.7 MB | 102 / 134 MB | 660 ms | 25 s |
| Tokyo (grid) | 98,576 | 8.3 / 3.8 MB | 87 / 122 MB | 420 ms | 16 s |
| Toronto (grid) | 75,442 | 6.2 / 2.3 MB | 45 / 72 MB | 200 ms | 3 s |
| Windham (grid) | 12,688 | 1.0 / 0.4 MB | 10 / 33 MB | 17 ms | 1 s |

Per-cell arrays are 83-89% of every pack's bytes. In the worker the cell-bound part is now about
250 bytes a cell (New York: pack bytes 17.6 MB, commute engine 12.5, everyday samples 21, the
everyday set-up 23 of which most is `edge_pair` and pairs); the rest (about 25 MB a New York
worker per 3 periods, plus the market's 45 MB in the real game) follows pairs and the network,
not cells. Solve time does not follow cells (it works on subzone pairs).

**Offline build**: cells barely matter. In Tokyo's build the cells step took 271 s (census meshes
and GHSL pixels spread onto cells, per pixel), gravity 1.8 s (2 km zones), the rest OSM and
per-point enrichments. A merge as a last step costs seconds; T-297's joint gravity is untouched.

## 2. What merging saves

| Pack | cells merged at 1k / 2k / 5k trip ends | `.bin` raw | gz | heap after solve | peak | everyday opening |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New York | 43% / 51% / 62% | 11.8 / 10.6 / 8.9 MB | 6.2 / 5.6 / 4.8 | 81 / 78 / 72 MB | 112 / 109 / 103 | 578 / 563 / 571 ms |
| Tokyo | 21% / 29% / 39% | 6.8 / 6.2 / 5.5 | 3.2 / 3.0 / 2.7 | 82 / 80 / 77 | 117 / 116 / 112 | 354 / 359 / 338 |
| Toronto | 50% / 53% / 59% | 3.4 / 3.2 / 2.9 | 1.7 / 1.6 / 1.5 | 35 / 35 / 33 | 62 / 61 / 60 | 120 / 148 / 117 |
| Windham | 70% / 73% / 74% | 0.39 / 0.37 / 0.36 | 0.20 / 0.19 / 0.18 | 7.6 / 7.5 / 7.5 | 30 / 30 / 30 | 6 / 6 / 6 |

So at 1k: New York's worker about -21 MB (about a tenth of the real game's 222 MB steady with the
market), Toronto -22%, Tokyo -5% (dense, little to merge); packs -35% raw and -29% gzipped in New
York. Solve time, set-up time and per-period memory unchanged. Merging whole parents across zone
edges would merge 6-8 points more cells (New York 49% at 1k) but changes zone totals, so the
gravity is re-solved and the routes rebuilt. By density instead of trip ends: about the same cells
(residents + jobs under 500 per km² merges like 1.5k trip ends); trip ends are the better rule.

**At world scale.** Memory follows the cities a worker has reached, so the bad case is a network
over a region: the nine Northeast packs hold 764k cells, 381k after a 1k merge (-50%; 326k at 2k,
257k at 5k), about -90 MB a worker if all nine were reached, and 65.7 MB of packs become 37.8.
Across the sampler a 1k merge removes 21-70% of cells (Tokyo, Shanghai, Mumbai, Berlin, Paris
24-36%; US and Toronto packs 43-66%; Windham-like rural packs 70%). A Los Angeles-like sprawl is
the case it helps least (estimate, no pack yet): its urban fabric at about 2,700 people per km²
puts 8-9k trip ends in each parent, above even 5k, so only the hills, desert and outer edges merge,
perhaps a quarter to a third of its cells, while it will be among the largest packs.

## 3. How results change

**Totals** (sums over periods 0, 1, 4): rail trips +0.06-0.08% at 1k, +0.1-0.24% at 2k, +0.3-1.2%
at 5k (Windham +2.0 / +2.5 / +4.5%). Everyday trips lost to the 150 m rule (trips inside one cell
vanish) -0.01% at 1k, at most -0.1% at 5k in the metros, Windham -0.2 / -0.4 / -0.8%. Walk and
ride access to stations within 0.5%.

| Pack | stations (1k+ boardings) moving over 5%, 1k / 2k / 5k | worst station at 1k | worst line (1k+ riders), 1k / 5k |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York | 1.5% / 4.1% / 9.3% | +11% (5,821) | -2.8% / -8.2% |
| Tokyo | 2.6% / 4.5% / 12.2% | +23% (1,843) | +0.6% / +3.7% |
| Toronto | 0.6% / 2.0% / 16.2% | -7% (1,191) | +2.6% / +6.8% |
| Windham (100+) | 25% / 31% / 56% | +11% (118) | +9.4% / +8.3% |

**How far trip ends move.** A merged person's home or activity moves to the merged centre: mean
250-270 m, 95th percentile 395-430 m, at most about 715 m, at every threshold and in every pack,
weighted by people or by trip ends alike (the two differ by under 15 m). Over everyone in the pack
that is 13 / 25 / 58 m on average in New York at 1k / 2k / 5k (Tokyo 3 / 5 / 13 m, Toronto 9 /
14 / 30, Windham 153 / 181 / 211), because only 5.2 / 9.6 / 22% of New York's people are merged.

**A merged person's chance of rail.** The model already gives every cell of an access subzone
(the cells of one zone that share their nearest station) one rail probability, from the subzone's
walk averaged over its cells (docs/demand.md 4.4 step 3). A merge does not change that grain; it
changes the subzone's average a little. Change in a merged person's rail probability:

| | commute: mean / 95th / worst change | everyday: mean / 95th / worst |
|---|---|---|
| New York 1k / 5k | 0.02 / 0.06 / +33 points; 0.10 / 0.37 / +37 | 0.5 / 2.3 / +31; 0.7 / 2.7 / +43 |
| Tokyo 1k / 5k | 0.03 / 0.11 / +26; 0.09 / 0.35 / +29 | 0.6 / 2.8 / +22; 0.7 / 2.9 / +28 |
| Toronto 1k / 5k | 0.03 / 0.14 / +13; 0.18 / 0.75 / +13 | 1.0 / 4.2 / +16; 1.2 / 4.3 / +22 |
| Windham 1k / 5k | 0.13 / 0.59 / +9; 0.23 / 0.76 / +9 | 1.8 / 5.8 / +15; 1.9 / 5.5 / +15 |

Everyday moves more than commutes because its walk share uses the sampled cells' own distance.
The worst cases are a handful of cells (likely ones a moved centre carries across a subzone or the
4 km station reach; not traced). Merged people living within 500 m of a station whose merged centre
is 300 m+ further away are rare (New York 158 people at 1k, 5,645 at 5k; Windham 1.0-1.3% of its
people), since stations stand where people are and merges happen where they are not; their
probabilities moved under 1 point on average.

**Worked example** (Toronto at 5k): a cell of 8 people 306 m from a station merges with four
neighbours (604-951 m away, 189 people) into a centre 826 m from it. Commute rail 22.8% to 21.6%
(its siblings were at 21.1% before: two subzones), everyday rail 29.0% to 24.0%. If the model
priced that person's own walk instead, 300 m costs 5.9 perceived minutes and 900 m 17.6: +11.7
minutes, which at 25% rail would take rail to about 16% (-9 points); with a ride to the station
on offer (a trip of 5 km or more, about 16 minutes for a station 1 km off) the access logsum
rises 8 minutes instead, rail about 18% (-7 points), and walking's share of that person's access
falls from about 88% to 42%: most of them would ride to the station rather than walk. The
subzone averaging already blurs this today, before any merge; a merge adds 1-5 points for such a
person, not 7-9.

## 4. Design

**Least invasive: a last pipeline step, `pipeline/merge_cells.py <city>`**, after the enrichments
and routes, writing the published pack from a res-9 copy kept in `data/work/` (enrichments and
`routes.py` always run on the res-9 copy, then the merge is run again). Within-zone rule, so
nothing in the gravity, zones or routes changes. Merged cells live in the same arrays: the H3
index already carries its resolution, so no resolution column is needed. Changes:

- pipeline: the new step (about 150 lines, `merge.py` here is most of it); `read_pack.py` and
  `look_pack.py` accept res 8 cells (they check every id is res 9 and that ids are sorted);
  `build_city.py` and `tier0.py` call the step; the header gets a `merged` block (rule,
  threshold, cells before). `lumps.py`, `outside.py` and the enrichers keep reading res 9.
- sim: nothing required (h3 is only the everyday seed; distances are from x, y). The 150 m rule
  loses at most 0.1% of a metro's everyday trips at 5k, so no intra-cell distance is needed.
- app: the bubbles' grouping (`commuters.worker.ts` `setCells`) masks digits but not the
  resolution field, so a res-8 cell would group apart from the res-9 cells under the same res-7
  parent: clear the resolution bits in the key (one line). A merged cell draws one bubble 7 times
  the area's worth at the finest level, in sparse places only.
- checks: `every_pack_everyday`, t066 and t149 worker checks, smoke and the real save.

Effort about 2 days (down from the survey's 3-5: enrichments stay on res 9), low to moderate risk.

**The other way**, merging at load in `pack::parse` with packs left at res 9: about 1 day and
reversible, but no byte saving, and every Rust reader of per-cell arrays (commute engine, the
everyday `cell_weights`, the market's sites after T-362) must share one cell map.

**Stages**: (1) `merge_cells.py` and the readers, Windham and Toronto compared as here; (2) the
bubble fix, every pack merged, checks and smoke; (3) DECISIONS line, docs/demand.md 4.1 rewritten.

## 5. Decisions for Anita

1. **Threshold**: 1k trip ends a parent (recommended: 43% of New York's cells; 0.6-2.6% of
   stations move over 5%), 2k (51%, 4% of stations past 5%), or 5k (62%, 9-16% of
   stations past 5%, worst station -41% to +67%).
2. **Where**: in the pipeline, saving bytes and memory (recommended), or at load in the sim,
   memory only.
3. **Across zone edges**: keep merges inside a 2 km zone (recommended; gravity unchanged), or
   merge whole parents for 6-8 points more cells with the gravity re-solved.
4. **When**: after T-302 and T-060 (recommended; it saves about a tenth of a New York worker,
   more in rural-heavy regions), or now.

Anita chose 1k, in the pipeline, inside zones, now (2026-10-15).

## 6. Built (2026-10-15)

`pipeline/merge_cells.py` (rule as in section 4; usage in its docstring and docs/adding-a-city.md
4), `read_pack.py` accepting res-8 cells with a `merged` block, `look_pack.py`'s title, and the
bubble grouping in `commuters.worker.ts` clearing the resolution field. Two choices beyond the
design: a group whose weighted centre falls on the water mask or outside its zone square stays at
res 9 (33-739 groups a pack on water, none off a square), and cells are sorted by id with the
resolution field cleared, then zone, since a parent cut by a zone edge gives two cells one index
(duplicates are harmless: the sim uses h3 only as the everyday seed and the bubbles group them
together anyway).
