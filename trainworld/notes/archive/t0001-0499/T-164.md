# T-164 platforms that fill and empty, trains that leave people behind

Drafted 2026-10-12 by the orchestrator from Anita's request. Phase 1 built 2026-10-12 (working
notes at the end); phase 2 (feeding waits back into demand) is Anita's call.

## What Anita asked for

- Show people waiting on platforms.
- "Ideally we also sort of simulate people being refused boarding ... if in a 1 hour interval we
  have like 10k people trying to board a line that has 8k capacity, could we just draw the extra
  2k people as waiting for later times for less full trains to come?" Yes: that is the queue below.
- She agreed this middle option is the way forward (not individual passengers), and that areas out
  of view may be simplified.

## What stays as it is

Demand keeps deciding who travels, by which mode and route, per period (SPEC 2, docs/demand.md).
This task adds nothing per person. It turns demand's per-period counts into a per-train account of
boarding, so each train has its own load and each platform its own crowd.

## The model

For each line and direction, in departure order, at each stop:

1. **People arrive** at the platform at the period's rate for that line and stop: demand's
   boardings there (kept per line since T-069) spread over the period. Use the busiest-hour shape
   (T-087's factor) or a simple in-period profile so the peak of the peak is sharper than the
   average; decide and record.
2. **A train arrives** carrying its own load from the previous stop. Some alight: the line's share
   alighting at this stop (demand's alightings over the load arriving, per period).
3. **Boarding**: `boarded = min(waiting, crush - on board)`. Whoever does not fit stays on the
   platform for the next train of that line. That is the refused boarding: 10k arriving in an hour
   on a line that carries 8k leaves a queue that grows through the hour and clears when trains have
   room again, possibly into the next period.
4. Where several lines serve the same next stops (T-069 common lines), the waiting riders take the
   first train with room among them; split the queue by frequency share as T-069 does if a shared
   queue is too fiddly.

**Why this is cheap.** Trains do not block each other (no signals, SPEC 2.1), so a line's boarding
account is a simple loop over its departures and stops, independent of every other line and of
what is on screen. It can be computed ahead, one game hour at a time, in the clock worker when the
hour turns (as the next hour's trip list already is), and the renderer reads each train's load per
stretch and each platform's queue per minute from a small table: still a function of the clock,
nothing stepped per frame. The queue left at the end of an hour carries into the next. Roughly
trains x stops operations an hour: tens of thousands for a big city, well under a millisecond to a
few milliseconds. If a huge network makes it heavier, compute lines in view first and the rest at a
coarser step (per period instead of per train), which is the simplification Anita allowed.

## What the player sees

- **Platform crowds**: people drawn at the platform, growing between trains and dropping when one
  leaves; a crowd that does not clear when the train leaves is the visible sign of an overloaded
  line. Drawn as small figures or a filled patch along the platform box (style call for Anita,
  show options). Bucketed counts, not one mark per person.
- **Each train its own fill**: the gauge (T-084) reads the train's own load instead of the period
  average or busiest hour, so a train after a gap is fuller. Revisit the Average / Busiest hour
  display choice (T-087): it may no longer be needed.
- **Station inspector**: waiting now per line and direction, and how many were left behind this
  hour (and the longest wait). **Train inspector**: riders aboard from its own account.

## Later, Anita's call: feeding it back into demand

Phase 1 changes what is drawn and counted, not who chooses rail. Today the crowding penalty
(docs/demand.md step 7) already makes overfull routes less attractive, softly. A phase 2 could add
the average extra wait from being left behind to the perceived wait of that line and stop in the
next solve, so a line that leaves people behind loses riders to other routes or the car. That
needs the same averaging as the crowding rounds to settle, and moves the calibrated numbers, so
ask her before doing it.

## Signals: not this task

Anita asked whether signals could be simulated only for what is on screen. The difficulty is that
trains pass in and out of view, and a delay off screen should still arrive on screen later; with
two rules (signalled on screen, scheduled off it) trains would jump or bunch differently as the
player pans. If signals come, the cleaner route is SPEC 2.1's: an event-driven block simulation per
city (cheap per train, but it must run whether or not the train is visible). Keep it as its own
task once this one shows how per-train accounts feel.

## Working notes (built 2026-10-12)

The design as built is docs/track-and-service.md 6.6; the open choices taken are in DECISIONS
2026-10-12. Where things are:

- `sim/src/track/boarding.rs`: `Boarding` (riders in, accounts per line, `hour(epoch)` out),
  `simulate` (one direction of one line for a day), the arrival shapes. `Service::run_trips` was
  split out of `Service::trips` so the account's departures are the drawn ones to the bit.
  `TrackApi.set_riders`, `clear_riders`, `boarding_step`, `boarding_hour`, `boarding_redone`.
- `app/src/workers/clock.worker.ts`: a `riders` message sets the riders and steps through the lines
  3 at a time (`setTimeout` between steps); `sendTrips` adds the hour's account to each `trips`
  message (and finishes any lines still out of date, at once, if an edit lands mid-way). Riders
  are cleared when a game is loaded.
- `app/src/workers/demandClient.ts` keeps each line's `board` and `alight` per period and route
  node (`LineDemand`); `clockClient.ts` packs them (`game/boarding.ts` `packRiders`) and posts them
  on each new `demandView`.
- `app/src/map/renderer.ts`: per trip `a_load` (first load, seconds to its last stop), the hour's
  loads as a texture, each train sample's stretch as a texture; the train's gauge reads its own
  load where the layers' fill says loads are shown. Crowds: `updateCrowds` (each platform's crowd
  summed per station side, at most once a game second), `placeCrowds` (side from the line's
  direction at the stop against the platform's drawn axis), a third station pass (`u_halo == 2`).
- Inspectors: `ui/inspector/others.tsx` `OnBoard` (own load, typical train) and `Waiting`.
  Settings: `ui/SettingsTab.tsx` "Riders on trains and platforms"; `display.platformCrowds`.

Checks: `cargo test boarding` (shapes keep period totals and hit the busiest-hour factor; a line
with room carries demand's load and leaves nobody, arrivals = boardings = alightings; an
overloaded line (20,000 an hour against 16,000) builds 16,000 by 10:00 within a headway, every
peak train leaves full, midday clears it by 13:30, nobody lost; per-period loads match demand's
boardings within a headway's riders under the real shapes; a queue lasting the night takes the
warm run; an hour's slice lines up with `trips(epoch)`). `notes/t164-browser.mjs` on the real NYC
save (results `../../artifacts/trainworld/notes-outputs/t164-browser.json`).

Real NYC save, 08:20 on a weekday morning: 1,466 trips drawn that hour, 3,294 platforms with
riders, 1.25M arriving in the hour; full trains leave riders behind at 30 platforms (6,300 in the
hour), mostly LIRR branches into Manhattan (Ronkonkoma, Babylon, Hempstead; waits of 50-90 min,
their trains being few and long) and some subway: the D at 36 St (273 left in the hour, longest
wait 10 min), the 4 at 161 St-Yankee Stadium (260), the Z. At 36 St, 320 wait before the 08:20 D
and 104 are left on the platform after it; that D leaves packed full (1,440) against a typical
train of 866 for the period.

Cost (headless Chrome, hardware GPU, other agents busy): new riders 40-60 ms of the clock worker,
in steps of about 5 ms (first step up to 11-14 ms while the code warms up); an hour's slice
1-2 ms in the worker and about 450 kB transferred, twice an hour (the hour and the next); main
thread drawing 0.13-0.15 ms a frame without crowds and 0.27-0.38 ms with them at zoom 15, the
crowd update 0.1-0.3 ms of it; frame gaps unchanged (16.7 ms average, worst 17-19 ms either way).
Before stepping, the one-day run took 34-55 ms in one go, and the first version (two whole days)
53-69 ms.
