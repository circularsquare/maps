# T-069 - Common lines in demand

Implemented 2026-10-10. Riders at a shared stop can take the first useful train of several lines. Their wait is half the combined headway, with the existing platform/home wait weights. Assignment divides boardings by each accepted line's share of combined departure frequency. The graph still keeps separate line segments, so the train load, income, crowding and demand views continue to report each line's own riders.

The set of useful lines depends on the destination. A reverse label-setting search runs from each destination over the station/route graph. At a station it maintains accepted departures per hour and the frequency-weighted downstream cost. A further line joins only if its added frequency lowers the complete expected trip cost. A wrong branch, an off-period line, a very slow train or a costly/crowded alternative therefore does not make the useful service appear more frequent. Once aboard, riders follow their least-cost ride, alight or walking-transfer continuation. Base fares still charge once for the whole trip; distance fares stay on ride edges and are not multiplied by crowding.

Search stores destination strategies rather than one predecessor tree per origin. Loading walks each destination's settled nodes in reverse order, splitting station flow over accepted frequencies and conserving riders through ride and transfer edges. Added memory is one Float32 departure-weight matrix of stations by route nodes, plus graph reverse adjacency; no worker protocol or save changes.

Validation:
- `cargo test --manifest-path trainworld/sim/Cargo.toml --features demand --lib demand:: --quiet`: 19 passed, 1 ignored.
- Two identical 6 tph services have the exact same station-to-station cost as one 12 tph service.
- 6 and 12 tph lines share a combined 18 tph wait and split forward/reverse flow 1:2; station and route boarding/alighting totals conserve riders.
- Shared trunk service combines, while a trip to one branch excludes the other branch when transferring makes it worse.
- Off-period and very slow services contribute no attractive departures.
- Unequal ride times produce their frequency-weighted expected ride time. Expensive and crowded alternatives leave the attractive set.
- Existing fare, asymmetric run-length, demand view, walking-transfer, merged-station and crowding tests pass.
