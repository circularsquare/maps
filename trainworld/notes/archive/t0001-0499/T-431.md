# T-431: Japan stage 2a, Keihanshin and Chukyo

2026-10-15. Two Japanese packs built by `pipeline/tier0.py` with `pipeline/japan.py`'s inputs, as
Greater Tokyo (notes/T-420.md): Keihanshin (`osa`) and Chukyo (`ngo`). Both registered with
`market: -1`, `utcOffset` 9, country JP, and joined to the world market by `market_zones.py`.
Fukuoka, Sapporo, Sendai and Hiroshima are T-432, built at the same time by another agent.

## Sources

As notes/T-420.md: census 2020 500 m mesh population (T001141), economic census 2021 500 m mesh
jobs (T001162) scaled per municipality to the census's table 4 workers by place of work, the curve
fitted on table 4's flows (`notes/t420_curve.py <id> T-431`), KSJ N03 2025 outlines, KSJ P29 2023
schools. Telework: MLIT's 2024 telework population survey (research/japan.md), Kinki 24.4% and
Chukyo 19.6% of the employed. OSM: Geofabrik `asia/japan/kansai` (353 MB) and `asia/japan/chubu`
(512 MB, read 2026-10-15). No login; no request carried a name or email.

**Geofabrik regions overlap.** The region polygons are buffered: Kansai and Chubu share a strip
of 0.18 deg² along their border (Kansai-Shikoku 0.15, Chubu-Kanto 0.14), and Mie, on Chukyo's
side of Nagoya, is in Kansai. Every municipality of `osa` lies inside Kansai's polygon, and every
one of `ngo` inside Chubu's or Kansai's (checked on the N03 polygons). So `osa` reads its features
from Kansai alone; `ngo` reads both, and `osm_features.read` now takes a list of extracts and
keeps an object found in two once (by OSM id). Water takes both extracts for either pack (a
polygon drawn twice on the mask is the same pixels). Keihanshin's water box also covers corners
of Okayama, Tottori and Tokushima (about 17% of the box, mostly sea), outside the outline, where
inland water is missing.

## Decisions

- **Origins** at the main stations (Osaka Station at Umeda, Nagoya Station).
- **Work at home** `osa` 0.17: the census's 8.07% of workers by place of work at home plus 0.4 x
  24.4% (Kinki) of the rest; `ngo` 0.16: 8.83% plus 0.4 x 19.6% (Chukyo). 2 days in 5 as Tokyo.
- **Curve** Tokyo's d^-2.2 exp(-d/80 km) for both (below).
- Airports at tier 0's defaults (every passenger origin or destination; no transfer share set).

## Long trips (world market)

Every Osaka cell selected as homes, a new game with no rail (`T-431-bubble.json`, script in the
scratchpad): longer trips a day from Keihanshin's homes to Nagoya 162k, Tokyo 80k, Fukuoka 8.4k,
Sapporo 3.6k, Shanghai 1.8k; from Chukyo's to Osaka 162k, Tokyo 53k; from Greater Tokyo's to
Osaka 80k, Nagoya 52k. Symmetric both ways, as the market's gravity gives. Plausible against the
Tokaido Shinkansen's roughly 400k riders a day (memory, not checked) carrying most Tokyo-Osaka
and Tokyo-Nagoya trips. No commutes between packs: Japan is not in the joint commute gravity.

Seven wakes after idle waits cost about 2.2M (each re-reads the whole context at 200-350k).

Not worth its cost: two browser runs that timed out on my own setup (a trailing slash in `BASE`,
a dev server holding a stale registry), about 0.7M; the second osa build; Overpass tries for
station positions (504s; the extract's `.pbf` had them in seconds).

Proposals:
1. **`city-browser.mjs` should strip a trailing slash from `BASE`**, and the playbook should say
   to restart a no-watch dev server after `market_zones.py` (the registry is a module import).
2. **Fewer, longer waits**: start the curve fit and the build's downloads together, and the
   build right after the curve in one script, so a pack is three waits (fetch, curve and build,
   routes and checks), not seven.
3. **Japan's metro curve is settled** (d^-2.2 exp(-d/80) ties the best fit on three metros): the
   next Japanese packs, and stage 3's prefecture zones, can skip `t420_curve.py` (39 minutes here)
   and make the signals in the build.
4. **e-Stat downloads are the slow step** with two agents at once (20-24 minutes a pack): fetch
   the whole country's 500 m meshes once for stage 3 rather than per pack.
5. **Brief**: right and complete. The Mie-in-Kansai note saved a wrong extract; the overlap of
   Geofabrik's region polygons was the one surprise.
