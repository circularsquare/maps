# T-159: work demand across the Delaware (diagnosis)

Anita: "figure out better work demand model... people should not be commuting from downtown philly
to camden". Diagnosis only, 2026-10-12; no pack, pipeline or sim change. Scripts (read-only on the
packs, scratch output outside the project):

- `notes/t159_flows.py`: the shipped Philadelphia zone gravity aggregated to counties, Center City
  and Camden city, against LODES OD 2023 (block level, both ends in the 11 counties) and ACS
  2016-2020 county flows (Census commuting table 1).
- `notes/t159_borders.py`: model against ACS and LODES for county pairs sorted into land state
  border, water inside one state, water plus state border, same side (Philadelphia and New York).
- `notes/t159_variants.py`: the Philadelphia gravity re-solved in numpy (Furness) under candidate
  fixes. It reproduces the shipped gravity exactly with no factor.
- `notes/t159_nyc.py`: the same border factor tested on the New York pack.

New downloads in `data/raw/`: `pa_od_main/aux_JT00_2023.csv.gz`, `nj_xwalk.csv.gz` (Camden city
blocks), `acsflows/table1.xlsx` (its Philadelphia rows cached as `acsflows/phl_county_flows.csv`).

Groups. Center City is Vine St to South St, Schuylkill to Delaware (a lat/lon box). Camden city is
the LODES crosswalk place. "Share" is the share of the home group's commuters going to that work
group, so the model (commuters, net of work from home) and LODES (jobs) compare without scaling.
LODES totals are about 1.22x the model's (work from home, T-054), so a LODES count times 0.82 is
roughly the commuters the model should have.

The four PA counties against the four NJ counties: the model sends 10.1% of PA commuters to NJ
(LODES 3.1%, ACS 2.2%) and 43.8% of NJ commuters to PA (LODES 20.3%, ACS 16.1%). All told, 17.1% of
the model's in-region trips cross the river; LODES has 6.8%. ACS own-county shares run above
LODES everywhere (T-034: LODES places multi-site employers' staff at the head office); LODES is
what the decay was fitted to, so it is the yardstick here.

**The pair Anita named is the mildest one.** Center City residents to Camden County is 1.7x LODES:
1,363 trips against about 870 commuters (1,060 jobs x 0.82). Some Center City residents do
reverse-commute over the Ben Franklin Bridge and PATCO. What looks wrong on the map is mostly
the other direction and the wider flows. A Center City selection shows the homes of Center City's
workers, and its Camden County bubble holds 2.0x the LODES share (25,221 trips). Camden city alone
is 11x: 22.6% of Camden city's commuters work in Center City in the model, against 2.0% in LODES.
Philadelphia County as a whole sends 3.1x LODES to Camden County and 2.6-2.9x to Burlington and
Gloucester.

By distance, model Philadelphia -> Camden County trips are 2.2-4.1x LODES in every crow-fly band
from 1.5 to 40 km. The overshoot is a near-constant factor, not something confined to a few long
or short pairs. 31 of the 3,173 zones hold cells from both banks (88k commute weight). Their
intrazonal trips cross the river at 1.04 km, but they are a small part of the total.

## 2. Cause: the state line, not the water

Same-side pairs are on LODES (Philadelphia to Delaware County 0.99x, to Montgomery 0.98x; Camden
to Gloucester 1.02x ACS). Cross-river pairs are 2-3x over. To separate the river from the state
line, `t159_borders.py` sorts county pairs in both packs by kind (model share / LODES share,
New York pack):

| kind | pairs | model/LODES |
|---|---|---|
| same side, land | Bergen-Passaic, Essex-Union, Nassau-Queens | 0.89-1.06x |
| water in one state, many crossings | Kings, Queens -> Manhattan (East River) | 0.86-0.95x |
| water in one state, few crossings | Hudson-Union (Newark Bay) 0.63-0.70x; Richmond-Kings (Narrows) 0.71-1.11x; Queens-Bronx 0.92-1.03x; Rockland-Westchester, Orange-Dutchess (Hudson) 1.0-2.0x | mostly 0.7-1.6x |
| water in one state, **no bridge** | Westchester, Suffolk <-> Nassau (Long Island Sound) | 1.27-1.54x |
| **land state border** | Rockland <-> Bergen 1.94-1.97x; Orange NY <-> Passaic, Sussex 2.8-4.3x | 1.9-4.3x |
| water plus state border | Hudson, Bergen, Essex, Union -> Manhattan 1.6-1.9x; Arthur Kill 3.3-4.4x; Westchester-Bergen 2.7-6.1x | 1.6-6x |

In the Philadelphia pack (ACS only, since DE and MD OD were not downloaded), the land borders
New Castle DE <-> Chester and Delaware County PA, and Cecil MD <-> Chester, run 1.3-3.9x. The
water-plus-border pairs (the Delaware) run 2.2-6.6x.

Water inside one state, even Long Island Sound with no bridge at all, is within about 1.5x of
LODES. A state line with no water is 2-4x over. So the crow-fly gravity's error is that it does
not know people largely work in their own state. That is the same error as T-034's Hudson
overshoot, which is also a state line. Possible reasons include separate labour markets, licensing
and residency rules (NJ and Philadelphia both require public employees to live there), bridge tolls
and habit. The model need not know which: one factor captures it.

**Not the doubly constrained form as such, and not head-office lumps.** Camden County's jobs total
is right (model 180k against LODES 174k). The constraint fills them from wherever the decay says,
and the crow-fly decay rates Philadelphia as near as Cherry Hill. Head-office placement inflates
LODES's cross-county flows, if anything, so the overshoot against LODES is a lower bound.

**What the border does not explain.** Camden city -> Center City stays 6.6-6.9x after any fix
below. Camden city's residents work in Center City far less than its distance suggests
(low-income residents, jobs that do not match). That is a labour-market effect. The US fix would be
a gravity split by earnings (LODES SE01-03), which is US-only data, so it is not proposed.

**Road travel time (T-072's candidate) barely moves it.** The stand-in routes every PA/DE <-> NJ
zone pair through the best of nine Delaware crossings (Delaware Memorial to the Trenton bridges;
approximate points, diagnostic only). The Delaware has a crossing every 3-15 km, so the detour
averages 1.10x crow-fly on the model's cross-river trips. It closes about a tenth of the gap
(17.1% to 16.1% against 6.8%). An OSM road time would differ in detail, not in size: Center City
to Camden is one bridge and ten minutes. It would cost an OSM road extract and routing per pack.
The game would also need a zone-pair impedance matrix: today the decay is a small table by grid
offset (`kernel::decay_table`), and Philadelphia's 3,173 zones would need about 10M pairs (40 MB)
shipped or recomputed. It would help the few real no-bridge cases (Long Island Sound, 1.3-1.5x),
which are not the problem.

**A water-crossing penalty from the shipped water mask is wrong.** It would cut the East River and
Newark Bay flows, which are already at or under LODES (0.63-0.95x), and it cannot tell the Ben
Franklin Bridge from open bay.

**A border factor fixes it, with one number that transfers.** Multiply the decay by k when home

## 4. Recommended fix (new task T-162)

**Region-aware gravity: a "different first-level region" factor on the decay, and zones that do
not straddle a region border.**

- Inputs: a region id per cell. US packs already have it (the state FIPS in every block geoid), so
  no new data. Other countries: any global admin-1 layer (geoBoundaries ADM1, CC BY 4.0; or OSM
  `admin_level=4`). National borders are the same mechanism one level up.
- Pipeline: the region per cell, and zones keyed by (2 km square, region), so a square cut by the
  border becomes two zones (+91 zones in Philadelphia, +116 in New York). `read_pack.py`'s "two
  zones share a grid square" check becomes per region.
- Pack: `zone_region` (u8 per zone) plus `border_factor` in the gravity header. A pack without them
  means factor 1, so old packs still load.
- Kernel (`sim/src/demand/kernel.rs`, `api.rs`): `Gravity::t` and the Furness loops multiply by k
  when the regions differ. That is one comparison per pair, about 23 call sites on the ftab and
  base pattern. `walk_trips_by_zone` must look up a list of zones per grid square, not one.
  `pack_gravity` gets `--border-factor`. The pipeline solve and the game's loader stay one
  implementation.
- Fit: k jointly with `decay_km` on New York and Philadelphia LODES (expect k of about 0.25), then
  rebuild all nine packs (about 1.5 minutes each).
- Effort: about one agent-day, including the rebuilds, `read_pack` checks and the New York
  real-network comparison rerun. The regional market (`intercity.py`, 6 km zones) is a separate
  model and is not touched.

**What it moves.** The local commute bubbles, then everything downstream: rail riders, loads,
crowding and fares on lines that cross a state line. In New York, the inner NJ counties' commuters to Manhattan
fall by 41-58% (Hudson 41%, Bergen 51%, Essex, Passaic and Union 56-58%). The T-090 real-network comparison has NJ counties' rail share over ACS (Bergen 9.3%
against 3.5%, Union 12.7% against 5.7%, Passaic 8.8% against 1.2%, Hudson 27.9% against 21.5%),
so this moves them the right way. Brooklyn's (41.3% against 48.5%) should rise a little. NJ
Transit (276k against about 250k) will fall; PATH, already far under (68k against 212k), falls
further. That is a separate PATH problem to note, not to fix with the gravity. Commuting between the PA and NJ
sides of the Philadelphia pack falls by 60% (17.1% of trips to 6.8%), and lines crossing the
Delaware lose riders to match. Existing saves' rider and fare
numbers change on load.

**Anita's calls.**

- The factor outside the US. The US value (about 0.25) is fitted. For other countries an admin-1
  factor is a guess: Tokyo's prefectures exchange millions of commuters, so 0.25 there would be
  wrong. Suggested default: national borders get a factor, admin-1 gets 1 (no effect) until a
  country's own OD check (Japan census, UK census flows) says otherwise.
- Accept that Center City -> Camden lands under LODES (about 300 trips against about 870) in
  exchange for fixing the larger flows. A separate factor for "border plus water" against "land
  border" is not supported by the data (both are 2-4x) and is not proposed.
- Camden city -> Center City stays about 6.6x. It is a labour-market effect, and its US-only fix
  (earnings-split gravity) breaks the global-data rule.
