## Done 2026-10-12

**The map** (`app/src/ui/inspector/ComplexMap.tsx`, an inline SVG like the junction inspector's
`JunctionMap`), first thing in the station inspector's complex view (`others.tsx`
`ComplexSummary`). Spec text: docs/interface.md, "The complex map".

- North up, true relative positions and headings at one scale: fitted to the inspector's width
  (measured with a ResizeObserver, so 1 SVG unit = 1 CSS px and the 12 px text stays 12 px) and
  120-240 px tall, at most 1.2 px a metre so a two-platform complex is not blown up. A round
  scale bar (10 m to 1 km, about 40-80 px) in the corner.
- Each line stopping at a platform is a band in its colour through the platform and 7 px past
  each end. Width = riders on that line's trains at that platform a day (`ridersAt`): in each
  direction the busier of the arriving and leaving segment loads (`LineDemand.loads`, every period
  summed), the two directions added. 1.5 to 6 px by square root against the complex's busiest; 3 px
  before demand is known; a platform no line stops at is a 4 px white band. Boardings a day stay
  in the Lines list below (the complex's per-line boardings, T-146) and in each line's hover.
- **Bundles.** The real New York save gives every service its own station, so most of a big
  complex's members lie on top of each other (Grand Central's Metro-North lines; Penn's LIRR and
  NJ Transit). Drawn one box each at true position they made an unreadable pile of outlines and
  overlapping lines (the first browser run; its screenshots were overwritten). Platforms
  parallel within 15 degrees whose centre lines come within 12 m (`segmentGap`, as T-146) are
  drawn as one bundle: axis of the longest, length spanning all, centred on their mean offset,
  lines side by side in the order the platforms lie across. 12 m is about one island platform's
  width: two services sharing a platform or adjacent tracks bundle; the next platform over does
  not.
- Bundle outline 1 px dark ink at 70% (dashed if every member is blueprint); the selected
  station's bands get a 2 px outline. Each band is its own hover and click target (at least 4 px
  high): hover names the station, line, boardings and riders; a click selects that station and
  complex view stays.
- Labels only when the complex has more than one name (one name is the inspector's title
  already): one per name (T-169), busiest name first, the part before a slash ("Times Sq-42 St"),
  tried above, below, right and left of that name's platforms and kept only where clear of every
  bundle (sampled every 3 px with its half-width), earlier labels and the scale bar.
- The map ignores the purpose filter: the inspector's totals count every purpose (T-158).

**T-163.** `NetworkRenderer` has a per-instance `a_sel` attribute (1 = drawn selected); the
vertex shader enlarges a box when it is the selected instance or `a_sel` is set. `DrawOptions`
gained `selStations` (station buffer indices); main.ts passes the selected station's complex in
complex view (`complexOf`), else nothing. The buffer is rewritten only when the set changes
(compared as a joined key) and reset with the network. Picking (`stationAt`) treats those boxes
as selected too, so their larger box is what a click hits.

## Transfers (T-176, done 2026-10-12)

Before T-176 nothing counted transfers: `assign` added a rider leaving a train to the station's
flow and split it over the departures like everyone there, so the line they came from was lost.
Spec text: docs/demand.md 4.4 "Transfers", docs/interface.md "Transfers on the complex map".

**Counting** (`kernel.rs` section 6). `assign_with_transfers(g, sk, od, ix, xfer)`: when a route
node's riders leave the train at a station that is not the destination (own station or a walking
transfer), (arriving edge's first slot, riders) goes on that station's list for this destination
(a linked list in two vectors, reset per destination). Stations are walked after every node that
feeds them, so when one splits its flow its list is whole: each arrival adds riders x share to
the slot of (arriving edge, departure), over the departures with a nonzero share only (about half
go the wrong way for a destination). `xfer_index(g)` lays the slots out once per graph (every
edge from a route node to a station, then that station's boarding edges); `transfer_pairs`
reads them back as (route node in, route node out, riders) for route nodes marked in a byte mask,
either end. `assign` is the same function without the counting, so the loads are identical.
Commutes (`api.rs`), the everyday pass (all purposes into one array, `everyday.rs`) and the
market (`intercity.rs`) count every round and average across rounds with the loads' MSA weights;
each API has `transfers(sel)`. Test switch `kernel::XFER_OFF` (tests only) turns counting off.

**Every round, not the last only.** Counting costs about 5 ms on a 36 ms commute assignment and
is lost in the noise of whole stages (below), so the cheaper "last round only" (off by the last
round's change, and absent when an uncrowded round is skipped) was not worth it.

**Query.** Worker message `transfers` with one complex's route nodes, answered from a finished
round like `station`: every population and period of the worker added up by pair. Client
`transfers(members)` adds the workers', names both ends by station and line (both runs together)
and returns `ComplexTransfers`; `game/demand.ts` `complexTransfers(members)`. Asked when the
complex map shows and at each published round; never pushed with results.

**Drawing: line to line, not bundle to bundle.** The plan above (bundle centres) would give the
real save's Penn Station and Grand Central three bundles each, so at most three curves, and they
would not say which lines change. Curves join one line's band to another's instead, from where
the two bands come nearest across a gap, or middle to middle when they cross or come within
16 px, drawn over the platforms with a halo: the busiest 8 between different bundles, both
directions together, none under 2% of the complex's changes, 1.5-8 px by square root. Browser
runs: first under the platforms from band middles (on Penn they vanished under the commuter
bundle), then on top from the nearest points (crossing platforms made a knot of loops at the
crossing), then the rule above. Changes inside one bundle go into each line's hover with the line's total changes.
8 holds: Penn's and Times Sq's 8th curve is still 19k and 22k a day.

## Known limits

- Bundle widths are a diagram, not metres: Penn's commuter bundle (about 17 services) draws about
  45 px across, its width set by the lines rather than by the platforms' real spread.
- A label that finds no clear spot is left out (Grand Central-42 St); the names are in the list
  under the map.
- The map's per-platform riders are a day's total; it does not follow the clock's period.
