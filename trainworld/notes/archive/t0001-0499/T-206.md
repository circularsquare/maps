# T-206: trains join and leave at the ends of a line

Anita (2026-10-12): "when we change the number of trains on a line, the trains instantly teleport
around to evenly space. this breaks the illusion of there being real trains. we should only add or
remove trains at the ends of lines".

## Why they jumped

Trains were never trains: the drawn trips were each period's departure lattice (period start plus
k headways, both directions), a pure function of the line's trains an hour. A new train count gave
a new headway, so every departure of the day moved, including the ones already on the line, and
every train jumped to its new spacing. Edits that changed a line's profile (capacity holds moving
with the new count, or track edits) also moved running trains, by tens of metres (58 m in the
test line going from 6 to 9 trains, whose holds grew 171 s). Period changes did not jump, but at
each one the old trains ran out their trips and vanished at the terminals while the new period's
trains appeared there, often in pairs (the new lattice's first departure right behind the old
one's last).

## What it does now

The design as built is docs/track-and-service.md 6.3 ("Departures", "Trains join and leave at the
ends"); the decision is in DECISIONS 2026-10-12. In short:

- **Eras**: an edit at game time T that changes a running line's pattern records the old pattern
  until T (`Service::eras`, `note_changes`). Departures before T keep it, later ones follow the
  new. The clock worker passes the game time (`TrackApi.set_now`) before each edit and each hour's
  trips. Eras are pruned once no train of theirs can be drawn; never saved.
- **Shifts**: a train running when its line's profile changed is drawn from where it was
  (`Service::shifts`, worked out by `before_edit` / `note_changes`): the same place on the new
  profile, or the same stop with the dwell it had left; a train standing at its terminal stands
  as long again after the new profile's arrival.
- **Hand-over at the terminals** (`Service::hand_over`): a train that has finished its run and
  turnaround takes the next departure from that terminal if it comes within that departure's
  headway (at most an hour), standing at the platform until then (the trip's `end`, sent to the
  renderer as `ends` beside the trips and read by the train shader as `a_end`). A departure no
  train waits for is a train entering service; a surplus train leaves at the end of its
  turnaround. A train that had left service before a change never comes back (`Era::drawn`).
- **No pairs**: at any change of pattern (period or edit) a departure closer than half a headway
  after the old pattern's last is left out.
- **The boarding account** reads the same departures (`Service::run_deps`). While a line has eras
  its account's day starts six hours before the latest change, so the trains drawn around the
  change are the ones in the account: a train not yet in service carries nobody, and trains that
  left before the change keep exactly their loads (a test checks this).

## Limits

- A reload re-spaces a line once (eras are not saved; saves are Anita's only, so not worth a format
  change).
- Picking, the hover tag and the train inspector follow a train standing past its trip
  (`tripAt`'s `end`, from `renderer.tripEnds`).
- A line whose stops change keeps its running trains on their departure times but on the new
  path, so they can move: that is a reroute.
