# T-052: Short-turn and single-track station capacity

Short-turns at a station with track continuing beyond it now share capacity with through services. The incoming terminating run occupies its arrival platform for turnaround + 60 seconds. It competes with stopping and passing trains on that platform, replacing the isolated pooled terminus resource at through stations. On double track, per-movement crossover resources model 90 seconds per reversing or conflicting through move: each reversal conflicts with the opposite through track and reversals arriving from the other side. Each through movement sees only the reversals crossing it. Independent opposing through directions never load each other's crossover resource. Same-side through trains already share the arrival platform's full turnaround occupancy.

Crossover delay is a queue-increment approximation: the pooled conflict queue minus the owners-only queue, clamped at zero. Ordinary track/platform resources already charge queues between trains making the same move. Subtracting that baseline avoids charging it twice, makes added through delay tend to zero as reversing frequency tends to zero, and preserves opposite-side reversal conflicts as nonowner traffic. A crossover resource is inactive at demand levels without active reversing traffic or active owners. Existing legacy-frequency delay rounding still applies; allocated-count resources retain continuous delay for convergence.

Single-track geometry remains single track: one shared platform resource covers both directions, stopping and passing trains, and full short-turn occupancy. There is no automatic second platform or passing loop, and no extra crossover resource on one track. The original T-052 note repeated that one-platform behavior as a gap even though normal through trains already shared it correctly; the missing sharing was terminating trains bypassing the platform resource. No construction cost or station track count changes.

True dead ends retain pooled turnaround capacity (turnaround + 60 seconds)/platform tracks. Only constructed edge ends classify a station as through or determine its active track count: a double-track blueprint extension cannot reclassify a single-track terminus or double its capacity. Reversal resources use the existing Terminus kind, so markers and delay inspectors keep their current API. An internal Turnback tag remembers the prefix of reversing users, allowing per-demand-level activity gating and normal node-local invalidation. Planning geometry and construction costs retain their existing APIs.

The specification's single-track passing-point wording conflicted with its explicit route/platform track count. Section 6.1 now states the consistent implemented rule: one shared platform, with sections split at stations and no implicit second track or passing loop. T-040's historical notes retain their earlier wording.
