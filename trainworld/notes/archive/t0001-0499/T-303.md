# T-303: demand loading and responsiveness, stage 1 (measure and propose)

Anita, 2026-10-14: "demand loading performance is definitely becoming an issue"; bubbles feel slow
"in the first like minute or so of playing"; T-122's frozen copy per worker rejected (40-60 MB a
worker, growing with open cities): "try to predict things that are likely to be looked into by the
player ... prioritizing things that are currently on screen ... be smart about what we don't
precompute". Later the same day, on a new Toronto line whose trains looked full while the panel
said "available after the next demand update": "ideally we like super fast track whatever trains
we're looking at. we should definitely prioritize whatever is the currently selected city, at
least."

This stage measures and proposes. Nothing is built. Folds in T-301 (packs never close) and T-302
(zone files on demand).

## How it was measured

The served build (localhost:8800/trainworld/, commits 01c619c and, from the Toronto runs on,
2b854b5, which changed no demand code), headless Chrome with its own profile, 1400x900, the real
New York save (`T-007-real-nyc.save`) and new games. Script: `checks/t303-measure.mjs` (script removed 2026-10-24, in git history) (modes
`prepare`, `timeline`, `clicks`, `toronto`, `pan`); outputs in
`../artifacts/trainworld/notes-outputs/t303/`. Bubbles on (Destinations), weekday shown.

Times come from `?debug=1`: `twDemand.log` (each solve: sent, first estimate, final, per-period
stage times, setup, WASM memory per worker), `twDemand.start` (each pack: requested, downloaded,
open in every worker), `client.qlog` (each query's replies) and `twViews.stats.commuters.farAt`
(a click's far end handed to the overlay). Memory is each worker's WASM linear memory
(`wasm_memory_bytes`), not the browser's total.

**Noise.** Other agents were building on the machine throughout (40-50% CPU busy before the runs).
Repeats agreed within about 15%: 13 page loads of the New York save gave a first estimate at
9.9-12.4 s and a final at 19.5-22.8 s.

## The pool

Six demand workers: five that solve and one auxiliary ("aux") that holds no period. The rule is
half the logical cores less one, at most five (one per period of the day), so this 16-thread
machine gets five, an 8-thread laptop three, a 4-thread one a single worker. Each solving worker
holds every open pack's commute and everyday layers and the regional market, and solves its period
for all of them. The aux worker holds each open pack's commute layer only and answers the bubbles'
cell sums. Besides these there is the clock worker, the bubble worker, and one more bubble worker
for each nearby zone shown.

### The first minute, New York save, no panning

At the starting view (Midtown, zoom 11.2) and zoomed out to 9.4 nothing new opens: New York's pack
covers everything on screen, and New Haven and Litchfield are already open because the save's
stations reach them. So there is exactly one solve in the first minute, the startup one.

A click on the biggest Destinations bubble, from the mouse down to the far end drawn:

| Clicked at | Wait | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 5 s (no bubbles until 11.3-11.6 s; clicked then) | 9.4 s, 10.0 s | the far end lands with the final round at about 21 s |
| 15 s | 5.4 s, 7.0 s | the same |
| 30 s | 0.38 s, 0.33 s | idle |
| 60 s | 0.43 s, 0.41 s | idle |

During the solve the click is asked again at every round published and refused every time: by the
time a round is published, every worker has started the next round and no longer holds the one
shown. **So "the first minute" is the startup solve's 20 s, and a click before then waits for its
end.** Once idle, a click at zoom 11.2 takes 0.33-0.43 s: each worker's everyday answer
(`purposes`) 150-200 ms for these big zoomed-out bubbles (T-285 measured 18-21 ms on smaller ones
at zoom 12.3), the longer trips queued behind it 40-50 ms, then `flows` 20-30 ms.

### A new line in Toronto (Anita's case)

A five-station line up Yonge St (Union to Eglinton, tunnel, 10/8/6 trains), built with the New York
save loaded (New York, New Haven and Litchfield open). Times from the `addLine` edit:

| Case | Line's first figures | Final |
|---|---|---|
| Toronto open and shown for a while, then the line built | **17.4 s, nothing before** | 17.4 s |
| built 0.8 s after arriving in Toronto (Toronto just opened) | 7.7 s (an estimate) | 21.3 s |
| (Toronto just viewed, no line: opening re-solve) | | 17.7 s |

- The Toronto line's riders come only from Toronto's people (Toronto is outside the regional
  market, 500 km from every other pack), yet every Toronto edit re-solves New York, New Haven,
  Litchfield and the market as well. Those are nearly all of the 17 s.
- In the first case nothing shows for 17 s because after an edit only a final result replaces
  figures (T-232); the new line has no earlier figures, so it has none at all until then. With more
  packs open (after a pan) that wait is 22-28 s.
- **Why the trains looked full**: a train with no figures has no fill value, and the renderer then
  draws its body in the line's colour (`renderer.ts`, `v_fill < 0`), which is exactly how a full
  train is drawn. The train panel says "available after the next demand update".
- Memory: opening Toronto took each solving worker from 354 to 433 MB, the line to 490 MB.

### Downloads at startup (T-302)

Every page load fetches all 14 zones' boundaries (0.6 MB, for the focus and the reach test) and
water masks (14 headers and 14 Range requests, 4.2 MB, before the clock worker reports ready),
whatever the game needs. On localhost all of it is done by 0.35 s, so it costs nothing measurable
here; on the published host it is 42 requests before the map's track can draw, and it grows with
every zone added.

## Proposal, ranked

The principle: work for what is on screen and likely to be looked at first, and never re-solve for
something that cannot change a figure. Sizes are build stages, each its own task and report.

### 1. Zones no station reaches stay out of the solve

Today a pack opened for its bubbles (nearby) or because it is viewed joins every solving worker and
re-solves everything, though with no station within 4.5 km its trains carry nobody and nothing
else changes. Instead: such a pack opens **in the aux worker only**, with its everyday layer, and
its bubbles come from a no-rail pass there (walking and driving only, which do not depend on the
network). No re-solve, nothing added to the solving workers. When a station is built within reach,
the pack joins the solving workers as it does today (loading bar, solve waits for it).

- Gain: panning or zooming over unbuilt zones costs 0.3-2 s of opening in one worker instead of a
  22-28 s re-solve that blocks clicks; solves keep the size of what is built (after this pan, 16-20
  s to final instead of 27 s, stages 3 s instead of 5-7 s).
- Memory: each such pack once (its everyday layer, 10-90 MB) instead of five times. After this pan,
  about 350 MB per solving worker instead of about 950 MB; the aux worker perhaps 400-500 MB.
- Results: every figure of built cities identical (T-149 already checked this on unreached packs).
  The zone's own bubbles should match what a solve gives with no subzones; stage (a) checks it to
  the bit. Its longer trips need the market, which the aux worker does not hold: a click on such a
  zone's bubble asks the solving workers for those (they hold the market and answer between
  stages), or the aux worker opens the market too (about 50 MB more, once). **Anita's choice** only
  if the second is wanted; I would ask the solving workers.
- Stages: (a) open unreached packs in aux only, no-rail bubbles, no re-solve; bit check against
  today's solved bubbles. (b) clicks on such zones answered there (everyday far end, longer trips).
  (c) spec and zones.md.

### 2. New and changed lines show figures as soon as any exist; unknown is never drawn as full

Small, mostly client-side, and it answers the Toronto complaint directly.

- **Trains with no figures are drawn as unknown, not full.** **Anita's choice of look.** Options:
  (a) the body striped in the line colour and white, read as "not worked out"; (b) the body in the
  empty colour with the line colour as an outline only; (c) the train drawn faded. I would take (a):
  (b) reads as an empty train and (c) as a hidden one, both also untrue. The hover tag says riders
  are being worked out. No animation.
- **The panel**: "Riders aboard" says "working out" with how far along the solve is ("round 2 of
  4"), and the line's estimate as soon as one exists ("about 1,200 aboard, refining") rather than
  nothing.
- **A line with no figures takes the solve's estimate when it lands**, while every other line keeps
  its last final figures until the final (T-232 kept for them). For the Toronto line that is about
  5-7 s instead of 17 s today, and under a second with item 3. **Anita's choice**: this relaxes
  T-232 ("figures change once per edit") for lines that had no figures; I recommend it, since a
  blank or wrong-looking train is worse than an estimate that is refined once.
- Gain: the misleading full train is gone at once; new lines show riders 10+ s sooner.
- Memory: none. Results: final figures unchanged; only what shows before them changes.
- Stages: one (renderer fill state, `OnBoard` and line panel text, the publish rule in
  `demandClient.ts` for lines missing from the shown result).

### 3. Solve separate clusters separately; an edit re-solves only its own

"Prioritize whatever is the currently selected city". Lines and populations fall into clusters
that share nothing: a cluster is the lines whose stations a population can reach, joined by shared
stations, complexes and the regional market. Today Toronto and the Northeast are two clusters, and
in a world save (T-304) London, Shanghai and New York will be many. A cluster's results depend only
on its own lines and populations: crowding sums loads per route node, and another cluster adds
exact zeros there. So:

- Each worker solves cluster by cluster and keeps each cluster's results. An edit marks only the
  clusters it touches dirty; only those re-solve, and each is published as soon as its own final
  round lands. The viewed (or selected) city's cluster goes first when several are dirty.
- Gain: a Toronto edit re-solves Toronto's people alone: an estimate in well under a second and a
  final in perhaps 1-3 s instead of 17-28 s (an estimate: Toronto's pack is a third of New York's
  and its network has five stations; stage (a) measures it). A Northeast edit is unchanged: New York,
  its neighbours and the market are one cluster through the market. In a world save every edit
  pays only for its own city, which T-304 will need anyway.
- Memory: about none; each cluster's result arrays are what the one network's are now, split.
- Results: identical by construction if the clusters really share nothing; a check solves the
  Toronto line with the New York network together and apart and compares every figure to the bit.
- Risk: the route node and station layout is the whole network's today (the clock worker's
  snapshot); clusters need either a layout per cluster or a mapping, in the worker and in
  `demandClient.ts` publish. This is the biggest item here.
- Stages: (a) the worker solves per cluster on the existing message (no change to the client),
  bit check, timing; (b) only dirty clusters re-solve, published per cluster; (c) the viewed or
  selected cluster first; spec.

### 4. Clicks during a solve, without copies

Measured: a click during the startup solve is refused at every publish and waits for the final
(5-10 s). With items 1-3 most solves get shorter or rarer, but the startup solve stays about 20 s.
Without keeping a copy, a worker can answer only about the round it holds now. **Anita's choice**:

- (a) While estimates are shown (startup, a city just opened), let a click's far end come from the
  newest round every worker holds, one round ahead of the bubbles it was clicked on; the view asks
  again when that round is published. Wait: up to one stage (3-4 s), about 2 s typically, instead
  of up to 10 s. The far end may differ from the near end by one crowding round (up to about 10%
  on crowded lines, under 1% elsewhere), and lines up again within seconds.
- (b) Leave it: clicks wait for the final, as now.

I would take (a) for startup only. After an edit the workers hold the new network, so the same
trick would show the edited network's far end on the old network's map; I would not do that, and
rely on items 1 and 3 to shorten those waits instead.
- Memory: none. Stages: one (worker `live` test, client merge accepting a common newer round).

### 5. Show the last final result at once on reload

Keep the last final published result (lines, stations, the top bar's split, the bubbles' cell
sums; a few MB) in IndexedDB beside the save, keyed by the network, the packs and the build. On
reload, if the key matches, show it at once: figures and bubbles at about 2 s instead of 10-12 s,
and the clock could run. The workers still solve as now, and clicks still wait for them (or item
4). Results identical (it is the same result). **Anita's choice** whether the clock should run on
the cached result before the workers agree. Stages: one or two.

### 6. Fewer crowding rounds before the first publish: not recommended

Startup is free flow 4.4 s plus three rounds of 3.1 s. Two rounds, or starting the rounds from the
save's last crowding, would save 3-6 s but change results (T-022: the third round still moves New
York's lines up to 10%). Item 5 gives the visible part of the gain with identical results.
**Anita's choice** only if she wants it anyway.

### 7. Close packs no longer needed (rest of T-301)

After item 1 the solving workers hold only reached packs, which close only if their construction is
removed. The aux worker's unreached packs pile up as she pans: close those off screen for a few
minutes, and since WASM memory never shrinks, restart the aux worker when it passes a cap (say
400 MB); it reopens what is on screen in under a second each. Gain: memory bounded on long pans.
Results identical. Stages: one, after item 1.

### 8. Zone files on demand (T-302), and the startup setup (T-281)

- T-302: ship one small index of every zone's bounding box and simplified outline for the focus and
  reach test, and fetch a zone's boundary and water mask only when it is on screen or reached. No
  gain measurable on localhost; on the published host it removes 40-odd requests from before the
  track draws, and it is needed before the world has hundreds of zones. Results identical. One
  stage.
- T-281: the walk search on opening a save costs about 1.0 s of the 1.6-2.6 s setup in every
  worker, on the path to the first estimate. Halving it takes about 0.5 s off startup. Results
  identical. One stage.

### Smaller things seen

- Packs opened one by one start one solve each (Worcester then Boston, 0.8 s apart: the first
  solve thrown away). Wait until the packs requested by one view change are all open before
  solving. Folded into item 1 for unreached packs; still worth it for reached ones.
- An idle click on a zoomed-out bubble spends 150-200 ms per worker on the everyday far end; the
  longer trips wait behind it in the same worker. Not worth a task yet.
