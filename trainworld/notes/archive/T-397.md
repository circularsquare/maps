# T-397: everyday samples per cell scaled to its trips

Each live (cell, purpose) held 16 destination samples (airports and medical 2). Now a cell holds
one sample per 8 trips a day for that purpose, rounded up, clamped to 2-16 (airports and medical
stay at 2). The samples keep their seeds and stratified offsets (`(k + offset) / n`), so a cell
with 16 samples gets exactly the samples it had; the count depends only on the cell's budget, so
results are the same from load to load. Per purpose, `first` holds where each live cell's samples
start (4 bytes a live cell); the sample loops go in order and find their cell from the last answer.

## Rules tried

Natively, a temporary probe (scratchpad `t397/probe.rs.txt`): New York on the real track
(`nyc_real_track.json`, 1,756 stations, 109 lines) with the commute engine and the market beside
it, all five periods, free flow and three crowding rounds; Windham and Mumbai opened alone (no
network reaches them), compared by zone trip ends. "Moves" = more than 5% against 16 samples.

Trips, walk share and trip length by purpose move under 0.05% in New York and Mumbai for every rule
but 0.5 sqrt (up to 0.33% in school trip length); Windham up to 0.1% trips, 0.2 points walk share
and 1.2% social trip length under trips / 8. What does move is the activity end of a single zone:
under trips / 8, 24% of New York's zones with 1,000 or more everyday trip ends arriving (9% in
Mumbai, 17% in Windham) move more than 5%, worst -33%. Station boardings sum many zones and do not:
the largest station change is 287 of 11,756 boardings a day, the largest line change 400 of 668,563.
Home-end totals hold within 3% in every zone (each home's trips are its budget, whatever the count).

Chosen: **trips / 8**. It holds station boardings and line riders as well as the densest rule
tried (2 sqrt, which keeps half again as many slots) while keeping 31% of the slots; the sparser
rules start moving stations (0.5 sqrt: 2% of stations past 5%).

## Memory and time

T-398's probe (scratchpad `t397/probe398.rs.txt`), one solving worker natively (periods 0, 1, 4,
free flow and three crowding rounds, then a layout edit, a train-count edit and the first network
again), 16 samples against trips / 8:

| | New York peak | steady | open everyday | set up everyday (first / edit) | nine cities peak | steady | nine openings |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 samples | 259 MB | 225 MB | 1085 ms | 402 / 327 ms | 469 MB | 431 MB | 3.96 s |
| trips / 8 | 198 MB | 166 MB | 655 ms | 215 / 189 ms | 350 MB | 315 MB | 1.59 s |

So about -60 MB a New York worker (the survey's -80 counted slots only; the pairs fall less,
848k to 501k) and -118 MB with nine cities open; New York's everyday opening 40% shorter, the nine
openings 60%. Solve times unchanged (24-25 s a full network either way; the pass works on pairs).
Rail a day on the probe's network (commute, everyday and market, five periods): 6,953,499 against
6,953,088.

## Checks

`cargo test --release --features demand` 172 passed (two fixtures adjusted: the school test now
expects `sample_count` samples, 12 for 90 trips; the parks test's home has 1,000 residents so it
keeps sixteen social samples); `everyday_real_network` (selections against visiting every sample)
passes; `npm run wasm`; `npm run check` clean; t066 and t149 worker checks pass; smoke passed,
demand final 7,596,985 rail trips a day (7,597,261 before, -0.004%).

Noise left for a player to see: the destinations of one sparse home cell are fewer (a cell of 50
residents sends its errands to 2 cells, not 16), and one zone's arriving everyday trips can move
a third. A view that shows a single cell's or zone's everyday trips would show that; station
and line figures do not.
