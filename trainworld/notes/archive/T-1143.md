# T-1143: the fare graph with three nodes

Anita, 2026-10-24: three nodes (base fare at 0 km moving up and down only, 20 km, 100 km), drags on
one axis only with hover arrows, and a slope that stays put when zooming.

## How the sim charges a bent curve (restored from T-883, c0fb0ac^)

- `set_fares(base, perKm)` is the first stretch (node 1 to node 2) as a line: the base once a trip
  in mode choice, the rate on each ride edge in route search.
- `set_fare_curve(knots)` (empty for a straight line) puts `FareCurve` and `rn_km` on each period
  graph. `DestSearch::run` adds `extra(km)` to each station pair's route cost, `km` being the
  pair's expected fare km under its strategy (`kernel::trip_km`, a free function over a stored
  strategy now, so the assignment can run it too). `SkimKey` holds the curve and `rn_km` again.
- The trip explorer spreads each route's extra over its rides by km (`Leg::km`), as before.

## Demand and income agree (T-887's gap closed)

Before T-1114 income took each line's trips at their mean length (T-887). Now the assignment puts
each station pair's extra (trips x `extra(K)`, K its expected km, the same figure mode choice
charged) on the route nodes its riders first board, split over the departures by frequency as the
assignment splits them (`kernel::board_extra`, called from `DestAssign::walk` and everyday's
`assign_lanes`; `Loads::rn_fare_x`, averaged over crowding rounds like the loads). Each population
returns it in US$ as `fare_extra(q)`; the demand worker sums it per route node like `seg`
(`fareX` in the result), the client sums it per line and period (`LineDemand.fareX`), and income
adds it to the base and rate (`money.ts`), regions with the base by boarding stop
(`regionMoney.ts`).

Left as is:
- The trips home ride the reverse strategy, so their K is the reverse pair's (mode choice charged
  the outward pair's). The income is what the assigned riders' pairs cost; the difference is in
  route asymmetry only.
- Weekend and holiday days scale each route node's extra by its boardings' day factor, where the
  seg and boardings scale purpose by purpose; the weekday is exact.
