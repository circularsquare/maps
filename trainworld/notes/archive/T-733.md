# T-733: simpler track (crossovers, single track, platform length), for discussion

Anita, 2026-10-19: "do we really need crossovers? would we be able to simplify quad track so that
crossovers are sorta autoplaced. what is single track used for? i struggle to see the use case, i
think we can maybe leave it off for now. platform length also?"

Crossovers (option 2) and hiding single track are built (2026-10-19, rules in
docs/track-and-service.md 6.1), and so is the train length per line (6.1 "Train length",
ui-inspectors.md). The detail below is the
design as written before.

## For Anita: the short version

**Crossovers.** The game already places them for you in the two places most lines need one: where
quad track meets double track (the two pairs join in a junction at each end of a quad stretch), and
where a line turns back (assumed in the capacity sums, drawn since T-658). The crossover tool is only
for changing between the local and express pair in the middle of a quad stretch, or joining two
separate routes drawn side by side. None of the imported real networks has one. *Recommendation:*
drop the tool from the Build tab and put one crossover automatically just past each quad station, so
a line can change pairs between any two neighbouring quad stations. The sim keeps crossovers as they
are, so nothing breaks. Medium size, mostly in one sim function.

**Single track.** It is cheaper track (0.6x the price) where both directions share one track, with
stations as the only passing places. Its use is a quiet branch or a long rural line, where you trade
capacity for money: a 2 km single-track stretch at 60 km/h carries about 10 trains an hour each way
before it is full. None of the imported real networks has any (every line there is double). It is
also a trap: the setting is remembered, so a player who picks it once draws single track until they
notice, and there is no way to turn single into double afterwards except redrawing. *Recommendation:*
take it out of the Build tab now (two lines of code), keep it in the sim for old saves and later
imports, and bring it back if regional or rural building wants a cheap option.

**Platform length.** It does more than it looks: it is the game's only control over **train
length**. Every new line runs the longest train that fits the shortest platform it stops at (20 m
cars), and train length sets how many riders a train holds (160 a car, crushed), what the fleet
costs (US$2.5M a car) and what running costs (per car-km). So a quiet line on 200 m platforms runs
10-car trains and pays for all 10. Removing the setting outright would remove that choice too.
*Recommendation:* take the stepper out of the Build tab (stations go in at 200 m, 10 cars) and give
each line a train length setting instead, in cars, capped by its shortest platform. Later, platforms
could lengthen themselves to the longest train that stops. The imported real networks already work
this way (each line has its own cars, and platforms were sized from them).

## Questions for Anita

1. Crossovers: one automatic crossover just past every quad station (my pick), or only where a line
   you make actually needs to change pairs (no wasted money, but you cannot see in advance where
   changing is possible)? And should the automatic ones cost money? At their full price (two
   junctions) they add US$6.75M at ground level and US$22.5M underground to every quad station,
   about as much again as the station itself underground.
2. Single track: hide it now and bring it back for rural or regional lines later, as recommended?
3. Platform length: is train length per line (a stepper in the line panel, cars) the control you
   want, with platforms staying 200 m unless lengthened in the station panel? Or should platforms
   grow on their own when a line wants longer trains (more work; a platform that cannot grow, say
   against a junction, would cap the train)?

## Detail

### Crossovers today

- **Sim.** A crossover is two diagonal edges in an X between two parallel tracks 6-15 m apart,
  120 m long, 50 km/h, flagged `crossover` (`sim/src/track/net.rs:147`, saved as edge flag 32,
  `save.rs:142`). Built by `TrackApi::add_crossover` / `crossover_op`
  (`sim/src/track/wasm.rs:1677`, `:1736`): splits both tracks 60 m either side of the click and
  adds the diagonals; refused near a track end, junction or station, or with nothing parallel.
  Constants in `params.rs:27-30`. Routing needs nothing special: `Network::find_path`
  (`net.rs:1648`) is a fastest-path search over edges, so a line uses a crossover when it is the
  quickest way to its next stop. Capacity: each end node is a junction (90 s conflicting moves);
  the X's own flat crossing is in its price. Price: two junctions, 0.5 km of double track at its
  level (`JUNCTION_KM`, `params.rs:71`), US$6.75M at grade, US$22.5M at -1.
- **Already automatic.** (a) Quad meets double: the express pair swings in and meets the drawn pair
  at a junction at each end of a quad stroke drawn from or to other track
  (docs/track-and-service.md 6.1 "Quad track"; doubling up does the same at a junction end).
  (b) Turn-backs: a line ending at a through station is priced as a turn across the other track
  (`capacity.rs:483-490`, the `Turnback` resources at `:520-547`), a dead-end terminus as pooled
  platform tracks (`capacity.rs:491-494`); no crossover edge is needed and T-658 stage d draws one.
- **App.** A Build tab tool (`ui/tabs.tsx:55`, key 6), its own ghost and refusals
  (`map/tools.ts`), its own inspector title and Remove/Construct (`ui/inspector/others.tsx:649-699`),
  no flyover at its ends (`others.tsx:805`), nodes that cannot be dragged.
- **What it costs the player.** One more tool and a placement rule to learn (6-15 m beside, 60 m
  of plain track each way, not near a station or junction). The real problem is the hidden rule
  behind it: a line can only change pairs where a crossover or a quad end is. A line stopping at
  a local platform and then an express platform with nothing between them either cannot be routed
  or detours, and nothing tells the player that a crossover is the fix.
- **Counted.** Crossover diagonals in every save on disk: 0 (T-007-real-nyc, T-125-northeast, and
  the realnet saves for London, Paris, Seoul, Sendai, Tokyo and Toronto). Anita's own browser slots
  are not on disk, so not counted.

### Crossovers: ways to automate, and their size

1. **Hide the tool only** (tiny: `tabs.tsx` tool list and `TOOL_KEYS`, the hint in `Toolbar.tsx`).
   Lines change pairs only at quad ends. Saves keep their crossovers.
2. **One crossover past each quad station** (recommended; medium). When a station goes on quad
   (`station_at`'s twin branch, `wasm.rs:1405`) or quad is made by doubling up (`double_up_op`,
   `:1544`), call `crossover_op` at a point just beyond the platform (half the platform plus about
   70 m from the station, in the drawn direction) and add it in the same edit and undo step. Skip
   it silently where there is no room. One per station is enough: between any two neighbouring quad
   stations there is then exactly one place to change, used in either direction. Open points:
   removing the station removes its crossover; a later longer platform must not run into it (the
   station panel's stepper is refused as it is today against a joint, or the crossover moves).
   Routing, capacity and drawing need nothing new. An unused crossover adds no delay to trains
   running straight past (its ends are junction nodes, and straight-through moves do not conflict).
3. **Placed by the line tool when a line needs one** (medium to large). When a line's next stop is
   on the other pair, find the last stretch of plain parallel track before it and add a crossover
   with the line edit. Nothing wasted, but line edits start changing track and money, and the
   player cannot see beforehand where changing is possible.
4. **Pair changes with no geometry** (large). Let `find_path` hop between twin stations, priced as
   a capacity resource like a turn-back and drawn like T-658d. Needs the run geometry to step 8 m
   sideways with no edge under it. Not worth it next to 2.

**What breaks:** nothing in saves for 1-3 (the sim keeps crossover edges; existing ones load and
work). Imported networks have none. Checks: `checks/t658-lanes.mjs` places a crossover through the
sim API (still works); sim tests such as `crossover_joins_the_pairs` keep testing the edit 2 uses.
Capacity: unchanged for lines that do not change pairs; with 2, a line that does gets a 50 km/h
stretch and junction conflicts at the crossover, as now. T-657's terminal question is not affected:
turn-back capacity never used a crossover edge. T-657's tail tracks would fit the same pattern as
2 (a station setting drawn by the game, not track the player lays).

### Single track today

- **Sim.** `tracks = 1` on an edge (`ClockEdge.tracks`). Capacity: a single-track section between
  passing points is one resource for both directions, occupied for its run time plus 60 s
  (`capacity.rs:389-417`, `SINGLE_TRACK_MARGIN_S`, `params.rs:100`); a single-track station has one
  shared platform track (`capacity.rs:456`, `:488`) and no passing loop is added. Price 0.6x track
  (`SINGLE_TRACK_COST`, `params.rs:66`), stations 0.7x (`:70`). Doubling up refuses it (`Tracks`).
  There is no edit that turns single into double.
- **App.** The Single/Double/Quad choice in the Build tab (`ui/tabs.tsx:104`), remembered per
  browser (`game/ui.ts:183`). Drawing: one track down the middle close in, with a 40 m taper where
  it meets double (T-658, `map/network.ts`); "Single track" in the track inspector
  (`others.tsx:661`); its own capacity kind (`game/capacityKinds.ts`).
- **What it costs the player.** One three-way choice on every drawing session, and a capacity rule
  that is easy to fall foul of: a 2 km section at 60 km/h is about 120 s of running, so 180 s a
  train both ways together, 20 an hour in all, 10 each way, and a long section between stations
  far fewer. Remembered per browser, so a stray click persists.
- **Counted.** Single-track edges in every save on disk: 0 (same saves as above; all edges are
  double). The real-network importer (`sim/src/bin/realsave.rs`) gives every line its own double
  track and never makes single.
- **Hiding it** (tiny): drop `[1, "Single"]` from the Segmented in `tabs.tsx:104` and read a
  remembered 1 as 2 in `ui.ts:183`. Everything else stays, so saves with single track load and run
  as before. Removing it from the sim as well would touch capacity, costs, drawing and tests for
  no gain.

### Platform length today

- **Sim.** Stored per station, 60-400 m in 20 m steps (`params.rs:44-46`). It decides:
  - **train length**: `build_line` (`service.rs:371-375`) gives a line `min(platform)/20 m` cars,
    or the line's own `cars` if set and smaller. The app always sends `cars = 0` for a new line
    (`workers/clock.worker.ts:1323`), so in practice train length is the shortest platform.
  - through that, **riders a train holds** (`CRUSH_PER_CAR` 160, `boarding.rs:27`, also the demand
    side's crowding, `demand/api.rs:427`), **fleet cost** (US$2.5M a car) and **running cost** (per
    car-km);
  - **station price**: `0.4 + 0.6 x length / 200` of the 200 m price (`cost.rs:241-243`);
  - where trains stop at a dead end (the platform's middle, `service.rs:345-351`) and where a
    turning train is held (`capacity.rs:479-480`);
  - placement: the platform must fit on plain track (joints under it are merged, crossings alert;
    T-244, T-622), so longer platforms are harder to place.
- **App.** A stepper in the Build tab (`tabs.tsx:113`, default 200, not remembered) and another in
  the station panel (`others.tsx:343`; on a built station the longer platform is paid at once).
  The line panel says "N car trains" (`LineInspector.tsx:523`) but nowhere says that platforms set
  it.
- **What it costs the player.** A setting before every station whose main effect (train length,
  so capacity and running cost) shows up somewhere else and is never explained. A player who
  shortens one platform to fit a tight spot quietly shortens every train on every line through it.
- **Counted.** Platform lengths in the real saves are sized from each line's cars by the importer
  (`realsave.rs:51-52`): New York 60-260 m (757 of 1,756 at 260 m, the 13-car lines), London
  60-240 m, Paris 60-220 m, Tokyo 60-220 m, Toronto 60-320 m. Each imported line carries its own
  `cars`, so the imports already treat train length as the line's.

### Platform length: options and size

1. **Fixed for new stations, Build tab stepper gone** (tiny). Stations go in at 200 m. The station
   panel's stepper stays for the rare case. Train length still follows platforms.
2. **Train length on the line** (recommended; small to medium). A cars stepper in the line panel,
   1 up to what the shortest platform fits, default the most that fits (today's behaviour). The
   sim already has `line_cars` and caps it (`service.rs:373-374`); the work is the line edit
   message carrying cars, the stepper, and a hover when the platform caps it ("The platform at X
   fits 8 cars"). Combined with 1.
3. **Platforms follow the longest train** (medium, later). When a line asks for more cars than a
   platform fits, the platform lengthens as a blueprint change, priced on Construct; where it
   cannot (a junction, crossing or track end in the way) the train is capped and the line says
   where.
4. **Fixed everywhere, no train length choice** (small, not recommended): every train 10 cars;
   quiet lines can no longer save running cost, busy lines cannot grow past 1,600 riders a train.

**What breaks:** none of 1-3 changes saves (platforms and line cars are already stored; `cars = 0`
still means "most that fits"). Imported networks keep their platforms and cars. Checks: the smoke
test's counts do not depend on the Build tab stepper. Capacity: trains per hour are not affected
(track and platform occupancy do not depend on train length); train length only moves where a
held train waits and how long a train keeps a speed limit (`profile::widen`, `service.rs:357`),
so run times shift by seconds when a line's cars change. Demand: unchanged unless the
player changes a line's cars, which then changes crowding as a platform change does today.
