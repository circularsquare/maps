## What the dot is

The schedule's crowding dot (`CrowdDots`, LineInspector.tsx; `Boarding::crowding` fields 9 and 10)
is the average wait for a train with room **of the riders at platforms where trains come full**
(average wait there at least 60 s), a rider turned away counting 30 minutes, pooled over the
level's periods by those riders. Riders at platforms with room do not count. So the dot measures
how bad it is where it is bad, not how many riders it is bad for.

## Cause

1. **The dot's pooling, mostly.** On both lines every level-wide measure says High is worse:

   | Line | Level | Dot | Avg extra wait, all riders | Share turned away |
   |---|---|---:|---:|---:|
   | NJCL #82 | High | 8.2 | 2.60 min | 8.7% |
   | NJCL #82 | Medium | 15.0 | 1.95 min | 6.5% |
   | BNTNM #94 | High | 9.4 | 4.21 min | 14.0% |
   | BNTNM #94 | Medium | 10.9 | 2.34 min | 7.8% |

   The dot is close to 30 min x (turned away / riders at busy platforms). In the evening peak the
   overload is spread over many platforms (46-62% of riders), in the midday it piles up at a few
   (18-28%), so fewer riders turned away still give a higher dot. The morning peak has no busy
   platforms, so it neither helps nor dilutes High: High's dot is the evening peak's alone.
2. **Midday really is busy on lines with many everyday riders.** Errands (55% out, 49% back),
   social (35%, 25%) and school returns (82%) leave in 10-16. On 41 of 102 lines midday riders per
   hour match or beat the morning peak's. Its flows run both ways and are shorter, so its busiest
   stretch beats the morning peak's on only 4 lines and both peaks' on none. On NJCL and BNTNM the
   midday is overloaded (load/cap 1.05 and 1.15, 4/10 and 10/23 trains full): the trains really
   are short then, only a bit less short than in the evening peak.
3. **Not the busiest-hour rule**: Medium's factors (1.15, 1.3) are lower than the peaks' 1.4, so
   that rule favours Medium. **Not the train dealing**: same trains/h in both, departures by the
   same rule; midday has more trains in total only because it is 6 hours.
4. **The evening 20-24 is never the problem**: no same-train line has a busy platform there.
5. No bug: the pooling is T-1255's design (DECISIONS 2026-10-25) and works as written.

## Options and their effect on these figures

- **A. Shift the boundaries** (say High 6-10 and 15-20). Midday's crowding is at its middle hours
  (the tent peaks at 13:00), not its shoulders, so moving 15-16 into High leaves the midday's
  busiest hour where it is; the dots would barely move (estimated, not run: it needs every share
  table in demand-timing.md re-derived for new hours and a re-solve). A fourth level for midday
  alone would let the player set it apart but changes nothing at the same trains. Not worth it.
- **B. Grade the dot over all the level's riders** (the average extra wait of every rider, or the
  share turned away). NJCL: High 2.60 / Medium 1.95 min (8.7% / 6.5% turned away); BNTNM: 4.21 /
  2.34 min (14.0% / 7.8%); High worse on both, and no change in order on the other 23. But this
  is the line-wide average T-1255 dropped on 2026-10-25 because it diluted a 7 cut to a third to
  5 minutes (DECISIONS), and the share turned away is the "riders against room" figure Anita
  ruled out on 2026-10-24. So B reopens two of her calls, for two lines out of 25.
- **C. Keep the dot, explain it better**: the hover lists each of the level's periods with a
  train-count fact ("10-16: 4 of 10 trains full, 2,479 riders went another way"), so the player
  sees Medium's red is the midday's and that the midday really is short of trains. Figures
  unchanged.

Pick: C. The Medium red on these lines is real (the midday turns away 2,479 and 6,506 riders a
weekday); what misleads is only the comparison with High, whose evening peak is worse but spread
over more platforms. Adding Medium trains is the right move for the player either way.

## Part 2: fewer or different service levels

### How levels and demand's periods are tied

`PERIOD_HOURS` (sim/src/track/params.rs) is both demand's five periods and the schedule's hours;
`PERIOD_LEVEL` says which of the three train counts runs in each period. Demand solves each period
on its own (its own graph and rounds, `demand/api.rs` `Period`), on its level's trains only, with
its own share tables (`TO_WORK`, `TO_HOME`, 14 purpose rows, the weekend tables), busiest-hour
factor and arrival shape. So a level must be a set of whole periods.

- **Re-mapping levels onto the existing periods** (only `PERIOD_LEVEL` and its copy in
  `game/periods.ts`, plus the docs and any test that assumes High is the two peaks) is cheap: no
  table changes, the same five solves.
- **A boundary inside a period** (22, 17 or 5) needs a new period: every share table re-derived for
  the new hours (NHTS hourly, `research/weekend/tables.py`), new factors and shapes, the boarding
  account, money and tests. Each period is one more solve: about +20% solve time and memory a
  period, so +40% for two new boundaries.
- **Two levels** means `LEVELS` from 3 to 2: every `tph[3]`, the save's three counts and its `LAU1`
  headway block, the schedule table. Or keep three slots and hide one.

### Old saves

The save holds three counts per line (and three headways). A re-map keeps them loading: each count
just runs at its level's new hours. Under High 6-20 a line's High count also runs 10-16 and its
Medium count only 20-24. On the real save 77 lines have High above Medium, so their midday trains
rise to the High count (no new cars: the High count is already each line's biggest). Two levels
would need a migration (or the third slot hidden). Saves are Anita's only, so none needed beyond that.

### What each option does

- **Two (Day/Night).** The confusion goes: one daytime dot. The one Day count has to carry the evening
  peak, so midday and evening run peak trains: +11% running cost at today's counts, or peaks short of
  trains if the player sets Day lower. The midday lever is lost. The biggest code change (`LEVELS` 3 to 2)
  and a new boundary at 22 (or Day runs to 24 with no new period).
- **Hers as written.** Puts the evening peak (17-20), the busiest period on most lines, into Medium.
  At today's counts the evening peak runs Medium trains: turned away 362k to 470k, and Medium's dot
  is above High's on 63 lines. The confusion gets worse, not better. Two new periods (17, 22) plus 5.
- **Hers, High to 20.** The confusion goes: Medium is now only the shoulders (5-6, 20-22), which are
  never busier than the peaks. Midday runs the High count: +9% cost, turned away 240k, rail trips
  +2.9%. Needs new periods at 5 and 22 (+40% solve time, every share table re-derived).
- **Three, no new periods.** The same crowding as High to 20 (it differs only at 5-6 and 22-24, where
  nobody is turned away now), for 2% more running cost, and only `PERIOD_LEVEL` changes.

### Best boundaries for the four-band shape

High from 6 to 20: 16-20 and 6-10 have the busiest stretches, midday's 143k turned away now come
from its lower Medium counts (21k on High counts), and 20 is where demand drops (20-24 needs a median third of the evening peak's room and turns away 2k a day
at today's Medium counts). Ending High at 17 puts the worst period in Medium; ending at 22 runs
peak trains through two hours that need a third of them. A Low start at 22 and a Medium hour at 5
save about 2% of running cost and cost two new demand periods (+40% solve time, all share tables
re-derived), so I'd leave them out: High 6-20, Medium 20-24, Low 0-6.

### The trade-off

Every option that ends the confusion does it by taking away the midday's own count. Midday needs
about half the evening peak's trains on a typical line, so at today's counts it then runs twice
the trains it needs: +11% running cost on the real save, for 2.9% more rail trips and a third of
the riders turned away. A player who wants to save money can only trim High, and that starves the
peaks. Keeping today's levels with option C's hover keeps the lever and explains the dot.

### Recommendation

If Anita wants fewer bands: **three levels, High 6-20, Medium 20-24, Low 0-6.** It ends the
High/Medium mix-up (0 lines), costs one constant and its docs to build, keeps old saves loading
(each count at its new hours), and adds no solve time. Otherwise keep today's levels with option C.
Her call, since it changes what the player can schedule.
