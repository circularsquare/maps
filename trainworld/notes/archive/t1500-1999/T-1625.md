# T-1625: the all-Japan save with services that follow MLIT's counts (2026-10-28)

`artifacts/trainworld/t737-japan-trains.save` rebuilt by `pipeline/realnet.py` (`net`, `geom`, the sim's
`realsave` bin, `gamesave`, city `jpn`) from `pipeline/realnet_n02.py`. 807 lines, 9,200 places (9,529
station nodes, one per line per stop), 16 Shinkansen lines on 3,458 km of high-speed track. Sandbox,
today's default fares (a new game's curve at the baseline country; the high-speed multiple at its
default 1.5), cash 6,000, fleet 12,671 cars and 1,930 high-speed cars. It replaces the save T-737
made (709 lines, 10,320 nodes).

So the game carries about half of Japan's rail volume even with services sized to the real counts. Most
of the shortfall is on conventional lines (0.41 of their counts; the Shinkansen come to 0.89). The
counts themselves, D x km over these 807 lines with the kind's median for the lines that have none,
add up to 945M a day, so the counts leave about 150M for MLIT's figure to cover (lines left out,
private railways whose real counts are above the median guess). Demand was not tuned to any of this.

## How the services are made (realnet_n02.py)

- **Counts per hop.** japanriders' `segments.geojson` (MLIT passing counts, people a day past a section,
  both ways; 475 of N02's 576 lines have them): each hop between two stops takes the count of the
  section its track lies nearest. A line with none takes the median count of the lines of its kind
  (that is 15,153 for private railways, 3,357 for JR).
- **Cut where the count changes** (`_split`): optimal partitioning of a route's hops by log2(count),
  pieces of at least 4 km, a cut costing what a twofold step between two 12 km halves gains. Each piece
  is its own line with its count D, the length-weighted median of its hops. Shinkansen are not cut: one
  line end to end at its median count, plus a line over any piece 30% busier than that at the difference
  (Tohoku: Shin-Aomori-Tokyo, Sendai-Utsunomiya, Utsunomiya-Omiya, Omiya-Tokyo).
- **Cars** = sqrt(D / 1000), at most 15 JR, 10 subway and private, 6 third sector, 2 tram, 8 monorail;
  Shinkansen 16 above 60,000 a day, 12 above 25,000, else 8.
- **Trains per level** (`_service`): one hour of a level's riders each way, D / 2 times the level's share
  of a weekday an hour, carried at 56 riders a car (35% of the 160 a car holds at crush), over the
  round trip, as whole trains: high at least 1, medium and low rounded. Shares an hour from the game's
  own period split (T-1616's dump: 83.0%, 15.0%, 2.0% of passenger-km over 14, 4 and 6 hours):
  5.93%, 3.75% and 0.33%. At most 24 trains an hour each way. The round trip uses average speeds
  (incl. dwell) calibrated against the track model: Shinkansen 156, JR 65, private 57, third sector 61,
  subway 37, tram 26, monorail 36 km/h; realsave prints the model's run times against these (-1%).
- **Shinkansen**: `hsr` and `fast` in the network JSON; `realsave` lays double high-speed track traced
  at the 320 km/h design radius (7,183 m) and sets the line to high-speed trains. In the game 122
  high-speed edges (3,458 km), top speeds 264-320 km/h on the long lines.
- **Whole trains**: `realsave` sets each line's `trains` straight from the JSON (the game's `ceil(tph x
  round trip)` would give a one-train line another); the header's fleet is the cars those need.
- **Routes shorter than 500 m** (two platforms of one station) are not lines; 50 stretches (713 km) whose
  count cannot reach 12 riders a car-km even at a train and a car join their busier neighbour
  (`_pieces`) or, when alone, are left out.

## Game runs, `pipeline/n02_trim.json`

The least service in the game is a train and a car for the whole high level (14 hours), which
shuttles a short or thin line every few minutes. Pass 1 (counts only, 896 lines, final in 976 s) left 175
lines under 10 riders a car-km: stubs of a few hundred metres, parallel tracks that riders skip, ghost
lines. `notes/T-1625/trim.py` reads a run's dump and cuts every line under 15 (12 in later rounds) to
the cars and trains that carry its riders at 25 a car-km, cars first, or leaves it out where even a
train and a car gives under 12. Entries are keyed by `op|line|first stop|last stop` and `realnet_n02.py`
reads them. Rounds: 175 under 10 (896 lines), 6 (819), 2 (811), **0 (807)**. The file has 315 entries:
226 cuts and 89 lines left out, together under 0.2% of rail trips (50,000 riders a day in round 1).

Left out are mostly stubs and branches of 0.1-5 km (the Yamanote, Tohoku, Tokaido and Chuo lines'
platform links at Shinjuku, Omiya, Nagoya and Tachikawa; Seibu Ikebukuro Line's Higashi-Hanno-Hanno,
Keihan's Chushojima-Fushimi-Momoyama) and the thinnest rural stretches (Tadami, Hisatsu, Kisuki, Isumi,
Watarase Keikoku, Hita-Hikosan, Echimi-Hoku, Yunomae, parts of the Iiyama and Oito lines). The 50
stretches the adapter found too thin are `too_thin` in the network JSON; the lines the runs left out
are the null entries of the trim file.

## Reproducing

    REALNET_OUT=<dir> python pipeline/realnet.py net jpn      # jpn_real.json: lines with key, cars, trains, count
    REALNET_OUT=<dir> python pipeline/realnet.py geom jpn
    data/work/target/release/realsave.exe --city jpn --dir <dir> --out <dir>   # cargo build --release --features demand --bin realsave
    REALNET_OUT=<dir> python pipeline/realnet.py gamesave jpn  # <dir>/jpn_real.save

`REALNET_OUT` keeps all of it out of `data/`. Measuring: Vite on its own port, then
`SAVE=<save> OUT=<dump.json> node notes/T-1625/run_japan.mjs` (headless Chrome, 2 demand workers; the
final takes about 15 minutes), `python notes/T-1625/analyze.py <dump.json>` (lines under 10, pkm
against 1,100M), `python notes/T-1625/trim.py <dump.json> <jpn_real.json> [--trigger 12] --write`.
Dumps of round 1 and the final: `../artifacts/trainworld/t1625/`.
