# T-1541: corridor rail is low where an inter-city line runs alone through a big city (2026-10-27)

Analysis only, no game code changed. From T-1521 round 3 (notes/T-1521.md in tree t1521): on the
every-zone corridor runs, Shanghai - Beijing was 35% rail of rail + air (86% published), London -
Edinburgh 8% (45%), and the Northeast's rail 0-2% of all trips (5-20%).

**The three pairs fall short for three different reasons.** Only Shanghai - Beijing is about access
to the station, and that part comes from how the test is set up.

1. **Shanghai - Beijing: rides reach only 15 km, and most of both cities is farther from the lone
   station.** Rail takes 96% of rail + air on the zone pairs it serves, but only 37% of the trips are
   on served pairs: 63% of Shanghai's people live over 15 km from Hongqiao, 50% of Beijing's over
   15 km from Beijing South. With a stand-in metro feeding each station, rail goes to 96%; with rides
   reaching 40 km and no metro, 91%. Mostly a test artefact (no metro), but a player meets it too
   when the high-speed line comes before the metro or is not linked to it (T-1504's cause 3).
2. **London - Edinburgh: rail against the plane at 4 h 20, not access.** Even between the station's
   own zones rail and the plane come out level (582 against 581 perceived minutes, 46% rail of rail +
   air). London's real network plus a metro at Edinburgh serves 98% of the trips and leaves rail at
   12-13%. Away from King's Cross, rail's access grows while the plane's stays the same for every
   zone: its airport drive is the place's people-weighted mean, in plain minutes at 85 km/h. A real
   gap a player would meet.
3. **The Northeast: rail loses to the car even between the city centres** (Penn to Union Station
   zones: rail 446 perceived minutes, car 361). The fare ($85, 166 minutes) against the car's $21 a
   head (42 minutes) is most of it. No access change moves it: reach, feeders, 40 ride lines, every
   variant stays at 1-2% rail of all. "Rail of rail + air" there is 1% over 1% + 5%, and the plane's
   5% matches the published 5.3%. This is T-1542 (the US car), plus the US rail fare.

How many stations a zone may use does nothing here (40 ride lines: identical on the Northeast and
London). A lighter ride weight (drive counted 1.33 instead of 2.66) adds 1-7 points alone.

## How it was measured

Scripts in `notes/T-1541/`: `t1541_copy.py` copies tree t1521's crate (read only) to a scratch folder
with `t1541_test.rs` and two switches in the copy (the plane's fare; the airport drive counted
`mult x minutes + add`); `t1541_rows.py` builds the market rows (the packs moved on after T-1521's
round 3, Saanich joined, so round 3's rows no longer open: the main tree's rows with round 3's built
`fw`, `fg`, `cu` per country); `t1541_run.py` runs case:variant jobs; `t1541_table.py` writes the
tables. Outputs in `../artifacts/trainworld/T-1541/`.

The test is T-1521 round 3's corridor run (regional layer alone, every zone, every period, three
crowding rounds, the built fares: market rates, the 0.4, real fuel and tolls, plane $100 + 5c).
Then, for the midday period, it rebuilds every zone pair of each focus corridor as `demand` chooses
it and splits rail's cost for the best station pair into access and egress (`-ln(weight) / 0.10`
from the access lists), wait + ride (the station-to-station skim without its fare), and the fare (at
the group's factor). Rebuilt midday shares match the solve's day figures on every built run, so
the breakdowns are the model's own. Perceived minutes below are for the middle income group (China
7.3 minutes a dollar, the UK 2.6, the US 2.0).

Cases: `china`, `eu`, `nec` are round 3's. `china_feed`, `eu_feed`, `nec_feed` add a stand-in
metro at the big stations (12 radial lines of 30 km from the inter-city station, a stop every
1.5 km at 35 km/h, 12/8/6 trains an hour, sharing the station). `eu_lon` puts London's real network
(T-712's `lon_real`) under the lines; `eu_lonfeed` adds a stand-in metro at Edinburgh.

What it says:
- **Reach is the whole of Shanghai - Beijing's gap and about half of Beijing - Guangzhou's.** Once
  served (metro or 40 km rides), China overshoots (96% against 86%; Beijing - Guangzhou 59-82%
  against about 25%): the plane at $100 + 5c weighs 1,117-1,411 minutes there. That is T-1521's
  plane price question (its "plr" variant), not access.
- **London - Edinburgh does not respond to access on the rail side** (reach, lines, London's
  network, an Edinburgh metro: 8-15%). It responds only when the plane's access gets dearer. Counting
  each zone's own airport drive at plain or 1.33x minutes changes little (6-9%): London's airports
  sit as close to most of London as King's Cross. Counting it like a ride (2.66x + 25 an end) brings
  London - Edinburgh to 56% (79% with the networks), but the plane then vanishes from Paris - Lyon,
  Madrid - Barcelona (18% published) and the Northeast (5% published). No one airport weighting
  fits both.
- **The Northeast's rail does not respond to any of it.** Rail of all stays 0-2% in every variant;
  the car takes 93-99%. The plane's share (5% New York - Washington) is right as built.
- Feeding the far end (Edinburgh, Washington) adds a few points (London 8% to 14%, New York -
  Washington 12% to 18% rail of rail + air), no more.

| Part | Artefact of the test | Real, a player meets it |
|---|---|---|
| China's lone lines with no metro | Yes: with a metro, 96% | Partly: a line built before the metro, or not linked to it, draws only from 15 km (T-1504's China save) |
| The 15 km ride reach | | Yes: a lone station's catchment, whatever the trip's length. On a 1,000 km trip people travel 30-40 km to the station |
| Far ends with no network (Edinburgh, Washington, Boston) | Yes, small (a few points) | |
| Rail against the plane at 4-5 hours (London - Edinburgh) | No | Yes: the station's own zone ties with the plane; every other zone loses. Paris - Marseille (33% against 73-82%) has the same shape, and Tokyo - Fukuoka goes the other way (34% against 11%) |
| The plane's access: a place-wide mean, plain minutes, 85 km/h | No | Yes: the plane costs a far suburb what it costs the centre, while rail's ride is per zone, at city road speeds, counted 2.66x plus 25 minutes |

| Stations a zone may use (8 + 2, 6 + 2 ride lines) | | Not a cause here (40 lines: no change) |

## Proposal

1. **Rides to a station reach farther for the longer trips (T-712's idea 3).** The longer trips'
   home and far ends ride up to 40 km (`ride_max_m` 40,000 for the market's longer-trip access
   only); commutes and everyday outings between zones keep 15 km. Expected, from the corridor runs:
   Shanghai - Beijing 35% to 91%, Beijing - Guangzhou 25% to 59%, Paris - Lyon 73% to 88%, Madrid -
   Barcelona 67% to 78%; London - Edinburgh and the Northeast unchanged. With a metro in place it
   changes little (the metro already serves those zones), so the game still rewards feeders. Cost:
   `access_options` would run a second time with the wider reach for the longer trips' lists (the
   commutes share the 15 km ones today), on a 40 km station grid: time it on the China and Japan
   saves (T-1511's probe) before landing. Measure: these corridor runs (`t1541_run.py
   china:<variant> eu:<variant>`), T-1511's China pairs (served share and rail), and the real New
   York and Tokyo saves' longer boardings. A sliding reach (15 km at 100 km, 40 km past 400 km) is
   the alternative if a flat 40 km moves the 100-300 km pairs too much; not measured.
2. **Not proposed yet: the plane's access.** Making it per zone is a consistency fix (the plane
   would cost a far suburb what it really does), but measured alone it barely moves anything, and
   weighting it like rail's ride overshoots every pair under about 650 km. London - Edinburgh's gap
   is rail against the plane at 4-5 hours, which Paris - Marseille shares and Tokyo - Fukuoka
   inverts; it should be read together with T-1521's plane price (one world fare against one scaled
   by price level) rather than fixed through access. I would file it as a follow-up after T-1521
   lands, with these three pairs as its targets.
3. **The Northeast goes to T-1542.** Centre to centre, rail's fare (166 minutes) against the car's
   money (42) is most of the 85-minute gap; the US car at $0.104 a km with no tolls, and Amtrak's
   real $106 fare, are where to look.
