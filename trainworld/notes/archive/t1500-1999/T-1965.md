# T-1965 Drawn trains never pass through each other

Anita, 2026-11-02: on her test track (one express, one local, sharing double track end to end) the
express was drawn running straight through the local. Agent t1965.

## Cause

Every train is drawn from its own line's timetable (keyframes on the GPU, independent of every other
train). T-1924's following loss gave the express the *average* loss of an express meeting locals at
random places in their headway (`D - (H - s)/2`, or `D²/2(H - s)`). With one local and one express
that average was about a minute, so the express's timetable ran 16 minutes a round trip against the
local's 31, and it was drawn overtaking the local every other trip. But on a line where both turn at
the same two ends there is no passing anywhere, so order on the line never changes: in reality the
express ends up behind a local on every trip and takes the local's time. The random-phase average
is the wrong model there.

## What changed

1. **Numbers (sim)**: a following pair whose runs are the same path end to end, the slower one
   reaching the end at least 30 s later, is *locked*: the faster line's loss is the whole
   difference (`capacity::FOLLOW_LOCKED`, `FOLLOW_LOCK_MIN_S`). Her test track: express round trip
   983 s -> 1849 s (local 1856 s).
2. **Phases (sim)**: `spacing::place` puts a locked follower's departures the spacing `s` behind
   one of the slower line's, not half a headway away, so it is drawn following rather than catching
   up midway.
3. **Display guard (app, `map/trainGuard.ts`)**: per frame, for trains in or near the view on lines
   sharing track, no train comes within 30 m of the back of the train ahead on its track (edge and
   direction), nor onto a merge while another is on it; held trains are drawn late in their own
   run (a per-trip `a_lag` attribute), make it up at 0.3 s a game second, and carry what is left
   into their next trip. Picking, hover tags, the train panel and following use the lag.

Not done: T-1936 (waits at every station the slower trains stop at). With the locked loss and the
guard, the express's four waits a stretch plus the guard already draw it behind the local; the
single pass would only make its drawn motion closer to its timetable (smaller lags).

## Flat crossings (left off, T-1966)

The guard can hold trains at flat crossings too (`TrainGuard.crossings`, off). On the real save,
whose imported network has 340 flat crossings (92 of 109 lines touch one), it held 100-150 trains
at a time and lagged 600-900 trips by up to the 20-minute cap at 960x: trains on the busy Park
Avenue and Lower Manhattan crossings queued behind each other's bodies (240 m commuter trains)
frame after frame. A crossing guard needs the crossings' real occupancy (a train's time over it,
not its body at 960x frame steps) or a cap per crossing; T-1966.

## What is still a lie

- A held train is drawn behind where the boarding account has it: its platform crowd boards and
  the stop's chime plays at the timetable time, while the train is drawn still outside.
- Its lag carries into its next trip, so it leaves its terminal late on screen while the account
  has it on time.
- Trains out of view are not held; one coming into view is placed at once, so it can jump.
- At 960x a frame is 16 game seconds: a train can be pulled back several hundred metres in a frame.
- Zoomed out, trains are drawn longer than they are (a pill at least 11-26 px); held by true
  lengths, two trains on the same track can touch on screen there, but lines sharing track are
  drawn side by side then (T-1799).
- Not guarded: opposing trains on single track (T-1967), flat crossings and crossing junction
  moves (T-1966).
