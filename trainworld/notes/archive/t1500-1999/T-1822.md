# T-1822: a tutorial (design for Anita)

Anita's TODO, 2026-10-29: an askew post-it with steps to get started, a tick by each step, a ripple
that points at the UI element to use, and +1 as the starting elevation. Design only; nothing built.

## What the player sees

**The card.** A post-it on the map's upper right, under the search button, 16 px in from the edge.
About 240 px wide, as tall as its steps (about 200 px). Turned 2 degrees anticlockwise. Flat: one
fill, no shadow, no paper texture, no curl. Light theme: a pale yellow mixed from `--yellow` and
the background; dark theme: the same yellow at low strength over the dark background, text in the
theme's text colour. Zen Maru Gothic like the rest, two sizes (heading, steps), two weights.

- Heading "Getting started", an x at the top right (hover "Hide the guide").
- Six rows: a 12 px square tick box, the step's words, its counter right-aligned in tabular figures.
- The step in hand is in the normal text colour; steps after it are muted; done steps have a tick
  and stay in the normal colour. Ticks show at once, no animation.
- A click on a step's row fires its ripple again (below).
- **Out of the way**: the card takes clicks only on itself, and while the stop editor or the Select
  tool's selection bar is up it stays where it is (neither uses the upper right). It hides while a
  confirmation or the saved games panel is open, like the rest of the map chrome.
- **Narrow screens** (the phone layout, dock under the map): the card shrinks to its heading row
  and the current step only, about 200 px wide, same corner. A tap on the heading opens all six.
- **Hidden and back**: the x hides it for this game. Settings > Game gets a row "Getting started"
  with Show (only while the guide is unfinished). No floating restore button.
- **Finished**: after the last tick the steps stay ticked and a line under them reads "Your first
  line is open." with a Done button (closes it for good in this game).

**Steps** (exact wording; counters are live; a counter's hover says what counts):

| # | Words | Counter | Hover on the counter |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lay 5 km of track where the bubbles are big | 2.3/5.0 km | Track near busy places counts |
| 2 | Put stations along it, about one a km | 1/3 | Stations on that track |
| 3 | Construct your blueprints | | |
| 4 | Make a new line | | |
| 5 | Click your stations to make them stops | 1/3 | Stops on the line |
| 6 | Give the line a train | 0/1 | |

Counters cap at their goal ("5.0/5.0 km"). Undoing never unticks a step; counters still move.

## How each step is detected

All from state, checked whenever `networkSnapshot` or the blueprint changes (no new event bus).
The step in hand is the first unticked one; a later one can tick early (a player who knows the game
can race through).

1. **Track**: length of track (blueprint or built, not disabled) that counts as busy (rule below),
   summed. Done at 5.0 km.
2. **Stations**: stations (blueprint or built) on the counted track, at least 500 m apart from each
   other (so three side by side count as one). Done at 3.
3. **Construct**: at least 5 km of built track and 3 built stations from steps 1 and 2. Detected
   from the snapshot's `built` flags, so constructing by C, a piece's own Construct button or a line's
   Construct all count.
4. **New line**: any line exists (New line makes an empty one, `newLine` in `lineActions.ts`).
5. **Stops**: the line with the most stops has min(3, built stations) of them.
6. **Train**: any line has at least one train at any demand level.

**"High demand" for step 1.** Destinations is already on in a new game (ui-demand-views.md). A
100 m stretch of track counts when a res-9 cell within 500 m has destination legs at or above the
80th percentile of its zone's cells: in plain terms, the top fifth of the bubbles. The bubble worker
already holds per-cell legs (`cellModes`); it publishes the zone's 80th percentile once per result
and the check samples track every 100 m. A start on the world map counts nothing until the player
picks a zone; the counter then reads "0.0/5.0 km" with the hover "Pick a city first".

## The ripple

A rectangle drawn round the target element's box, 2 px line in the accent colour, starting on the
element's edge and growing 16 px outward on every side over 0.7 s while it fades from full to
nothing. Square corners, like the buttons. No shadow, no glow, no fill. Three ripples 0.25 s apart
make one pulse. Drawn in one fixed overlay over everything (above the dock and the map), taking no
pointer events, positioned from `getBoundingClientRect()` each frame so a scrolling dock keeps it on
target.

**When**: one pulse when a step becomes the step in hand; again after 20 s with no input while that
step is still open; again on a click on the step's row. Never while the player is mid-drag or the
stop editor is open on step 5 (the markers there already say where to click). A target out of view
(dock scrolled, tab closed) ripples the thing that brings it into view first: the Build or Lines
tab icon.

**Targets**: 1 the track tool button (the Build tab icon if Build is not open); 2 the station tool
button; 3 "Construct blueprints" at the pane's bottom; 4 New line in the Lines tab (the Lines tab
icon first); 5 the line panel's Edit stops button when not editing, none while editing; 6 the
Schedule's trains stepper on the first demand row. Each target gets a `data-guide="<step>"`
attribute so the overlay finds it by query; nothing else changes on those elements.

**The house rule.** ui-panels.md says "No motion but trains. Instant state changes, no CSS
transitions." The ripple is motion, so it needs Anita's exception, written into that line as
"No motion but trains and the guide's ripple". It is drawn by a `requestAnimationFrame` loop on a
canvas or SVG overlay (like the logo's trains), not a CSS transition, so nothing else in the UI
gains easing. With reduced motion asked for, the ripple is one still outline at +8 px, held 1.5 s.

## When the guide shows

Every new Normal game, in this browser, until the player has finished the guide once (a remembered `guide` entry in the UI state, `game/ui.ts`). Not in sandbox
games, not with a real network, not on `?new=1` checks (smoke must not see it), not on saves made
before it existed. The guide's progress (ticks, hidden or not) is kept in the game's save, so a
reloaded game picks up where it was. Settings > Game's Show row brings it back in any normal game
that has not finished it.

## Starting elevation +1

Now: `buildLevel` in `game/ui.ts:244` falls back to 0 (ground) when the browser has nothing
remembered; once the player picks an elevation it is remembered per browser. Ground is $45M a km
(0.5x), +1 is $90M a km (1.0x), before terrain and water. Ground track turns into a bridge over
water and ramps over other track (T-656, T-1615), which brings refusals like "There is not enough
land for the bridge ramps" and a ghost that rises and falls. +1 avoids those.

Changing it touches: the fallback in `game/ui.ts` (0 to 1, one line); ui-building.md's elevation
paragraph; the new game's first track costs twice as much (5 km is $450M of $6B, fine). Only
browsers with nothing remembered see the change, so Anita's own browser keeps whatever she last
picked. Smoke draws track in a new game: its cost or count figures may need a look; t1805-placement
sets its own level and is unaffected. Recommendation: +1 for every first visit, not only for the
guide, since a picked elevation is remembered anyway.
