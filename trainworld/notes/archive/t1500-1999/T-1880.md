# T-1880: do buses act as feeders?

Anita (2026-10-30): "we should check wherher buses actually act as feeders? like is it possible to make a bus
line that is unprofitable by itself but considered with the stuff it links, actually increases total profit?"

Measurement only, 2026-11-03. No game code changed.

## Answer

Yes, but only just. Of six buses a player might draw as feeders on the real New York save, none worked at the
game's default 8-minute headway. Thinned to 12 minutes, the Throgs Neck bus to Pelham Bay Park did: it lost
$19k a day by its own figures and raised the network's profit by $98k a day (the same to $0.5k when rerun in
another order). Fresh Meadows to Jamaica-179 St at 15 minutes did too, by $32k, which is inside the noise.

The window is narrow because the rail lines gain little from a feeder: at most about $170k a day in these
runs, often a loss. A feeder can lose money by itself and still help the network only when its own loss is
smaller than that, so within about 10-20% of breaking even. And the line panel can mislead the other way: the
Church Av crosstown shows +$655k a day by itself while it costs the network $250k, because it takes riders
off the subway.

## The buses

bus at a time through the game's edit API (`addBusLine`, `construct`, `setHeadway`), straight between the
listed points, so road km is about 1.2 times the straight line; removed, and the baseline solved again before
the next. Money is game dollars a day (the economy's 100x), counted as the Lines tab does: fares from
`incomeByDay`, running cost as `runningToday`, upkeep from the line's stats. Build cost is the rule
($1M a road-km plus $0.6M a bus), since a sandbox charges nothing. Headways are high demand; medium and
low run 1.25 and 2.5 times as long, as the default 8/10/20.

Baseline: rail fares $3,451M, running $1,261M, upkeep $237M, profit $1,953M a day; 11,297,375 rail trips,

the network's profit when that figure is over about 6.1, whatever its own figures say.

## Why it is rare

1. **A bus boarding takes about half a base fare from the rail lines.** Fare income shares each trip's base
   fare evenly over all boardings in the network (`incomeFor` in `game/money.ts`: trips over boardings, 0.50
   here). A rider who already took the F and now rides a bus to it adds a boarding but no trip, so the bus
   gets half a base fare (about $0.75 of New York's $1.50) and every rail line a little less. Each bus
   boarding brings the bus $0.95 to $1.13.
2. **Most bus riders were rail riders already.** New trips per bus boarding are 0.19 to 0.32 for the feeders
   (T-1466 found four in five boardings were people changing how they reach a station). The new trips' fares
   (about $3 a trip on this network, often long rides into Manhattan) have to outweigh the base fares the bus
   takes, and only the pure feeders managed it: one station at the end, rail fares +$34k to +$166k. Where a
   bus meets several stations it also carries people between them in place of the train (Church Av: rail
   riders -5,814; Red Hook at 15 minutes: -2,563), and rail loses $230k to $905k.
3. **New trips are few.** The 6-minute boarding cost (T-1576) and the slow bus (70% of the car's street speed,
   T-1577) keep a bus at 900 to 2,000 new trips a day here, against $0.4M to $1.3M a day to run it.
4. **The running cost per bus-km is the big number.** At $6 a bus-km a bus at 8 minutes runs 1,200 to 2,100
   bus-km a day. Thinning the headway cuts that faster than it cuts riders (Throgs Neck 8 to 12 minutes:
   bus-km -33%, boardings -23%, the rail gain kept), which is how Throgs Neck got into the window.
5. The transfer fare is $0 in this save, so a change from bus to train adds nothing. A transfer fare would
   be paid to the rail line on each bus-to-train change; not tried, since it changes every train-to-train
   change on the network too.

What would have to be true for feeders to work more often: more new trips per boarding (a feeder into rail
where there is little else, as in T-1466's new game, where nine in ten bus riders were new trips), a running
cost under about $5 a bus-km at these loads, or a fare rule that pays the rail lines more of a feeder
rider's fare. The first is the player's (where they build); the other two are Anita's money rules.

## Proposal for Anita (not built)

No demand change is suggested: the feeder effect is there and in the right direction, it is just small
next to a bus's cost.

The one thing worth her look is how fares are split between lines, which decides what each line's profit
shows. Today every boarding gets the same share of the base fare, so a 2 km bus leg of a 15 km trip takes
half of it. Splitting a trip's base by the km ridden on each line would give that leg about an eighth.
Feeders would then show a bigger loss of their own and the rail lines they feed a bigger gain: the network's
total stays exactly the same, but the panels would show the pattern she asked about, and a crosstown that
takes riders off the subway (Church Av) would no longer look like a $655k-a-day earner. Side effect: the
same rule moves base fare from short rail lines to long ones (the LIRR and Metro-North would gain, short
subway lines lose), which would change the Lines tab's ranking. A money rule, so her call (T-2104). Lowering
`RUN_COST_BUS_KM` (T-1718's options) is the other lever; it makes buses profitable by themselves rather than
making feeders visible.

## How it was measured

`checks/t1880-bus-feeders.mjs [url] [out.json] [ids, id@seconds]`, from the maps root on a Vite of its own
(port 6164); about 5 minutes a bus. Raw: `../../artifacts/trainworld/t1880/` (`feeders.json` the six at
8 minutes, `headways.json` the six variants, `recheck.json` Red Hook twice and Throgs Neck at 12 minutes in
another order, with the rail lines that moved most).

Two things the script does differently from lib's helpers, both found here:
- A loaded save shows the average day, which has no income of its own (`incomeByDay` holds the six kinds of
  day), so it reads the weekday from `twDemand.days()[0]`.
- lib's `demandFinal` waited for ever on this save with `?demandWorkers=2`: the newest log entry was a
  version the view never reached (the focus pool's rough solve, by the look of it), so it skips entries with
  `rough` set and then wants 15 s with no newer version (T-2105).
