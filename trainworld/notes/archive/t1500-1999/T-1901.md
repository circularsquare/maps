# T-1901 Crowding: one full stretch or the whole line

Design note for Anita, 2026-11-02. No code changed. Mock-up:
`artifacts/trainworld/t1901/crowding-options.png` (and its .svg).

## What shows crowding today

- **Line panel, a dot per demand level** (ui-inspectors.md): coloured by the average wait for a
  train with room, at the platforms where trains come full (green to 0.2 min, yellow 6, orange 9,
  red 15, then crimson to indigo). A ring outside gives the weekday's. Hover: "When trains come
  full, riders wait ~11 min for a train with room" and "10-16: 4/10 trains full" per period.
- **Lines tab**: each line's "Trains" count coloured orange or red by the same wait.
- **Map, Traffic display**: a red dashed edge and darkening on each stretch between two stops where
  riders wait for room, as strong as the wait; a red band on track at capacity.
- **Line panel diagram** (Traffic display): stretch widths by riders, crowded stretches with the
  same red edges.
- **Past 24 hours**: average load, trains left full x/y, riders left at platform x/y.
- **Stations**: platform crowds, the frown on transfer bubbles.

The dot and the Lines tab count, the two places a player decides "add trains", only say how long
riders wait where it is full. A line full on one stretch and a line full end to end can show the same
red. The map and the diagram do show where, but only in the Traffic display and only one line at a
time.

## Options (mock-up shows all three)

Common rule: a **stretch** is the run between two stops in one direction; it is **full** when riders
there wait a minute or more on average for a train with room at that level (the platforms rule the
dots already use, `BUSY_PLATFORM_S`). Counted in the fuller direction, so a 15-stop line has 14.
The data is there: `RunAccount::stop_waits` per stretch, `line_periods` per period.

**A. Pie dot (her idea).** The level dot becomes a pie: the filled wedge, clockwise from 12 o'clock
like the station crowd pie (T-228), is the share of stretches full; its colour is the wait as today;
the rest is white inside a ring of the same colour. A sliver means one hot spot, a full disc means
the whole line. Hover first line "Full on 2 of 14 stretches", then the worst stretch and its wait
("Market St to Harbour: riders wait ~15 min for a train with room"), then the periods as today. The
same pie before the Lines tab count. Small: one number per level from data already computed, and a
pie drawer.

**B. Crowding strip.** A thin bar per line (about 155 x 10 px), one cell per stretch end to end,
each coloured by its wait, grey with room. Says how much and where at once, in the Lines tab too,
so a list of lines can be scanned for hot spots. Each cell hovers its stretch. Larger: a new widget
and per-stretch numbers sent for every line in the tab.

**C. A number.** A "Full" column in the Lines tab, "2/14", coloured by the wait. Cheapest, but one
more number to read, and no picture.

## Recommendation

**A, the pie** (T-1926), in the line panel's level dots and before the Lines tab count. It is her idea, fits
where the crowding colour already sits, and answers the one question she asked: one stretch or
throughout. Where exactly is already on the map and in the line panel diagram (Traffic display), and
the pie's hover names the worst stretch. B is the better choice only if she wants "where" in the
Lines tab without opening a line.

No player text lands without her review; the hover lines above are drafts.

## Built (T-1926, 2026-11-02)

Anita chose neither pie nor a new widget: the dots and the Lines tab count stay, and their hovers get B's
strip. Pictures: `artifacts/trainworld/t1926/`. The stretch figures are the tail of `line_periods`
(per period and stretch, the fuller way: rider-seconds waited, then riders arriving); a level's stretch
is the riders' wait over the periods in which it is full (a minute or more, as the dots count a
platform), so the strip agrees with the dot's own wait. Real save, High level: line E has 1 of 19
stretches full (9 min), PASC 14 of 17.

## For Anita (asked before the build)

1. A (pie), B (strip) or C (number)? I would do A.

Decided (mine): the pie counts stretches, each gap between stops alike, not track length: "2 of 14"
is easy to say and stops are roughly evenly spaced on most lines.
