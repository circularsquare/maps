# T-1003: China walks a lot at baseline (design, 2026-10-23)

Anita (2026-10-23): "People seem to be walking a lot in China. At baseline some of the cities are
over 50% walking. I guess it's because they're dense and so parking is expensive. Do we think this
is a problem? It feels a little like a problem."

## Summary

**Is it a problem?** Mostly not for the game, a little for the look of it. With no rail, Chinese
zones walk 38% of their trips (trip-weighted over all 621 built zones; median 37%), against 30-37%
in New York, London, Paris, Tokyo and Munich. Only 12 of the 621 zones are over 50%, 2% of China's
trips: Shenzhen (51%) and a few dense districts (Shunqing 68%, Zhongshan District 62%, Xiashan 58%,
Zhuhai 54%). Everyday trips walk more than commutes, 46-57% in the big metros, which is likely the
"over 50%" Anita saw. Rail is not less rewarding there: the same cost that makes people walk (a
car that is dear to park and slow on dense streets) makes rail beat the car more easily, so the
ceiling tests pass with more room in China than in the Western cities, and a 20 km test line
through the centre carries about as many riders as one in London or Paris.

**Why.** Parking by density, not trip length. Chinese metros have about the same mix of trip
lengths as the Western cities (27-35% of trips under 2 km, against 28-34%), and swapping in
London's mix moves their walking by 0-5 points (New York's, which has more long trips, 0-9). What
differs is what a short drive costs: parking at home about 8-10 perceived minutes a trip in most
big Chinese metros (Chengdu 5.3, Hangzhou 4.7; Western cities 3-6), paid parking at the far end
about the same or higher, and streets at 2.3-3.1 minutes a km (1.9-2.3), because the 2 km zones
hold 10,000-40,000 people a km². So the walk wins a 2-3 km trip 51-62% of the time in the big
metros (Chengdu 44, Hangzhou 39) and 28-44% in the West. At the same density the cities walk alike:
China simply has more of its people in the densest bands. The rural county zones are different:
there the trips are shorter (37-46% under 2 km), and the swap explains up to 10 points (Honghe 48%
to 38%). The levers, measured on 24 Chinese and 12 other zones: no parking price by density at all
takes Chinese metros down 8-18 points (West 3-11); no parking at home alone 5-10 (West 1-6); the
walk's 10 minute head start 8-11 everywhere alike; faster dense streets (25 km/h) 1-3; a lower cap
on parking (20 minutes) 0-5, Mumbai and Hong Kong 6-7. Parking at home is the lever that is
China-heavy; the walk's head start moves as much but moves every city the same.

**Against real surveys** (sources below). The game has walk, rail and "car", which stands for the
car and every other way (bus, bike, e-bike, taxi). Beijing's central city walks about 30% of trips
(2022: walking and cycling 49%, cycling 17.5%), Shanghai's walking and cycling together are 55%,
and Paris city walks over half. In the game's terms a Chinese metro with no rail at all should walk
somewhat more than the surveys' 30% (some of today's rail riders would walk), and once a network
exists about the surveys' figure. The game gives the big metros 35-51% with no rail and 24-33%
under a typical metro line everywhere (the T test), so the baseline is 5-15 points high and the
built state is about right. The rest of the gap is the e-bike: China's short trips that do not walk go by bike or
e-bike, which the game prices as a parked car.

**Options** (simplest first; "walk" is walking's share of all trips with no rail, "A / B" the
ceiling tests as rail of rail + car, targets 85 / 50; "Chinese cities" the 16 city zones of the
sample, "Western" New York, London, Paris, Tokyo, Munich, Lyon, Leeds and Milan).

| Option | Walk, Chinese cities | Walk, Western | A / B, China | A / B, West | Real save (New York rail a day) | Rebuild |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Leave it | 31-51 | 30-37 | 92-97 / 80-92 | 89-94 / 75-85 | 11.0M | none |
| 2. Halve parking at home (`home_park_f` 0.4 to 0.2) | 30-47 (down 1-5) | 28-35 (down 1-3) | 91-96 / 78-90 | 89-93 / 74-83 | 10.3M (-6.6%); London -5.5% | none (sim parameter) |
| 3. An "other way" beside the car (bus, bike: 15 + 5 perceived minutes a straight km, no parking) | 23-31 | 23-30 | 90-92 / 77-82 | 88-91 / 74-80 | not measured; the T test falls 2-16 points (New York 48 to 39, Beijing 60 to 44), so roughly a fifth fewer | none, but code in the sim (a cost in the choice) |

- **Option 1** is what I recommend. The rule is the same worldwide and the places that walk most
  in the game are the densest ones anywhere (Hong Kong 65%, Mumbai 64%, Taipei 46%); in China it
  makes rail more rewarding, not less. If the 50% reads badly on the bars, T-632's parked idea
  (showing the trips no train could win apart from the demand the player plays against) is the
  display fix, and it would cover Hong Kong and Mumbai too.
- **Option 2** is a pure parameter and the least invasive cut, but it is small (China down 1-5
  points, the West 1-3) and costs New York and London 5-7% of their rail, since parking at home is what makes
  dense neighbourhoods take the train. Lowering the paid-parking slope (`park_paid_min` 15 to 10)
  does the same to walking and costs the same rail (New York -6.8%), and it would also need the
  market packs rebuilt (`intercity.py park_by_cell` mirrors the rule), so option 2 is the better
  of the two.
- **Option 3** is the honest fix (it is T-634: short trips need somewhere to go besides the car)
  and brings China's walking to the surveys, but it lowers rail against the "car" everywhere
  (B in Western cities 74-80, T down by up to a fifth) and adds a cost to the choice, which CLAUDE.md asks to
  think hard about. Worth doing, if at all, as T-634 with its own design, not as a China fix.
- Ruled out: a steeper walk (`walk_trip_w` 2) or dropping the walk's head start cut walking 9-14
  points everywhere alike, and New York's everyday walking is already a third under NHTS (T-636),
  so they would buy China's figure by breaking the West's. A per-country constant for China is
  what Anita asked to avoid.

**Questions for Anita**
- Leave it (my pick), or halve parking at home (China down 1-5 points, New York rail -6.6%)?
- Where did you see "over 50%": a city's mode bar, or a zone's everyday trips? With no rail only
  12 Chinese zones are over 50% across all trips, but the big metros' everyday trips walk 46-57%.
- Should T-634 (an "other way" for short trips, bus and bike) move up? It is the change that fixes
  China's walking properly, at the cost of rail everywhere.
