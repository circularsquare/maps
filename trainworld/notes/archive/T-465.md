# T-465: Tokyo commutes to the centre

2026-10-16. Anita: selecting Inokashira on a weekday, work purpose, "of about 83k people there,
about 1 works near Shibuya and none near Chuo". Scripts: `notes/t465_prep.py` (script removed 2026-10-24, in git history) (the res-9 `tyo`
pack's zone gravity and table 4's flows, both at municipality level, to
`../artifacts/trainworld/T-465-prep.npz`), `notes/t465_compare.py` (tables and curve trials).
Screenshots `../artifacts/trainworld/T-465-inokashira-z*.png`.

## Short answer

- The pack does send people from the west to the centre: Musashino's commuters 21% to the five
  central wards (census 36%), Mitaka 22% (31%). It is under, by about 0.6-0.9x, not by 1,000x.
- The panel matches the pack. "83k" is trip legs (or the area's 74k residents), not workers: the
  res-7 area at Inokashira holds 74k people and 25k commuters. "Where they go" counts trip legs
  (two per commuter), and the centre is split over a row per station, so it looks small.
- The curve is not the cause. Every curve tried, flatter ones included, sends the same 12-15% of
  the west's commuters to Chiyoda, Chuo and Minato (census 17%). Flatter curves only lose the
  own-municipality share.
- One cause is a plain input error: work at home is a flat 0.23 at every job, but the census's
  at-home share runs from 0.5% (Chiyoda) to 21% (Suginami). The centre's commuting jobs are 13% too
  few. Fixing that is the proposed fix; it needs a rebuild.
- The rest (west to centre 14.6% against 17.1% after the fix, Musashino 15% against 23%) is
  structure that distance alone cannot give: fast rail corridors at 20-30 km, and who lives where.

## 2. Why

**Jobs in the centre are right, commuting jobs are not.** Jobs by municipality equal the census's
workers by place of work exactly (Chiyoda 812,726). But commuters at the work end are jobs x (1 -
0.23) everywhere (`tier0.py`, `cfg["wfh"]`), while the census's at-home share by place of work is
Chiyoda 0.5%, Chuo 1.9%, Minato 1.8%, Shinjuku 3.0%, Shibuya 4.2% against Suginami 20.7%, Nerima
19.2%, Mitaka 15.1% (all 9.6%): shopkeepers and farmers work at home in the suburbs, office workers
do not. So the centre's share of the commuting jobs is low: Chiyoda 45.8 per mille of the pack's
commuters against the census's 52.5, Minato 44.2 against 49.8. In a doubly constrained gravity
the column totals fix how many come to the centre, so no curve can make this up: under every curve
tried, everyone to the three central wards is 12.1% (census 13.7%).

**Who fills the centre** (shipped pack, share of the three central wards' commuters by home ring
from Tokyo Station, model / census; and that ring's commuters going there):

| Ring | Share of the centre's commuters | Ring's commuters to the centre |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5 km | 16.4% / 11.4% | 54.1% / 45.3% |
| 5-10 km | 19.2% / 16.5% | 26.3% / 28.0% |
| 10-15 km | 18.7% / 20.0% | 19.2% / 24.4% |
| 15-20 km | 13.2% / 14.4% | 14.3% / 17.3% |
| 20-30 km | 15.5% / 20.5% | 9.6% / 14.0% |
| 30-40 km | 11.1% / 11.9% | 6.2% / 7.5% |
| 40-60 km | 5.1% / 4.9% | 4.0% / 4.2% |

The model fills the centre too much from the centre itself and too little from 10-30 km, which is
where the west Tokyo municipalities are. By prefecture the shortfall is Kanagawa (6.9% of its
commuters to the centre, census 9.8%), Chiba (8.8%, 11.8%) and Tokyo outside the wards (8.8%,
10.6%).

"Same municipality" multiplies the decay by 1 + (k-1) x the chance two zones' commuters share a
municipality; "own zone" multiplies the diagonal. Flatter curves, with or without a municipality
or own-zone term, and two-part mixtures all leave the west-to-centre share at 13.6-14.7%: what a
flatter curve frees up goes to the 30-60 km ring (Chiba, Saitama, Ibaraki), not to 10-30 km. The
shipped curve stays the best of them. The same held before the at-home fix (12.2-13.1% for every
curve, census 17.1%).

Not the cause, checked: jobs in the centre (exact to the census); the home end (Musashino 3.76 per
mille of the pack's commuters against the census's 3.54; using table 4's commuters by residence
instead of population moves misplaced to 16.0% and west to centre to 14.0%, about nothing); the
merge (T-453 does not touch the gravity; the merged pack's zones and factors are the res-9 pack's);
2 km zones (the intrazonal 1.04 km is equivalent to the own-zone term above, which did not help).

**What is left** after the at-home fix (west 14.6% against 17.1%, Musashino 14.7% against 23.4%,
the 20-30 km ring 10.7% against 14.0%) is what a single crow-flies distance curve cannot hold: the
radial rail lines make 20-30 km along the Chuo, Odakyu, Keio and Tokyu lines cheaper than the same
distance across them, and Musashino's residents are more office workers than its distance says.
Fixing that is a different model (a travel-time cost on a base network, or workers split by
occupation), not a refit.

## 3. Proposed fix: at home by municipality

Work end: commuting jobs = jobs x (1 - census at-home share of the municipality of work, table 4)
x (1 - telework 0.15), in place of jobs x (1 - 0.23). Commuters in all: 13.63M (13.66M now). With
the shipped curve: misplaced 16.9% to 16.5%, west to the three central wards 13.0% to 14.6%
(census 17.1%), to the five central 23.0% to 25.6% (29.0%), everyone to the three central 12.1% to
13.2% (13.7%), Musashino to the five 21.0% to 23.8% (36.4%). The curve stays: a refit on the
new margins would not move it (the trial grid's best is still 2.2/80).

The telework share stays uniform (0.15, 2 days in 5 of MLIT's 37.5%). Telework is likely higher
in central office jobs than in suburban ones, which would take some of the gain back; no source
by area was looked for (judgement, open).

**Rebuild it needs** (after T-453 stage 2 is finished with the packs):
1. `japan.py`: a per-cell at-home share by municipality of work from table 4's "home" rows (the
   function `workers_at_work` already reads them); `tier0.py` takes a per-cell `wfh` array when
   the config gives one, in place of the scalar (line 723: `work_c = jobs * (1 - wfh)`). The home
   end needs no change (it is scaled to the work total).
2. `build_city.py tyo` from the cached signals (cells, commute arrays, gravity: the pack's Furness
   solve, ~3 s natively), then the merge, `read_pack.py tyo`, `register.py tyo --market --trips`.
   Enrichments are unchanged (they do not read the commute arrays), so a re-run of the commute and
   gravity steps on the res-9 pack and the merge would do if `build_city.py` allows it.
3. The other Japan packs (Osaka, Nagoya, Fukuoka, Sapporo, Sendai, Hiroshima and the 38 zones)
   have the same flat share; the at-home spread is likely smaller outside Tokyo but the same fix
   applies, and their curve fits should be redone on the new margins (the zone fit grid takes
   minutes a zone). Best done together with T-463 (Japan's joint gravity), which reruns every
   Japanese pack's factors anyway.
4. Other countries: any census with at-home workers by place of work can feed the same rule
   (England's census, Canada's place of work "worked at home"); the US already applies ACS
   at-home by tract and industry.

(5.1k commuters, 10.3k legs); the rows near central stations add to about 10k legs. So the panel
shows the pack's flows. What reads wrong:
- **Units**: the rows are trip legs, both directions, so "1k near Shibuya" is about 500 people. The
  all-purpose figure in the purpose section of the same selection is 148k legs; Anita's "83k
  people" is most likely a legs figure or the 74k residents.
- **The centre is split by station**: each central station gets its own row of 1-2k while
  everything within 3 km of Kichijoji is one row of 22k, so the centre never shows as the 20% it
  is. A row per ward (the pack's places or N03 municipalities) or a "central wards" grouping would
  read better; a design call (open for Anita), not built.
- **Only the 40 biggest res-7 areas were counted** before the rows were summed (76% of this
  selection's commuters; the centre's many small areas lost most). Fixed (below).
- A row is named by the nearest station within 3 km, so without a player station near Chuo ward
  there is no "Chuo" row; areas with none are "2 km south" and the like.

**Fixed**: `app/src/map/commuterView.ts` `places`: every far-end area is named and summed, not the

## Built (2026-10-16, with T-463)

Section 3's rule is in every Japan pack (`japan.at_home_share`, config `at_home` and `telework`;
details and the rebuild in notes/T-463.md). Telework kept each pack's own MLIT share (0.15 Tokyo,
0.098 Keihanshin, 0.078 Chukyo, 0.067 elsewhere). Tokyo's curve stays d^-2.2 exp(-d/80): on the
new margins the zone grid's choice ties it (16.6% misplaced both). Tokyo's pack on the new margins:
misplaced 16.6%, west to Chiyoda+Chuo+Minato 14.6% (census 17.1%), to the five central wards 25.6%
(29.0%), Musashino to the five 23.1% (36.4%), everyone to the three 13.2% (13.7%). The joint gravity
(T-463) then sends 1.3% of Tokyo's commuters to other packs and takes as many in.
