Anita: "is there no setting of crowding params that gets this more realistic? would like to cap
demand and profit by trains room yes." Then: keep the 60-minute cap on the extra wait; a rider
waiting 60 minutes should be charged on the order of 60 minutes; show crowding and the wait for
a train with room in the trip explorer.

## 1. The crowding settings that exist

Native runs (commutes and everyday, no regional market; complexes and the T-842 service span as
the page sends them; a weekday, five periods; one thread). The real save's network was taken from
the page's solve message (`T-007-real-nyc.save`, 109 lines). The Lexington test is the real
save's 4 from Bowling Green to 161 St alone (12 stops, 8 cars), a train every 30 minutes at

**Answer: no setting does it.** The wait cap and the curve's steepness barely move an overloaded
line: past a few rounds the riders left on it are the free-flow pass's share of the average
(a quarter after three rounds, a 220-minute wait charged at the Lexington test's first stop and
19,000 still boarding there). More rounds do work, slowly and at their cost (10 rounds: the
Lexington test still 2.7 times its room, the real save's solve 2.3 times as long). Leaving free
flow out of the average makes the rounds ring and ends higher. Everything together still leaves 41
of the real save's line-periods over crush and costs three times the solve.

**The charge for a full train is the wait the loads give**: 11.2 minutes charged against 12.5
from the final loads on the real save, 50.5 against 46.0 on the Lexington test, counted minute
for minute like any wait (T-585) and capped at 60 minutes a boarding. Nothing undercharges it. It
was hidden in the explorer's wait, now shown on its own (below). Once the cap holds a line to its
room the final loads leave almost nobody behind (2.0 minutes on the real save), while the riders
who stayed were charged the wait that turned the others away: the charge is what the crowd would
cost, not what the riders who fit then wait.

## 2. The cap

`kernel::capacity_fit` and each population's `cap_with_external` (DemandApi, EverydayApi,
IntercityApi), called by the worker's `step` once a period's last round is in, from every
population's final loads. Rule: no segment carries more in a period than its trains hold at
crush in the busiest hour. Each direction of each line is walked from its first stop: riders
staying aboard keep their places, a stop's boardings get on up to the room left, the rest go by
car or on foot as their pair's other riders do. Deterministic in the final loads, one pass over
the route nodes and the pairs: no new oscillation, no measurable solve time (43 s either way
natively on the real save).

Why this rule: a final scaling of the averaged figures is the only way to hold the line exactly
(a cost term in the last round would still be averaged with free flow, and a pass that replaced
the average would ring). Its price is attribution: a rider turned away at a change is taken off
every entry of the population alike (their first station is in no table), and their rides before
the change stay counted (a little fare on the feeder line for a trip that went by car). Turned
away at a first boarding, they are that station's. The trip explorer's shares do not apply the
cap.

Effect on the real save (native, a weekday, commutes and everyday): rail trips 12,794,363 to
12,287,310 (-4.0%), boardings 24,260,378 to 23,753,325 (-2.1%), line-direction-periods past crush
in the busiest hour 128 to 0, riders over room 474k to 0. In the app (smoke on master fbe9690
plus this, the market open): 12,783,191 rail trips a day with the cap switched off, 12,268,883
with it (-4.0%); demand's final 108 s and 100 s, within noise. Ceiling tests (New York pack,
no network, so the cap cannot touch them): A 93.4% (92.2% with T-1056's rule), B 83.5% (82.5%).

T-1069: fares already follow demand's riders (`money.ts`), and demand's riders are now the ones
who fit, so income and demand agree; the account's "go another way" (T-1067) is left for the few
riders a peaky arrival still strands. No "gave up" figure was added to the line panel.

## 3. The trip explorer

`Leg::full_wait` (the 13th number of a leg, `LEG_LEN`): the part of a ride's wait that is for a
train with room. The rail breakdown shows **Crowding** (the rides' perceived minutes beyond their
real ones) and **Waiting for a train with room** where either is above 0, and the effective
time's equation has them as their own terms (`routeCrowding`, `routeTerms` in game/trip.ts).

Scripts (scratchpad, not kept): `capture.mjs` (the page's solve network), `lex.py`, a temporary
`sim/src/bin/t1070.rs` with switches for the averaging and the wait cap, `variants.py`.
