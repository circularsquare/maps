# T-1149: London - Paris goes 95% by plane in the trip explorer (2026-10-24)

Anita: the trip explorer gives London - Paris about 95% by plane, while her London - Paris line
carries about 11k riders a day of the corridor's roughly 30k trips. Real: Eurostar has about
70-85% of rail + air (notes/T-712.md's sources; Eurostar's "nearly 85%" for London to Paris,
Brussels and Lille), the car a small share.

## Outcome

Not a fault in the demand rule, and the explorer gives the same split as the solve. The 95% is
the split of the trips with no train, which is what the explorer shows wherever rail is not
offered for the two ends it was asked about.

## The costs, perceived minutes (market-rows.json, as `intercity.rs` computes them)

- Car: 346 km between the centres; the drive 320 (15 + 1.25 x 346 at 85 km/h), running cost and
  parking shared by two 107, the Channel 285 (60 minutes plus $150 at 3 minutes a dollar, halved):
  712.
- Plane: Heathrow 43 and Charles de Gaulle 34 minutes from the centres, 90 at the airports, 28 in
  the air plus 30, the fare $117 at 3 a dollar 351: 577. The fare is 61% of it.
- Scale at that length: 0.10 x sqrt(60 / 712) = 0.029 a minute, so the plane's 135 minutes' lead
  is odds of 50 to 1: with no train, 98% plane and 2% car between the whole places (zone pairs
  farther from an airport come out nearer 95/5).

The Channel's price is what keeps the car off this corridor. Without it the car would cost 427
and take 98% of the non-rail trips; with it, 2%. Real London - Paris car traffic is small, so the
Channel is doing its job.

## So where does 95% come from

An explorer answer whose train part is empty: the "By train" section then says "No train serves
both ends in this period" (or an end is not open), and the bar shows the non-rail split, 95-98%
plane. That happens when the ends asked about are not ones the line reaches (a bubble or trip
tool end in a 6 km zone with no station in reach), or in a period the line does not run. The
worker answers each period from the market part with the most rail, so a part without the line
gives this only if no part has rail for those ends.

What the explorer could show instead (for T-1148, which owns `MarketTrip.tsx`): when rail is not
offered (`why` is not OK), say so on the bar itself, for instance a "Train: not offered here" row
in place of a 0% share, or name the bar "Without a train"; so a plane share there is not read as
the corridor's split. With rail offered the bar is already demand's split.

## Is the rule wrong?

No fault found. The lone line's 45% against Eurostar's real 70-85% is the access cost of one
station at each end of a 14.5M place; with the cities' networks the model overshoots (93%).
The plane's door-to-door cost, the Channel and the damped scale are each plausible at this
length. Nothing changed.

One oddity for the fare work, not this task: on all three corridors rail hardly moves between
$0.30 and $0.50 a km (London - Paris 24.1% and 23.8%), as if the per-km part stopped counting
past some point. Worth a look by whoever owns the market's fares.
