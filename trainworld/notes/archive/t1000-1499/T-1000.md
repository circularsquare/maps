# T-1000: super long commutes inside huge zones (design, 2026-10-23)

## As built (2026-10-23, code only; no pack, registry or market file written)

Anita approved option 1 (cap 20, add-back 1% to 50-150 km; existing between-zone commutes kept, the
cap applying in the joint solve too). The code is in; the built countries' joins are rerun later in
a fix batch, and the US joins carry it from the start.

- **Cap**, `kernel::PULL_CAP` = 20, in `kernel::gravity` (pack alone, also the browser's fallback
  when a pack ships no gravity) and `joint_gravity`'s `balance` (per pack: the band is around each
  pack's own job-weighted mean log pull). A held column is set at the band's edge; then **each
  pack's columns move by one factor** so their total still meets the pack's jobs. That factor
  (`fill`, the uncapped columns' fill of their jobs) is what the note's Python got implicitly from
  normalising by the geometric mean; without it the balance drifts and never meets its tolerance.
  It must be per pack, not per group: with one fill per group the 20-pack closed Qinghai solve
  drifted at 28% a step forever (a pack that needs 0.59 alone, gol, cannot import the rest). With
  nothing held the factor is exactly 1 and the solve is bit for bit as before.
- **Add-back**, `joint_gravity` `LONG_SHARE` = 0.01, `LONG_KM` = [50, 150]: per home zone, its ring's
  jobs x the home pack's curve (and border factors), its own pack and across, unbalanced. Fixed
  packs' homes are included for pairs into solved packs. Every zone with a ring keeps 99% of its
  workers for the balance, and each solved pack's jobs are scaled to its homes' total (kappa, about
  0.99), not reduced by the add-back's own arrivals: so the balanced shares by pair do not move
  (the note's Python subtracted the arrivals; same figures to 0.1 km). The pairs go to the commutes
  list with the cross-pack ones; the drop of pairs under 1 a day runs on the two separately, so a
  dropped add-back commuter stays on an add-back pair (gol keeps 90% of its add-back, the Northeast
  97%; home zones with no pair of 1 a day lose theirs, as before).
- **Header**: `gravity.pull` = {cap, band of col/jobs, fill, held (share of jobs at the edges)},
  from either solve; `gravity.joint` gains `long_share`, `long_km`, `long_out`, and
  `commuters_out`/`in` leave out a pack's own add-back. `read_pack.check_gravity`: with a `pull`
  block, a pack's own solve checks columns inside the band against `fill`, ones at the high edge
  for no more, at the low edge for no less, none outside; a joint pack's kept work may reach
  `fill` of its jobs, any amount at the low edge.
- Flags for trials: `--pull-cap X` (0 none), `--long-share S` (0 none).
- Tests: `kernel::tests::pull_cap_leaves_a_far_job_island_short`, `joint_gravity`
  `pull_cap_holds_far_surplus`; `cargo test` (198) and `--features demand` (308) pass.

## Summary

**Cause.** The local commute model fills every zone's jobs exactly (doubly constrained), so where tier 0's
jobs and its workers don't match locally, the balancing factors grow without limit (in gol from 1e-13
to 1e15 between zones; New York 0.7 to 6) and drag workers to the surplus jobs however far away they
are. The distance curve is steep (d^-1.4 exp(-d/16 km), a 300 km pair weighs about 1e-11 of a 5 km one)
and the 4 km blocks only touch pairs under about 10 km: with the same curve and zones but no job filling,
gol's mean commute is 3.7 km, not 164.

## How the two halves would be set

**The pull cap.** In the balance, each zone's jobs get a factor: how much harder they pull than their
count and distance alone would. Measured against the pack's typical factor (job-weighted geometric
mean), it is held between 1/20 and 20. Rows stay exact (every worker commutes). A zone whose jobs would
need more pull keeps some unfilled; a zone with more workers than jobs nearby takes its extra workers
into those jobs anyway (they overfill; read it as working locally). On China's countryside curve a pull
of 20 is worth moving the jobs about 50 km closer (16 km x ln 20), which is the "past a distance" in
Anita's words. Why 20: the shipped factors of ordinary places never reach it (New York's largest 9.9,
London 4.4, Paris 4.6, Tokyo 19.8, Beijing 13.4, Shanghai 64 in one zone but 0.25% of its jobs past
20), while gol, cea and cnu reach 1e15, 1e28 and 1e3. At 10 Shanghai moves 0.7% of its trips and
Jincheng (chq) 1.5%; at 20 neither moves.

**The add-back.** A share s = 1% of every gravity zone's workers commute to jobs between 50 and 150 km
from home, shared by jobs times the pack's curve and not balanced, so no oasis can pull more. 150 km
is the between-zone commutes' existing cut; 50 km is about where a daily commute becomes a trip between
towns. They are written to the market pack's commute list (`commute_home`, `commute_place`,
`commute_work`, `commute_trips`) next to today's between-zone commutes, and the local layer keeps the
rest (`zone_home`, `zone_work` already carry the trips each zone keeps). A pair inside one pack is just
a list entry whose work place is the home place; `intercity.rs` (around line 1342) accepts any pair whose
6 km zones differ. 1% is a judgement for "low": about Japan's census-fitted share of commuters crossing
a prefecture line (1.6%, its 45 packs).

**Constants:** the cap 20 and the share 1%. The 50 km inner edge could be a third; the 150 km is the
existing cut.

**Complexity.** Small to moderate, two stages:
1. The cap: `kernel::gravity` (sim/src/demand/kernel.rs, after `colc[k] = z.jobs[j] / acc[k]`) and
   `joint_gravity`'s `balance` (sim/src/bin/joint_gravity.rs), about 10 lines each;
   `read_pack.check_gravity` and the merge check ("a zone keeps more than it has") must allow columns
   off their jobs where the cap holds; docs: demand-kernel.md 1, zones.md 4, demand-plain.md,
   demand-worldwide.md. No runtime change: the runtime rebuilds zone trips from the shipped factors.
2. The add-back: in `joint_gravity`, per home zone, the ring's jobs times the curve over every pack of
   the group (it already walks those pairs within 150 km), written to the joint commutes file, kept
   trips reduced to match; about 100 lines. To verify then: the regional layer's corridors and the
   selection tails with a commute whose work place is its home place.
Then each country's join.

**Side effects.**
- Anita's ceiling tests (notes/T-636.md, New York and London): unchanged, estimated, not rerun. The cap
  leaves both matrices exactly as they are (no factor near 20), and the add-back takes the same 1% from
  every home zone of the local layer, so its shares by pair do not move; the tests read the local layer.
  Expect A and B within 0.1 point of today.
- New York and London as loose checks: means 18.9 to 19.4 km and 10.2 to 10.8 km, commuters over 50 km
  7.3% to 8.3% and 1.8% to 2.9%, all from the add-back.
- Work ends at tier 0's proxy job sites no longer match their jobs: under the cap Golmud's oil and potash
  sites keep most jobs unfilled (Lenghu 4% filled, Qarhan 14%, desert sites with 6,000-12,000 proxy jobs
  and almost no one living near 0-13%), and herding counties take about twice their proxy jobs. Station bubbles there
  show commuters, not jobs. Over a whole pack: gol 38% of work ends move off the proxy jobs, cea 24%,
  cnu 11%; ordinary places 0%.
- The gate's mean commute relaxations for the 65 Chinese rows over 25 km would mostly no longer be needed
  (all four cases land at 6-13 km).
- T-998 (Xinjiang's balance stalls with its huge neighbours' factors at 1e17-3e35 held fixed): the cap
  bounds exactly those factors, so it should converge; untested.

### The existing commutes between zones

They are the same doubly constrained gravity solved over every pack of a group at once
(`joint_gravity`): pairs across a pack line on the home pack's curve, times the pack line factor
(`borders.json`: China has none of its own, so `pack_line_default` 0.4, Japan's census fit), cut at
150 km, every zone's jobs filled. So their amount is not set either; it follows the jobs and workers.
Workers crossing a zone line, from the joined packs' headers: China 13.9% (median pack 15%; hum 64%, heu 54%), Germany and Great Britain 9.3%, Italy 7.0%, France 5.8%, Spain 2.3%, Japan
1.6% (fitted to the census). China's zones are counties, smaller than Japan's prefectures, so more
crossing is expected, but the imbalance pull feeds it too; the cap reaches these as well, since the
joint solve is where it goes.
