## T-1286: the market weighs the player's fare scale

- `IntercityApi::set_fare_factors(json)` takes, per country the player set a scale for, that scale over
  its income's (`fareFactors` in `game/income.ts`; countries left out are 1, and a factor of exactly 1
  is left out). Each zone gets its country's factor less 1 (`zone_fx`, empty while all are 1).
- In the market's `demand`, a trip from a zone with a factor pays `factor - 1` times its station
  pair's fare on top: base + rate x expected fare km + the curve's extra, the expected km from
  `trip_km_on` over the pair's strategy as searched (S x S, built only while a factor is set). Routes
  stay as the curve gives them, as a bent curve's extra already does. A commute or outing takes its
  home's factor. The trip explorer between zones uses the same.
- Rust test `fare_factors_weigh_trips_by_their_origin`: factors of 1, of a country outside the
  market, and none give the same bits; 2 loses rail riders, 0.5 gains them; streamed and whole
  passes agree to the bit.

## T-1300: the estimate

- Demand's own fare sensitivity: in every mode choice (local commutes `kernel::mode_choice`, everyday
  pairs, the market's three parts) each pair's rail share is worked out again with rail's cost 1
  perceived minute dearer (`FARE_D_MIN`), the market's times the trip's fare factor. The difference
  summed is `FareSense::d` (rail trips per minute), averaged over crowding rounds and capped like the
  rail trips. Also summed weighted by each trip's straight-line km (`d_km`) and the rail trips so
  weighted (`km`). Summary slots 10-12 of each population.
- The worker turns them into rail trips per curve dollar (times each population's minutes a curve
  dollar: its country's weight times its scale; the market's at India's weight) and a reach per layer:
  the sensitivity-weighted mean km over the rider-weighted one (under 1: a km of fare moves short
  trips more than long ones). `DemandView.fareSense` carries the day's figures.
- `game/fareEstimate.ts`: each layer's rail trips times exp(sensitivity / rail trips x dF), dF the
  curve's change at each line and period's mean trip length stretched by the reach (the market's on
  the "longer" purpose's loads). Fare income: today's plus the charged change on today's riders,
  times the share of riders kept. The base curve is the one the shown demand was solved with
  (`noteFareEdit` from `fareUndo.send`), until a final view newer than the edit lands.
- Money tab: "Riders a day" (percent) and "Fares a day" (dollars) under the fare rows while a drag is
  under way or an edit is unsolved; hovers give both figures.

Without the reach the rider changes came out 35-45% too large for whole-curve moves (the base
alone was already within 8%). What is left is mostly crowding: riders who leave make room, and the
re-solve wins some back. The weakest case is a step that cuts the base and raises the rates at once:
fare income's sign comes out wrong there (+6% estimated, -2% solved), since each line's mean trip
stands in for its trips' spread of lengths.

Cost: one more share evaluation per pair in each mode choice (the logsums are not redone).
