# T-1202: the Japan save's first solve (2026-10-24)

`artifacts/trainworld/t737-japan.save` (709 lines, 10,320 stops, 45 zones), loaded in a fresh
headless Chrome on my own Vite (6147), 3 solving workers and the auxiliary, the machine shared
with the China rebuild (T-1184). Script: scratchpad `t1204/japan.mjs` (the solve's progress every
15 s from `window.twDemand`), not kept.

## It crawls, it does not hang

| | master (63d1dca) | with T-1204 |
|---|---|---|
| packs open in every worker, solve sent | 21-39 s after the import | the same |
| round 0 published (first figures) | 238 s after the solve was sent | 250 s |
| rounds 1, 2, 3 | about 490, 650, 841 s | about 465, 666, 846 s |
| solving workers' WASM after the final | 2,889, 2,983, 2,670 MB | 2,303, 2,373, 2,111 MB |
| Chrome, most seen | 13.8 GB | 11.5 GB |

So the first solve takes 14 minutes on either build, about 200 s a crowding round. T-780 measured
the same network at 965 s to its final in Node on 2 workers (2026-10-20), so this is how long all of
Japan has taken since then, not something new. Where it goes (T-780's measurements): the market's
pass, 62-70 s a daytime period natively, half of it its two search sweeps over 8,502 destination
stations, and the cities about 30 s a period. T-884's 54 s was the loading card, not the final.

T-1202's run (no round 0 after 15 minutes, renderer at 11 GB) was on a machine busy with the data
join; this machine has 64 GB, so not swapping. With T-1204 each solving worker is 0.6 GB further
from the 4 GB a worker can hold (about 3.0 GB at its most on master).
