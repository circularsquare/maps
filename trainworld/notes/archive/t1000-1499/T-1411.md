# T-1411: is the plane against long-distance rail fair? (2026-10-26)

Analysis only, no code changed. Scripts in `notes/T-1411/` (`pairs.py` every case, `variants.py`
the proposal, `planefare.py` the plane's fare), outputs in `../artifacts/trainworld/T-1411/`. They
read the market rows, the routed drives and the income table and apply the rule as
`marketRule.ts` and `intercity.rs` do. Rail is a line the player built with the real best train
time (Acela, TGV, Nozomi, AVE, LNER, CR) and the starting fare curve. "Centre" means a 10 minute
walk to the station at each end (30 perceived minutes); "suburb" means a 15 minute drive to the
station at each end (65 perceived each, the ride's rule).

## The short answer

Yes, her guess is right: a train pays half its headway as a platform wait, and a flight pays
nothing for how often it flies. But the plane is not what that wait loses to. Since T-1277 the
plane takes almost nothing under about 1,900 km, because its $100 base fare counts 440-770
perceived minutes. Rail's real rival in the market is the car, which has no timetable either.
So the wait rule moves rail against the car, a lot, and against the plane hardly at all.

## Where the two are treated differently, biggest first

| | What the game does | A fair version | Size, perceived minutes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Fares | Both a base plus per km, at the same minutes a dollar per country (rail at its default scale). The plane's $100 + 5 c/km is a world price; rail's starting curve is a metro curve ($1.50 + 10 c/km in the US). | Same weight for both (already). The level is the issue: a dollar counts 4.4-7.7 minutes, set for metro fares. | Plane fare 508 (New York - Boston) to 1,189 (Beijing - Shanghai); rail 165-580. The gap alone is 340-610. |
| 2. Waits | Rail: half the headway at the first boarding, plain minutes, plus the tolerance penalty past 30 minutes. Plane: none. | A long trip is planned, so a sparse timetable costs the gap to the wanted departure: grows with the headway, weighted lower, capped. The same for the plane. | Rail today: 31 at an hourly train, 63 at 2 hours, 127 at 4. Plane: 0. |
| 3. Access | Rail: walks at 1.5 a minute; rides 15 + 2.66 x the drive + a 10 minute drop-off. Plane: the plain drive to the airport (15 + the drive), people-weighted over the place. | The same weights for the same drive. | About 35 per end for a 15 minute drive. Rail pays it. |
| 4. Terminal | Plane: 90 minutes at the airports and 30 fixed. Rail: none. | Rail gets a few minutes of arriving early. | Folded into item 2's 5 minutes. |
| 5. Crowding and capacity | Rail rides cost more from 70% full and turn riders away past crush. The plane has no capacity. | Fine as is: the plane is not built. | 0 until a line fills. |
| 6. Comfort, reliability | None for any mode. | Fine as is. | 0. |

Items 3 and 4 tilt against rail, item 1 against the plane. T-712 settled 3 and 4 by measuring
corridor shares with the 90 minutes in place, so I would leave them.

### Item 1 in numbers

New York - Boston, centre, hourly train: rail 444 (30 access, 31 wait, 215 ride, 168 fare), the
plane 738 (86 airport drives, 90 terminal, 54 flight, 508 fare), the car 336. Without fares the
plane is quicker door to door (230 against 276). The fare decides it.

Rail's share of rail and air is 99-100% on every pair tried up to Beijing - Guangzhou (1,890 km),
centre or suburb. The published shares are lower: Madrid - Barcelona 78%, Tokyo - Osaka 85%,
Tokyo - Fukuoka 11% (T-712). It is not a rule that differs between the two: both tickets weigh the
same minutes a dollar, but the plane's ticket is three times rail's starting fare. A player who
raises long fares to real levels brings the plane back. Moving the plane's base fare is a cliff,
since the long trips' logit is flat: at $50 Tokyo - Fukuoka goes to 49% rail of rail and air, at
$25 London - Edinburgh flies 62% of all trips. That wants its own task, not this one.

## Proposal: a planned trip's wait

One rule, for the market's inter-city trips only (visits, business and leisure, 25 km and more,
all planned trips). Commutes and outings across zone lines keep today's wait.

**An inter-city trip's first wait is the least of: half the headway, 5 minutes plus a sixth of
the headway, and 30 minutes. No tolerance penalty on it.**

| Headway | 10 | 15 | 30 | 60 | 120 | 180 and more |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today (with the tolerance penalty) | 5 | 7.5 | 15 | 31 | 63 | 95-127 |
| Proposed | 5 | 7.5 | 10 | 15 | 25 | 30 |

Why this shape: someone who picks a departure is on average a quarter of the headway from the
time they wanted, and that displacement is worth less than standing on a platform (time at home
or at work). A sixth of the headway is that quarter at two thirds weight; the 5 minutes is
arriving early. Under 15 minutes it is today's rule, so frequent lines change nothing. With the
cap, anything sparser than a train every 3 hours costs no more: you leave at another time. A change of
trains on the way keeps today's wait, since that wait cannot be shifted.

**The plane**: its frequency is not modelled, so it takes the same rule at a 2 hour headway, a
flat 25 minutes on every flight (the extra hour for no direct flight stays).

| Beijing - Shanghai | 98.8, 99.2 | 97.5, 98.9 | 0, 0 |
| Tokyo - Fukuoka | 93, 95 | 86, 94 | 0.5, 0.1 |

From the suburbs the moves are smaller in points (Paris - Lyon every 2 hours: 10% to 31%). Rail
against the plane does not move, because the plane was already out. Against the car, a line every
2 hours now does about what an hourly one does today, which is her "a 2 hour wait is fine".

**The real save** barely notices: inter-city trips are about 0.02% of its boardings (smoke in
T-1004), and New York's lines are frequent. Under a few thousand trips a day either way out of
12.2M; I did not run it.

**Building it**: a small change in `intercity.rs` and the kernel's graph, plus `marketRule.ts`
for the plane's 25. The first boarding has to be told apart from a change: one entry copy of each
station whose boardings use the new wait, the inter-city skims read from it, and the commutes
and outings read the stations as now. `docs/regional.md`, `demand-plain.md` and
`demand-worldwide.md` follow.
