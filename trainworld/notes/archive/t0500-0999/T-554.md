# T-554: platform crowds jump when a train comes in

Anita, 2026-10-16: at a transfer station the platform a train pulls into empties quickly, but the
other platforms only grow at a steady rate; riders changing from that train should appear on
them shortly after it comes in.

## How crowds were fed (before)

- Demand reports, per line, direction, stop and period, boardings and alightings. Boardings
  include riders changing lines there; nothing in what reaches the boarding account says which.
- The boarding account (`sim/src/track/boarding.rs`) treats each platform (one stop of one
  direction of one line) on its own: its boardings arrive over the period by a smooth shape
  (`Arrivals`), each train takes whoever waits up to crush, the rest wait. Lines never feed one
  another in it: an arriving train's alighters simply leave the account.
- Each game hour the clock worker sends, per platform, the trains leaving it (and who each left
  behind) and one arrival rate (at mid-hour). The renderer draws the crowd as "left by the last
  train + rate x time since". So a platform empties at its own trains and refills at a constant
  rate; a train arriving on another line changes nothing on it. That is what Anita saw.
- Demand does know the platform-to-platform transfers (T-176, `assign_with_transfers`), but only
  the complex map and the station-use display asked for them, never the account.

## How hard

Moderate, done in the task. The drawing only needed per-platform pulses: the account itself was
left alone, so trains' loads, riders left behind, waits and demand's crowding feedback (T-188,
which runs the same rule inside demand) are unchanged, and each platform's arrivals in each
clock hour stay the account's. Pieces: one query over every route node (the existing transfers
message), a new clock-worker message, the pulses in `Boarding::hour`, and two readers.

## The rule (docs/track-and-service.md 6.6, "Riders changing lines arrive with their train")

- Demand's transfers a day between platforms, for the solve shown, every population, give each
  platform `q` the share of its boardings coming from each feeding platform `f`
  (`riders(f->q) / boardings(q)`, the shares scaled down together if they ever pass 1).
- Each clock hour, `f`'s share of `q`'s arrivals that hour is split over `f`'s trains leaving in
  the hour by the riders each has aboard arriving at `f` (the account's load), and reaches `q` a
  transfer walk after the train leaves: demand's walk, straight line x 1.3 at 1.2 m/s (0 at the
  same station node, 65 s for 60 m). A feeding platform none of whose trains carries anyone that
  hour, or under one rider a train, leaves its share steady (keeps the slice small).
- The rest arrive steadily (the old mid-hour rate times the share left).
- Pulses of the hour before are sent too when they come after the last train to leave before the
  hour, so a crowd carries across the hour boundary.
- The fares' timing within the hour (T-284 `shapeHour`) counts the pulses too.
- Day totals by pair, not by period: the transfer pattern by period is not sent (the pairs are
  directional, so the morning and evening flows are already different pairs).

## Checks

Unit test `riders_changing_lines_arrive_with_their_train` (boarding.rs): a line every 6 minutes
fed by a line every 15 ending 60 m away; loads and departures bit-identical with and without
transfers, four pulses an hour each 65 s after a feeding train reaches its end, pulses plus
steady equal the hour's arrivals.

Real New York save, weekday 08:00-09:00, demand settled (`t554-report.json` in
../artifacts/trainworld/):

- Transfer pairs: 12,005 platform pairs, 7.63M changes a day; the query takes about 20 ms.
- Hourly totals: every one of the 3,294 platforms draws exactly the account's arrivals for the
  hour (1,442,587 in all; largest gap 1e-7 of a platform's figure, float rounding), with the
  pulses and with them taken out. 573,544 of the hour's arrivals (40%) now come as pulses.
- Slice: 47,919 pulses, 866 kB against 492 kB; worked out in about 11 ms against 3.4 ms in the
  clock worker (ten slices each), so about 15 ms more of the worker per game hour (it makes two).
- Main thread: crowds worked out in 0.5 ms against 0.2 ms (once a game second while drawn);
  average draw 0.49 against 0.37 ms a frame at zoom 15 with the clock running.
- What it looks like: at 34 St-Penn Station a train brings 1,497 riders changing at once; line 0's
  two sides go 367 and 241 waiting to 1,187 and 1,215 at the moment they arrive. At Marble Hill
  (screenshots c1-c3) a Metro-North train's riders put 12 to 248 on the 225 St side.
- `smoke.mjs` passes on the agent's own Vite.

Not done: the transfer shares are per day, not per period; a line whose trains run less than
once an hour leaves its share steady in the hours it does not run.
