# T-539: automatic joint commute gravity groups (pipeline stage 7)

2026-10-16. Design: notes/T-535.md. Rule: docs/zones.md 4. Code: `pipeline/intercity.py`
(`auto_groups`, `solve_auto`, `solve_group`; `--group auto [ids]`, `--groups [--plan]`). Outputs of

## The rule

Two registered packs are in one group when their outlines (`<id>.boundary.geojson`) come within
`GROUP_GAP_KM` = 50 km of each other, chained (union-find over an STRtree, distances in an
equirectangular frame at the pair's mean latitude), across national borders too. Within a group
`joint_gravity`'s 150 km cut and border factors are unchanged. The Northeast (cities with a
`market` id) keeps its own solve through `intercity.json`, and must be exactly its group; every
other group runs `joint_gravity --packs` (150 steps at most, the best kept) and writes
`data/work/<first member in registry order>.joint_commutes.json`, removing any older file holding
one of its packs (so Japan's `jp.joint_commutes.json` becomes `tyo.joint_commutes.json` on the first
real run). `MAX_GROUP_ZONES` 200,000 stops a group about three times Japan's.

Why 50 km and not the 150 km cut: the two groups come out the same at any gap from 25 to 200 km
(at 0 km Japan falls into four), and the commuters lost between packs farther apart are few.
Today's cross-pack commuters by the outline gap between the two packs:

| Gap | Northeast | Japan |

| touching (0.5 km) | 96.96% | 95.01% |
| up to 10 km | 0.08% | 2.24% |

| 25-50 km | 1.68% | 0.67% |

A larger gap mostly chains continents: over the world plan (`world_plan.json` of 21:16 on 2026-10-16, after T-556, 584 rows) with the
built packs, the largest groups at 150 km are Japan with Korea (55 packs), western Europe from London
to Berlin (43) and eastern China (40); at 50 km Japan (47: Busan and Daegu join through Tsushima, at
the national factor 0.05), the Rhine-Ruhr and Benelux (12), England's north and Midlands with London
(8), the Yangtze delta with Shanghai (8).

## Size and solve time

A Furness step sums every pair (within packs, and across within the 150 km cut) twice: about 130M
pairs a second a thread on the Northeast, 100M on Japan, where cross pairs dominate. So time grows
with zone pairs: a pack's own n squared plus about half the products of its neighbours' zones within
the cut, roughly linear in members for packs of similar size, times the steps (40 converged, 150 where
the balance stalls). Estimated over the world plan at 50 km, Japan (0.5 billion pairs) stays the
largest, then the Northeast (0.2), Iraq's Baghdad-Najaf-Karbala and Cairo's (0.1 each); Europe's groups are under 0.02.
The only larger one is Dammam's (15 billion), whose planned outline is 557,000 km2 (the Eastern
Province as one unit), which the gate's 40,000 km2 cap refuses anyway. No split rule is needed; a
group over 200,000 zones stops with a message. The runner should solve a group once per batch, not
per pack, since a member added rewrites every member's factors.
