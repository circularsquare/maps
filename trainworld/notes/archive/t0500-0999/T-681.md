# T-681: airport staff commute, not air trips (Anita's playtest, 2026-10-18)

Anita: "airport employee demand should be different from airport flying demand, airport employees
should come from nearby (im looking at haneda and it seems the bubble near where the people work has
all air demand when it should be work)".

## What to find out first (an investigation, read-only; report before changing anything)
Demand rules are Anita's to approve (CLAUDE.md: propose before adding a factor), so stage 1 only
measures. On the Tokyo pack around Haneda (the airport's cells, the terminal's `airports` entry from
OurAirports and the pack's `boardings`):
1. How many jobs does the census 500 m mesh put in the cells at and around Haneda, and how do the
   commutes to them split in the destination bubble there: work trips from the jobs gravity,
   against airport trips from the airport purpose (`airport` rates, T-142, and `docs/demand-worldwide.md`
   Airports)? Anita sees the bubble nearest the workers as all air: say whether the airport's
   purpose trips and its staff's commute land in different cells, and in which cell the bubble's
   hover reads "air".
2. Whether staff are counted twice (jobs in the mesh *and* an airport trip count that includes
   them), or counted in the wrong cell (jobs placed by the mesh at the terminal while the airport
   point sits at the terminal's centre; the 1.5 km `purposes` window T-615 mentions).
3. The same question for another airport in a different build route (Heathrow from BRES, Pearson
   from StatCan, JFK from LODES), to know whether this is Tokyo-specific.

## What the fix probably is, if the finding is what it looks like
Airport flying demand (passengers) is a trip to the terminal from anywhere and a destination bubble
at the terminal; employees are ordinary commuters, so the work purpose's jobs in the airport's
cells stay with the gravity (from nearby homes), and the airport purpose must not count them. If
the bubble's mode and purpose hover merge the two on one cell, the fix is display (separate
purposes in the hover) and not demand. Report which.

Files to read: `sim/src/demand/` airport code, `pipeline/enrich_airports*.py`, `pipeline/japan.py`,
`map/bubbles.ts`, `map/airportMarks.ts`. Output: `notes/T-681.md` findings appended here and a
proposal in a short paragraph.

## Findings (2026-10-19, read-only; nothing in demand code or packs changed)
Measured on the packs (`airport_trips`, `jobs`) and on the worker's own legs after the first final solve of
a new game with no rail (`cellModes` for work, airport and all, the bubbles' source; scripts in the
session scratchpad `t681/`: `dump.mjs`, `an.py`, `grav.py`). "Legs" are trip legs a day, as the bubbles count.

**Haneda, Tokyo pack.** Six terminal cells take the airport purpose: 228k legs (pack: 115k outings, so
twice that, matching), 99.9% driving with no rail. The census mesh puts only 2.9k jobs in those six cells
(3.5k work legs). The airport's jobs sit one cell over: 36.6k jobs within 1.5 km of the busiest terminal
cell, 16.6k in cell 41957 (692 m away) and 9.8k in 41930 (346 m), which receive work legs only (20.0k and
11.9k, 0.0% airport). So per cell the two purposes do land in different cells, and the cells where a hover
reads "air" are the terminal cells: 41931 (82k air, 596 work, 99.2% air), 41943 (67k air, 96%), 41932, 41927.
The workers' cells read 0% air.
Where it turns into "all air": the destination bubbles are H3 parents of the cells, and the mix is by mode,
not by purpose. At the zoom of her view (res 8, zoom 11.8 to 13.6) the bubble holding the busiest
terminal cell and the next cell holds 13.6k work legs against 89.5k airport legs (87% air); at res 7 the
bubble with the most jobs, 9.2k work against 136k air (94%); at res 6 (zoom under 10) 55.8k work against
228k air (80%). Only at res 9 (zoom 13.6 and in) are the workers' cells separate bubbles. Under the Work
filter the same bubble is work only (13.6k at res 8), and the airport hover is not shown. Two display
effects add to it: the bubble hover (under All purposes) says "N trips per day to here" with a mode bar and
nothing about purpose, so a bubble that is 87% airport reads as all one thing and looks like the staff's;
and the plane (T-674's replacement, on the busiest terminal cell) has a 12 px hover, which at zoom 12 is
about 370 m and so reaches the nearest job cell, 346 m away; its text is the whole airport (228k), not the
bubble's.

**Double count and wrong cell: neither.** The airport purpose's own ends (home and visitor side) exclude
every cell within 1.5 km of a terminal (`AIRPORT_CLEAR_M`, `everyday.rs`), and its volume comes from
boardings, never from jobs, so staff are not in it; staff are the work purpose's jobs (work legs 44.2k for
36.6k jobs within 1.5 km, about 1.2 legs a job, as everywhere). Terminal cells are placed from OSM terminal
footprints, and the jobs from the mesh by where the establishments are: cargo, maintenance, offices and
the like are not in the passenger terminal's cell. That is a true placement, not an error, at 346 to 700 m.

| JFK (nyc, LODES) | 149k | 2.4k | 11.2k | work 0.3k, air 81.1k: 100% air |

Heathrow and Pearson behave like Haneda (terminal cells pure air, the staff's cells next door pure work,
bubbles merging them at res 8 and coarser). JFK is the extreme the other way: its jobs are mostly
a km out (11.2k within 1.5 km, 0.25k within 500 m), so its destination bubbles over the terminals are
about 100% air, and the staff are in bubbles 1 to 1.5 km away. So it is not Tokyo-specific and not
a build-route bug.

**What T-674's fix leaves.** Its stale airport bubble sat over exactly these destination bubbles and is gone;
that part is solved. What remains is the above: terminal cells are air, and the bubbles that merge them
with the job cells (res 8 and coarser) read mostly air under All purposes with nothing in the hover saying
so, and the plane's hover radius reaches the job cells at zoom 12 and out.

**A second thing, in the work purpose and not airport-specific.** "Staff should come from nearby" is not what
the zone gravity gives Haneda. Workers into Haneda's biggest job zone (3313, 20k jobs) come from 22 km on
average, 12% from within 5 km and 28% within 10 km, against a median of 64% within 5 km and 78% within
10 km for the 200 biggest job zones in the pack (Heathrow's zone: 46% within 5 km, 67% within 10 km). The
zone has no resident commuters of its own (4.8k within 3 km, 57k within 5 km) and Tokyo has about twice as
many jobs as resident commuters within 20 km, so the balancing factor on that zone is 33 times the median
(2.0 per job against 0.89). Any zone with many jobs and few homes does this (24 zones in the pack have
over 5,000 jobs and under a tenth as many resident commuters); Haneda is one of them.

**Proposal.** Display only, for the airport finding, no change to demand (the airport purpose already
excludes staff and the mesh places them correctly): (1) under All purposes, add a thin purpose bar under
the mode bar in the destination bubble hover (purpose colours exist: work blue, airport olive), so "87%
airport" is visible and the staff's work share reads as work; (2) shrink the plane's hover to the plane
itself (about 8 px) and word it as the airport's, so it stops catching the job bubbles. Both are in
`map/commuterView.ts`, `map/modeBar.ts`, `map/airportMarks.ts`. The wide catchment of job-heavy,
home-poor zones is a separate demand question for Anita (a commute rule, so hers to approve; it would
apply to every such zone, not to airports, and the evidence is the 28% within 10 km above); I would file it
as its own task and not widen this one.
