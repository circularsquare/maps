# T-511: homes without a car

2026-10-16. Rule: docs/demand.md 4.3 "Homes without a car"; sources and caveats:
docs/demand-worldwide.md "Driving alternative and homes without a car"; docs/data-register.md
"Car ownership". Patch script `pipeline/nocar.py`; curve fit `notes/t511_fit.py`. Backups of every
pack and res-9 copy before the patch: `data/work/pre-T511/`. Logs: `../artifacts/trainworld/T-511-*`.

## What was built

- **Data.** Per cell `nocar` (f32, people in a household with no car) in all 67 packs, res-9 copy
  first, then the merged pack by merge_cells' own sum (checked to reproduce every other per-cell
  array exactly), both append-only with the .bin replaced before the .json; the merge stamp
  updated, so `merge_cells.py` reruns as before. No re-solve. Chain after: `read_pack.py` 67/67
  OK, `routes.py --check` all current.
- **Curve.** Households with no car from cars per household c: `exp(-1.30 c^1.33)`, households
  weighted, on US tracts (B08201) and English LSOAs (TS045) of 50+ households. US alone 1.34 /
  1.29, England 1.24 / 1.29; rmse 0.040 (Poisson exp(-c): 0.106). By band of c the fit is within
  0.03 of both countries everywhere except c < 0.4 (0.03-0.04 high).
- **Households to people**: ACS national, no-vehicle households 1.74 people, others 2.51 (ratio
  0.693): 8.3% of households, 5.9% of people. England's 23.5% of households becomes 17.6%.
- **Model** (`kernel::mode_choice` lanes, `trip::pair_choice`, everyday `pass`): per home subzone
  the share c without a car; their rail against walking the whole way (road km x 15 perceived
  min, damped like the car), walks to stations only; `r = r_car + c (r_walk - r_car)`. On a
  lane using the rides' row, their trips are assigned on the walks' row. `Params::nocar` (per cell,
  from the pack), `set_extra("nocar", k)` scales it (0 = off). Test: `pair_choice_reproduces_mode_choice`
  now runs with shares 0-0.8 (worst origin 7e-7; breaking the split path fails it at 0.32).
  The regional layer is unchanged: its sites have no subzones or home shares, so it is not trivial.

## Reading it

- Rail rises where homes are car-free, as expected, and most where the car-free also live near
  stations (the Bronx +22 points, Kings +17, Manhattan +13). The suburbs barely move.
- It overshoots in the dense counties because the car-free's only other way is walking: the bus,
  out of scope, carries many of them in reality. So T-509's rail constant will come down, which also cuts rail for car owners.
  A cheaper "other" for the car-free (a bus-like mode, e.g. the drive's time at a slower speed
  with no parking) would be one more constant to fit; not done (Anita's call).
- Richmond falls: its car-free homes are mostly beyond a walk to the SIR, and they lose the ride.
- The everyday layer moves more than commutes (shorter trips, where walking the whole way is the
  only alternative and a 2-4 km walk loses to rail).
- Brief said no other tuning: nothing else changed.
