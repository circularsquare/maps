# T-682: a real-world rail layer for reference (Anita's playtest, 2026-10-18)

Anita: "add ability to show real world network just for reference" and, in her longer list, "add rough
outlines of like 'where does track exist irl' so that people can follow that? would be nice if we
could just like few click fast reconstruct the entire route from tokyo to osaka."

## Source: noritetsu already has it
`maps/noritetsu/` is a global rail map with lines drawn per region from registers (N02 Japan, FRA North
America, RINF Europe, OSM elsewhere; ~118 regions) built into `noritetsu/dist/data/<region>.pmtiles`
(vector tiles per region, lines with owner, type and name; HANDOFF.md ~line 1054). Reusing its tiles
means one source of truth for "real track", no new pipeline. The game's map is MapLibre, so
a PMTiles source and a faint line layer is a small client change; the data goes to R2 beside the
game's packs (`reference_map_publishing`: data to R2 `anitamaps`; Pages breaks pmtiles).

## Stages
1. **A reference layer** (view only): a Settings or Build toggle "Real network", off by default. Faint
   lines under our own network (drawn beneath the overlay), colour by type (high speed, main, metro,
   tram off), loaded for the regions in view only. Dark and light. No clicks. Decide: tram and light
   rail on or off; heritage and disused off (noritetsu's `line_types.md` has the classes).
2. **Trace**: "few clicks reconstruct the route from Tokyo to Osaka": the track tool snaps to the
   reference line (like it snaps to endpoints today), and a **follow** mode: click two points on a real
   line, get blueprint track along it, fitted to our curves (straights and circular arcs up to 350
   degrees, T-418/T-442) with a tolerance in metres, stations offered at the real stations on it.
   This is a fitting problem (polyline to arcs with a bound on the offset) and needs a design of its
   own: do it as T-682b once stage 1 is in.
3. Later: copy a real line's stops and timetable headways (GTFS where noritetsu has them).

## Stage 1 as built (2026-10-19)
`map/realNetwork.ts`; tiles `noritetsu/dist/data/<cc>.pmtiles`, layer `track`, properties k kind, r rank
(0 main, 1 other), p route-confirmed, n no line runs, c colour, and from zoom 10 only w (way id) and nm
(the OSM way's name, native script). **No line type in the tiles**: `types.json` is per line and ways.json
(2.2 MB for Japan) maps ways to lines, and below zoom 10 the tiles hold merged chains with no way id. So
classes are metro (subway), main line (rail rank 0), other rail (rank 1 with p=1). No high-speed class and no
hover. Getting both cheaply needs a tile property from noritetsu's build_tiles.py (say `t` for the type and the
line's English name from zoom 6), a change in noritetsu, not here; the style would then switch on `t`. The
alternative, per-line geom files of the high-speed lines, is 0.5 MB (Japan, register lines only) to 11 MB
(France). File sizes of the regions most likely played: jp 10.5 MB, us 39 MB, gb 8.9 MB, cn 37 MB, ca 8 MB;
read by range, so a view fetches a few hundred KB. regions.json (1.5 MB, outlines) is fetched once when the
setting is first on. Frame cost at zoom 7 over Japan: median 0.8 ms with or without, per forced redraw.
