# T-551: demand review for Anita (2026-10-16)

Measurements only; no parameter changed. Prompted by Anita's New York playtest: rail 14% of trips
against walking 17%, parking seeming to make rail hopeless outside the centre, no car ownership
cost, cars seeming slow. How it was measured is at the end.

## 1. The ceiling

Rail's share, all trips / trips that do not walk. (a) a straight train between every pair at
140 km/h door to door, free, no wait. (b) 100 km/h, a 4-minute walk at each end (weighed 1.5, so 12
perceived minutes), a 5-minute headway (3.4 perceived of wait), $1 + $0.02/km. (c) the real network
today. Walking is reserved by distance before rail and car compete, so it is the same in all three.

| | New York (a) | (b) | (c) | London (a) | (b) | (c) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Walk, all trips | 16.2 | 16.2 | 16.2 | 23.5 | 23.5 | 23.5 |
| Rail, all trips | **59.4** | **43.8** | **12.8** | **54.9** | **41.1** | **17.6** |
| Rail, of non-walk | 70.8 | 52.3 | 15.3 | 71.8 | 53.8 | 23.0 |
| Commutes, of non-walk | 89.3 | 76.0 | 25.4 | 88.2 | 75.9 | 41.0 |
| Everyday, of non-walk | 63.5 | 42.9 | 11.3 | 64.2 | 43.5 | 14.6 |

By purpose, New York, rail of non-walk (a / b / c): commutes 89 / 76 / 25; errands 63 / 42 / 12;
social 64 / 43 / 11; school 62 / 41 / 8; university 75 / 57 / 19; airport 85 / 70 / 11; medical
67 / 46 / 11. The everyday purposes cap lower because their rail constant is 1.1 below the commute's
(about 14 minutes of the car's favour on a short trip).

By straight-line distance, all purposes, rail of non-walk:

| km | 0-2 | 2-5 | 5-10 | 10-20 | 20-50 | 50+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New York, share of trips | 17% | 36% | 25% | 13% | 7% | 2% |
| New York, walk | 77% | 9% | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| New York (a) | 50 | 62 | 71 | 85 | 92 | 95 |
| New York (b) | 29 | 41 | 52 | 70 | 79 | 84 |
| New York (c) | 10 | 13 | 15 | 24 | 17 | 10 |
| London (b) | 33 | 45 | 57 | 76 | 84 | 88 |
| London (c) | 11 | 17 | 24 | 40 | 41 | 37 |

The car's share is the rest: in New York (b) 40% of all trips, (c) 71%. Over half of New York's
trips are under 5 km, where even the free 140 km/h train gets only 50-62% against a 5-10 minute
drive. Past 20 km the ceiling is 80-95%, and the real network's 10-17% there is coverage (most of
those pairs have no station within a walk or a short ride), not the car.

Real New York for comparison (NHTS 2017, New York CBSA households, weekday legs): walk 25.8%, rail
10.2%, bus 6.2%, car 54%, taxi 2%. Rail of non-walk by band: 3 / 9 / 21 / 26 / 21 / 12. London
(TfL Travel in London 2024, table 1, 2023): walk 26%, Underground and rail 20%, bus 13%, car 35%.
So the game walks less than either city, not more: walking at 16% beside rail at 13% is about the
real ratio for the New York region (26 to 10). The game's short trips are fewer than the survey's
(17% of trips under 2 km against NHTS's 43%), because it has home-based trips only; within 2 km it
walks more than NHTS (77% against 58%).

## 2. The car as modelled against real figures

**Speed.** New York commutes that do not walk, door to door on the road (parking time not
included), against NHTS 2017's reported car legs for the same straight-line band (reported time
includes parking and the walk to the car):

| km straight | 2-5 | 5-10 | 10-20 | 20-50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Game, minutes | 10.5 | 18.0 | 29.1 | 39.7 |
| Game, km/h on the straight line | 19.0 | 24.2 | 28.9 | 46.4 |
| NHTS, minutes | 12.9 | 20.2 | 31.9 | 49.0 |
| NHTS, km/h on the straight line | 15.2 | 21.0 | 26.6 | 36.3 |

With the game's 3 parking minutes added the two agree to 10% up to 20 km; past 20 km the game is
about 20% faster. Other sources agree it is not slow: Manhattan's CBD runs 13-16 km/h (Google Maps
data, NBER 2025; 11 km/h in taxi data, June 2024) against the game's dense 15 km/h; TomTom 2024 puts
10 km at 31 minutes in New York city and 19 minutes over the metro area, against the game's 18
minutes for a 10 km road on a commute of 5-10 km. London: the game's 29-35 km/h road speed for
2-20 km trips against TomTom's central 16-17 km/h, about 34 km/h outside the centre at rush hour,
and about 30 km/h metro-wide (10 km in 20 minutes, 2024).

**Parking** is T-380's rule, already checked against real prices (notes/T-380.md): Midtown a paid
space 50 perceived minutes a one-way trip plus the $9 cordon (real 48-54), Downtown Brooklyn 22
(real 19-35), Croydon 18 (30), Canary Wharf 40 (21-33), office parks and suburban centres free
(US: 95% of car commuters park free). Averaged over drivers, a New York commute pays 12-18 perceived
minutes ($4-6) and an everyday trip 7-9 ($2.50); London commutes 8-37 ($2.50-12, the congestion
charge included, highest for 10-20 km trips).

**Car cost.** The car pays parking and cordons only: no ownership cost and no running cost. AAA 2025
(Your Driving Costs): $11,577 a year for a new car at 15,000 miles, of which depreciation, finance,
insurance and registration are $7,972 (about $22 a day) and fuel plus upkeep 24 cents a mile (15
cents a km). Rail pays the game's $1.50 + $0.10/km, so on a 15 km trip rail costs $3.00 (9 perceived
minutes) and the car's real running cost of about $2.25 is not charged.

**Adding an ownership cost** as a sensitivity (`park_min` raised, which every driver pays):

| | New York rail, all trips | London |
|---|---|---|
| Today | 12.8 | 17.6 |
| $5 a car trip ($10 a day over a commute's 2 trips; +15 min) | 14.6 | 20.5 |
| $3.33 a car trip ($10 a day over 3 trips; +10 min) | 13.9 | 19.3 |

## 3. What limits rail outside the centre

Example commutes on the real network, morning peak, perceived minutes (the trip explorer's own
numbers; the rail propensity is worth 8 minutes to rail on these):

- **Mineola to Hicksville** (9.5 km, both on the LIRR main line): car 27 (18 real minutes, free
  parking). Rail 60: walks 19 + 17 (about 1 km at each end), wait 7, ride 8-11, fares 4.5 + 3. Rail
  takes 29%. The walks alone are more than the whole drive.
- **White Plains to Stamford** (18.7 km): car 53 (35 minutes, 7 parking). Rail 94: a ride to the
  station 38 (15 minutes' drive plus the ride's fixed 15 and parking), the walk at work 14, ride 26,
  wait 7, fares 9. Rail 21%.
- **Morristown to Newark** (27.6 km): car 72 (44 minutes, 14 parking). Rail 110: walks 22, rides
  47 + 8 with a change, waits 14, fares 15. Rail 23%.
- **Kingston to Croydon** (London, 14.6 km): car 52 (32 minutes, 10 parking). Rail 79: walks 17,
  two rides 33 minutes with a change, waits 10, fares 12. Rail 48%.
- Against these, Hempstead to Midtown is 80% rail and Jamaica to Downtown Brooklyn 74%: where
  parking is paid (63 and 17), rail wins even with a ride to the station.

Outside the centre parking is free in the game as in reality, so nothing offsets rail's access: the
walk at each end (often a kilometre, about 18 perceived minutes) and the waits and changes of a
network not built for that pair. Parking is not what makes the suburbs hard; it is what makes the
centre easy.

**Levers, measured** (rail's share of all trips; ceiling (b) and the real network (c)):

| Lever | NY (b) | NY (c) | London (b) | London (c) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 43.8 | 12.8 | 41.1 | 17.6 |
| Car ownership $5 a trip | 53.6 | 14.6 | 49.7 | 20.5 |
| Car ownership $3.33 a trip | 50.3 | 13.9 | 46.9 | 19.3 |
| Car running cost $0.15/km (ceiling only: not a model input) | 46.9 | | 43.4 | |
| Cars 25% faster on every road | 40.9 | 12.2 | 38.9 | 16.8 |
| No paid parking anywhere | 42.6 | 11.3 | 40.0 | 15.8 |
| Paid parking from 1,500 jobs/km² (half free at 3,000) | 45.2 | 13.6 | 42.7 | 18.6 |
| Station walks weighed 1.0 instead of 1.5 | 46.0 | 14.5 | 43.0 | 20.5 |
| Rail propensity +0.5 | 51.8 | 15.4 | 48.3 | 21.3 |

In plain terms:

- The ceiling is high: with a straight 100 km/h train between every pair, rail would carry 44% of
  New York's trips (52% of those that do not walk), over three times today's 13%. What holds the
  real network down is how few pairs it serves well, not the car's cost or speed.
- On the real network the propensity moves rail most (+2.6 points in New York, +3.7 in London),
  then an ownership cost (+1.8 and +2.9 at $5 a trip) and the walk weight (+1.7 and +2.9). The
  propensity and the car's cost move the ceiling 2-5 times as much as the real result, because
  most real pairs are far from the margin; the walk weight moves both about equally, since the
  walks to stations are what real trips mostly pay.
- Faster cars would lower rail (-0.6 and -0.8 points at 25% faster), and the game's cars are not
  slow against NHTS, so that change is not supported by the New York data. Paid parking today is
  worth 1.5 points to rail in New York and 1.8 in London; spreading it to lower densities adds
  0.8 and 1.0.
- Walking does not take trips from rail (it is reserved before the choice) and is below the real
  share in both cities; lowering it would hand short trips to the car more than to rail (the 0-5 km
  bands give rail 10-13% of what does not walk).

## How it was measured

The model's own Rust code, in a private copy of the crate (`notes/t551_copy.py` (script removed 2026-10-24, in git history) copies `sim/` to a
scratch folder and adds the test `notes/t551_ceiling.rs`; the shared crate is untouched). Commutes
are taken by zone pair (2 km zones, the gravity's own zone-centre distance, 0.52 x 2 km inside one
zone), everyday trips by the everyday layer's own cell samples, both directions. Every choice goes
through the model's expressions (walk reservation, `drive_min_f`, `park_parts`, `mode_beta`,
`rail_share_parked`, `nocar_choice`); only the ceiling's rail cost is made up. The real network (c)
is `calib`'s run (realnet's `nyc_real.json` and `lon_real.json`, close to the real save; the game's
starting fares $1.50 + $0.10/km; three crowding rounds), with each pair's rail read out of the
copy's pair loop.

Checks: rail by pair sums to the model's own totals (New York commutes 4,177,111 against 4,177,104,
everyday 4,710,621 against 4,710,621; every lever and London the same within 0.001%); and for the
example trips the test's choice function, given the trip explorer's costs, returns the explorer's
share (Morristown 22.9 against 22.9, Mineola 28.7 against 28.6, Croydon 94.5 against 94.5); trips
with a ride at the home end differ by up to 5 points, because people without a car are priced on
walks there and the check prices them on the ride.

Outputs: `../artifacts/trainworld/T-551-nyc.json`, `T-551-lon.json`, `T-551-tables.txt` (every
table here and more), `T-551-nhts.txt`. To rerun: `t551_copy.py <dir>`, then in `<dir>/sim`
`cargo test --release --features demand --lib t551 -- --ignored --nocapture` with `T551_CITY`,
`T551_OUT`, `T551_DATA` (the tree's `data` folder) set; then `t551_tables.py`. One run of both
cities with all levers is about 10 minutes on one core.
