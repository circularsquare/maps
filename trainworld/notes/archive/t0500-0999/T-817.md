# T-817 High-speed track

Stage a (built): a **design speed** in the Build tab, 160 / 200 / 250 / 320 km/h, 160 by default.
Newly drawn track curves wide enough for it (radius 1,796 / 2,806 / 4,385 / 7,183 m at
a_lat 1.1 m/s²; docs/track-and-service.md 6.1). Trains still run at most 160.

Stage b (this note, design only): high-speed track as its own track type, so lines can run
faster than 160 where the player pays for it.

## What it is

- A fourth track type beside single, double and quad: **High speed**, always double track (4 m
  apart, like double). No high-speed quad: a player wanting four tracks draws HSR and double side by
  side, or quad then HSR. In the Build tab it is a third option on the Double / Quad row.
- Picking High speed sets the design speed to its default (below); the player can still step
  the design speed down for a tight approach into a city. Picking Double or Quad again puts back
  the design speed they had.
- What makes it high speed is the edge's type, not its curves. A high-speed edge with 2,000 m curves
  is still HSR; its trains just run at the curve speed there (169 km/h at 2,000 m).

## Default design speed: 320 (recommended)

- 320 km/h is the top speed of the LGV Est and Sud-Est Europe, the KTX on Gyeongbu and Honam, and
  Sanyo/Tohoku Shinkansen stretches; Spain's AVE lines run 300-310. The game's lateral
  acceleration was set from LGV design (7,000 m for 320, params.rs), so 320 is the speed the
  curve rule was calibrated on, and 7,183 m comes out where real 320 lines are drawn (7,000 m
  minimum on LGVs, 7,000 m on Chinese 350 lines).
- 300 would give 6,314 m curves, a fraction tighter, and match Spain and most of Germany. The
  difference on a 100 km line at full speed is 1 minute (20:00 at 300 against 18:45 at 320).
- 320 is also the top of the design speed stepper, so "HSR = the widest curves the game offers",
  one number for the player to learn. China's 350 is not offered; it can be added to the stepper
  and train later if anyone misses it.

## What it costs

Real figures (open sources, infrastructure only, all terrain mixed):

| | High speed | Conventional double track | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| China | 129M yuan/km at 350 km/h, 87M at 250 (World Bank 2014, 27 lines); US$17-21M/km at 350 | GuiGuang (mixed 200/120 km/h, mountain) about US$14.6M/km, project total | 350 against 250: 1.5x (ITF/OECD: 1.9x, mostly the viaducts slab track needs) |
| Spain | €14M/km average (AT Kearney 2015), €17.7M/km (Ineco 2017) | Girona - Figueres upgrade €2.3M/km; new mixed lines with tunnels €25-30M/km (Santander - Bilbao estimate) | about 2x on comparable terrain |
| Korea | Honam HSR 8.35 trillion won for 182 km: about 46bn won (US$40M) a km, many viaducts and tunnels | Iksan - Yeosu double tracking and electrification: 0.98 trillion won for 154 km, 6.4bn won a km | large, but the conventional figure is an upgrade |

The ratio that holds across sources: like for like (same terrain, same share in tunnel), a
350 km/h line costs about 1.5-1.9x a 250 km/h one, and conventional new double track sits below
that again. What makes HSR dear is the alignment (long viaducts, tunnels instead of following the
ground), which the game already prices through elevation, plus slab track, catenary and signalling.

**Recommendation: HSR track costs 1.5x the level price of double track** (`HSR_COST` in params.rs,
beside `SINGLE_TRACK_COST` 0.6). At ground level that is US$40M a route-km against double's 27M;
on a viaduct (+1, 0.8) US$108M. Simple and predictable (Anita, 2026-10-20). Stations on HSR cost
the same as on double track; junctions and flyovers the same, 1.5x because their km share is
priced as track.

## What lines may do on it

- **Speed cap by track type**: double, quad and single track keep 160 km/h; HSR allows up to 320.
  The cap is per stretch, so a train's speed profile is `min(curve speed, the track's cap, the
  train's top speed)` piece by piece. Curve speed already follows the real radius.
- **Trains**: the line's trains are high-speed stock when the line runs on any HSR, ordinary stock
  otherwise. No separate setting: the player builds HSR and the line gets fast trains. High-speed
  stock has top speed 320 and costs more:
  - **Price**: 1.5x a car (US$3.75M per 20 m car against 2.5). Real: Talgo Avril about €22.5M for a
    200 m train (Público, the tender), Velaro-type sets more; about 1.5x a commuter EMU per metre.
  - **Running cost**: US$12 a train-km plus US$3 a car-km against 9 + 2 (energy grows with speed;
    the faster turn of a set over a day pays some back, and the game counts that already).
  - **Acceleration**: a third band, 0.3 m/s² above 200 km/h (0.5 from 60 to 200, 1.0 below 60), so
    0-320 takes about 3.5 minutes and 11 km, near real sets. Ordinary stock is unchanged.
  - A high-speed train off HSR runs like any train at 160; it pays its higher price and costs
    there too, which is the reason to keep fast trains on lines that use the fast track.
- **Capacity** needs nothing new: a section's headway already grows with the speed reached on it
  (`70 s + v / 0.6`), so an HSR section at 320 takes at most 16 trains an hour against 25 at 160.

## A line mixing HSR and normal track

It runs each stretch at that stretch's limit: 320 on HSR, 160 on double track, slower in curves
and at stations. Joints between the types are plain nodes with no speed limit of their own (the
train brakes for the lower cap ahead, as for any curve). A line that touches HSR anywhere gets
high-speed stock for all its trains, so running a commuter line over 1 km of HSR costs its whole
fleet the dearer stock; the line inspector names this in the cost hover.

## How it draws

Same width as double track, with a thin light centre line between the two tracks (dark in the
dark theme), so HSR reads at a glance at every zoom without a new colour (the elevation colours
stay). The ghost while drawing shows the same. The track inspector names the type ("High speed")
and its speed cap.

## Demand

None beyond travel time: faster trains shorten in-vehicle time, which the demand model already
uses. No HSR bonus, no separate fare (fares stay base plus per-km).

## Saves

An edge's flags gain `| 64` for high speed (`save.rs`, the next free bit after crossover's 32).
Old saves have none and load as before. The design speed is not saved: it only shaped the corners
when drawn, and set radii are already in the save.

## Build stages

1. **b1 Track type** (built 2026-10-21): `EdgeData::hsr`, save flag, cost 1.5x, the Build tab
   option (sets design speed 320), the draw look, inspector name, the quote's "high-speed track"
   rows. Lines run as before (160). Anita's calls the same day: over 160 needs HSR, 320 by
   default, and high-speed stock is the player's choice per line (not automatic as above; b2/b3
   follow that).
2. **b2 + b3 built 2026-10-21** (as Anita's calls, not the automatic stock above): per-piece
   `min(curve, track cap, line top)` in `service::build_run`, three traction bands in profile.rs
   (0-320 in 206 s, 11 km), `LineData::fast` saved as a trailing `LHS1` block, fleet `[ordinary,
   high speed]` in wasm.rs, `HS_CAR_PRICE` 3.75 and 12 + 3 running, the line panel's Trains switch,
   Top speed row and stop-time hovers (`line_stretch_speeds`), the track inspector's Speed limit.
   Check: London to Paris via Lille, 454 km all HSR, 6/4/2 trains: Paris at 2h52m24s with
   ordinary trains, 1h29m55s with high-speed ones; with Lille to Paris ordinary track, 2h07m14s.
   The Max speed colours still stop at 160 (`SPEED_STOPS`), so HSR draws as 160 there.
   Original plan for b2, kept for reference: **b2 Speed** (medium-large): per-piece speed cap by track type in `profile.rs` (the train
   profile now caps at the global `V_TOP`), high-speed stock's 320 top speed and third
   acceleration band, line stock chosen from its path. Touches profile, capacity and service: run
   after T-797's reversals land. Smoke's real-save figure must not move (no HSR in it).
3. **b3 Money** (small): car price and running cost for high-speed stock, the fleet quote, line
   inspector hover.
4. **b4 Docs and look pass** (small): ui-building.md, track-and-service.md, SPEC, a screenshot
   check with HSR in a new game.
