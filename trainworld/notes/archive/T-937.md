# T-937: do the trip explorer's car and walking legs follow real roads?

For Anita, 2026-10-22. Read-only; no code changed.

## What is drawn

Straight. `app/src/map/tripView.ts` `build` draws only the train trip: the walk or drive to the
first station and the walk from the last one are dashed straight lines (their hovers say
"Straight-line distance"), a change between stations is a straight dashed line, and only the rides
follow the track. Driving or walking the whole way is not drawn at all; the panel shows its
minutes.

## What the times come from

Neither real road routing per trip nor a bare factor: a factor per pair of zones, measured once
on real roads.

- **Drives**: road length = straight line x a routed factor x 1.074. `pipeline/routes.py` (the
  `routes` build step) loads the OSM extract's drivable roads (every public road but service
  ways), runs Dijkstra (scipy) from each 2 km zone's representative to every zone within 6 squares
  (`drive_near`) and between 4 x 4 supercells beyond (`drive_far`), and stores road metres over
  straight metres as one byte a pair. The speed is not from the roads: it is the density rule
  (local streets for the first 2 km, main roads for the next 8, open highway between). A pack
  without routes uses 1.3 x the straight line. `drive_factor_median` is the pack gate's check on
  those bytes (fails outside 1.1-2.0).
- **Walks**: a 50 m barrier raster (water, with OSM's walkable bridges as land). Walks to stations
  are searched on it in the game per station; walks between cells use per-part factors
  (`walk_zone`). It knows water and bridges, not streets: on dry land a walk is the straight line
  x 1.3.
- The trip explorer (`sim/src/demand/trip.rs`) uses exactly these, so its minutes match the
  demand.

## What drawing legs along roads would cost

No road graph is kept: routes.py reduces the roads to the byte tables and drops them. The cached
OSM roads (`data/raw/osm/*.ways2.npz`) are on disk only for the older packs; a world run
(`world_run.py`) deletes each region's downloads and road caches when it is done.

New York's pack area, from the cached NY, NJ and CT roads:

| Roads | Ways | Points | Way ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| Every drivable road | 561k | 6.2M | 722k |
| Tertiary and up | 203k | 2.1M | 186k |

- **Data**: today's pack is 7.4 MB (its drive tables 1.2 MB). A drawable graph of tertiary and up,
  simplified to about 10 m and stored as 16-bit points, would be about 3-5 MB more; every drivable
  road 15-25 MB. Fetched by Range request only when the explorer opens, like the walk raster.
- **Runtime per click**: an A* search on the main-road graph (about 150k junctions) in TypeScript,
  10-50 ms for a city trip; the full graph 50-150 ms. Fine for a click, too slow for hovers.
- **Build**: a new output from `routes.py` (the graph it already builds, minus residential, plus
  the geometry) and a rerun of the routes step for every pack (10 s to 8 minutes each, with the
  extract downloaded again where it was deleted).
- **Simplest way**: draw the drive along tertiary-and-up roads from the nearest road point at each
  end (ends straight), and leave walks straight: walking legs are short and the walk model has no
  streets, so a street-following walk line would be invented. No OSRM-like service is available
  in the browser; the pack graph is the only option.
- **The catch**: the drawn route would be the shortest by length on real roads, while the minutes
  come from the zone factor and the density speeds, so the line and its minutes can disagree
  (most where the factor is averaged over a zone, or for drives under about 2 km).

I would not build it unless the straight lines bother her in play: it costs a pipeline rerun of
every pack for a picture, and changes no number.
