# T-793 The loading bar's last 5%

Anita (2026-10-21): "loading bar: the last like 5% takes up most of the time". The bar was already
weighted by expected time (T-243); this measures it again and fixes what no longer fit.

Method: headless Chrome, its own temporary profile, own Vite on 6147 (dev server), the bar's fill
read from the DOM every 100 ms (`.load-fill` width), start menu for the new game (a), the real save
(T-007) by `?continue=1` (b), a pan to a zone not loaded (c). Scripts are in the scratchpad
(`measure.mjs` and friends), not kept. The machine was busy with other agents throughout (3 solving
workers, 16 threads): the real save's solve took 40-65 s here against about 20 s in T-243, so read the
shapes, not the seconds.

## Where the time went

| Load | Stages (this machine, busy) |
|---|---|
| New game (a), 2.1-2.7 s | downloads 0.4-0.9 s (nyc.bin 8 MB, market 3.4 MB); the viewed zone opens bare in the aux worker, 2.0-2.4 s after its download; the market solve is 0.1 s. The card goes when the bare figures arrive |
| Real save (b), 43-65 s | downloads to 1-2 s; network known at about 2 s (New Haven and Little Rock join); opening to 6-7 s (seven market packs about 0.65 s each in a row, then New York); solve sent at 6-7 s |
| Real save's solve | 5 periods on 3 workers is two waves. Round 0 (free flow, setup 1 s) 10.8 s, round 1 11.9 s, round 2 11.5 s, round 3 6.2 s (periods 3 and 4 carry over, 0.4 s). Final at about 47 s. The solve is 80% of the load |
| Pan to a zone (c), 0.5-2.1 s | its pack downloads (0.1-0.5 s) and opens bare (tor 2.6 MB 0.6 s, tyo 6.7 MB 1.9 s, lon 3.9 MB 1.2 s, about 250-300 ms per MB) |

What stood behind the last 5%:

- **The solve's expected time came from one remembered number.** Its expectation was the last
  startup's solve scaled by stops, or with no record 600 ms + 2.2 ms a stop times 2.5. Real: 4-5 times
  that on this machine (the model was measured with one worker per period, and the final takes 3.7
  first estimates, not 2.5). The bar reached 99% when the first estimate was due and stood there for
  the other rounds: no record, 99% at 16 s of 48 s; a record a third of the truth, 99% at 20 s of 44 s.
  Nothing in the solve corrected the guess while it ran.
- **A finished download kept growing.** Its expected length was the time since it started, so after
  the download ended its weight grew with the clock and pushed the fraction up (new game: 99% at
  1.6 s of 2.1-2.5 s).
- **Opening was expected at 130 ms per MB**, measured on 16 MB packs; New York is now 8 MB and
  opens at about 250 ms per MB (it is not size alone), and a market pack costs about 0.45 s whatever
  its size. So the new game and zone bars finished their opening step early and sat at 98-99%.

## What changed (`workers/demandClient.ts`)

Weights (expected ms against the first estimate, one unit = round 0 with its setup):

| Step | Expected |
|---|---|
| Download | its rate so far; a finished download stays as long as it took (`Download.end`) |
| Open a city pack | 220 ms per MB of .bin (was 130) |
| Open a market pack | 450 ms + 20 ms per MB (was 60 ms per MB) |
| Solve, round 0 | 1 unit; first estimate 600 ms + 2.2 ms per stop, times the periods a worker takes in turn (`solveWaves`, 2 on 3 workers) when nothing is remembered |
| Solve, rounds 1, 2, 3 | 1.1, 1.05, 0.55 units (`ROUND_WEIGHT`, from the round times above) |

The solve stage is `solveStage`: each round published is real progress; the pace (ms per unit)
measured over the rounds so far sets the expected length of the rest, so a slow machine or a big
network corrects the bar after one round instead of at the end. Inside the round in hand the fill is
what has landed of its periods or the time against its expectation, creeping past it, never done until
it publishes. It counts the rounds of the cluster solved ahead (T-402) when the card goes at that
cluster's final, and drives the other zones' bars after the card goes. No Rust change: progress is
per round and period, which the workers already report; reporting inside a period would need the
solver loop.

## Not done

- The bar reads about 96% (not 100%) when the new game or a zone arrives: it is never full before
  the work is done, and the work finishes slightly before the model says.
- The first load of a browser with no record still opens low (45% at 5 s on the real save) because
  the stop count is unknown until the network arrives (about 2 s in); a record fixes it.
- Reporting from inside a period (Rust) was not needed.

All are under the 25% line. Where the remaining time goes:

- **Tokyo is a heavier solve than New York at the same stops:** its round 0 is 26-34 s against 10-12 s
  (the cost follows the zone, not just the stop count), final at 77-98 s against 33-38 s. With no
  solve remembered the bar guessed New York's cost, so it stalled at 58% from 20 s to 40 s waiting for
  round 0 (13.8 s flat). Once round 0 landed the rest ran evenly.
- **Cold real save:** before the network is known (about 2 s in) the bar assumed a small game, so it
  stood at 51% after 4.5 s of a 45 s load.
- **A warm Tokyo load can have a short card:** when the zone in view is solved ahead (T-402), the
  card goes at its final (10 s here) and the remaining 84 s of solve show as the zone's own bar. A
  cold start from the new game sheet did not solve ahead in the same game (the card stayed up for
  all 106 s), so a record made there (final at 98 s) is wrong for the warm path (final at 10 s): the
  card bar stands at about 12% for 8 s and jumps. The next load of that path remembers its own time.

Follow-up change (`workers/demandClient.ts`: `lastSolve`, `rememberSolve`, `solveEstimate`,
`solveStage`, and the period-receive handler):

- the remembered solve is kept per zone in view (the last 8), the last one made standing in for
  the others, so a Tokyo game and a New York game no longer overwrite each other's estimate;
- with nothing remembered and the network not yet known, a game of 1,000 stops is assumed (it was 0);
- once the first period of a round is in, the round is expected at that time times the waves of periods
  a worker takes (`Solve.roundFirstAt`), so a game heavier than guessed corrects the bar after its first
  wave, not at the end of the round.
