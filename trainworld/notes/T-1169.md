# T-1169 and T-1545: a demand worker's memory, measured, and a plan (stage 1, 2026-10-27)

Stage 1 is measurement and design only: nothing in sim/ or app/ changed. The design goes to Anita
before any stage is built. T-1545 (the market pass's station x station tables) is folded in here
as stage 3.

## For Anita

- **Where a worker's memory goes, all-China save** (169 zones reached, 370 market packs open, a
  solving worker with three periods): 1.56 GB held between solves, 2.04 GB at the solve's peak.
  Of the 1.56 GB, **the regional market's composition is 0.73 GB** (the parsed market packs kept
  0.26, the pair rules 0.17, commutes and outings between zones 0.12, the rest 0.18), **the city
  zones 0.73 GB** (their packs 0.48, set on the network 0.18, solved periods 0.07; 3.0 MB a zone
  typically, 30 MB for Shanghai) and the market's network and solved periods 0.10 GB. The peak's
  extra 0.47 GB is **one wasteful table**: the market's graph puts every station in a 300 m grid
  spanning the whole network (2,400 km across), 63 million mostly empty buckets, for a moment.
- **All of Japan** (45 zones, 10,320 stations, a worker with two periods): 0.97 GB held, **2.90 GB
  at the peak**, of which 1.93 GB is the market pass's temporary tables: four station x station
  tables of 326 MB each (route costs, the same with the planned first wait, fare km, trips), the
  kept routes 0.26 GB, and 0.27 GB for a list of commuters' station pairs grown by doubling.
- **All 723 Chinese zones would need about 4.3 GB a worker held** (est., scaling each part by its
  packs' size), against the 4 GB ceiling: the city zones about 2.8 GB of it, the market's
  composition about 1.3 GB.
- **The plan** (below): first three cheap, exact fixes (no figure changes): the grid, the pair
  rules and the market packs' duplicate copy (stage 2); then the market pass worked out a few
  hundred stations at a time instead of all at once, which also drops one of its two route searches
  (stage 3, T-1545: Japan's peak about 2.9 to 1.5 GB, and faster); then the coarse zones you already
  approved (T-1223, about a quarter off every zone not looked at). With those, all of China comes
  to about 2.6-2.9 GB held a worker: under the ceiling, but how much WASM that becomes (the heap
  fragments: 1.07-1.38 times, T-1205) decides whether it really fits, so it is close. Dealing zones
  between workers is the change that would make it comfortable, and is what the whole world needs
  in the end; it is a big redesign of the worker pool (stage 5).
- **Your call:** stages 2-4 change nothing you would see and I would just build them in order.
  Stage 5: design it now, alongside, or wait until stages 2-4 are measured on an all-China save?
  I'd wait for the measurement, since it may not be needed for China.

## How it was measured

A diagnostic build (scratch copy, reverted; `T-1169/diag.patch` holds the change against today's
master): every allocation carries a 16-byte header with a tag, the tag being the WASM call it was
made in, named by the worker per class, method and zone (`EverydayApi.set_network@sha`), and
finer scopes inside the market's composition (`comp.*`) and pass (`mkt.*`). The allocator keeps
each tag's live bytes and, every 2 MB of new heap peak, a snapshot of all of them, so the peak is
broken down too. Reports go over a BroadcastChannel to the page. To measure a later stage: apply
the patch in an agent tree, build `wasm:demand`, put `measure.mjs` in that tree's `checks/` (it
imports `lib.mjs`), run it against the tree's own Vite with its deps cache outside the main tree's
`node_modules/.vite` (`WINDOW=1` stops after the first market pass). `T-1169/measure.mjs` loads a save
on its own Vite and collects them to the weekday final; `T-1169/analyze.py` sums them. Runs on my
Vite (6163), headless Chrome, the default pool (three solving workers and the aux; the all-China
save drops to two by T-1205's cap). Heap figures are exact; the instrumented build's WASM sizes are
not representative (2,364 MB on all-China against T-1205's 2,077 in production), so everything
below is heap. JSON per run in `../artifacts/trainworld/t1169/`.

Saves: `t1156-all-china.save` (maps root; 123 lines, 2,363 stations, 169 Chinese zones reached),
`artifacts/trainworld/t737-japan-trains.save` (709 lines, 10,320 stations, 45 zones), the real
New York save.

Per Chinese zone (city parts only): median 3.0 MB, 90th percentile 8.6, Shanghai 30, Beijing 24;
the top ten 157 MB, the other 159 zones 3.5 MB on average. Per zone the commutes' pack is 1.3 MB
(median), the everyday pack 1.0, its network 0.6, solved periods under 0.1. A market pack costs
2.0 MB on average, and 370 are open for 169 zones reached (2.2 a zone: every zone with a station
within 30 km). The aux worker holds 293 MB (a commute copy of every open zone, which the bubbles'
commuters by mode read); it is not on any solving worker's 4 GB, only on the page's total.

**Why the grid.** `build_graph` finds the stations within the 300 m transfer walk of each station
through `StationGrid`, a dense array of 300 m buckets over the stations' whole extent, built twice
(`off` and its copy `fill`), 8 bytes a bucket. The market's part is set on the whole cluster, here
all of China; on a network from Lisbon to Shanghai it would be several GB at once.

## 2. All of Japan, a solving worker (periods 0, 3)

Rail 24,207,996 a day; s = 9,249 served stations, 8,466 searched, 3.1 million zone pairs.
Heap at rest 967 MB, peak 2,900 MB (the worker with one period: 777 and 2,710).

| part | held | at the peak |
|---|---|---|
| the market's route costs `r`, s x s | 0 | 326 |
| the same with the planned first wait `re`, s x s (T-1430) | 0 | 326 |
| the fare km a station pair `km`, s x s (T-1431's income groups) | 0 | 326 |
| the longer trips' station to station trips `od`, s x s | 0 | 326 |
| the search and the routes kept for the second sweep (256 MB cap) | 0 | 259 |
| the trip count's commute pair list and its sparse form (`od_c`, below) | 0 | 268 |
| the far-zone table of the period being solved (`st`) | | 90 |
| the market's solved periods (102 MB a period: the far-zone table `st`, pair figures) | 205 | 205 |
| the market's part set on the network | 150 | 150 |
| city zones set on the network (everyday 127, commutes 24) | 151 | 151 |
| city zones' solved periods (commutes 89, everyday 77) | 165 | 165 |
| city packs (commutes 85, everyday 63) | 148 | 148 |
| market composition (45 packs; corridors 53 of it) | 138 | 138 |

Per Japanese zone: median 6.1 MB, Tokyo 99, Osaka 56, Nagoya 45. The four s x s tables are 1.3 GB
of the 1.9 GB that comes and goes; with s growing they grow as its square (T-1545).

**Inside the trip count** (`demand`; a second run stopped after the first daytime pass, one-period
worker, peak 2,606 MB): the commutes' station pair list `od_c` reaches 193 MB (a `Vec` of
(destination, origin, riders) grown by doubling, two entries for each of 167,576 commute pairs'
terms), and its sorted sparse form 75 MB is made while the list is still held; the far-zone table
`st` is 90 MB, which the period keeps. The grid of section 1 is 197 MB on Japan, outside the
pass's peak.

**How many columns a pass needs at once** (measured in the same build, first daytime pass): the
logsums read 32.8 million of the 85.5 million station pairs (38%: every station is in some zone's
access list), so a sparse table with an index per entry would be no smaller. But each station's
column is needed only by the destination zones whose access lists hold it. Going through the
10,989 destination zones in their own order, at most 562 stations' columns are needed at once
(277 on average); in order of longitude 293 (83). So the four tables need about 562 x 9,249 x 4 x
4 = 83 MB at once, not 1.3 GB, and the routes towards those stations can be kept until their
columns are assigned, which drops the second search sweep.

## 3. New York (the real save), for scale

Heap at rest 197 MB, peak 291 MB; the market's composition 98 MB of it (77 the composition, 12 the

## 4. All 723 Chinese zones (estimated)

Scaling the 169 zones' city parts by their packs' size (4.7 bytes held per byte of `.bin`) gives
the other 554 zones about 2.1 GB, so 2.8 GB for all 723. The market's composition scales with the
market packs open (184 MB of JSON open here, 328 MB for all of China; the pair rules with their
count, 370 to 723): about 1.3 GB. With the market's part (about 0.13 GB) and the solved periods,
**about 4.3 GB held**, over the 4 GB ceiling before any peak. T-1206 estimated 4 GB the same way.

## 5. Options, ranked

Savings in MB a solving worker; "China" is the all-China save, "all CN" the 723-zone estimate.

| # | option | saves | effort | speed | figures |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | `StationGrid` sparse (buckets in a sorted list or a hash, only the occupied ones) | peak: China 482, all CN about 500, any network spanning continents GBs | an hour | none | to the bit |
| B | pair rules only for open-to-open place pairs, the rest worked out when asked (as other pairs already are), or a compact row (f32, the three fields the hot loops read) | China 153, all CN 280 | small | to measure: `zone_far` style loops ask open-to-any pairs | to the bit (a cache) |
| C | the parsed market packs not kept beside the composition: keep them compact (f32 sites, no copy of the commute rows the composition holds) or recompose from the composition | China 130-259, all CN 250-460 | medium | none, or a slower recompose | to the bit |
| D | the trip count's commute list grown by doubling and sorted (`od_c`, then `SparseOd::of`): count first, place by destination, in the order added | Japan peak about 190 | small | slightly faster | to the bit |
| E | the market pass a window of destination zones at a time (T-1545): `r`, `re`, `km`, `od` and the routes held for the window's stations only, each station's column assigned once its last zone is done, one search sweep | Japan peak about 1.2 GB (1.3 GB of tables to under 0.1, plus the 256 MB of kept routes) | large | faster: one search sweep instead of two (the sweeps are half the market's time, T-782) | the same trips, summed in another order: rounding-level |
| F | coarse zones (T-1223, designed in T-1206, approved): every zone not looked at for 10 minutes at a third of the everyday samples | 25-35% of a zone's city parts: China about 0.2, all CN about 0.7-0.9 | large (already a task) | faster opening and solve | approved as designed |
| G | `site_key` and the composition's rows in leaner types (sorted Vec for the HashMap, u16/f32 where they fit) | China 30-60 | small | none | to the bit |
| H | zones dealt between solving workers instead of periods: each worker holds its share of the city zones for all five periods, the loads added up on the page each round (as T-1543 adds a period's parts) | city parts divided by the pool: all CN 2.8 GB to 1.4 with two workers | very large: the pool, the clusters, every question about a zone | unknown: one exchange a round | to the bit possible |
| I | sparse `r` (only the pairs the logsums read) | none: 38% of pairs read, an index per entry costs more than it saves | | | |
| J | half-precision route costs or fare km | 326-652 MB on Japan | small | none | changes figures everywhere; not proposed |

The aux worker's 293 MB of commute copies (China) and each worker's 0.7 GB of JS heap outside WASM
(T-1205) count against the page's memory budget, not the 4 GB a worker; out of scope here.

Stages 2-4 together (est., all CN): B 0.28, C 0.25-0.46, G 0.1, F 0.7-1.0 off 4.3 GB held. Whether
2.6-2.9 GB held fits depends on fragmentation: T-1205's fit puts a fresh worker's WASM at the most of
1.38 times the held heap and 1.07 times the peak, and on all-China today both come to the same
2.08 GB because the grid's 0.48 GB transient sits on top of the held heap. With the grid gone the
peak is close to the held heap, and which factor then applies is not known until measured.

T-1169 stays open as the umbrella until an all-China save with every zone reached loads and solves
within the ceiling: building that save (the T-1156 tooling, `t1156-build.mjs`-style edits through
the edit API) is part of stage 5's measuring, or of T-1223's stage 5 check. T-898 (pack opening
time) and T-782 (market routing time) stay as they are; E helps T-782.

T-1543 (one period in parts) lands in demand.worker.ts and demandClient.ts: it does not change
where memory goes (every worker still holds every zone), and its page-side adding up of parts is
the precedent stage 5 would build on. Nothing in stages 2-3 touches the files it changes, except
that the diag patch's worker part will need re-applying to the new demand.worker.ts.
