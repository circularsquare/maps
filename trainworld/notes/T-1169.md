# T-1169 and T-1545: a demand worker's memory, measured, and a plan (stage 1, 2026-10-27)

Stage 1 is measurement and design only: nothing in sim/ or app/ changed. The design goes to Anita
before any stage is built. T-1545 (the market pass's station x station tables) is folded in here
as stage 3.

## For Anita

- **Where a worker's memory goes, all-China save** (169 zones reached, 370 market packs open, a
  solving worker with three periods): 1.56 GB held between solves, 2.04 GB at the solve's peak.
  Of the 1.56 GB, **the regional market's composition is 0.73 GB** (the parsed market packs kept
  0.26, the pair rules 0.17, commutes and outings between zones 0.12, the rest 0.18), **the city
  zones 0.73 GB** (their packs 0.48, set on the network 0.18, solved periods 0.07; 3.0 MB a zone
  typically, 30 MB for Shanghai) and the market's network and solved periods 0.10 GB. The peak's
  extra 0.47 GB is **one wasteful table**: the market's graph puts every station in a 300 m grid
  spanning the whole network (2,400 km across), 63 million mostly empty buckets, for a moment.
- **All of Japan** (45 zones, 10,320 stations, a worker with two periods): 0.97 GB held, **2.90 GB
  at the peak**, of which 1.93 GB is the market pass's temporary tables: four station x station
  tables of 326 MB each (route costs, the same with the planned first wait, fare km, trips), the
  kept routes 0.26 GB, and 0.27 GB for a list of commuters' station pairs grown by doubling.
- **All 723 Chinese zones would need about 4.3 GB a worker held** (est., scaling each part by its
  packs' size), against the 4 GB ceiling: the city zones about 2.8 GB of it, the market's
  composition about 1.3 GB.
- **The plan** (below): first three cheap, exact fixes (no figure changes): the grid, the pair
  rules and the market packs' duplicate copy (stage 2); then the market pass worked out a few
  hundred stations at a time instead of all at once, which also drops one of its two route searches
  (stage 3, T-1545: Japan's peak about 2.9 to 1.5 GB, and faster); then the coarse zones you already
  approved (T-1223, about a quarter off every zone not looked at). With those, all of China comes
  to about 2.6-2.9 GB held a worker: under the ceiling, but how much WASM that becomes (the heap
  fragments: 1.07-1.38 times, T-1205) decides whether it really fits, so it is close. Dealing zones
  between workers is the change that would make it comfortable, and is what the whole world needs
  in the end; it is a big redesign of the worker pool (stage 5).
- **Your call:** stages 2-4 change nothing you would see and I would just build them in order.
  Stage 5: design it now, alongside, or wait until stages 2-4 are measured on an all-China save?
  I'd wait for the measurement, since it may not be needed for China.

## How it was measured

A diagnostic build (scratch copy, reverted; `T-1169/diag.patch` holds the change against today's
master): every allocation carries a 16-byte header with a tag, the tag being the WASM call it was
made in, named by the worker per class, method and zone (`EverydayApi.set_network@sha`), and
finer scopes inside the market's composition (`comp.*`) and pass (`mkt.*`). The allocator keeps
each tag's live bytes and, every 2 MB of new heap peak, a snapshot of all of them, so the peak is
broken down too. Reports go over a BroadcastChannel to the page. To measure a later stage: apply
the patch in an agent tree, build `wasm:demand`, put `measure.mjs` in that tree's `checks/` (it
imports `lib.mjs`), run it against the tree's own Vite with its deps cache outside the main tree's
`node_modules/.vite` (`WINDOW=1` stops after the first market pass). `T-1169/measure.mjs` loads a save
on its own Vite and collects them to the weekday final; `T-1169/analyze.py` sums them. Runs on my
Vite (6163), headless Chrome, the default pool (three solving workers and the aux; the all-China
save drops to two by T-1205's cap). Heap figures are exact; the instrumented build's WASM sizes are
not representative (2,364 MB on all-China against T-1205's 2,077 in production), so everything
below is heap. JSON per run in `../artifacts/trainworld/t1169/`.

Saves: `t1156-all-china.save` (maps root; 123 lines, 2,363 stations, 169 Chinese zones reached),
`artifacts/trainworld/t737-japan-trains.save` (709 lines, 10,320 stations, 45 zones), the real
New York save.

## Stage 2 (T-1558): the exact fixes, built and measured (2026-10-27)

A, B, D and G are in `sim/src/demand/kernel.rs` and `intercity.rs`; C is T-1559. Every figure the
page shows is the same to the bit before and after, on the real New York save, all-China and all of
Japan.

**What changed.** A: `StationGrid` keeps the occupied buckets only (a sorted list of bucket ids,
bisected), so a network from Lisbon to Shanghai costs its stations, not its area; the same order of
stations in each bucket. B: the pair rules are a table of the open places by the open places (`out`;
`inn` is gone); a pair with a closed place at one end is worked out when asked, as the closed-closed
ones were (`Market::pr`). `zone_place_eachway` asks each of its two rules once. D: the commute
station pairs are counted by destination first (`rail_term_counts`, no logs) and each entry is placed
in its destination's range as it comes (`SparseOdBuilder`): 8 bytes an entry instead of a 12-byte
list grown by doubling, then sorted whole (a 6-byte scratch each) and copied into the sparse form.
`SparseOdBuilder::finish` sorts each destination's few entries by origin, stable, and adds a pair's
riders in the order they came: the dense table's sums. `SparseOd::of` (the everyday outings) now sums
over its own list's front. G: `SiteKeys`, the sites in key order (4 bytes a square, bisected with the
key worked out from the site) instead of a `HashMap` of 32-byte entries. Test:
`sparse_od_builder_matches_of`.

**Exactness.** `measure-sig.mjs` (here, with the diag build) loads a save to the weekday final and
writes t328's signature of every figure the page holds (view, lines, stations, purposes, cities,
the market and its corridors' tables, 231,997 / 323,576 / 1,360,597 float64s) with the bubbles' modes.

(`cmp-t1558.py`): hashes cbecbe89 (New York), f1a5d88 (China), 80348bae (Japan); rail 12,483,326.38,
41,711,745.29, 24,207,996.07 a day. One all-China run differed in its cities, not its market:

## Stage 2b (T-1559): the market packs held in less room (2026-10-27)

`MarketPacks.add` still parses each pack's JSON once and keeps it for recomposing; what it keeps is
now `ZoneIn`, the parsed `ZoneRaw` with its long arrays in 2 or 4 bytes an entry
(`sim/src/demand/intercity.rs`). Recomposing from the composition was ruled out: the composition's
site, zone and commute numbering depends on which places are open, so a recomposition needs the
packs' own ids and f64 weights. The WASM interface and `app/` are unchanged.

- Sites: x and y as the float32 the composition reads (`as f32` was the only use), people and jobs
  in `Col`, the zone as `Ix`. A `Col` is 4 bytes an entry when every value in the pack's column is
  exactly a float32 (all of `cell_pop`) or exactly whole thousandths (people and jobs: `17.422`
  gives `17422 / 1000.` to the bit, the same f64 the parser gave); otherwise the column stays f64. An
  `Ix` is u16 when every index fits. Both are checked per column when the pack is added, so a pack
  that does not fit the cheap forms costs more room, never a different figure. Test:
  `compact_columns_give_back_the_numbers_they_were_given`.
- Commute and everyday arrays: their three indices each in u16; the f32 arrays and the others shrunk
  to their length (parsing leaves each up to 2x its length).
- Per entry: a site 40 to 20 bytes, `cell_pop` 8 to 4, a commute 16 to 10 plus no slack.

**Exactness.** Same method as stage 2, on master 06c7692a with and without the change (production
build, no diag): every one of the 231,998 (New York), 323,577 (all-China) and 1,360,598 (Japan)
signed figures equal as float64, and the bubbles' modes: hashes 5e73bc11, 4874ea4b, 2e6f2e74.
`cargo test --release --features demand`: 426 passed; smoke 20/20; `npm run check` clean.

## Stage 3 (T-1545): the market's pass by a window of destination zones (2026-10-27)

**For Anita.** All of Japan now peaks at 1.33 GB a solving worker (WASM 1.35 GB), down from
2.69 GB (WASM 2.74 GB): under T-1169's 2 GB line for Japan, with about 0.65 GB to spare. A cold load of the Japan save to the
weekday final took 1,107 s against 1,627 s before (-32%), on 3 solving workers. All-China and New
York change little in memory (their market pass already used the cities' shared table): all-China's
peak 1,359 to 1,302 MB, New York 265 to 256 MB. All-China's load measured about 10% slower
(325 to 358 s back to back), which the pass itself does not explain; T-1594 looks into it. Every figure moves only by the order numbers are added
in: rail trips a day on the real save 12,483,326.4 to 12,483,325.9, Japan's 24,214,316.33 to
24,214,316.29, the worst line or station anywhere about 5 in a million. No player could tell.

**What changed** (`sim/src/demand/intercity.rs` `pass_window`, `kernel.rs` `DestSearch::run_col`;
the WASM interface and `app/` are unchanged). The pass no longer builds four station x station
tables (route costs, the same with the planned first wait, fare km, trips) nor keeps routes
between two search sweeps. It goes through the destination zones in the order of their centres'
x; a zone's pairs (longer trips ending there, commutes working there, everyday outings going there)
read the route costs towards that zone's own stations and fill those stations' columns of trips,
and the trips home fill the home stations'. Each station holds:

- its route costs, the same with the planned first wait, and fare km (a "read slot"), from the
  first zone that reads them to the last;
- its columns of trips (longer trips, their lean, commutes, each everyday purpose; a "trip slot")
  from the first zone that touches it to the last, when they are assigned along its strategy;
- its strategy, from its search (at its first read) to its assignment, within 128 MB
  (`WINDOW_STRATEGY_BYTES`), keeping those assigned soonest; the rest are searched again.

The commutes' and outings' sparse trip lists (`SparseOd`, its builder and term counting) are gone:
their trips are columns in the trip slots. On a small cluster the same window reads its columns
from the shared table (`with_skims`), so there is one code path; the searched one gives the same
figures to the bit (`a_streamed_pass_is_the_pass_to_the_bit`, at caps of every strategy, none and
three). New test `the_window_keeps_every_trip`; benches `t1545_dump` (one period's figures to JSON)
and `t1545_window` (window sizes by zone order).

**The window, measured** (all of Japan, 9,253 stations, 8,475 wanted; `t1545_window`, most at once
/ mean):

| zone order | route costs read | trips held |
|---|---|---|
| x (west to east, built) | 197 / 74 | 1,954 / 1,045 |
| y | 214 / 85 | 3,392 / 1,339 |
| Hilbert | 253 / 151 | 3,826 / 2,657 |
| zone id | 562 / 304 | 6,508 / 3,557 |

The reads alone are what stage 1 measured (562 in zone order). The trips held are far more because
of the commutes' trips home: a home station's column is filled by every work zone its commuters go
to, and Tokyo's commuters come from 100 km around, so a sweep line through Tokyo holds most of its
stations. Hence the split into read slots and trip slots and the capped strategies. Japan's window

## T-1594: where all-China's slower load went, and the memory grown ahead (2026-10-27)

**For Anita.** All-China's cold load to the weekday final now takes about 97 s, against 253-293 s
before (T-1545's 325 to 358 s were on a busier machine). The time was the WASM memory growing in
small steps: each grow costs about as much as the memory a worker already holds (1.3 GB), and the
allocator grew it 64 KB at a time. Now it grows 32 MB ahead whenever it has to grow. Every figure is
identical to the bit; Japan 954 s (T-1545: 1,107 s), same peak; New York 62 s either way.

**Where the time went.** Alternating builds on one fresh Vite per run, back to back, quiet machine
(`T-1169/t1594-runab.mjs`, which runs `t1594-measure.mjs` copied into `checks/`; it adds the solve
log's per-period, per-round times and the pack loads to measure-sig's output; JSON in
`../artifacts/trainworld/t1594/`), 3 solving workers and the aux, import to final:

| build | runs (s) | packs opened by | round 0's first period (each worker) |
|---|---|---|---|
| before T-1545 (51f83da4's sim) | 292.6 (first run, cold Vite cache), 253.1 | +176, +157 | 41, 31 s |
| T-1545 | 292.7, 287.1, 289.3 | +165, +160, +159 | 61, 59, 61 s |
| T-1545 + memory grown ahead | 96.5 | +35 | 5 s |

Timing each population's call in the worker (a scratch log, reverted): every worker's first market
solve took 26.4-26.9 s before T-1545 and 56.7-57.0 s after; every later market solve and crowding
round 1.0-1.1 s in both. Natively the same market (`t1545cn`, plain with `checks/t761-plain-net.mjs`)
solves in 0.40-0.44 s in every period, on the shared table (2,195 destinations x 28.9 KB = 60 MB of
strategies, under 256 MB). So the first call's extra 25-55 s is WASM only and happens once: the
heap growing. T-1545's pass allocates its slots as 9 KB columns (about 400 trip slots of up to 8
columns on all-China), the old pass five 22 MB tables; with the allocator growing the memory by
what each request lacks, the new pass grew it hundreds of times more. T-780 saw the same on Japan's
first pass (390 s against 51) and reserved the strategies' 768 MB in one live block, which filled
fast but left the block mostly empty (3.5 GB workers). Opening the packs had the same cost: 150-170 s
down to 30 s.

**The fix** (`sim/src/demand/wasm.rs`, `AHEAD`; docs/architecture.md). The global allocator checks
the memory's size around each allocation; when it grew, it allocates 32 MB more and frees it at
once, so the allocator holds that much at the top and the next allocations are carved from it. The
block is freed, so it is not held: the heap figures do not count it and a worker's memory ends at
most 32 MB above what it would have reached. Measured WASM sizes: all-China 1,320 / 1,320 / 1,346 MB
against 1,321 each, Japan 1,372 / 1,362 / 1,171 against 1,348 / 1,340 / 1,150, New York 313-366
against 337-350 (aux 71 against 54). Heap peaks unchanged (all-China 1,302, Japan 1,334, New York 256).

**Figures.** All-China rail 41,712,256.0596 and signature e8484f72 with and without the change (the
same as T-1545's runs); Japan 24,214,316.2912, d6bae768, equal to T-1545's; New York 12,483,325.944,
2e848a7c both builds, twice each. One of the four T-1545-build runs of all-China gave ed424acb (its
lines, stations and purposes sections differ, rail equal), and the two pre-T-1545 runs 872296fc and
4874ea4b, so a load's signature can vary between runs of one build (a separate question, not
looked into); the grown-ahead run equals the T-1545 runs that agree with each other.

**Why a grow is that dear** is not pinned down (Chrome on Windows; possibly a copy of the memory on
each grow). 32 MB a step is a guess between the grow count and the memory left unused; 64 MB would
halve the grows again for up to 32 MB more a worker.
