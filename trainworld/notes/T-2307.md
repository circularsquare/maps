# T-2307: the walking rule's cost after T-2265

T-2265 made Anita's walking rule count real minutes (waits and rides), so `Skims::walk_max` (the
most a station takes on to any destination) passes almost no commute lane under 10 km, and the
everyday pass's own bound passes almost no pair: every trip within walking's reach looks at its
station pairs. On New York's calib (`calib --net data/work/realnet/nyc_real.json --attractions
app/src/game/attractions.json`, one core) that was about 10% on the solve.

## What changed

- **Commute lanes** (`kernel::mode_choice_part`): per origin subzone a row per egress station of
  what each access entry takes before alighting there (`till`, filled when a lane first reaches the
  station), so a lane tests each egress entry with one pass over its access entries and reads no
  table where all pass. Only refused pairs read `er`. Exact: the same refused set, the same sums in
  the same order.
- **Everyday pass** (`EverydayApi` solve): each pair's station pairs are looked up once into a bit
  each, used by its sum, the ferry fans and the assignment (were three lookups).
- **The sliver** (`WALK_SLIVER`, 1%): a trip whose refused pairs carry at most 1% of its
  station-pair weight is left whole, in the lanes, the everyday pass, `walk_kept` and the trip
  explorer alike. The lie: such trips keep rail worth under about 0.1 perceived minutes that the
  rule would have taken. On the calib, 5.8M of the 13.3M commute lanes in reach were slivers; their
  loading and taking off was the biggest part of the cost.
- `acc_band`: each access entry's distance band once, for the taking off.

Tried and dropped: a bound by the row's weight into the egress entries with any refused pair (it
caught almost nothing); reading only access and egress entries over 1% of their end's heaviest
(+2.2% commute rail, visible).

## Figures

CPU seconds of the whole calib, the builds run side by side (`par.ps1` in the session scratchpad),
the machine shared with a country build, three rounds:

| | pre-T-2265 (bb48b358) | T-2265 (8c965a29) | now |
|---|---|---|---|
| every layer | 47.2, 48.1, 47.0 | 51.9, 53.3, 52.0 | 51.2, 51.5, 50.7 |
| commutes only (`--no-everyday`, other rounds) | 28.4, 28.6; 30.7; 29.3 | 32.0, 32.5 | 30.4, 30.4; 32.5; 31.2 |

So +10.5% became +7.8% on the whole solve, and the commutes alone +13% became about +6%. Rail trips a
day: commutes 3,992,274 to 3,992,284, everyday 7,832,341 to 7,832,494 (all +165, 1 in 72,000).

Where the rest goes: the 13.3M commute lanes in reach each run the row test (about 140 ns each),
and the everyday pass still reads every pair's station pairs (8.4M pair-directions a run checked,
about 48 reads each). T-2324 took the next step (notes/T-2324.md).
