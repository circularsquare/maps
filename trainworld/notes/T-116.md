# T-116: Shared Northeast world

> T-125 (2026-10-10) supersedes the frame decisions below for new games: they are now in a Lambert
> conformal world frame; saves made before it, including the shared Northeast ones, stay in the
> fixed New York frame described here. notes/T-125.md.

Both New York and Philadelphia need to be reachable/buildable in the same save, not isolated
games, before intercity dynamics can be tested. Assess common coordinates, per-city workers,
water coverage including corridor, city selector/navigation, city attribution and old-save
compatibility. Keep native local demand separated by city and avoid double-counting borders.

## Assessment before implementation, 2026-10-10

This historical assessment preceded the implementation below. T-115
added the Philadelphia data pack separately. T-117 supplies actual trips between
the cities and intervening towns: two city commute packs alone produce no intercity market.

Keep the existing NYC equirectangular network frame and origin (-73.985, 40.758), so existing
track coordinates and saves remain valid. The Philadelphia pack (`phl`) has its own origin at
City Hall (-75.1636, 39.9526). Convert world-frame station coordinates through longitude and
latitude into each demand pack's frame. Do not change the global origin while loading another
pack, and do not merge the packs' independently balanced gravity tables. The city county sets
are disjoint; Mercer/Trenton belongs to neither demand pack.

The track network is already spatially unrestricted. Rendering already converts network-frame
positions to longitude/latitude and then NYC-relative Mercator coordinates, so Philadelphia's
approximately 150 km offset remains within centimetre-scale Float32 position precision. A
fixed NYC equirectangular plane has about 1.2% east-west distance scale difference at
Philadelphia; acceptable for the first Northeast prototype if documented. T-053 remains the
broader world-coordinate architecture task.

### Files and implementation stages

1. **Fixed frame, water and saves.** `app/src/workers/clock.worker.ts::loadWater` currently
   replaces the global origin, while `TrackApi.set_water` replaces the only water mask. Add
   transformed/composite water masks in `sim/src/track/cost.rs`, with explicit append/clear
   methods in `sim/src/track/wasm.rs`, then load both masks in the clock worker without moving
   the NYC network frame. Validate known land/water points in both cities and across the
   corridor. `pipeline/water.py` includes water from all counties intersecting each pack's
   bounding box, not just its demand counties, but `RunMask` treats everything outside its
   grid as land; check the final pack bounds rather than assuming the corridor is covered.
   `app/src/workers/saveFile.ts` has a single `SaveHeader.city`, and `clock.worker.ts` rejects
   differing cities in file and slot loads. Introduce optional region/city metadata with
   legacy NYC acceptance; never reinterpret existing coordinates or create disconnected
   Philadelphia-only games as the shared-world solution.
2. **City registry and navigation.** Add `app/src/game/cities.ts` with pack ID, display name,
   origin, centre and bounds. Add `phl` to `app/vite.config.ts` pack distribution. Wire
   navigation and focused-city attribution in `app/src/main.ts`, `app/src/ui/TopBar.tsx` and
   the City tab. `app/src/workers/clockClient.ts::toWorld` currently hardcodes NYC identifiers
   and the New York name; separate world identity from the currently focused city.
3. **Per-city solves and combined network demand.** `app/src/workers/demandClient.ts` is a
   singleton: a second `startDemand` returns the first client. Each client also directly writes
   `demandView`, `baseSplit` and the global query provider. Refactor it into city clients and a
   coordinator, transform station coordinates for each city, and preserve whole routes so
   an intercity line is not clipped at a city boundary. `app/src/game/demand.ts` must separate
   focused-city mode share from combined line loads, boardings, station totals and fare
   income. Aggregate segment loads before deriving fullest-train percentages; do not add
   percentage maxima. Use one total worker budget, rather than allocating three workers per
   city, and benchmark the real combined NYC/Philadelphia service graph.
4. **Queries and visual layers.** Carry city identity with cell/zone indices, or concatenate
   transformed cells and zones into the world frame with stable offsets and route each query
   to its city. Preserve line/station IDs from the one track network. Review
   `app/src/workers/demandProtocol.ts`, `app/src/game/demand.ts` and
   `app/src/map/commuterView.ts`: the latter caches its cells once and currently assumes
   `clockClient.packOrigin`. Reset per-city cached layers on focus changes or build a combined
   layer. Do not let one city's result overwrite another city's queries or income.
5. **Shared crowding feedback.** Independent city crowding rounds undercount congestion on
   lines carrying passengers from both cities. Combine all city segment flows, derive shared
   capacity penalties, and feed those penalties into the next round before claiming that
   shared-world crowding is complete. Version results coherently so a new service edit never
   combines cities solved on different service snapshots. Per-city dirty filtering can follow
   after correctness; using the whole global station graph initially is simpler but its
   station-to-station search cost needs measurement.

### Required checks

- Existing NYC file and browser-slot saves load without coordinate or money changes.
- One save can contain track, stations and a continuous line in both cities; save/reload
  preserves its route and schedules.
- Pack/world coordinate round-trips and known water crossings work in both cities and the
  intervening corridor.
- Both local demand solves contribute to shared line/station totals and income, while the
  top bar reports the focused city's share.
- Demand cell selections and station catchments use the correct city indices after changing
  focus; stale versions do not mix.
- A line loaded by both cities receives crowding penalties from their combined flow.
- Combined real-network solve time and memory remain within the existing CPU/worker budget.

## Water and save implementation, 2026-10-10

- `TransformedMask` maps world points back into the pack raster; `UnionMask` combines water
  coverage rather than allowing an out-of-grid land answer to erase another city's river.
  `TrackApi.append_water` takes grid fields plus world offsets and positive x/y scales.
  `clear_water` starts a region load; `set_water` retains its old replacing behavior.
- The clock worker keeps (-73.985, 40.758) permanently. Philadelphia's raster uses the offset
  from its origin and x scale `cos(NYC latitude) / cos(Philadelphia latitude)`. Latitude scale
  is one. Both water fetches settle before the empty-game save is recorded, including failures.
- Both masks cover the Trenton corridor: NYC grid runs from x -103925 to 181325 and y -142050
  to 149125; Philadelphia grid runs from x -93200 to 67975 and y -75650 to 74950 in its own
  frame. NYC demand does not include Mercer County, but its water raster does cover it.
- New save headers retain `city: nyc`, with optional `region: northeast` and `cities: [nyc,phl]`.
  Legacy NYC headers remain compatible; isolated Philadelphia-coordinate or other-region saves
  are rejected. Header format remains one, network bytes and coordinate/money conventions stay
  unchanged. File imports and slot loads use the same compatibility check.
- Native tests cover raster transformation/cell boundaries, union preservation, replacement,
  clearing, and byte-identical legacy network roundtrip. The explicit generated-pack test
  `northeast_pack_water_crossings` passes for Hudson/Times Square, Philadelphia Delaware/City Hall,
  and Trenton Delaware/station, and refuses a ground-level river crossing.
- `node trainworld/notes/t116-save-check.mjs` passes legacy/shared compressed-file roundtrips
  with the real NYC track-save fixture: exact network bytes, cash, clock, fares and ledger.
  Results in `../../artifacts/trainworld/notes-outputs/T-116-save-check.json`. Root owns final WASM regeneration/browser integration.


## Regional demand coordinator implementation, 2026-10-10

The existing default pool cap (at most three workers) is shared by the entire Northeast.
Each worker owns both local city APIs and the separate IntercityApi, solving its assigned
periods. Stations retain the fixed NYC world coordinates in the clock snapshot and route
node layout; each local API receives a world-to-city coordinate copy. Whole line routes
are preserved, including lines connecting cities.

At each period refinement, the worker freezes the sum of all three populations' prior
segment flows, then passes that identical vector to every population's
`crowd_with_external`. Shared capacities and period peak factors are identical. Loads,
boardings and alightings remain separate in the APIs and are summed for network drawing,
line/station totals and fare income. Crush ratios use the same denominator and therefore
sum correctly. Regional boardings are also retained separately per line.

The client stores results by version AND round. A faster worker's later rounds cannot
replace one period of a slower worker's first estimate. A publication requires all five
periods at the same round; previous-round buffers are released after publication.
The network demandView contains all populations. cityDemandViews contains each city's
local commute split and trip denominators, with the shared network line/station maps;
the top bar's modeSplit follows activeCity. intercityDemand contains the distinct regional
trip totals and market-pair attribution.

Queries are explicitly scoped to the focused city. Static cell/zone XY returns in world
coordinates; IDs remain local to that city's pack. Focus changes invalidate client caches,
terminate/reset the commuter aggregation worker and layers, and clear city-indexed commuter
selections. Station query caches reset too. A focus epoch rejects late answers even after
switching away and back. Query validity checks the solve version and exact uniform worker
round, so partially advanced periods cannot produce mixed-round cell summaries.

Checks: `node trainworld/notes/t116-coordinator-check.mjs` passes controlled unequal-worker
completion, aggregate/regional line boardings, focused city share, stale network results
and focus-away-and-back cancellation. Evidence: ../../artifacts/trainworld/notes-outputs/T-116-coordinator-check.json.
Native `shared_crowding_uses_complete_loads_without_overwriting_own_assignment` passes:
own-only feedback exactly equals legacy crowd(), external overload changes this population's
rail choice, and malformed total vectors leave the existing period intact.
Production WASM, combined-real-network timings and browser integration are verified below.

## Browser acceptance and performance, 2026-10-10

Both WASM builds, TypeScript and Vite production build pass. Native suite: 81 passed, five
optional fixture/profile tests ignored; the generated-pack water-crossing test also passes.
`t116-browser.mjs` runs in an isolated browser context: both empty city commute denominators
are positive, rail is zero before service, navigation/top-bar and city query indices change
correctly. Philadelphia returns 110,578 cells in the shared world frame; NYC returns 202,337.
A funded five-station corridor with local feeders at both ends carries local riders from both
cities and regional riders on all three lines. Stopping the express removes regional trips;
restoring service reproduces them within one trip/day. Loads remain finite, allocated train
counts converge and the regional save exports/reloads all five stations and three lines.
Evidence: ../../artifacts/trainworld/notes-outputs/T-116-browser.json and ../../artifacts/trainworld/T-116-northeast.png.

`t116-real-browser.mjs` imports the legacy real NYC track file (1,756 platforms, 109 lines),
then adds Philadelphia service in the same world. Both cities contribute rail riders; focused
Philadelphia station/cell queries and commuter bubbles work without browser/WebGL errors.
Three workers publish combined free flow in 4.07 s and round three in 15.91 s, using
114/120/120 MB WASM memory. These timings cover a full NYC graph plus sparse Philadelphia
service, not two complete metro graphs. Evidence: ../../artifacts/trainworld/notes-outputs/T-116-real-browser.json and
../../artifacts/trainworld/T-116-philadelphia.png. Legacy/shared compressed-save checks separately preserve exact
network bytes, cash, fares, clock and ledger.
