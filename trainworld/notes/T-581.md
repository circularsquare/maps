# T-581: cheaper change nodes

T-575's change nodes (a station reached off a line its strategy boards gets a node with its own
pooled strategy over the station's other lines) made New York's commute solve 22% slower. This
makes the search and the assignment cheaper with every result the same to the bit.

## What changed (`sim/src/demand/kernel.rs`, `everyday.rs`)

- **The search's queue** (`IdHeap`): a 4-ary heap of (cost bits, id) packed in a u64, each id in
  it at most once with its place kept, so a lower cost moves the entry up instead of pushing a
  copy. It pops ids in exactly the order the `BinaryHeap` of copies did (cost, then id; every
  push for an id lowers its cost strictly, and nothing settled is pushed again), so the settle
  order and every tie are unchanged. Pops per destination: 6,710 to 4,808.
- **A change node's departures** are a bit per boarding edge of its station (`ChangeNodes::bits`,
  a word for a station of up to 64 edges) instead of a list of route nodes sorted at the end of
  each search (that sort was about a tenth of a search). `ChangeNodes::tph` keeps its meaning for
  the trip explorer.
- **Whether a station has another line** comes from a table made once per graph (`Search::lone`)
  instead of a scan per change node; the candidates are sorted only when there are two or more.
- **The assignment's splits** (`kernel::departures`, used by `DestWalk` and everyday's `LaneWalk`)
  list a station's or change node's accepted departures once, in edge order, and walk only those;
  before, every edge of the station was visited three times, with a search through the change
  node's list for each. An edge not accepted has share 0, and adding 0 to a sum or a flow changes
  no bit, so the sums are those over every edge.

Fewer change nodes was not worth it: 721 of the 769 change nodes a destination search makes
(New York, peak period) end up as some node's next node, so knowing in advance which can never
help would save at most 6% of them.

## Timings (one core, this machine, 2026-10-16/17)

`realnet --net nyc_real_track.json --rounds 3` (commutes, stage sum), separate target dirs:

| build | runs |
|---|---|
| HEAD^ (before T-575 and T-558) | 23.0 s |
| HEAD (T-575, T-558) | 29.4, 29.5 s |
| T-581 | 25.8, 25.8 s |

T-558's walking was measured at +4%, so HEAD^ with it is about 23.9 s: T-581 takes back 3.7 of the
5.5 s, about two thirds. Search and assignment alone (a scratch bench: the same network's ten
graphs, free flow and after three rounds in each period, fares on, all 873 destinations, a dense
OD; per pass):

| | search | assignment |
|---|---|---|
| HEAD^ | 2.68 s | 0.58 s |
| HEAD | 5.31 s | 0.86 s |
| T-581 | 3.18-3.37 s | 0.59-0.64 s |

About three quarters of the search's extra cost and most of the assignment's are gone. What is
left is the change nodes' own work: each pools its lines (a division or two per line offered),
goes through the queue and relaxes its arrivals, and the assignment splits riders again at each
change node that carries any.

Table size: a change node is now 16 bytes (station, offset, one word of bits) against 8 plus 4 per
accepted departure (3.2 on average) before: 12.6 MB against 16.4 MB for a peak period's change
nodes searched towards every station; the whole table 32 MB.

## Results identical

- The bench writes every skims cost (`r`), every destination's accepted-departure bits, each
  node's next node (a change node by its station and accepted departures, since its id could have
  changed), the dense OD's loads, boardings, alightings, entries, exits, passenger minutes and
  transfers, and the trip explorer's answers for four trips in each period: HEAD and T-581 are
  equal byte for byte (264 MB).
- `realnet`'s result files (every period's round summaries, entries, exits, boardings,
  alightings, segment loads, loads of crush) are identical apart from the milliseconds: 5,234,707
  rail trips a day in both.
- `cargo test --features demand`: 195 passed.
- Smoke passed after `npm run wasm` and `npm run check`; the real save's demand final is
  10,541,788 rail trips a day, as before.
