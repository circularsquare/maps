# T-1205: give back a demand worker's memory, and size the pool by memory

WASM memory never shrinks, so a demand worker keeps its high-water mark. Built (2026-10-26):

- **Heap figures from Rust** (`sim/src/demand/wasm.rs`): the allocator wrapper T-1174 added now also
  counts live bytes and a resettable peak: `wasm_heap_bytes()`, `wasm_heap_peak(reset)`. The worker
  resets the peak as each solve starts and sends `heap: [live, peak]` with `ready`, `opened`,
  `closed` and `done`. Figures unchanged (a counter per allocation).
- **Restart when idle and well above need** (`demandClient.ts` "T-1205: memory"): `needOf` estimates
  what a worker started afresh would reach; `memCheck` restarts, together, every worker at least
  `MEM_FREE` (384 MB) and `MEM_OVER` (15%) above its need, once `quietIn` finds nothing in flight
  and the network still for `MEM_QUIET_MS` (20 s). It goes through T-1174's `workerDied`/`reopen`
  path with `planned`, which counts toward neither the panic limit nor the "second panic" stop.
  `respawn` became a map, so several workers can be replaced at once and the network solved once.
  A solve after any worker change (`freshParts`) sets `refreshing`; until its final, `target` asks
  questions of that final (with `wait`), since the new worker holds no earlier result.
  `restarted[i]` keeps a worker from restarting again until its memory or need has moved by MEM_FREE.
  A replacement skips `freshParts`' second composition (`solvedOnce` in the worker): its one
  composition is already from every open pack.
- **Pool cap** (`capPool`, before each solve): solving workers drop while `MEM_OTHER` (0.6 GB) +
  n x (need + `MEM_WORKER` 0.7 GB) + the aux worker's need + 0.7 GB passes `memBudget()` (8 GB with
  `deviceMemory` 8, which Chrome caps at; x 0.75 GB below; 6 GB unreported). On a cross-origin
  isolated page `measureUserAgentSpecificMemory` (after each final) replaces both constants with the
  measured page less the workers' WASM; the game is not isolated today, so that path is untested.
  `shrink` terminates the last solving workers, moves the aux worker down, re-indexes the per-worker
  state, and sends each worker `periods` (taken up between solves; `held`/`heldQ` cleared). Never
  grows back in a page (T-1366). The demand cache's key keeps the starting pool size (`poolN`).
- `?perfOff=restart,cap`; `?memFree=MB` for checks; dev `twDemand.client.debugRestart(...i)`,
  `twDemand.client.memory()`.

## Measurements (own Vite 6125, headless Chrome, private bytes of the check's Chrome)

How a worker's WASM after a solve compares with its heap (MB; heap at rest after the solve, the
solve's heap peak):

| | WASM | heap at rest | solve peak | WASM / rest | WASM / peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| New York, after load (3 workers) | 280 | 194 | 275 | 1.44 | 1.02 |
| New York, restarted worker | 341-354 | 194 | 275 | 1.76 | 1.25 |
| all-China, after load (2 workers) | 2,077 | 1,473-1,502 | 1,949-1,978 | 1.38-1.41 | 1.05-1.07 |
| all-China, restarted worker | 2,077 | 1,473-1,502 | 1,918-1,945 | 1.38-1.41 | 1.07 |

So `need = max(rest x 1.38, peak x 1.07) + 64 MB` (within 4% of both games' fresh workers). The
peak is forgotten when anything closes (it held what closed). A first try with a second
composition in the replacement left New York's at 374 MB; `solvedOnce` took that to 341-354 (the
rest is the allocator's order: a worker that reopens everything at once fragments more than one
that opened piece by piece, which matters on small workers only).

Chrome on the all-China save: 10,118 MB with 3 solving workers (WASM 2,077 x 3 + aux 296), 7,323-7,553
with 2 (the cap now drops the third at the load's own solve, before any solving); same figures
(39,290,203 with 3 and then 2 on one page). From those two, each worker costs about 0.7 GB outside
its WASM (its JS heap: the packs' headers, rows) and the rest of Chrome about 0.6 GB: MEM_WORKER and
MEM_OTHER.

After T-1204 a selected zone's market adds little (Tokyo selected on all-China: one worker 2,077 to
2,317 MB, from more periods after the shrink), and closing it left nothing to give back: the load's
own WASM is already within 7% of its solve peak. What does leave a high-water mark is zones opened
and closed again: lines built in Tokyo, Osaka and Seoul, then removed.

All-China runs of `t1205-memory.mjs` (2 solving workers after the cap; MB):

| stage | Chrome | workers' WASM | heap at rest | rail trips a day |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| loaded | 7,277-7,553 | 2,077, 2,077, aux 295 | 1,502, 1,473 | 39,292,448 |
| lines in Tokyo, Osaka, Seoul (cap: 2 to 1) | 5,316-5,455 | 2,558, aux 339 | 2,109 | 39,609,561 |
| lines and stations removed, solved | 5,518-5,866 | 2,798-3,038, aux 339-346 | 1,932-2,041 | 39,292,448 |

So one worker sat 0.7-1.0 GB above the load's 2,077 with the same items open, the high-water this
task is for, but no restart came: its heap at rest still held about 450-540 MB of what had closed
(deferred frees, old market parts), which put its need at 2,730-2,881 (T-1367). Getting there also
hit T-1368 (the demand network kept two zones' removed stations until another edit). The last run
added an edit after the close to force a solve; that solve had not published its final 20 minutes
later and the run was stopped at the hand-back. The all-China bar is T-1367's to finish.

A planned restart of both solving workers on all-China after load (`debugRestart`): the reopen of
541 items and the re-solve put each back at exactly 2,077 MB of WASM (heap 1,502/1,473, peak
1,945/1,918), so there was nothing to give back there and the need formula holds for fresh workers.
