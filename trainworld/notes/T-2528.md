## Built (diagrivers, 2026-11-05, tree `.tw-trees/diagrivers`)

Rules now: docs/ui-diagram.md "Water" (How wide; The sea in the drawing) and "The layout" (the dial at 0.5). Code:
`layout.ts` `DISTORT = 0.5`; `water.ts` (`scaleAt` in `makeWarp`, the wanted widths `course0`, the room `roomAt`, runs of
one width `byWidth`; the channels now after the rivers left out); `waterSea.ts` `farShore`; `draw.ts` strokes one path per
width (`DiagramWater.rivers` is a list, `bands` their widths); `checks/t2169-water.mjs` and `t2169-shots.mjs` pass the
list on.

1. **Dial at 0.5.** T-2556's one fail at 0.5 (all Japan, "river -15 in 2") was a real gap: channels joining the pieces of
   one sea were placed before the narrow rivers that cross other lines were left out, so a channel was skipped where a
   river later dropped had joined two pieces. Channels now come after. t2169-water passes everywhere at 0.5.
2. **River width** (`water.ts`): the river's real width (median per arc) times the drawing's scale round it. The scale is
   a smooth field (`scaleAt`): the 16 nearest stations' own local maps' scales, a geometric mean weighted by
   exp(-d^2 / 2 R^2), R 1.5 km or the fourth nearest's distance. Smoothed along the arc over 1.5 km each side; floored at
   half the river's widest drawn stretch times the real widths' ratio (so the Hudson narrows gently, the Harlem River
   stays narrow); clamped to [three lines, 18 pitches]. Then cut to the room: never over a pill, nor over a line running
   alongside it (within 30 degrees) that it does not cross; lines across it pass over it as at a crossing. Widening is
   tapered (a quarter pitch per pitch). Drawn as runs of one width in quarter-pitch steps.
3. **Long Island Sound** (`waterSea.ts` `farShore`): the Sound was land with lakes because the drawing puts Connecticut's
   stations much further from Long Island's than the Sound is wide at their scale (Connecticut's coast at 4.3 pitches a
   km, the Sound 23 km wide, drawn ~370 pitches apart): each Connecticut station's map carried a cell in the middle of
   the gap 30 km south, over the Sound, onto Long Island (diag.mjs: Rowayton, Stamford, Old Greenwich and South Norwalk
   all carried pixel 850,360 onto Long Island land). Now land a voter carries a cell to across the sea (2 km and 30% of
   the way), nearest a far-shore station within 8 km that is not one of the cell's voters, votes sea: that shore is drawn
   by its own stations. General: any sound or wide estuary whose shores the layout draws apart.

## Tried and left out

- **Room in the layout for wide rivers** (`seaTerms`): a hop whose track crosses 400 m of river water (the coarse grid's
  water, also 300 m either side) with that part at twice its real length at the local scale, and bank stations within
  5 km across such water held apart the same way. 14-16 hops and a few hundred pairs took it in New York; the river
  drawn under half its wanted width went 32% -> 33-38%, because the crossings still land inside Manhattan's lines. Left
  out: it changes every layout for no gain; the real fix is a side rule (stations kept on their bank of the river's
  drawn line), filed as a follow-up.
- **Routing a wide band down the middle of the room** (the proximity cost out to the band's half width): 375 -> 348
  pitches under half width, 20-60% more river time. Left out.
- **Carrying a cell back along the way between two voters on either side of it** (where the drawing has them further
  apart than their scale says): no change to any measure (the Sound's cells had all four voters on one shore). Left out.

## Looked outward

- **Vignelli 1972** (the only image reached: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/7/7f/Vignelli_1972.jpg, 289 px,
  from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massimo_Vignelli): water is flat beige areas at 45 and 90 degrees, not bands.
  Manhattan is a broad white strip (about 170 px of 900 in a 3x crop); the Hudson beside it is about a quarter of that
  (about 45 px), the East River and Harlem River much narrower (10-15 px strips), the harbour and Jamaica Bay large
  flat areas, and the Sound at the top right a big beige area. So Anita's "a quarter of Manhattan" matches Vignelli for
  the Hudson; the East River was drawn thinner.
- A research pass (subagent) found no published widths for the MTA map (Hertz) or the Kick Map: Wikipedia says the MTA
  map draws Lower Manhattan and Downtown Brooklyn larger than real; Jabbour (O'Reilly interview) dropped small waterways
  but kept the Gowanus where a station crosses it. Wu et al.'s survey (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7539984/)
  says rivers and bays should be simplified to the schematic style and placed so nearby stations stay identifiable, and
  that dense centres are enlarged; Wang and Chi (focus+context metro maps, TVCG 2011) argue fisheye-style smooth
  distortion breaks octilinearity. Pages: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway_map,
  https://oreilly.com/content/redesigning-the-new-york-city, https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/vintage-massimo-vignelli-subway-map.
- What this took: water width follows the land's drawn scale (a smooth field, as Anita asked), wide water next to land
  drawn large; open water left open where the layout leaves room, never land made up from a far shore.
