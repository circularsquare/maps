# T-962: where batch time goes after T-928 and T-929, and what would make batches faster

Research only (Anita, 2026-10-22: China has about 470 rows left after wave 2, then the world).
Nothing in `pipeline/`, `checks/`, `sim/` or `data/` was changed: China's wave 2 ran from the main
tree throughout. Measured from the batch logs of waves 1 and 2, a CPU poll of wave 2, and profiles of
one wave 2 row (xif, Xinfeng, 2.3M people, 31k cells) on a scratch tree through world_run's
`TW_RUN_ROOT` redirect, at most 2 cores. Scripts in the session scratchpad `speed\` folder
(throwaways): `steps.py` (step times per batch), `gatephases.py` (a one-session gate's phases from
its files' times), `brstats.py` and `brpos.py` (browser seconds a zone, by place in the page),
`regions.py` (download, read and gate per region), `poll3.ps1` (CPU by kind of process),
`poll3sum.py`, `prof.cmd` (one step under cProfile in the scratch tree), `seatime.py`, `seasame.py`,
`cutreal.py`, `cutparts.py`, `timeouts.py`.

## 2. CPU: is the machine busy?

`poll3.ps1`, 23:35 to 00:00, every 3 s, CPU-time deltas per process grouped by kind (`poll3sum.py`):

- **A build step uses 0.91 cores** (build_city's interpreter; its Rust children `pack_gravity` and
  `pack_walk` 0.84 cores each while they run), whatever its threads: the runner gives each build
  `jobs / workers` threads (2 in waves 1 and 2), and almost nothing in a row uses the second.
  So `--jobs 4 --workers 2` keeps about 2 cores busy, which is the 37% Anita saw (two such runs and
  her own programs, 5.2-6.0 of 16 cores, polled at 23:17).
- After the restart (7 builds at once), 23:35-23:45: machine 8.7 cores mean, of which builds 2.8
  (runs were starting their gates), Rust 0.5, Anita's Chrome and other programs about 2.
- **During the two gates (23:45-00:00): 6.6 cores busy, 9.4 idle.** A gate is one Python process
  (0.97 cores while it makes figures, 0.5 while it waits on the browser) and one browser session
  (headless Chrome about 0.7 cores, its node driver 0.1-0.2): about 1-1.5 cores a gate. A region's
  gate leaves the run's build workers idle, since the next region's builds wait for it.
- Memory: a build process 0.47-0.72 GB at its peak, `register` 1.7 GB, Rust bins up to 1.8 GB,
  headless Chrome 1.3 GB a session; 36.9 GB of 64 free with four builds. Memory does not bind
  below about 30 builds; disk did not show as a limit (a row at 7 builds took as long as at 4).

## 3. Hot spots

Profiles of xif's steps on the scratch tree (cProfile adds about a fifth to Python-heavy code):

**build_city (43.8 s in the scratch tree, 60 s in the run):**
- **`osm_units.cut_packs`: 27.8 s a row in the main tree, growing with the world.** It parses
  and `make_valid`s every `data/packs/*.boundary.geojson` (1,167 today) to cut the new outline along
  the packs it meets (11 for xif). Testing each file's bounding box first gives the identical
  geometry (`shapely.equals_exact`, `cutreal.py`) in 7.5 s, the rest JSON parsing of 1,167 files; a
  small index of pack boxes (rewritten when a boundary file is) brings it under a second. At 3,000
  packs it would be about 70 s a row. The scratch-tree profile missed it: `osm_units.PACKS` is meant
  to stay the real packs (`Redirect.KEEP`), but it is computed from `build_city.ROOT`, already
  redirected when osm_units loads, so a scratch build cuts along the scratch tree's packs only (a
  fault of the redirect worth fixing with this, since scratch builds are how such a change is tested).
- **`osm_water.sea`: 12.9 s, two calls a row** (the outline's coast trim and the water mask), each a
  box read of the 1.28 GB coastline shapefile through `/vsizip/`, which has no index: 6.1 s for an
  inland box, 8.9 s on the Pearl River delta, 12.5 s for a box at 0,0 (`seatime.py`). The same
  polygons from a FlatGeobuf with a spatial index (made once from the zip: 69 s read, 19 s write,
  1.28 GB) take 0.01-0.02 s, **same WKB in the same order** once sorted by the zip's feature index,
  on three boxes (`seasame.py`).
- `tier0.grid_to_cells`: 2.29M per-point h3 lookups in a Python generator, 6.7 s (pop's 2.14M points;
  nres_v and nres_s 76k each). h3-py 4.4 has no vectorised `latlng_to_cell`; `h3.api.basic_int`
  skips the `str_to_int` (0.9 s) and the generator (1.9 s).
- GHSL tiles unzipped per window: 3.4 s over four windows, of which `airports` re-reads the pop
  window (1.5 s) that `signals` already read.
- Smaller: cargo `pack_gravity` 3.3 s (the solve 3.2), the mask's erosion 2.2 s, osm_units.match
  2.0 s, imports about 1.8 s.

**routes (38.6 s):** `pack_walk` 25.6 s, Rust on one thread (`routes::walk_tables`: a walk field
per zone part, 2,206 of them, independent, so threads would give the same bytes); drive tables 9.9 s
(two Python workers); walk raster 1.9 s. The extract's ways are an .npz load, under 0.1 s.

**The gate's figures (hag, 3.9 s):** `read_pack.check_gravity` 2.3 s, the full gravity re-check
merge_cells already ran on the same merged pack; osm_signal 1.0 s (most of it importing tier0).

**Small steps:** stations, places, nocar, everyday_rates, coarse_zones and merge_cells are 8-9 s a
row together, most of it six process starts and imports; nocar is unread (T-878, T-926).

**The join** (wave 1): five province units solved in turn at 6 threads (the order matters, T-786);
each unit's merges (`merge_cells` over 27-59 packs, one process) and re-gate (`pack_gate --no-rust`
over the members, one process, about 4 s a pack) are serial loops over independent packs; the
market step builds 269 market packs one after another in Python (about 2 s each) before the rows.

Considered and not filed:
- **Units of the join solved at once.** 10.5 minutes a 100 rows are joint solves, one unit at a
  time on every core. Units solved side by side would hold each other's members at older factors,
  so the packs would differ from today's join (T-786's order; T-928 measured such drift at up to
  0.9% a zone). That is a change of result, not of speed only. How far `joint_gravity` scales with
  threads was not measured (no join ran in this session); worth a CPU poll during wave 2's join.
- **A browser check on a sample of zones.** It would weaken the gate (wac's and kai's faults were
  found there).
- An incremental join (measured and dropped in T-928).

## 5. Settings for wave 3

Wave 3 is about 193 rows (Sichuan, Chongqing, Guizhou, Yunnan, Guangxi, Hainan, Shaanxi, Shanxi).

One thread a build, as many builds as the core budget allows, and room for the gates. This needs no
code: `--workers` equal to `--jobs` already gives each build one thread (`threads = jobs // workers`).

- **Overnight (12-15 cores): two runs of `--jobs 6 --workers 6`**, 12 builds at about 0.9 cores
  each, so about 11 cores while both build and 13-14 with Anita's programs; a run's gate takes 1-1.5
  cores while its builds stand idle, so a gate does not take the machine over the budget. Three runs of `--jobs 4
  --workers 4` instead if the wave splits into three regions of similar size (the gate is serial
  per region, so three regions gate side by side).
- **Daytime (10 cores): two runs of `--jobs 4 --workers 4`**, about 7-8 cores with her programs.
- Routes' drive tables take 14 s on one thread against 10 s on two (xif); about 4 s a row, small
  next to the doubled builds.
- Run the small regions (Hainan, a single-province region) beside the big ones, as in waves 1-2:
  the serial gate of one big region is the longest part of a run.

With items 1 and 3 in before the wave (both small), a row's own steps fall from about 93 s to about
55 s and a 100-zone gate from about 25 to about 10 minutes of browser.

## 6. Notes on the method

- Step seconds are each row's `report.json` steps (or, for a live run, from one step log's `###`
  header to the next). A row's own steps exclude waiting for a worker.
- Gate phases come from the gate folder's file times: `<id>.row.json` (figures done),
  `<id>.browser.json` (the session's result for that zone), `<id>.json` (the verdict).
- `t928_breakdown.py`'s browser and "between" columns predate the one-session gate (T-928) and
  read wrong on it; `gatephases.py` replaces them for one-session gates.
- The profiled row ran in the scratch tree with wave 2's sim copy and Cargo target (nothing rebuilt);
  the main tree's xif files were checked unchanged after.
