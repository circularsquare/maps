# T-2040, T-1979, T-1978, T-1980: the gate's hang and three runner speedups (agent t2040, 2026-11-03)

Tree `maps/.tw-trees/t2040` (branch tw/t2040 from 7e730216). Ranked in notes/T-1974.md. Check outputs in

## T-2040: Jakarta's gate hang

**Cause.** Jakarta's page (37.5M, its pack and solve in one renderer) passes its eight checks; the
`removeLine` for the next zone then starts a new solve, and the renderer dies out of memory: a Chrome
crash dump with exception 0xE0000008 (Chrome's OOM code) about 25 s in, in each of three repros (jak, sur,
pro on Vite 6159, `cb1/cb-run1.log`, `cb-run2.log`). The machine had 39 GB free, so it is the renderer's own
limit. Playwright emits no `crash` event for it, and the `waitForFunction` waiting for the solve without
the line (its own 300 s timeout) stays pending: 420 s and counting in repro 1, the Node event loop alive
throughout (a 15 s heartbeat printed). The 300 s race added in T-1738 wrapped only the `removeLine`
evaluate, which returns at once, so it never fired. Meanwhile `pack_gate.browser_run`'s 300 s "40 of 60
rows in" line kept the gate's log growing, so the runner's quiet watch never noticed, and the step ran
to STEP_LIMIT_S (90 min). (t1738c's `cbtest` on jak and pro timed out on opening jak only because run 3
had taken jak out of the registry by then.)

**Fix.**
- `checks/city-browser.mjs`: a zone's result is written before its line is removed; the removal and the
  wait after it are each raced against a Node timer and the page's crash (`within`), so a dead page costs
  305 s and a fresh page (repro 3: jak PASS in 15 s, its removal given up at 305 s, sur and pro PASS on a
  new page); every zone's checks are raced against ZONE_LIMIT_S (1500) and the crash; a crashed page is
  retried once like a timed-out wait; a page's close is given 30 s. Each wait prints a `.. <id>: ...`
  line first.
- `checks/pack_gate.py`: a zone over `BIG_ZONE_PEOPLE` (20M) gets a session of its own, so it is its
  page's last zone and nothing is removed after it (the gate's browser step on jak, sur, pro and tan: 4
  of 4 PASS, 40 s, `t2040/gate1.log`). A session whose log has not grown for `BROWSER_STALL_S` (600 s) is
  killed (its process tree); its stuck zone is tried once alone and fails if it sticks alone too, and its
  later zones run in a fresh session; the stuck session's log is kept as `browser-stalled-<id>-<n>.log`.
- A metro's own session costs nothing extra: it runs beside the others, three at once.

Saves: Indonesia lost 3 h (two 90-minute stops) and two extra runs; now a stuck session costs at most 10
minutes and one zone's retry, and Jakarta ships in the region's gate.

## T-1978: gate in chunks while the region builds

`Run.region_held` starts a gate thread beside the builds: whenever `GATE_CHUNK` (10, `TW_GATE_CHUNK`; 0 is
the old one gate after the builds) built rows wait, it gates them (plan order) with their own snapshot,
register and Vite, then the rest once the builds end. Gate chunks go one at a time (`gate_turn`). The
run's `--workers` are now slots (`Slots`): a build takes one, a gate chunk two, and a chunk or a read
waiting goes before builds waiting (a builder gives its slot back and takes the next at once, so a
plain semaphore starved the gate until the region's last build, seen in the check). Logs: the first
chunk `region<k>.*` as before, later ones `region<k>.<n>.*`; the report's region times add `builds` and
`gate_after_builds`. A gate's run fault stops the run: no new build or chunk starts.

Saves: a chunk's overhead is about 35 s (snapshot 9 s, register 19 s after T-1976, Vite), so Mexico's
one 120-row region at 10 jobs (about 16 min of gate) leaves a tail of 2-3 min, its chunks taking 2 of 10
slots for about 23 min (builds about 5 min longer): **about 10 min a 100-row region**; about 2 min on
Cambodia-sized regions.

## T-1980: the next region read during this one's builds

`Run.run_regions`: each region in its own thread, holding its extract locks from its read to its clean.
Region k+1 starts (takes its locks, downloads, reads) once region k's builds begin, so never two reads
ahead (T-1080's 6 GB commit each); a read takes one slot (`threads=2`). Region k+1 builds once k's
builds end, while k's last gate chunk runs. Stop: no region starts; a waiting region returns.

Saves: nothing on one-extract countries (Mexico, Turkey, Vietnam); Indonesia's reads after Java (about 32
min: Sumatra 8.5, Sulawesi 7, Kalimantan 8.2, Nusa Tenggara 5, Papua 1.9, Maluku 1.1) hide behind the
builds before them, about 0.5 h; Australia about 20 min; India, Brazil and the US replan more.

## What only a real run shows

The chunked gate and the read ahead were tested with stand-in steps only (the Philippines run owned the
packs and run state). On the next country's run (Mexico is the best test: one 120-row region), read in
the batch report: `regions[].gate_chunks`, `seconds.builds`, `seconds.gate`, `seconds.gate_after_builds`
(should be a few minutes), every row shipped or parked once, `region1.2.gate.log` etc. present; that the
registry, the Vite on the run's port and the `gate-<batch>` lock behaved across chunks (no "port already
taken" stop between chunks); and core use (`cores.py`, Task Manager) about `--jobs` while chunks run. For
the read ahead, a multi-region country (India, Brazil, an Australia rerun): region k+1's download line
appears while region k builds, and no two `read` steps overlap.
