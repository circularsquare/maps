# T-3172: buses and trams on any road in the data (2026-11-11, agent roads)

## What the tiles carry

The basemap is OpenFreeMap (OpenMapTiles schema). Its `transportation` layer has lines with `class`, `subclass`,
`service`, `access`, `brunnel`, `layer`, `ramp`, `oneway`. Surveyed over New York, London, Tokyo, a rural English
tile, and busway towns (Cambridge, Brisbane, Curitiba, Bogota, Nagoya, Leeds).

Classes seen: motorway, trunk, primary, secondary, tertiary, minor (residential, unclassified, living streets),
service (subclass-less, plus `service` = alley, driveway, parking_aisle), track, busway, bus_guideway, path
(subclass pedestrian, footway, cycleway, bridleway, steps, path, corridor, platform), rail, transit (subway), ferry,
pier. `access=no` marks closed or private ways (all busways carry it, as it means "not for general traffic").

## Before: what a bus or tram could use

- In: trunk, primary, secondary, tertiary, minor, busway; motorway for buses only (trams keep to streets); a
  service way only as a short link (at most 60 m, no `service` value, no `access`, not on a bridge or in a tunnel,
  both ends on a street).
- Out: other service ways (alleys, driveways, car park aisles, access=no ones, long ones), tracks, bus_guideway,
  pedestrian streets, and all other paths.

## Where "can't go there" came from

1. The class filter above (`busRoad` in busRoads.ts): the main source. Pedestrian streets and service roads are
   whole districts in old towns, campuses, airports and estates; a click there was "Too far from a road" (100 m
   snap), or the route went the long way round.
2. Gaps the tiles simplify away: OpenMapTiles simplifies each road alone, dropping a junction vertex where one
   road runs through another. `joinRoads` puts junctions back (T-2873). A road end within 3 m of another road joins;
   a longer gap is a missing connection and the leg takes "No road path found". Not changed.
3. Join rules: bridges and tunnels join the ground only at their ends (T-3000); motorways join only motorways and
   their own ends. Not changed.
4. Search limits: the detour cap (3x the straight line and at least 1,000 m more), 100 tiles for a leg ("too long").
   Not changed.
5. Tram turns: a street tram search charges and refuses sharp turns (`turnPath`: 30 deg costs, 135 deg and past
   refused). Not changed, and buses have none.
6. One-way: not read at all, so a bus may drive any road either way and nothing is refused for it. Not changed.
   Ramps (`ramp`) are read as the class they have.

Bus-only roads: yes they are in the data, as class `busway` (highway=busway; access=no) and `bus_guideway`
(guided busways, Cambridge-style). `busway` was always allowed (never filtered on access). `bus_guideway` was not and
is now. A bus lane on an ordinary street is the street. `service=bus` is not a value the tiles carry; bus-only
service roads come through as plain service ways, now allowed. Trams may use them too (a street tram uses every road
a bus does except motorways).

## After

A bus or tram may use: all of the above "in", plus every service way (any `service`, any `access`, any bridge or
tunnel), track, bus_guideway, and path with subclass pedestrian. Motorways stay bus only.

Left out on purpose: footway, cycleway, steps, bridleway, platform, corridor and plain `path` (not drivable-looking;
steps and platforms cannot be driven, cycleways and footways would send buses through parks), raceway, pier, rail,
transit, ferry. Pedestrian streets are in (wide, paved, trams run in them).

`access=no` is not read for buses. The tile flag marks "no general traffic", which includes busways, and a car park
aisle with it is still a road. The hint layer (teal lines while the bus tool is held, green for trams) uses the same
list, so every road a bus may take is drawn. Speeds: the existing rule, unchanged.

## For T-3129 (trains following roads)

`decodeRoads` now passes `subclass` as a fifth argument to the keep function and `LINK` still exists in roadGraph
(unused by buses now), so a stricter keep for track can use the old link rule, drop service ways, or keep only
named classes. `BusRoads` takes the keep function as a third constructor argument.
