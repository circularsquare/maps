# T-1887: water in the track tag, dearer mountain track at ground level

## Prices per km, double track, US$M

Base 90 a km, times the level's multiplier (ground 0.5, +1 and -1 1.0, -2 1.1), times the terrain
factor at levels -1 and up.

Before (one factor at every level from -1 up: 1, 1.25, 1.6, 2.5):

| Class | Ground (0) | Viaduct (+1) | Cut-and-cover (-1) | Tunnel (-2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flat | 45 | 90 | 90 | 99 |
| Hills (low, 1.25) | 56.25 | 112.5 | 112.5 | 99 |
| Hills (high, 1.6) | 72 | 144 | 144 | 99 |
| Mountains (2.5) | 112.5 | 225 | 225 | 99 |

After (ground level has its own factor; only the mountain entry differs, 6 instead of 2.5):

| Class | Ground (0) | Viaduct (+1) | Cut-and-cover (-1) | Tunnel (-2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flat | 45 | 90 | 90 | 99 |
| Hills (low) | 56.25 | 112.5 | 112.5 | 99 |
| Hills (high) | 72 | 144 | 144 | 99 |
| Mountains | **270** | 225 | 225 | 99 |

Ordering after: flat and low hills ground < viaduct < tunnel; high hills ground < tunnel < viaduct;
mountains tunnel < viaduct < ground (ground 20% over the viaduct).

## Why this form

Anita's "+200%" read as a surcharge on the base: 90 + 200% = 270 a km, which is 6x the ground's
discounted 45. An additive surcharge at every level cannot put ground above a viaduct (ground's 0.5
against the viaduct's 1.0 always leaves ground 45 cheaper), so the rule is a second factor row for
ground level only, equal to the old one but in mountains. The tag and the blueprint breakdown stay
"name factor x" products. 5.5x (247.5, 10% over the viaduct) was the other candidate.
