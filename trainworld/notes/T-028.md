# T-028 money

Done 2026-10-09 by the gameplay agent (batch 4). Construction was already paid on Construct
(T-055). Now trains are bought, trains cost money to run, riders pay fares, and the Money tab
shows the day's figures and the fare controls.

## Prices (`sim/src/track/params.rs`)

| What | Price | Why |
|---|---|---|
| a 20 m car | US$2.5M | a New York R211 car is about 2.6 |
| running a car one km | US$6 | crew, power and upkeep; New York's subway spends about 9 all in, stations included |
| fare | US$1.50 a ride + US$0.10 a km to start, player-set | about New York's $2.90 for a typical 10-15 km ride |

## Trains

- The network owns **cars** (`TrackApi.fleet`). The running lines need, each, their busiest
  schedule's trains times the line's cars (`cars_needed`). One train kind, so any spare car
  serves any line.
- **Every edit pays for the cars it makes the running lines need** beyond those owned: a construct
  that starts a line, more trains an hour, longer round trips from added stops or from capacity
  delay another line's change caused, an undo that brings a line back. `TrackWorld::apply_paid`
  commits the edit, prices the cars on the edited network, and rolls back if construction plus
  trains comes to more than the cash. So **not enough money means the edit is refused** with the
  usual "Not enough money" line, and nothing changes, the same rule as Construct.
- Cars are never sold. Fewer trains an hour, a removed line or an undo leave the cars as spares,
  used first by the next line that needs them; going back up is then free. No refunds, as for
  track (SPEC 6.4). A planned line's inspector prices its trains after the spares
  (`line_train_cost`) and its Construct button shows track, stations and trains together.
- An edit that costs nothing is never refused for money, so a network in debt can still be
  edited (running costs can take the cash below zero; see below).

## Running cost and fares, hour by hour

- The game clock is the main thread's. It tells the clock worker `(t, rate, at)` whenever it is
  set, paused, resumed or sped up; the worker works out game time from that and **settles each
  whole game hour**: running cost (trains an hour in force x 2 directions x line length x cars x
  US$6) out, fare income in, both into the day's ledger entry. It settles once a wall second
  while the clock runs, before every edit, and before a save, so a save holds exactly the
  settled money and the hour in progress is settled after the reload.
- **Fare income is computed on the main thread** (`game/money.ts`), where demand's results are:
  for each running line and period, riders on each segment (`LineDemand.loads`) times the
  segment's km gives passenger-km; the line's boardings scaled to trips (city rail trips over all
  boardings, so a ride that changes lines pays the base once) times the base, spread over the
  periods by passenger-km. The result, US$ per game hour in each of the five periods, goes to the
  worker (`income` message) whenever riders, fares or lines change.
- **The fare does not change ridership yet.** The demand kernel prices rail with a fixed fare of
  6 perceived minutes. The snapshot demand gets now carries the player's curve (`fare` in
  `NetworkSnapshot`) for the demand agent to use; until then a higher fare only earns more. A
  follow-up for the demand side is in TASKS.md.

## The UI

- Top bar: cash, from `moneyState` (updates when an hour settles, without a new world state).
- Money tab: cash; yesterday and today: fares, running trains, new trains, construction, net;
  the fare curve (each ride, each km, and what a 10 km ride costs); a day at this schedule (fares
  from the riders, running cost); cars owned and cars the lines need; what is built.
- Line inspector: fares and running cost a day for a running line; a planned line's Construct
  price includes its trains.

## The $6B start (SPEC 6.4 asked for a recheck)

A 20 km line at -2 with 18 stations is US$3.78B of track and stations; at 20 trains an hour on a
~50 minute round trip it needs about 17 ten-car trains, US$0.43B. So $6B builds one real first
line with trains and leaves room for a short second line or extensions, which is what T-008 sized
it for. Kept.

## Pace (an open question for Anita)

At these prices the 5 km test line (four stations, 20/10/3 trains an hour) earns $1.41M a day in
fares and costs $0.17M to run. Batch 3's 12.4 km line had 757k riders a day: about $2.3M a day at
$3 a ride. A US$1.5B line therefore pays for itself in roughly two game years; at the top speed (a
game day in 90 s) that is about 18 hours of play. Money is generous at the start and slow after.
Options are in TODO.md: keep it, add a faster speed, or scale income.
