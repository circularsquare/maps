# T-3757: "saves of 6-14 GB" (savesize, 2026-11-19)

## What players quote

Not a file size and not browser storage. The game shows two memory figures, both "N GB":

- **Settings, Memory row**: "14 GB / 8 GB+" (`liveGB`), the reading of the running game.
- **Saves list card**: "14 GB" under a save, hover "Uses about 14 GB of memory" (`estimateGB`, from the station count).

Players' "14 GB used" may be either; both were too high on big games with several solving workers, both fixed.

Save files themselves are small: the biggest of 57,258 stored games on the server is 3.99 MB gzipped
(`/opt/shonei/trainworld/games`, read only). Slot storage keeps the slot and 4 backup tiers (`BACKUP_STEPS`), gzipped,
bounded. The feedback inbox (96 h) has no report quoting GB; the reports reached Anita elsewhere.

## Why players read far more than Anita

`liveGB` was 1.15 GB + 1.76 x the sum over every demand worker of (WASM memory + allocator live bytes).

1. The allocator's live bytes (`wasm_heap_bytes`) are inside the WASM memory, so each worker was counted about twice
   (T-3494 noted it; T-3329's note called it "JS heap").
2. The 1.76 slope was fitted on 1-solving-worker runs, where it stands for everything that grows with the network
   (clock worker, map, demand JS). Charged per worker, a player with 3-5 solving workers (12+ threads, or "Use more
   cores") got that growth 3-5 times over. Real cost of a further worker is about its WASM x 1.5.
3. Both grow with network size, so the gap is widest on players' 53-64 city games.

Measured (headless Chrome, private bytes of every process of the profile; `measure.mjs` in my scratch folder):

| run | stations | solving workers | WASM per worker + aux (MB) | real (MB) | old reading | new reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| player save, 64 cities | 7,070 | 1 | 2005 + 353 | 7,692 | 8.26 GB (+10%) | 7.5 GB (-2%) |
| same | 7,070 | 3 | 1699, 1731, 1699 + 353 | 12,044 | 17.3 GB (+47%) | 12.1 GB (0%) |
| player save, 53 cities, day 20,997 | 1,174 | 1 | 963 + 193 | 4,326 | 5.0 GB (+16%) | 4.2 GB (-0%) |
| same after the ledger fold | 1,174 | 1 | 963 + 193 | 3,243-3,302 | 5.0 GB (+55%) | 4.2 GB (+28%) |

At 5 workers the old reading would be about 1.15 + 1.76 x 5 x 3.4 GB = 31 GB on that 7,070-station save.

Refit (`fit.py`, T-3329's 22 runs plus these): `liveMemory.ts`, bytes = 1.17 GB + 2.69 x (largest solving worker's WASM
+ aux WASM) + 1.56 x (each further solving worker's WASM). Within about 20% on every run but all-China (+21%) and the
folded 53-city save (+28%); the old formula was +42% to +55% on those and +47% at 3 workers.

The saves card (`estimateGB`) refitted: 1.64 GB + 0.8 GB per 1,000 stations (was 1.8 + 0.65), each further solving
worker 0.1 + 0.3 GB per 1,000 stations (was 0.2 + 0.2), and with "Use more cores" on, further workers count only while
they fit in the budget (the pool cuts them there; before, `workersFor` returned the whole pool, so an 8,255-station
game read 14.6 GB at 5 workers). Card vs real: 7,070 stations 7.27 vs 7.51 GB (-3%), the 53-city save 2.58 vs 3.3 GB
(-22%: many zones for its stations).

## The pool's guess at the rest of the page

`capPool` and `growTo` counted 0.6 GB (`MEM_OTHER`) for everything outside the demand workers' WASM. On the 7,070-station
save that part is about 5 GB, so with "Use more cores" the pool kept 4 solving workers (13.1 GB real, reading 14.3 GB)
under an 8 GB budget. Now:

- `otherGuess()` uses the live model's outside part (`outsideWasmBytes`), at least 0.6 GB. New York: 1.9 GB, no
  change in workers; the default pool (17.5% share) binds first on big saves anyway.
- `capAhead` (the cut before a save's packs open) also runs with more cores, by the budget alone: 5 -> 3 there.
- `memCheck` runs `capPool` once the pool is quiet after a final, not only before the next solve, and then solves
  again (`solveAgain`, as a growth does: the workers left hold periods they never solved). Forced by hand after the
  final: 4 -> 1 solving workers, real 13.0 -> 7.5 GB, reading 6.64 GB. In the game as built, twice: 3 workers at the
  final (354 s), cut to 1 about 35 s later, a new final at 751-828 s, status ready, the memory re-solve flag cleared,
  same rail trips both runs. A station question (`stationRiders` on 20 line stops) answered nothing on this save
  before the cut as well as after (it does on New York), so that probe proves nothing here.
- Checks: t3072-oom-fallback (pool sizing without `demandWorkers`) PASS, t2684-years (month columns) PASS, smoke.
  Every standing check pins `?demandWorkers`, which turns off `capPool`, `capAhead` and `growTo`, so only my scratch
  runs exercise the more-cores path.

## What really grew: the day ledgers

`LEDGER_DAYS = Infinity` (T-2143): every region keeps an entry per game day for ever, sent to the main thread with
every money state and written into every save. Server's top saves by size were all these:

| save | game day | ledgers | header before | after fold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 53 cities | 20,998 | 57 | 98.9 MB (gz 3.85) | 5.3 MB (gz 0.64) |
| 64 cities | 10,094 | 94 | 70.1 MB (gz 3.21) | 4.6 MB (gz 0.61) |
| 64 cities | 1,560 | 404 | 47.2 MB (gz 3.53) | 12.0 MB (gz 1.19) |
| 64 cities, 7,070 st | 2,631 | 183 | 20.1 MB (gz 1.81) | 5.2 MB (gz 0.59) |

1.92M ledger entries -> 171k over the four. The top one was 6 KB under the server's 4 MB per-save cap
(`MaxTwSaveBytes`, shonei-server trainworld_games.go), so its cloud copy was about to stop. In memory: the 98.9 MB save
took 4.3 GB (peak 4.6), the same save with ledgers cut 3.3 GB (peak 3.6): about 1 GB of ledgers.

Fix (`workers/ledgerFold.ts`): once a day ledger's own entries span more than 90 weeks, its days older than 120 days
fold into one entry per calendar month (sums; the month's earliest day as its day; never the last entry). The History
chart's All time groups by month past 90 weeks, so its columns are unchanged: `foldtest.mjs` ran the chart's grouping
on 701 real ledgers before and after, columns and spans identical, worst relative difference 4e-13 (float sums). 30d,
the loan's 7 days and yesterday's figures read only the last 120 days. Folds on load (an old save shrinks on its next
save) and on each new day. No save level change: entries keep their shape.

Before / after on the fixed build: the 98.9 MB save loads at 3.30 GB settled, peak 3.62 (was 4.33, peak 4.61) and
exports at 0.72 MB (was 3.99 MB); its export loads at 3.24 GB.

Accuracy against a 20% bar: met on the 7,070-station save (reading -2%, 0%, +9% at 1, 3 and 4 workers; card -3%),
missed on the folded 53-city save (reading +28%, card -22%: many zones for few stations). No constants added to chase
two saves.

Visible side effect of the card refit: a game now reads over an 8 GB budget (red, and Open asks first) from about
7,950 stations, where it did from about 9,500.

## Left alone

- Real memory of huge networks is still large: 7.7 GB at 7,070 stations on 1 worker, mostly outside the demand WASM
  (clock worker, map, demand JS). Phones and 8 GB machines will struggle with those; T-3160's line.
- `game/memoryCheck.ts` `memoryNeed` (the load refusal's figures) still counts the whole pool under more cores, so its
  GB can now be above what the pool will run.
- `tools/ops/archive_bad_saves.py` has `MAX_RAW` 64 MiB, so the 70 and 99 MB saves read "unverified" there; after their
  next save they are far below it. No server change needed: the fold brings the biggest under 1.2 MB gzipped.
- T-3642 (shared saves near the 2 MB cap from per-day ledgers) is mostly done by this fold: the 1,560-day 404-ledger game
  goes 3.5 -> 1.2 MB gzipped.

Player saves used here were copied to my scratch folder only and deleted after.
