# T-434: what each crowding round moves

2026-10-15. Measurement and proposal, then the build (last section). A copy of T-401's scratch crate
(HEAD 5d44e40, release binary, one worker solving all five periods, commutes, everyday and market
layers as `demand.worker.ts` drives them) ran free flow plus 12 MSA rounds on the real New York save
(109 lines, 1,756 stations) and T-401's Tokyo grid (44 lines, 1,041 stations), 10 rounds on grid2
(60 lines, 2,001 stations). After each round it wrote every period's summed averaged loads, the
crowding factors and riders left behind that the next round would price (`kernel::round_costs`,
`kernel::left_behind`). The last round is the reference (its own round moves under 0.02% of rail
trips and no line over 0.6%). Scratch: `scratchpad/t434/` of the session (probe, `analyse.py`,
`periods.py`, `adapt.py`).

Figures are a weekday (five periods summed). Lines: those with 1,000+ boardings a day. Stations:
500+ boardings a day. Fullest train: busiest-hour load over crush, the highest segment of any
period. Left behind: riders a day not getting on the first train (demand's even-headway rule).

## Options

A solve is 20 passes (5 periods, free flow and 3 rounds), each about the same cost (T-401).

- **(a) Keep 3 rounds.** No change. New York is still 20 lines over 2% from settled.
- **(b) 2 rounds: -25% (5 of 20 passes).** On New York the player would see it: the fullest
  train reads 124% of crush instead of 96%, riders left behind 15,600 a day instead of 4,500, 11
  lines move over 2% (5 over 5%, largest 12%) and 18% of stations over 2%. On the grids it is
  milder (1-2 lines over 2%, fullest train unchanged) but left behind is still 44% higher on grid2.
  Rejected.
- **(c) Stop when a round moves results under a tolerance.** For the whole solve, any tolerance a
  player would not notice (no line over 2-5% in a round) needs 4-6 rounds on New York and 3-4 on
  the grids: as costly or more than now. **Per period** it pays: a period stops after the first
  round that moved its own summed segment loads by under 1% (rider weighted), at most 3 rounds.
  Measured: New York rounds by period 3, 3, 3, 2, 1, 17 passes (-15%); grid 3, 3, 3, 2, 1 (-15%);
  grid2 3, 2, 3, 2, 1, 16 passes (-20%). Against today's 3 rounds no line moves over 0.6%, no
  station over 2% (0.1% of them over 2% on New York), fullest train and left behind identical.
  At 2% it is -20% on all three with the largest line change 1.0%. The test is a sum over the
  segments the round already has (the summed loads `round_costs` receives); a stopped period
  answers its later rounds as T-119's skip does. Wall-clock saving depends on how the pool deals
  periods: with two workers split 3 and 2, a worker holding morning, evening and night does 9
  passes instead of 12 and the other (midday, evening peak) 7 or 8, so about a quarter off the
  slower worker; with periods dealt badly (both peaks on one worker) nothing.
- **(d) Other averaging.** New York, four schedules of round weights against MSA (1/2, 1/3, 1/4):
  constant 1/2 rings (fullest train 1.41 at round 3, left behind 53,000 at round 4); constant 1/3
  and a gentler 0.35, 0.25, 0.2 are worse than MSA at round 3 (21-46 lines over 2% from the
  reference); 1/2, 1/2, 1/3 is better at round 2 (30 lines against 41) but worse at round 3;
  0.5, 0.4, 0.3 is a little better at round 3 (18 lines against 20, largest 6.7% against 9.5%, left
  behind 3,603 against 4,451; reference 3,493). None makes 2 rounds as good as MSA's 3: the best
  round 2 (30 lines over 2%, fullest 1.19) is well short of MSA round 3 (20, 0.96). No saving.
- **(e) Not measured: start an edit's crowding from the solve before** (its factors instead of free
  flow). After a small edit the last equilibrium is much nearer than free flow (20% off in the
  peaks), so one or two rounds might do. But a network's figures would then depend on the edits
  that led to it (an undo would not restore the same numbers), which T-297 avoided on purpose.
  T-436.

## Recommendation

(c) per period: tolerance 1% of a period's segment loads, cap 3 rounds. 15-20% less work per solve
with no difference a player would see against today; peaks keep their three rounds. Keep MSA. If
more accuracy is ever wanted on crowded networks, the saving pays for a fourth peak round (cap 4,
tolerance 1%: 19 passes on New York, 6 lines nearer the reference by over 2%).

Approved by Anita 2026-10-15 and built (c, per period, 1%, cap 3).
