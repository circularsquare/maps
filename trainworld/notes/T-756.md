# T-756: each population solves on the stations within its reach (with T-757, T-760), 2026-10-19

Found by T-737 (notes/T-737.md): all of Japan is one cluster, and every population of a cluster was
set on the cluster's whole network, so its station x station tables (skims, `station_exp`, the
everyday OD) were the cluster's size: 10,320^2 x 4 B = 426 MB a table on the whole country.

## The reach rule (decided before building)

What trips a population makes, and so which stations it can use:

- **A city's commutes and everyday purposes** (`DemandApi`, `EverydayApi`). A trip starts at a home
  cell: it walks (4 km at most) or rides to a station (`ride_max_m` 15 km from the home group's
  centre, which lies up to about 9 km off the cell: so up to about 24 km off the cell). It ends by
  walking from a station to the work or activity cell (the pack's own cells). So every station a
  rider enters or leaves lies within about 24 km of the pack's cells' box. This is exactly what
  `CITY_REACH_M` (30 km past the box) was chosen to cover when it joins a city to its cluster (T-326).
  The everyday layer's longest trips are inside the pack too (its destinations are pack cells), so
  the everyday reach is the commutes'.
- **Between those stations the ride may leave the reach.** A best route between two stations
  inside box + 24 km that runs more than 6 km further out and back is a detour no strategy takes on
  a real network; T-737 measured Tokyo's pack on the whole country against the network cut to its
  box + 20 km: operator boardings 11,032,799 against 11,032,801.
- **The regional market** (longer trips and the commutes between zones, T-276, T-377) rides between
  any two 6 km zones joined by track: Tokyo to Osaka by Shinkansen. Its trips need the whole
  cluster, so its part keeps the cluster's network (the "market on the whole country" section below
  is what that costs).

**Rule**: a city's layers solve on the stations within `CITY_REACH_M` (30 km) of its cells' box,
and on each of the cluster's lines with a stop there, from the stop before its first such stop to
the stop after its last (every stop between kept, so a line that leaves the reach and comes back
needs no change of trains). The stops past either end are pads: no rider of the city boards there,
but the left-behind model (`kernel::left_behind`) runs each train from its run's first stop, so at a
pad the crowding input's boardings are the line's load on the hop leaving it (the riders already
aboard), its alightings 0. The market's part stays on the whole cluster. Crowding is still the
cluster's: every round all populations' loads are summed on the whole network's route nodes and each
population is given them on its own route nodes. A population's crowding rounds stop when its own
loads settle (T-434: before, all populations of a cluster stopped together).

`?perfOff=reach` (`wide` in the solve message) puts every population on its whole cluster, which is
the code path before T-756 to the bit (the same sums in the same order).

## The market on the whole country (T-761 built option 1: notes/T-761.md)

The market's part stays on the whole cluster, and on Japan's whole network that alone is past a
worker's 4 GB: per period pass `IntercityApi::pass_on` holds the skims (`Skims.r`, S^2 f32, 426 MB at
10,320 stations, plus per searched destination its next node, settle order and accepted boardings,
about 4 bytes a node: 31k nodes x 10k destinations, about 1.3 GB), the OD (`od`, S^2 f32, 426 MB),
the commutes' OD (`od_c`, the same) and the per-station far-zone table (`st`, S x 2 x served zones).
Every station is a destination, since every 6 km zone of Japan is served. Options, in the order I
would try them:

1. **Stream the destinations (exact).** A first sweep searches each destination and keeps only its
   column of `r` (the logsums need every station pair's cost); the pairs then give `od` and `od_c`
   by destination; a second sweep searches each destination again and assigns its column at once
   (both ODs into their own loads), dropping its arrays before the next. Memory: `r` plus the two
   ODs (about 1.3 GB at 10k stations; less with `od_c` folded into a per-destination pass). Twice the
   market's searches: on 10k stations perhaps 20-40 s a sweep in WASM, so minutes a period. Figures
   to the bit.
2. **A coarser station set for the market**: each 6 km zone's access and egress lists cut to its
   best few stations, or a trunk graph (stations where lines meet) for pairs past some distance.
   Fast and small, but it changes the market's figures, so it is a demand rule for Anita.

Figures, seven cities, wide against reach, every period: the network's rail and walked trips within
0.0023%, the market's rail within 0.0001%, each city's rail trips within 0.06% (Fukushima, about 540

The check's tolerance: 0.5% on totals and cities (a city within 10 trips), 1% on a line.

0.8 GB a worker. What is left is the market's part on the one whole-country cluster (T-761): in the
browser (2 workers) the whole country now runs 947 s before a worker runs out of memory (60 s
before), and the T-757 alert says so (`../artifacts/trainworld/t757-demand-stopped.png`).
