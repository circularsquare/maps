# T-658: double track drawn as its two tracks, close in (design)

Status: design for Anita, 2026-10-19. Nothing built. Background: `research/nimby-track-drawing.md`
(how NIMBY Rails does it), Anita's words in TASKS.md and `notes/playtest-2026-10-19.md`.

## For Anita: what you will see

**Nothing changes until you are zoomed right in.** From the region view down to a few streets
across (zoom 16 and further out), track, lines, stations and trains look as they do now.

**About zoom 17** (in New York two or three avenue blocks across a laptop screen; a 200 m
platform is about 500 px long), a double track opens into its two tracks:

- From zoom 16 the line starts growing with the ground (to the width the two tracks will take),
  then a thin pale stripe opens down its middle and widens, so one line becomes two tracks with a
  gap between them. Nothing jumps; the stripe opens over about half a zoom step. Single track stays one track, so from here you can see at
  a glance which stretches are single and which double.
- The two tracks are 4 m apart, as the game has always assumed. From here on the drawing is to
  scale: zoom in further and the tracks, the gap and the trains grow with the ground, like the
  station boxes already do.
- **Each direction has its own track.** Trains keep right (your T-319 decision: right-hand
  running everywhere, London and Tokyo too), so a train is drawn on the right-hand track for its
  direction, and two trains passing each other go by side by side instead of over each other.
- **Junctions show how they work.** Where a double track branches, the branch's two tracks peel
  off the main line's, and the inbound one visibly crosses the main line's other track: that is
  the flat junction the game already charges waiting time for. A flyover has no such crossing.
- **Quad track** shows as four evenly spaced tracks; crossovers as two double tracks crossing in
  an X; where single track meets double, the two tracks draw together over a short taper.
- **Stations** become two tracks straight through the box with a platform on each side: the
  thin blue platform edges move in to sit beside the tracks, and the riders waiting for each
  direction stand on the platform beside that direction's track.
- **Where trains turn back** (a line's terminus), a crossover is drawn in grey just outside the
  platform, because that is where the game already assumes trains change tracks (question 3).
  This is the part that helps think about terminals and T-657's faster turnbacks.

**What does not change in how you build: anything.** One stroke still draws a double track; the
single, double and quad setting, station placement, the crossover tool, junctions and flyovers
all work as now. There are no separate tracks to lay, no signals, no platforms to assign. The
two tracks are only a drawing of the one piece of track you built. Clicking and hovering pick
the same things as now (the track, its lines, the station, a train).

What it does not do: lines sharing track are still drawn over each other (T-190), and there is
no way to build a crossover between the two tracks of one double track or an island platform.
Those would be the "weird setups"; nothing here blocks adding them later.

## Questions for Anita

1. **The zoom.** Two tracks from about zoom 17, set by how far apart 4 m is on screen (opening as
   it goes from 7 to 10 px), so it looks the same in every city (zoom 16.7-17.2 in New York,
   16.4-16.9 in London, 17.0-17.6 on the equator). Earlier than that the two tracks would have to
   be thinner than a pixel or two to show a gap. *Recommendation: as proposed.*
2. **Stations: platforms either side, for every double-track station.** The tracks run straight
   through at 4 m, a platform each side. An island (tracks bending apart round one middle
   platform) would need the tracks to swing out before every station and gives the player
   nothing to decide. *Recommendation: side platforms only; an island as a station setting only
   if you ever want it.*
3. **Turn-back crossovers drawn where trains turn.** The game already assumes a crossover at
   every turn-back (a dead-end terminus uses both its platform tracks; a line ending at a through
   station crosses over behind the platform, and its capacity is priced that way, SPEC 6.2). Draw
   them, in bare-track grey, from zoom 17: a scissors (an X) just outside a double-track dead-end
   terminus, one diagonal just outside the platform on the arrival side where a line turns at a
   through station. *Recommendation: yes.* Without them the turnback is invisible and a train
   seems to change track by magic.
4. **Traffic thickness close in.** In "Line + traffic thickness" a busy line can be several times
   a plain line's width, which would make the two tracks overlap. *Recommendation: the thickness
   fades to a plain width as the tracks open (the hover still says the riders), as Elevation and
   Max speed already draw uniform width.* The alternative, each track as thick as its own
   direction's riders, is a nice later idea but more work.

## Technical plan

### What the model already has (no change)

- `ClockEdge.tracks` (1 or 2) per edge; quad is two double-track edges 8 m apart centre to centre
  (6.1), so drawing each pair's two tracks at plus and minus 2 m gives four tracks evenly 4 m apart
  with no special case.
- Right-hand running everywhere (DECISIONS 2026-10-14, T-319). A global toggle, if ever, is one
  sign in a uniform. (`docs/track-and-service.md` 6.1 still says "running side per country from
  the city pack"; that phrase is stale and stage 1 fixes it.)
- Junction conflicts are already worked out from track positions in order on the two sides of a
  node (6.2), which is what the drawn tracks show.
- Every edge end leaves a node along the node's heading or its reverse, so the two tracks of every
  edge meeting at a node start at the same two points: a branch's tracks peel off the main line's
  tracks with no fitting.
- A crossover diagonal has the track count of the narrower track it joins, so between two double
  tracks it is double: its two drawn tracks join the left track to the left track and the right to
  the right, and the X crosses itself as the model prices it.
- Lines: each line's strokes run along its path in run 0's direction (`StrokeBuilder.add` with
  `reverse`), so run 0 is the right-hand track of the stroke and run 1 the left-hand one.
- Platform crowds: the + side (trains along the platform axis) is drawn on `(-ay, ax)` of the
  axis; local y points south (`mercY`), so that is already the right of travel. Stage 3 confirms
  it on screen.

**The track model and the sim do not change.** Drawing only, plus a CPU offset in picking.

### The lane band

`t`, how far the two tracks have opened, 0 to 1, from the on-screen size of 4 m:
`G = 4 m in CSS px at the view's centre` (the renderer has `u_px_per_unit` and the anchor's
metres per local unit), `t = clamp((G - 7) / 3, 0, 1)`. At `t = 0` every pass is exactly as now
(one uniform, no extra draw). Widths, all in CSS px:

- a track's width as one stroke: as now (`LINE_PX`, 6.6 at zoom 15 and in), but at least
  `G + 0.6 G` once `G` passes about 4 px (the pair's footprint, 6.4 m on the ground), so the
  stroke has already grown to the pair's width when the stripe opens: no jump in its outline;
- each track at `t`: offset `t * G / 2` from the centre line, width `mix(stroke width, 0.6 G, t)`
  (0.6 G = 2.4 m, a sleeper's width); traffic width (`a_wmul`) mixed to 1 by `t` (question 4);
- the casing: one band under both tracks (`t * G + track width + casing`), drawn once, so the gap
  shows the casing colour (white, near-black in the dark theme);
- single track: one track at the centre, its width `mix(stroke width, 0.6 G, t)`.

### Tracks and lines

- **Lane factor per vertex** (`network.ts`): 1 along double track, 0 along single, ramping
  linearly over the last 40 m of a double-track edge where it meets single track at a node (the
  taper). Stored as a per-segment start and end value on track and line strokes (`Strokes.lane`,
  two Uint8 normalised, 2 bytes a segment); preview, highlight and placement strokes take it from
  the tool's setting (single 0, double and quad 1).
- **Stroke shader** (`STROKE_VS`): a uniform `u_lane` (0, +1 right, -1 left) and `u_lane_t`; the
  offset is `-nrm * u_lane * lanefactor * t * G / 2` (screen `nrm` is the left of the segment, so
  minus is the right). Track (bare, blueprint, selected) and line colour passes are drawn twice at
  `t > 0`, once per side; the second pass discards segments whose lane factor is 0 at both ends,
  so single track and faded lines are not blended twice. The casing pass is drawn once, widened.
  Round caps and joints (T-673) cover the small gaps an offset leaves at a bend.
- **Blueprint**: each track its own two rails and light middle (the rails pass per side, then the
  middle pass per side). Picked blueprint's ink band goes under the pair.
- **Selected line or track**: the extra width goes on the casing band round the pair, not on each
  track, so the two stay apart.
- **Junction and crossing badges** stay at the node (the junction is the node in the model). If
  they hide the tracks peeling apart in the screenshots, they fade out over the band (the drawn
  crossing then says it); decided in stage 1 from the screenshots.
- **Track ends**: each track ends flat at the end node (a buffer), not with a round cap, from the
  band on.

### Trains

- **Lane per sample**: a small R8 texture beside `u_samples`, one byte a sample (run 0 is
  resampled every 50 m): that sample's lane factor, from `sampleStroke` and the stroke's lane
  values. `lineAt` interpolates it with the position, so a train drifts across over the taper.
- `TRAIN_VS`: the train's centre moves `(run == 0 ? right : left) * lane * t * G / 2` across the
  chord direction it already computes (`sdir`, `nrm`).
- **Width**: `min(as now, 0.8 G)` in the band (9 px at most down to 8 px), then 3 m on the ground,
  so two trains passing never touch and a train covers its 2.4 m track.
- **Turning back**: the train stands on the arrival track, and its next trip starts on the other
  one, so it steps 4 m across as it leaves. It does not drive through the drawn crossover; that
  would need keyframes the sim does not have. Accepted.
- **Picking**: `tripAt` and `trainCarAt` (network.ts) add the same offset (the lane per sample on
  the CPU is a `Uint8Array` beside `samples`), so a click on either of two passing trains picks it.

### Stations

From the band on, for a double-track station (`STATION_FS`, uniforms only):

- the track and line strokes through the box are the two tracks; the 30% cap (`TRACK_IN_BOX`)
  applies to each track's width, not the pair's;
- the blue platform edges move from beside the rim to just outside the tracks
  (`t * G / 2 + 0.3 G + 1 px` from the centre line, mixed by `t` from today's place);
- each side's crowd fills the strip between its blue edge and the rim instead of the half box;
  the full side's bulge (T-652) is unchanged;
- a single-track station keeps one track down the middle with a platform each side, as now;
- the box keeps its width (about 20 m on the ground in New York, 17 m in London, 13 m at 60
  degrees north; with 6.4 m of track that leaves platforms of 3.5 m and more).

### Turn-back crossovers (if question 3 is yes)

Worked out in `networkFromState` from lines and stations, drawn as bare-track strokes with a new
flag (no edge, not picked, no hover of their own; the hover of the track under them stays) only
when `t > 0`:

- a double-track dead-end station that is a line's terminus: an X of two straight diagonals,
  50 m long, joining the two tracks just outside the platform's open end, along the line's path;
- a line ending at a double-track through station: one diagonal on its arrival side, just outside
  the platform, from the arrival track back to the departure track;
- a few segments per terminus, rebuilt with the line strokes.

T-657 builds on this: a tail track would draw the crossover beyond the platform instead of before
it, so the two layouts read differently on the map.

### Picking and hover

Picking stays per edge and per line, as now. The hit reach is `max(7 px, half the drawn width)`
(T-227), with the drawn width the pair's footprint once the tracks open, so a click on either
track or the gap picks the track or the line on top. Train picking as above. Hover tags are
unchanged. A later idea, not in these stages: the hover naming the direction of the track under
the pointer ("towards Van Cortlandt Park").

### Performance

- **Far and medium zoom: unchanged.** At `t = 0` no extra pass is drawn and the shader adds a
  multiply by zero; buffers grow by 2 bytes a stroke segment and 1 byte a train sample (New York's
  real save, about 2,000 km of line and track strokes: on the order of 100 kB in all), uploaded
  only when the network changes.
- **Close in** (`t > 0`): the track and line colour passes and the blueprint passes are drawn
  twice, the casing once; trains are the same one instanced draw. Every stroke segment in the
  network goes through the vertex shader whether in view or not, as now, so the cost is one more
  pass of the same size: well under a millisecond for New York on the GPU. Stations and trains:
  a few more uniforms, no new buffers.
- CPU: the lane values come in the same loops `networkFromState` already runs; the line and edge
  caches keep them. Turn-back crossovers are a handful of segments per terminus.

### Stage a, as built (2026-10-19)

- `network.ts`: `Strokes.lane` (2 bytes a segment, 0 single to 255 double, at each end);
  `lanePoints` adds a point 40 m from a double-track end that meets single track at a plain node
  (two edge ends) and ramps the lane value over it; speeds follow the added points. Cached per
  edge with the taper flags. At a junction a single branch keeps its own 0 (leaves the centre).
- `renderer.ts`: `laneState(zoom)` from 4 m on screen at the anchor; `STROKE_VS` modes 0 (one
  stroke, identical to before while growth is 0), 1 (band: casing under the pair, and for lines
  the pale band, line colour mixed `0.6 t` towards the casing, as wide as the pair or the traffic
  width), 2 and 3 (right and left track). Changed from the design: the gap opens from the middle
  as soon as the tracks start parting (each track half of what is left of the pair's width),
  rather than the tracks sliding apart, which showed no gap until 80% of the band.
- Picking and the track hover reach half the pair's width (`laneReachPx`, `pick.ts`,
  `trackHover.ts`). Previews take lane 255 (double), since the tool's setting is in `tools.ts`
  (T-707/T-708's file then): a single-track preview draws as two tracks close in. Follow-up.
- Not done: flat track ends (round caps stay), junction badges left at the node (they read fine
  in the screenshots).
- Checks (`checks/t658-lanes.mjs`, real save): the overlay at zoom 12 and 15 is identical to the
  last commit's build on 8800, 0 pixels of 700,000 differ; either track picks the line at 17.5.
  Overlay GPU time 1.3 ms at zoom 15 and 3.5 ms at 17.5 round 59 St (24 lines; up to six stroke
  passes instead of two, and the strokes cover far more pixels), CPU 0.25 ms at both. The real
  save has no double-track flat junction with lines on it, so no junction screenshots; quad,
  crossover and taper were not on screen either. Screenshots `artifacts/trainworld/t658/`.

### Stage b, as built (2026-10-19)

- `network.ts`: `NetworkBuffers.sampleLane` (one float a 50 m train sample, the lane value of its
  edge there, along the drawn points where the edge tapers); `tripAt(..., shiftM)` and
  `trainCarAt` put the train `shiftM` metres times the lane value to the right of its travel.
- `renderer.ts`: an R32F lane texture (texture unit 10); `TRAIN_VS` shifts the train `t` times
  half the 4 m gap to the right of its travel, and its width goes from the pill's to the true
  3 m by the same share, so the shift and the width move together with the tracks' parting and
  are 0 further out (pixel check still 0 differing at 12 and 15). `laneShiftM` (2 m once apart)
  for picking; `pick.ts` passes it. Hover tags and the camera follow stay on the centre line (2 m
  off at most).
- Check: two trains of one line at the same place in opposite directions, 59 St, zoom 17.6: drawn
  side by side, each picked by a click on its own drawn centre. No taper on the real save, so the
  drift across a taper is unseen. Turning back steps 4 m across, as designed.

### Stages c and d, and the preview, as built (2026-10-19)

- Stations (c): station flag 16 (on double track, from the platform's edge) and `v_lane` in the
  station shaders; in `STATION_FS` the blue edges move to 1 px outside the tracks and narrow to
  0.4 m, and each side's crowd fills from beside its edge out to 1.5 px inside the rim, both by
  `t` (so identical further out: the pixel check still 0 differing at 12 and 15). The + side was
  already the right of travel (local y south); the screenshots show each side's riders on the
  platform beside its own track.
- Turn-back crossovers (d): `turnbackStrokes` in `network.ts`, along each line's own strokes at
  its two ends where the end is double track, 50 m: a scissors outside a dead end's platform
  (node with one edge end), one diagonal outside a through station's platform on the arrival
  side. `F_TURNBACK` (128) strokes take signed lane values and are drawn once, in the right
  track's pass, only while the tracks are parted, in bare-track grey over the line tracks. 1,130
  segments on the real save (several lines ending at one station draw the same crossover over
  each other). Not picked, no hover.
- Preview: `strokesFromPreview(..., lane)`; the drawing tool (`tools.ts`) passes 0 for single
  track, the reshape preview (`edgeEdit.ts`) the edge's own track count. A node drag's preview
  (`nodeDrag.ts`, several edges, no ids) stays double: a single-track edge dragged close in
  previews as two tracks.

### Borrowed from NIMBY Rails, and not

Borrowed: a fixed-width stripe far out and real width close in; junction and crossover geometry
only when close in; the place where a train turns shown on the track. Not borrowed: each track
laid by hand as its own piece (its developer's main usability cost), signals, platforms assigned
per track, scissors built from branches.
