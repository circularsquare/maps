## Why Hokkaido lost shape (measured on master's all-Japan layout)

Scripts in the session scratchpad `diaghokkaido/` (`hok.mjs`: Hokkaido's IoU, where its drawn-only land comes from, its
skeleton against the ground, the drawn land pieces; `m.mjs`, `agg.mjs`, `crossdiff.mjs`, `timing2.mjs`).

- **The spit** was the water, not the layout: of Hokkaido's 7,327 drawn-only cells, 3,709 had Yunokawa (Hakodate's tram
  terminus) as nearest station, on average 179 pitches from it. Each voter carried a cell's offset back to the ground by
  its own local map, fitted on its tram hops at Hakodate's city scale (5.2 times Hokkaido's), so a cell 179 pitches out
  landed 30 km away on the Kameda peninsula: land. Elsewhere on all Japan 30 drawn land pieces held no station (54,588
  sq pitches): Awaji drawn from Kobe's and Naruto's stations, and land carried far off Wakayama's maps (the largest,
  22,805 sq pitches) among them.
- **The narrow island**: Hokkaido drawn at 0.10 of the Kanto core's scale (Honshu as a whole 0.30); its east and north
  at 0.08 (lonely hops at 0.4 of a target already compressed by the 0.3 power), Sapporo and Hakodate at 3 to 5 times the
  island's scale. So the cities swell and the broad body shrinks; Hakodate sat 1.8 times too far from Sapporo.
- T-2541's bays made it worse only through Hakodate: Hakodate Bay is opened at the city's scale (Hakodate's 25 km drawn at
  3.3 -> 5.2 times the island's scale), stretching the hook and feeding the spit (1,888 -> 3,709 cells).

## What changed

1. Water, far land: past its hops' mean drawn length a voter carries the rest of the offset by its region's similarity
   (its land mass's stations within 50 km), where that is the smaller scale. Alone, on master's layout: Hokkaido IoU
   0.350 -> 0.431, Kyushu 0.539 -> 0.594, Shikoku 0.528 -> 0.564, Honshu 0.572 -> 0.571; Yunokawa's cells 3,709 -> 713.
2. Water, whose land: land of another land mass with stations votes sea (that mass's stations draw it); an island with
   no station votes land only to stations within 15 km of the station nearest it; land drawn with no station on it goes
   unless it is mostly such an island. (A rule taking land as its nearest station's on the ground, wherever that is
   drawn, was tried: it cut holes inland where the layout distorts, Honshu IoU 0.57 -> 0.47, so it was left out.)
3. Ground, islands left out: a sea island no station is on, under a tenth of the land in the 100 km square round it.
   All Japan: 1,310 such masses, 1,187 left out (Shodoshima, Okushiri, the Seto Inland Sea's); kept: Amakusa (16% of
   the land round it), Awaji (12%), Sado (as far as the masks cover it).
4. Layout, big sparse regions: a region (60 km round each 10 km square) drawn under 0.25 targets per real metre has
   its hops' targets raised to it. A floor against the network's median (0.6, 0.75) was tried first: it lifted 10 to 15%
   of New York's hops and cost Manhattan's parallels (0.73 -> 0.62); the absolute floor leaves a city network almost
   untouched (Tokyo 3 hops, Toronto none).
