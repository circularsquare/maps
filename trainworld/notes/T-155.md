# T-155: demand forecast wording

Line ridership, stop boardings and train loads are outputs of the latest demand equilibrium for the
current network and schedules. They are steady-state forecasts. The moving trains have analytic
positions, but the game does not count people boarding them as time advances.

Player-facing line and train views now say this directly: the Lines panel introduces forecast
riders, the line inspector groups riders and peak load under Demand forecast, stop values say
forecast boardings, and a selected train says Forecast load with typical and busiest-hour train
values. Settings calls the visual a train load forecast and explains that it is not a live count.

## Opening ramp assessment

A display-only ramp is small but incoherent: it would make shown loads disagree with mode share,
fare income and network assignment. A coherent ramp affects mode choice so trips not yet attracted
to a new service choose walking, driving or another rail route. That requires a saved opening time
per line, a temporary generalized-cost penalty in the demand network, and scheduled recomputes as
the penalty decays. T-156 records a concrete proposal: when a line first begins service, add 20
perceived minutes to trips using it, halve that penalty every 7 game days, recompute at midnight,
and stop after day 28. Existing saves start mature; later route and schedule edits do not restart
the clock. The constants still need play testing before implementation. This does not belong in
the copy-focused task.
