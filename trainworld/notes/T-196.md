# T-196: parks as social/leisure destinations

Done 2026-10-12. Anita: leisure "doesn't feel quite as tied to jobs"; parks "shouldn't be weighted
by just size. maybe by size and by population nearby".

## What it does

Social/leisure outings (0.2 per resident a day, T-066) choose destinations by three independently
normalised draws: population (friends and family) 46%, food/recreation jobs (LODES CNS17+CNS18)
46%, and **parks 8%**. A pack without parks keeps 50/50 exactly. Parks move outings; they add
none (origin-constrained: each home's budget is fixed).

- `pipeline/enrich_parks.py <city>` writes the cell array `park_draw` and the header block
  `parks` (sources, counts by kind, the formula); an audit per place in
  `data/work/<city>.parks.audit.json`.
- `sim/src/demand/everyday.rs`: `PARK_SHARE = 0.08`; the social weights read `park_draw` when the
  pack has a non-empty one. Tests `parks_share_the_social_destinations` (fixture) and the ignored
  `parks_before_after` (every pack against `data/work/pre-T196/`).

## The share: ATUS 2023 (`notes/t196_atus.py`)

American Time Use Survey 2023 microdata (BLS, activity and respondent files, weighted by
TUFINLWGT). An outing is a travel episode for eating and drinking, socialising and leisure, or
sport and recreation (ATUS 1811-1813); its destination is where the next non-travel episode
happens. Trips home and the 14.9% whose next episode has no place recorded (sleeping, grooming:
mostly arrivals home) are left out. 4,131 outings from 2,873 respondents.

| destination | share |
|---|---|
| restaurant or bar | 28.8% |
| someone else's home | 22.8% |
| other place | 17.7% |
| gym or health club | 10.3% |
| **outdoors away from home** | **7.6%** (95% interval 6.4-8.7%, bootstrap over respondents) |
| workplace, school, worship, shops, library, other | 12.8% |

By purpose: sport and recreation outings go outdoors 15.1%, socialising and leisure 7.7%, eating
out 2.5%. A year's average, so no season, as the brief asked. NHTS has no place type (its
"recreational activities" code lumps parks with movies and bars), so ATUS is the source.

The park share is 8%, taken evenly from the population and leisure-jobs draws so T-066's 50/50
between them stays. ATUS says friends' homes are 23% of leisure outings, under the model's 46%
population draw; that is T-066's choice (the population draw also stands for neighbourhood
leisure LODES misses) and was not changed here (docs/demand-worldwide.md).

## The places and their draw

OpenStreetMap (Overpass), inside the pack's boundary: `leisure=park|common|recreation_ground|
beach_resort`, botanical gardens, named `natural=beach`, named nature reserves. Calls:

- **Nature reserves**: one named like a park ("Keney Park", "Osbornedale State Park", "... Recreation
  Area", "... Beach") counts as a park; any other named reserve at a quarter; unnamed ones not at
  all. New England maps its land-trust, watershed and wetland parcels as reserves (Hartford has
  1,350), and at half weight they were 58% of Hartford's draw, mostly places few people visit.
- **Playgrounds, dog parks, pitches**: left out (nearly all inside parks, and a trip to one is
  usually a walk under the model's 150 m cut). Golf courses, cemeteries, private and
  `access=no` places left out. A place inside a larger kept one merges into it.
- **Draw** = factor x sqrt(area in ha, at least 0.1) x D / (D + 2,000), D = residents per km² of
  land within 2 km of the edge (the place itself left out; land = inside the pack's county shapes).
  The square root: a park 100 times larger draws 10 times as much. The density term discounts
  parks few people live near and saturates in built-up places, since the gravity already sends
  more trips to parks with more homes around them. A first version used the residents counted
  within 2 km instead of a density: that count grows with a park's edge, and Harriman State Park
  (19,300 ha, 42k "neighbours") outdrew Central Park five times (94 against 18). By density:
  Central Park 17.2, Harriman 14.5. The exponent, the 2 km and the 2,000/km² are judgement; no
  US-wide park visit counts were found to fit them to (Donahue et al. 2018, Twin Cities social
  media visits, found visits higher in denser surroundings and with water and trails, which is
  the direction used here).
- The draw goes to the pack cells whose centres lie inside the place, split evenly; a smaller
  place to the cell under it or the nearest within 500 m. At most 0.4% of a pack's draw
  could not be placed (Boston 0.33%: places more than 500 m from any pack cell).

## Found along the way

- `out geom tags` gives relations no members, so every multipolygon park (Prospect Park, Pelham
  Bay Park, Flushing Meadows) had no polygon. Relations are now fetched separately with
  `out geom`. `enrich_medical.py` has the same query, so its multipolygon hospital campuses are
  skipped: T-203. `overpass.polygon` now also reads empty member roles as outer rings.
- The public Overpass servers answered 504 to most requests this evening; `enrich_parks.py`
  fetches in 0.5° tiles, three at a time, splitting a tile in four when every server gives up,
  and `overpass.py` now tries private.coffee first and waits 10 s (not 30) after a 504. A full
  refetch of the nine packs took about an hour.
