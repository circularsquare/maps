# T-3482: Tokyo - Sendai's low runs: the peak trains, not the passage

Agent passage, 2026-11-18. No code changed.

## Finding

The passage is counted every time. What swings is the intercity test line's trains at the high demand level (the
peak periods): 0 trains an hour in a low run, 2.35 in a high run. Demand follows that.

- **Quick update and reload agree.** On this tree, three live builds (r2, r3, r4) and a fresh-page reload of the same
  build (ri1) all gave 5,913 rail trips a day on the pair and 22,089 riders on the line. Those are exactly the 11-16
  low figures in notes/T-3454.md (5,913 and 22,089). The same reload agreeing with the live page means no demand
  state goes stale.
- **The passage reaches demand in every run.** The demand client's network had both passages
  (2155-2466 at Tokyo, 2465-2472 at Sendai) and both pairs in one complex (27 and 1445) in the low runs as in the
  high one.
- **The saves differ in the trains.** The 11-16 high save (feedsample `ts-ri-fix.save`) solved on today's code gives
  the high figure (30,534 and 105,077 riders), so the code is not at fault there. Diffed line by line against the low
  save (ri1.save), 132 of 151 lines are the same. Line 150, the intercity: high level 0 trains an hour (1 train)
  against 2.35 (9 trains); medium 5.24 and low 2 in both. Lines 42, 43, 56, 128 (Shinjuku, Oedo, Namboku,
  Tsukuba Express): high level 15 trains an hour each against 13.83. So the four metro lines and the intercity share
  something that is full at the high level: whatever the four keep, the intercity loses. Its first hop (Tokyo to
  Ueno, 2.85 km) takes 803 s at the high level against 125 s at the low; its capacity delay at the high level is
  2,280 s a round trip.
- **The swing happens with no edit.** Run r5 recorded the line after each build step and every 5 s after:
  - before the line: the four metro lines at 23.1, 20.1, 18.4 and 17.7 trains an hour at the high level
  - `addLine` (no trains yet): all four cut to 15.0
  - `setHeadway` (450/600/1800 s): the intercity at 0 / 5.24 / 2
  - about 3 minutes later, with no edit and the game paused, at a demand final: the intercity went to 2.35 and the
    four to 13.83. The next solve gave 30,364 on the pair and 101,742 riders, with the passage untouched.

  Sending the same headways again afterwards changed nothing.
- **Why a reload looked like the fix in T-3454.** A reload keeps the saved trains an hour. The two 11-16 reload runs
  were saved in the high state, and their live builds came out high or low depending on whether that later re-settle
  had happened before the figures were read.

## Where the shares are set (read, not changed)

`sim/src/track/world.rs` `settle_counts`: on an edit, a line already settled is left as it is (T-1787), and full track
is shared by `capacity.rs` `fill` only among the lines being stepped. So a new line on track that its neighbours have
already filled gets what is left, which at this peak was nothing. A later settle that steps them together shares it
(2.35 against 13.83 each). What started the late re-settle in r5 was not found; nothing in the page edited.

A fix would change how lines share full track on an edit. That is track and scheduling and outside T-3482's
brief, so it was left alone.

## How it was measured

Scratch scripts in the passage scratch folder, from T-3454's `feedsample/pairrun.mjs`: `run.mjs` and `run2.mjs`
(the build, the per-step and 5 s track readings, the re-sent headway), `dumpall.mjs` with `diffall.mjs` (every
line's trains an hour, trains, headways, round trips and delays in two saves), `dumpline.mjs`. The Tokyo - Sendai
merged save is `feedsample/saves/tyo_sdj.save`. Every run used `SNAP_OFF=900`: the default 150 m puts the Tokyo
platform across a real one. Runs used 3 cores (2 demand workers) on Vite 6191, about 4-9 minutes each.
