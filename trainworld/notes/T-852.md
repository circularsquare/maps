# T-852: a coarser zone grid for very rural areas

Anita, 2026-10-21: "use a coarser grid maybe for these super rural areas" (approved the idea; this note
is the numbers for her approval). Measured 2026-10-21 on the T-850b sample (notes/T-850.md 5.1) and a
survey of all 741 registered packs. Nothing in packs, pipeline or app changed; the probe sim was a
scratch copy.

## 1. What the 2 km zones cost

Every pack's gravity zones are 2 km squares (`gravity.zone_m` 2000 in all 741). A zone costs memory and
opening time whatever its cells: probe sim (native, opening the pack's commute and everyday layers, peak
private bytes, as notes/T-850.md 5.1), each pack solved on its own at 2 km and at 4 km with
`pack_gravity --zone-m`, no routes in either, same cells:

| pack | zones 2 km -> 4 km | MB 2 km -> 4 km | open ms 2 km -> 4 km | routes (live less 2 km) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| aad | 4,109 -> 1,402 | 9.9 -> 6.6 | 460 -> 135 | +2.8 MB, +120 ms |
| osj | 4,914 -> 1,536 | 14.4 -> 8.7 | 627 -> 174 | +3.1 MB |
| drh | 2,698 -> 814 | 6.8 -> 4.7 | 351 -> 82 | +2.1 MB |
| iwt | 2,579 -> 838 | 6.7 -> 5.2 | 317 -> 119 | +1.5 MB |

So a zone is about 1.2 KB a worker without its routes and 0.6-0.8 KB of routes, **about 1.9 KB in
all, and 0.12-0.14 ms of opening**: the bulk of a countryside pack's opening time (aad: 0.46 of 0.58 s).
Cells cost about 270 bytes each (T-850), so per pack the zones weigh more than the cells.

## 2. What counts as very rural

Per 2 km zone, by trip ends a weekday (as `merge_cells.trip_ends`). Over all 741 packs (951,077 zones):

| zones under | share of zones | their trip ends | adaptive 4 km (below) leaves | whole-pack 4 km leaves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 250 | 38% | 0.6% | -10% zones | -70% |
| 500 | 51% | 1.6% | -17% | |
| **1,000** | **65%** | **3.7%** | **-27%** | |
| 2,000 | 77% | 7.0% | -38% | |
| 4,000 | 85% | 11.8% | -49% | |

The 670 packs whose median zone is under 3,000 trip ends (the countryside zones and small metros)
hold 95% of all zones; the 71 big-city packs lose only 1-5% at 1,000. 1,000 trip ends is roughly 400
people and their jobs in 4 km²: farmland with a hamlet or two.

Two ways to coarsen:

- **Whole pack at 4 km** (`zone_m` 4000 for a pack judged rural). -70% zones, but no pack is rural all
  over: every countryside zone has its towns, which would go to 4 km too.
- **Adaptive**: a 2x2 block of 2 km squares (aligned to the pack's grid, one region) becomes one 4 km
  zone when every zone in it is under the threshold; towns and their edges stay at 2 km. At 1,000:
  aad 4,109 -> 2,740 zones, osj 4,914 -> 3,231, drh 2,698 -> 2,155, vek 2,687 -> 1,941, iwt 2,579 ->
  1,777, crn 1,748 -> 1,063, nyc 6,407 -> 5,852.

## 3. What it would change

Measured for the whole-pack version (probe sim taking the pack's `zone_m`, which the game's
`DemandApi::new` does not do today: it re-solves at 2 km when a pack's zone size differs), 2 km against 4
km, standalone gravity, test lines as notes/T-850.md 5.1:

| line | rail, commute / everyday | stations of 50+ entries past 10% | worst |
|---|---|---|---|
| town lines (aad, drh, vek, osj, crn, iwt) | -10% to +21% / -14% to +9% | 3-7 of 5-11 | -22% to +47% |
| rural lines (aad, drh, vek, osj) | -5% to +26% / +28% to +40% | 3-4 of 3-6 | +50% to +57% |

Trips rise about 1% and the gravity's mean commute 0.8-1.1 km (aad 23.6 -> 24.4 km). That is far from
"nothing visible", so the whole-pack version is ruled out. The rural lines' +30-40% everyday rail
suggests the coarse zone's within-zone trips spread over its cells as if 4 km apart (the intrazonal
distance is 0.52 x the zone size); the adaptive version would show this on lines through coarse blocks,
which hold 3.7% of all trip ends (5.1% in the countryside packs, aad 10.8%, osj 17%), and leave town
lines nearly as they are. It needs measuring once built (stage c below).

Other effects:

- **Gravity**: re-solved for every coarsened pack, and its country's joint gravity group with it
  (`intercity.py --solve`; T-786's country units), since factors and zone ids change.
- **Routes**: rebuilt (`routes.py`, 1-8 minutes a pack), and they need the region's Geofabrik extract,
  which the world runner deletes after each region: a fleet-wide rollout re-downloads every extract
  (Europe's whole set). The walk parts (`walk_part`, up to 4 a zone) are recut per coarse zone.
- **Merge**: `merge_cells` merges within a zone part, so cells in a coarse block could merge across the
  old 2 km lines; with T-850b's floor this takes a few % more cells.
- **Bubbles**: drawn per cell, not per zone: unchanged by the grid (T-850b's floor is what changes them,
  fewer and larger bubbles in thin country). The station panel names far-end zones by the station within
  3 km of a zone's centre; a coarse zone's centre is up to 1.4 km off its old one.
- **Walk catchments**: by cell, unchanged; the walk factor around water is per zone part, so coarser.
- **Market**: built from cells in 600 m sites and 6 km zones; unchanged apart from the joint commutes'
  spreading, which reads the gravity zones.

## 4. Recommendation

**Adaptive 4 km blocks at 1,000 trip ends a weekday per 2 km zone.** 27% fewer zones over the fleet
(28% in the countryside packs); per countryside pack about 2.6-3.2 MB a worker (aad 12.7 MB live ->
about 10.1, with T-850b's floor about 8.9, -30%) and 0.15-0.2 s of opening each time it opens (aad is large: the mean
countryside pack has 1,345 zones and would lose about 380, 0.7 MB and 50 ms). A network across 100
countryside zones saves about 70 MB per solving worker, 0.36 GB over five, against T-850b's floor's
0.25 GB (notes/T-850.md section 3). Towns keep 2 km, so town lines should barely move; lines through farmland will
move, by an amount stage c measures before any rollout. 500 would be the cautious choice (-17%, 1.6% of
trip ends coarsened); 2,000 the bolder one (-38%, 7%).

Not the whole-pack 4 km grid: twice the saving, but town lines' rail moves 10-20% and single stations
20-50%.

## 5. Build and run plan

- **T-852a, sim (Rust)**: zones of two sizes in one pack. The gravity block gains a per-zone `zone_size`
  (1 or 2 squares; absent means 1); `zone_gx/gy` stay the 2 km grid index of the block's lower-left
  square. Kernel and everyday: pair distances from zone centres (a decay table in half-square steps),
  intrazonal distance and the land cap from the zone's size, the station-to-zone lookup and walk/drive
  windows through a square-to-zone index. A pack without `zone_size` must give bit-identical results
  (t066, t301, every_pack_everyday, smoke). About 150-300 lines; a code agent with a worktree.
- **T-852b, pipeline**: `pack_gravity --coarse 1000` (blocks by the rule, before solving), `joint_gravity`
  zone centres from the size, `merge_cells`'s square test on the block, `read_pack`'s checks, `routes.py`
  and `pack_walk`'s windows by square, and the gate. `world_run.py` passes the rule for new packs.
- **T-852c, measure**: rebuild the sample (aad, osj, drh, vek, iwt, crn and New York) adaptive at 500,
  1,000 and 2,000 in a scratch tree (`--root`), measure memory, opening and the town and rural lines as
  here, and show Anita the line changes before choosing the threshold.
- **Rollout**: new packs from then on through `world_run.py`; existing packs a country at a time
  (undo merge, gravity, joint solve, routes with the extract re-downloaded, merge, market, gate),
  scheduled with the next routes rebuild or a country's own rebuild rather than a sweep of its own,
  since the extracts dominate the cost.

Scripts were scratch (`zone_survey.py`, `g_build.py`, `mem_probe.py`, `run_g.py`), not kept.
