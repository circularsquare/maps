# T-3526 Block more trains than single track can run (proposal)

Anita, 2026-11-17: "if we have a line that has a single track portion that severely limits capacity, we dont
actually stop people from assigning way more trains than can actually run, which is a bit silly. should probably
block this in analogous way to how we block adding tons of trains to lines past what their terminals can handle."

Stage 1 is this proposal; nothing is built. Measured on master d607c0b2 (worktree singlecap, Vite 6113), probes in
the agent's scratchpad (not kept).

## Short version

The block she means already exists and is not about terminals: it caps a line at **the trains its round trip fits
at its tightest place's headway, plus one**, and the tightest place can be a terminal, a platform, a junction or a
single-track stretch. On single track it leaks, for two reasons:

1. **A line's trains queue against themselves on single track.** Its two directions count as two streams of trains
   for the queueing wait, on top of the 4 minute meeting margin. So each added train mostly adds waiting at the
   passing stations, the round trip grows, and the cap (round trip over headway, plus one) grows with it. At a
   terminal one line's trains never queue against each other, so the cap there is tight. The spec already says
   "One line alone has no queueing delay (its trains are evenly spaced) until it is over capacity"; single track
   breaks that.
2. **With 0 or 1 train the cap uses the lone-train figure** (T-3201: a lone train has no 4 minute margin), so a
   number typed on a new line is judged against a limit that vanishes the moment a second train runs.

Two ways to fix it (choice 1 below): stop a line queueing against itself on single track, so the terminal cap works
unchanged (recommended; run times on single-track lines with 2+ trains get shorter), or leave run times alone and
work the cap out from the round trip without those waits (the capped line then runs short of the headway its hover
quotes).

## 1. How the existing cap works (the "terminal cap")

- **Sim.** Every shared thing is a resource with a hold per train (track-and-service.md 6.2). Turning at a station
  holds a place for max(turnaround + 60 s, 240 s), pooled: 15 trains/h, 30 with a Terminal. `Capacity::line_limits`
  (sim capacity.rs) gives each line, per demand level, the most trains/h it can run with every other line as it
  runs, and which resource sets it; `TrackApi::line_held` (wasm/service.rs) hands that to the app as a shortest
  headway, a kind and a place. `Capacity::fill` cuts rates so no resource passes 100%; trains that do not fit wait at
  the ends of the line out of service.
- **App.** `trainCap` (game/world.ts, T-3165) = floor(round trip / shortest headway) + 1, steps of 2 on a loop run
  both ways, 1000 when nothing limits. The schedule's train stepper (ui/inspector/schedule.tsx) refuses + past it
  with a shake and clamps a typed number to it; the + hover at the cap is "Turning at West takes one train every
  4m00s at most". At the cap one train is partly held: the headway turns red (hover "..., so the trains past that
  wait at the ends") and the alert under the schedule names the place ("Turning at X is full") with the trains held.
  `game/capLoad.ts` lowers a count above the cap once when a game loads (on a new game that pass came late and
  lowered a count the probe had just set by the edit API, A 5 to 3); a cap that falls during play (a track edit)
  leaves the count, red, as T-3165 decided.
- **Levels.** Each demand level has its own count, limit and cap.
- **Headway lines** have no cap in the panel. The sim takes the count the headway needs (`clamp_auto` trims it to
  what fits when the headway is at the limit), then `trim_auto` drops whole trains that would wait at the ends, so a
  headway under the limit settles at about the count cap (B at 5 or 3 minutes: 4 trains, the same as + allows). A
  headway under the limit shows red with the same hover. The first headway after switching keeps the trains the line
  had (T-752).
- **It is not turning-specific.** Kind 1 (single track) caps exactly the same way; on the real save every limit
  happens to be turning (below), which is why it reads as a terminal cap.

## 2. Single track today

- **Passing places.** Two-way single track is cut into stretches between passing points: every station except a
  one-track station (T-3332). Each stretch is one resource for both directions together, holding a train for its run
  time over the stretch + 240 s (`SINGLE_TRACK_MARGIN_S`, T-3112): about 10 minutes each way over 1 km, 12 over 2,
  14 over 5, 20 over 10. A lone train on a stretch no other line uses holds its run time only (T-3201). One-way single
  track counts as double track's directed track.
- **Trains meeting.** The sim is a rate model: the meeting is the 240 s margin plus a queueing wait (Kingman's
  formula, track-and-service.md "Delay per train", with the line's two directions as two streams, mix 0.5). The map's
  train guard (T-3202) keeps opposite trains off a stretch together, so the player sees trains standing at the passing
  stations.
- **More trains than the stretch carries.** Each train added stretches the round trip; trains/h creep toward the
  limit and barely any train is ever held. On B and C every second of the line's waiting is at the single-track
  stretch (kind 1 in `line_delays`, at 1, 2 and 3 trains). Visible as the single-track capacity dot (orange from 75%,
  red when full), the waits in the line panel's round-trip split and trains standing at stations; the held alert and
  red headway come only at the very end.

## 4. The two fixes

### Fix 1 (recommended): a line does not queue against itself on single track

In the queueing wait on a single-track stretch, a line's two directions count as one stream, as its trains are
timetabled to meet (the 240 s margin is the meeting). Waits between different lines on a shared stretch stay. Then a
line alone on single track has no wait until the stretch is full, `fill` holds the trains past that at the ends, and
the existing cap, red headway, held alert and headway-line trim all work exactly as at a terminal, with no app change.

Estimated from the free round trips (not built, so not measured):

| case | at the cap | runs | hover quotes |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 2 trains, about 1 held | 4.05/h, every 14m48s | 14m48s |
| B | 2 trains, about 0.2 held | 3.74/h, every 16m03s; round trip 28m54s (now 37m51s) | 16m03s |
| C | 3 trains, about 0.3 held | 5.65/h, every 10m37s; round trip 28m44s (now 36m20s) | 10m37s |

The lie: two trains on B really would wait about 3 minutes a round trip at the meeting station (they need 32m06s to
keep 16m03s apart and the free trip is 28m54s); the game shows the running trains' trip as the free one and holds 0.2
of a train instead. Throughput is right; ride times on such lines are a little short.

The lone train keeps T-3201 (no margin). A typed count on a new line is still judged by the lone figure (C: 8), so
the cap also takes the single-track headway with the margin when the line has 0 or 1 train. A lone train is never
red.

### Fix 2: run times unchanged, cap from the round trip without the single-track waits

cap = floor(T_free / H) + 1, where H is the line's shortest headway with the margin even at one train, and T_free is
the round trip minus the line's waits at single-track stretches. Same caps (A 2, B 2, C 3). But at that cap the line
still queues against itself and runs short of the headway the hover quotes, and no train is held, so the red headway
and held alert would need a new rule (red at the cap) and a new hover:

| case | hover quotes | runs at the cap | at one more |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 14m48s | 15m47s (3.80/h) | 14m48s, 0.24 held |
| B | 16m03s | 18m56s (3.17/h) | 16m57s (3.54/h) |
| C | 10m37s | 12m07s (4.95/h) | 11m05s (5.41/h) |

A headway line on B set between 16m03s and 18m56s would be accepted, not red, and not kept.

- new string for fix 2 only: "{text}, so the trains past that wait at stations to pass" (beside the existing
  "{text}, so the trains past that wait at the ends")

## Choices for Anita

1. **Which fix.** Fix 1: a line's own trains stop queueing against each other on single track; the terminal cap,
   red headway and held alert then work unchanged, and single-track lines with 2+ trains get faster round trips (B at
   2 trains 37m51s to 28m54s). Fix 2: run times stay as now; the cap is computed separately and the capped line runs
   short of the headway the hover gives (B every 18m56s against 16m03s). **Recommended: fix 1**: it is the spec's own
   rule for one line alone, and it gives the same block as terminals with no new text.
2. **Where the cap sits:** the trains that fit plus one (A 2, B 2, C 3), as at terminals, or one more (A 3, B 3,
   C 4). Under fix 1 one more only holds more trains at the ends. **Recommended: plus one.**

Counts already above the cap are lowered when a game loads (capLoad); a cap that falls during play, such as double
track rebuilt single (T-3408), leaves the count red, as T-3165 decided. Neither fix changes that.

## 5. Build

Fix 1, about 2 hours:
- sim: the single-track stretch's queueing wait takes a line's two directions as one stream (`solve_one` in
  capacity.rs); `line_held`'s limit with the margin at 0 or 1 train (`line_limits`, wasm/service.rs), so a new line
  is capped by what two trains meet. Rust tests that pin single-track figures updated, one new on case B. ~1 h
- app: none beyond `trainCap` reading the margin limit at 0-1 trains (game/world.ts, schedule.tsx, capLoad.ts). ~15 min
- a check `checks/t3526-single-cap.mjs` on B and C through the panel's control; t3165 and t3201 rerun. ~30 min
- docs: track-and-service.md 6.2 (single-track bullet, "Delay per train"), DECISIONS (the lie above), the wiki's
  line-capacity page if it states the wait. ~20 min

Fix 2, about 2.5-3 hours: `line_held` adds the free round trip and margin headway; `trainCap`, `limitsFrom`, the red
headway at the cap and its hover, `capLoad`; headway lines clamped in `auto_counts` / `trim_auto` (world.rs); the
same check and docs.
