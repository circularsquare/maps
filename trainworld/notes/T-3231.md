# T-3231: t1965-follow.mjs fails on master: two lines' trains on one place at a terminal

State 2026-11-11 (agent meet): diagnosed, two fixes tried; **not landed**, the tree is master. The
fix that clears t1965 breaks t2980's one-platform test, for a reason not yet pinned down (below). The
tried patch is `artifacts/trainworld/t3231/full-fix.diff` (against master `trainGuard.ts`).

## Cause (found)

t1965's layout: 8 km of double track, a local and an express, both ending at the same two dead ends
without a terminal, so each end has one place (`kind` 3, T-2986) shared by both lines. A diag that
dumps both trips' places, lags and positions whenever two lines stand together at an end
(scratchpad `diag1965*.mjs`, not kept) found two holes in `TrainGuard.holdForPlaces`:

1. **Held short of the crossover, then in one frame later at a fast clock.** "Standing at its last
   stop" is decided from the timetable and the lag *after* the frame's decay. At 960x a frame is ~17
   game s; a train held at the hold point has only the ~17 s from there to the platform as lag, so
   after one frame's decay (0.3 x 17 s) plus the frame its timetable says it has arrived. Counted as
   standing, it is neither held nor holding, and goes in onto the train there.
   Fix tried: a train counts as standing only if it was also drawn there last frame (`prevS` at the
   arrival point, within 2 m).
2. **Following another into the one place, right behind it.** The place counts as taken only once a
   train stands there or is leaving. The express, held behind the local all the way by the following
   guard, reaches the hold point while the local is still braking in, so nothing holds it, and the
   two stand on the one platform.
   Fix tried: every train due at a place marks it taken, and one due there too and nearer it (as
   drawn last frame) takes it first: the one behind stands at the hold point, or 30 m behind the one
   ahead's back if that is on the same track and further out; nearest-first so two held there don't
   stack.

## Results (my Vite on 6121)

| | frames |
|---|---|
| master (orchestrator, 8800) | 43 of 950 |

With both, the 4 left per run are an express 900-1200 s late: at the guard's 20-minute cap it is let
through by design. The test's express is stuck behind the local on plain double track with no passing
tracks for three game hours, so its lag climbs to the cap (T-1965's known limit).

t2980 (`ONLY_ONE=1`, T without a terminal, 3+3 trains, 60x), pairs of trains standing at T at once:

| | pairs | arrivals held | mean wait |
|---|---|---|---|
| master | 0 | 9 of 11 | 494 s |
| fix 1 only | 0 | 9 of 11 | 494 s |
| fixes 1 and 2 | 77-79 | 10 of 11 | 474 s |

With fix 2 the queue into T grows to 7-8 trips waiting (more than the six trains, so lateness is also
being carried onto next trips), lags reach the 1200 s cap and the ones let through stand on the
occupied platform. Making the `behind` cap same-track only did not change it. Not resolved:
- t2980's own count looks only at run 1 at T, but the diag showed line A's run 0 standing at T with
  the same place code (so T may be the end of one line's run 0 and the other's run 1). If so, master's
  "0 pairs" missed run-0 arrivals, and master may have kept the queue short by letting followers in
  behind (the T-3231 bug itself). Check this first.
- Whether a one-platform end that the timetable sends more trains to than one platform turns can be
  drawn at all without the cap; if not, the honest outcome is lags to the cap and the check's
  expectation needs to change (Anita's T-2986 rule: one train at a time on one platform).

The other t2980 FAILs (T's terminus dot not kind 14, nothing drawn in M's sidings at count 3+3 and
headway 5 min, "terminal: the moment came") fail on master too.
