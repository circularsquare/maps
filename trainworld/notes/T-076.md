## Measurement and outcome (2026-11-16, agent perf)

Not built: at every zoom where trains show, the fastest train on screen moves well over half a pixel a frame, so
the half-pixel rule never lowers the frame rate there. T-1640 (2026-10-28) already drops frames where it can (no
trains shown, below zoom 10: about 1 draw a second).

Setup: headless Chrome on the real GPU, 1920x1080, the real save (T-007-real-nyc.save) after its fresh demand final,
camera still over Midtown (-73.985, 40.758). CPU is every process of that Chrome (% of one core, 8-10 s windows);
GPU is the 3D engine use of its GPU process. The step is each train in view's move in one 60 Hz frame of game time,
from its timetable (profile) and line geometry, sampled every 250 ms. A frame cap (`stillWaitMs` held at 33 ms or
more) gave 20 draws a second (the timer plus the next vsync). Scripts: `T-076/t076-measure.mjs`,
`T-076/t076-probe.mjs`; rows: `T-076/measure-rows.json`.

The fastest train (about 45 m/s, a commuter line) moves 0.77 px a frame at zoom 10 and 1x, doubling with each zoom
and with the clock speed. So the half-pixel rule keeps 60 fps everywhere trains show. Dropping to 30 fps at typical
zooms (11-13, 1x) would draw the fastest train in 3-12 px steps, which is visible, so "motion looks the same" and
"cost halves" cannot both hold. A 20 fps cap would cut CPU by about 40% at zooms 11-13, so the saving exists if
smoothness can give.

Where the cost goes at z12 1x (the probe): the overlay's draw takes 2.2 ms of main-thread JS a frame, half of it the
train guard (`trainGuard.update`, 1.1 ms on this save); the rest of the main thread is mostly the browser's own
work. About 22% of a core stays at any frame rate while the clock runs (the clock worker's sim, the UI); each
frame costs about 0.35% of a core per frame a second. Note the totals are higher than this task's old 13-15%:
the save, the guard (T-1965, after T-076 was written) and the measurement (all processes) differ.

Options if wanted, none built: (a) a 30 fps cap where the fastest train moves under about 2 px a frame (zoom 10-11
at 1x), a smoothness call for Anita, saving roughly 15-25% there and nothing at 12+; (b) cut the per-frame JS: the
guard's 1.1 ms a frame on New York is the biggest single item, keeping 60 fps.
