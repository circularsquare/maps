# T-1782: trips that ride a train mostly on foot

Anita, 2026-10-29, her Tokyo save, an errand from Hodogaya at midday: by train 24 min walk, 7 min
on line 12, 30 min walk, $1.45, effective 101; by car 47; walking 91. Shares: train 7%, car 90%,
walk 3%. The train takes more trips than walking although it is slower than walking.

## Why

The walking is priced the same in both. Each minute of the walk to and from the station counts
1.5 (`w_walk` and `w_walk_far` are both 1.5), and so does each minute of walking the whole way
(`walk_trip_w` 1.5), at the same speed and detour. The train's 101 is 54 x 1.5 = 81 for the walks,
7 riding, about 1 waiting and 12 for the fare ($1.45 at 8.4 minutes a dollar in Japan). The
walk's 91 is 61 x 1.5.

The difference is how each is compared with the car, and that nothing compares the train with
walking:
- Walking against the car goes by minutes (`walk_no_rail`): the walk gets a 10 minute head start
  and every 10 minutes behind divides its odds by 2.7. Here it is 91 - 47 - 10 = 34 minutes
  behind, so 3.2%.
- The train against the car goes by how many times dearer it is (T-918's `ratio_share`,
  1 / (1 + q³)): 101 / 47 = 2.15 times, so about 9%, 7% once the drivers who park free and those
  who pay are mixed.
- The train then takes its share from walkers and drivers alike (walk = walking's share x (1 -
  train)), so it never has to beat walking.

So a trip that is mostly walking, and long enough that the car is far ahead of walking, gets the
times-dearer rule's leniency, which walking itself does not get.

## How common

`calib` (a weekday on the real network, every in-pack layer, attractions on) in a scratch copy of
the sim, counting rail trips by their cheapest walk at each end (so a lower bound on their walking;
perceived minutes, walks at 1.5):

| | New York | Tokyo |
|---|---|---|
| rail trips a weekday | 12.54M | 17.65M |
| walking 70% or more of the train's effective time | 171,000 (1.3%) | 747,000 (4.2%) |
| walking over 40 real minutes | 207,000 (1.6%) | 80,000 (0.5%) |
| either | 344,000 (2.7%) | 791,000 (4.5%) |
| the train slower than walking the whole way | 714,000 (5.6%) | 909,000 (5.1%) |

Nearly all are everyday trips (errands, visits), few commutes.

## Proposal: no train slower than walking

A trip has no train when the train's effective time is more than walking's. It is T-1059's rule
("no train that walks further than walking there") with time in place of distance, and it covers
T-1059's case too. No new number: both sides are the effective times the trip explorer already
shows, so a player can check it (train 101, walking 91: no train). The explorer would say "slower
than walking" as the reason, as it says "walks further" now.

- The Hodogaya trip: train 0%, walk 3%, car 97%.
- The ceilings (rail of rail + car, A at least 85%, B at least 50%) barely move:

| | A before | A after | B before | B after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New York | 93.6 | 93.5 | 84.0 | 83.6 |
| London | 93.9 | 93.6 | 84.8 | 84.3 |
| Paris | 94.7 | 94.5 | 86.4 | 86.0 |
| Tokyo | 94.8 | 94.7 | 86.4 | 86.1 |
| Houston | 88.8 | 88.4 | 74.2 | 73.3 |

- Rail trips a weekday: New York -5.5% (commutes -1.1%, everyday -7.6%), Tokyo -5.1%. On the real
  save, 12,758,872 would come to about 12.06M. Everyday walking in New York goes from 32.4% to 33.2%.

The catch is a step: a train 1 minute faster than walking keeps its whole share (12% here), one
1 minute slower has none. T-1059 has the same kind of step, and over a city the trips sit at every
distance from the line, so station counts move smoothly.

### The gentler alternative

A train slower than walking takes at most as many trips as walking, fewer the slower it is, at
walking's own scale (10 minutes slower, 1 rider for every 2.7 walkers). Hodogaya: train 1.2%,
walk 3.2%. Rail trips -0.4% in both cities, ceilings unchanged. Its step is smaller (12% to 3%) but
it is harder to say in one line and to check from the explorer.

Two shapes I tried and would not use: splitting everyone who leaves the car between train and
walking by minutes leaves the Hodogaya train at 3.2% against walking's 3.1% (and a train as slow as
walking still takes 2.4 times walking's trips); capping the train by walking's odds everywhere would
bring back the minute rule against the car on trips under 10 km and undo T-918.
