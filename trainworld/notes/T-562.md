# T-562: car ownership for every country

2026-10-16. The rule is in docs/demand-worldwide.md "Driving alternative", the sources in
docs/data-register.md "Car ownership". `nocar.py --plan` (log in
`../artifacts/trainworld/T-562-plan.log`) gives all 132 countries of the 515 planned rows a figure
with no stop: rows by rule, register 219, DHS 125, Eurostat 44, small-area census 11, GDP rule 116.

## The source

No open world register: WHO GHO `RS_194` is one total of all registered vehicles with
motorcycles, 2016 (Vietnam 50.7M); the World Bank's IS.VEH.PCAR.P3 (IRF) is gone from the API
(source 11 has 60 African countries for 2010, the WDI archive returns "data not found"); OICA's
vehicles-in-use pages are 404 and carried no licence. So a ladder of open multi-country sources:
DHS households possessing a car (open API), Eurostat (T-511's own source for France and Germany),
then a fit on GDP per head.

Surveys before Eurostat because the curve from registers runs low on households without a car
outside the rich world: Albania's register 328 per 1,000 x 3.7 gives 19%, its 2017 DHS 63%. Turkey's
DHS is 2013 (63%) against Eurostat 2025 (50%), hence "2015 on" first, 2010 only after Eurostat.

## The GDP rule (`t562_fit.py`)

logit(households with a car) = -15.10 + 1.502 ln(GDP per head, PPP), 100 countries (every country
with a figure above but Hong Kong and Singapore, plus the US from ACS households). rmse 0.142;
by GDP a head: under $5,000 0.047 (28 countries), $5-15k 0.136 (22), $15-40k 0.252 (19), over $40k
0.104 (31). Worst Kosovo -0.51 (Eurostat's 6.0 people a household). Income group medians: high
0.172, upper middle 0.63, lower middle 0.916, low 0.971. With Hong Kong in, it is the worst
(+0.66) and pulls the fit, hence the city states typed in.
