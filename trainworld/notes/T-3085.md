## 1. Flyover junctions today

A junction is a node with 3+ edge ends. Two moves through it conflict when their paths cross at one level (track
positions in opposite order on the two sides, `capacity.rs` `track_pos`: a double edge's tracks at rank x 2 and
rank x 2 + 1 by running side, a single edge at rank x 2 + 0.5); merges and diverges do not. Each crossing move is
held 90 s per train, Kingman delay on top (track-and-service.md 6.2). A plain double-track Y has one crossing pair:
the move that crosses the opposite direction's track (inbound when the branch is on the running side, outbound
otherwise). In the model a merge never waits, so "faster merges" means removing that 90 s hold and its queue.

A flyover today is a node flag, `NodeData::flying` (T-080, from the junction inspector): capacity skips every move
at it, `junction_cost` adds 0.6 km one level away, nothing is drawn. `add_route`/`preview_route` already take
`flying: bool` (`draw.rs` ~289 sets the flag on the junctions a route makes); the app passes `false`. The flat
badge ("Flat junction", `gradeMarks.ts` `M_JUNCTION`) is drawn on every non-flying node with 3+ ends, whether or
not any of its moves can cross.

## 2. Real flyovers (T-3092)

**Shape.** With the ramp switch on, a route that makes a new junction J1 on double track (a split, or a plain
two-end node) and is itself double, regular track gets a flying junction laid as real track. In the main's frame
at J1 (x along the main toward the side the branch leaves, branch side +y):
- **J3**: a new node on the drawn route 300 m out (the first point at least 300 m out clear of any station's
  platform). From J3 on, the route is the double-track branch as drawn.
- **Plain leg** J1 to J3: the drawn route's first 300 m, single track, one way in the direction of the move that
  crosses nothing.
- **Crossing leg** J3 to J2: single track, one way in the crossing move's direction. It leaves J3 back toward J1
  12 m (`BESIDE_SPACING`) on the main's side of the plain leg, crosses the main behind J1 at a skew (about x =
  -100 m), lands 12 m past the main's far side and runs beside it to **J2**, a new split on the main at about
  x = -450 m, joining along the main's heading. Corners at auto radius. The leg then goes through the same
  ramp-over pass as any route (`bridge::ramp_over` via `shaped`): a level up over the main (and anything else it
  crosses), or down from -1, 200 m ramps (`RAMP_PER_LEVEL`, 4%), 14.5 m clear each side, 25 m clear of its ends.
  J3 to the crossing is about 400 m, the crossing to J2 about 350 m, so both ramps fit with room to spare.
- Footprint about 750 m along the main, near a real flying junction's. Left-hand running mirrors it (`right_hand`).

**Blocking.** Real track, so every existing rule applies: at the crossing it sits a level from the main; track
already at that level there makes the ramp-over skip that way ("would make a flat crossing at the new level"), and
the other way is tried. Track drawn later at the flyover's level meets it as any track: ramps over or under it
with the switch on, else a flat crossing (a badge, a problem). A ramp crossing other track less than a level apart
is refused as always (`CrossKind::Bad`).

**No room: fall back to a flat junction**, as ramp-over falls back to a flat crossing. The whole route still goes
in, double track to J1, flat as today; the ghost shows the flat badge, and the drawing tag says why. Refusing would
make ramps-on (the default) block ordinary branches. No room means any of: the route shorter than 350 m, J2's
split refused (a station's platform, a junction, a crossover or a track end within the 450 m behind J1, the main on
a ramp there), the crossing leg's ramps failing both ways, or any of the edit's checks failing. A branch drawn from
a station (`branch_end`, J1 20 m past the platform) mostly falls back, since J2 would land in the platform. A
route whose two ends both make junctions gets a flyover at each end that fits.

**Not in the first cut:** quad (each pair ramps on its own already; flyovers for its end junctions later),
high-speed (always double; would need high-speed single track), tram, single-track routes and splits of single
track (no crossing pair to remove).

**Routing and capacity.** Nothing new beyond T-3094. The one-way flags send each direction over its own leg, so the
crossing move no longer exists. At J1, J2 and J3 every move is a merge or a diverge (the single leg sits outside the
double track's positions; T-3092's first test checks this by `track_pos`), so no conflict resource is made; the legs are one-way single-track sections under the
per-direction rule (25 trains an hour at 160 km/h). Lines already on the main are untouched; J1 and J2 split the
main's edge as any branch's split does.

**Cost.** Real track priced as real track: no `FLYOVER_KM` term. At grade, roughly: crossing leg 750 m single
(about 450 m at +1 counting ramps at the dearer end: US$20M; 300 m at grade: US$7M), plain leg 300 m single
(US$7M) in place of 300 m of double (US$11M), and two more junctions (US$19M): about US$40M more than flat, close
to the old flag's US$45M. Whether J3 (a double track splitting into its two tracks) is priced as a junction is
the agent's call; not pricing it saves US$9M.

**Map.** The legs draw as single track with the usual ramp triangles and T-3094's one-way arrows. No flyover badge.
T-3103 is needed first: the flat badge only on junctions where two moves can cross by `track_pos` (a per-node bit
from the sim in the node rows), else J1, J2 and J3 would each show "Flat junction". It also fixes today's badge on
merge-only junctions.

**Editing.** The legs are ordinary pieces: reshaped, deleted, constructed like any track (no unit). Undo takes the
whole route. Saves need nothing new (edges, nodes and T-3094's flag). The `flying` argument of `add_route` and
`preview_route` keeps its place and changes meaning to "lay real flyovers"; the app passes `ramp`. The preview
returns the crossing leg as an extra polyline, as it does quad's express pair (`route_express`, `draw.rs` ~615).

**The inspector's flag flyover** (Construct / Add a flyover on an existing junction) stays for now as the only fix
for a junction already built; laying real legs from the inspector could follow (not filed).

## 3. One-way track (T-3094)

Edge flag `oneway: 0 both, 1 a to b, 2 b to a`, single track only (Anita). Routing: both Dijkstras in `net.rs`
(`find_path_turn` ~2669, the one at ~2812) skip states against it; `check_line` refuses a path entry against it.
Capacity: a one-way single-track section uses double track's per-direction rule (`capacity.rs` ~489) in place of
run time + 60 s. Track inspector: a switch and a reverse button; arrows on the track. Lines running the other way
are re-routed through their stops in the same edit, listed in a confirmation; a line with no other way refuses the
edit (refusing every time would trap the player, since routing keeps using a two-way leg both ways). Saves: edge
bits 11-12, the decoder's unknown check widened to `tb >> 13`. Splits keep the flag; pieces join only alike.

## 4. Single track (T-3093)

In the sim since the start (0.6x, sections between stations, one shared platform) but hidden from Build since
T-733. Back in the Tracks row beside double and quad; not on high-speed or tram. T-1967 (opposing trains drawn
through each other) and T-2234 (passing loops between stations) stay P3.

## How other games do it

None builds a flyover for you; all model individual tracks, made one way by signals, and the player builds
flyovers by hand with elevation, which is what T-3092 automates.
- NIMBY Rails: individual tracks with elevation; one-way signals restrict direction:
  https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/Path_signal_setup
- OpenTTD: one-way path signals ("trains cannot pass from the back side"); flyovers built from bridges, catalogued
  in the Junctionary: https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Manual/Signals,
  https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Community/Junctionary/High%20Speed%204-way
- Transport Fever 2: one-way signals, flyovers by hand (a "Flying junction" mod exists):
  https://steamcommunity.com/app/1066780/discussions/0/5153834725008443057
- Subway Builder: tracks cross at grade within 0.1 m or need 4 m clearance; v0.11 added direction arrows on
  track: https://wiki.subwaybuilder.com/index.php/Construction, https://www.subwaybuilder.com/changelog
- Flying junction, real railways: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_junction
