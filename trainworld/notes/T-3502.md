# T-3502: big airports draw ground trips from farther away

Agent airfar, 2026-11-21. Issue 6 (Suzhou to Shanghai's airports, Seoul to Incheon); Anita
2026-11-17: "sure we can do big airports drawing ground trips from far away, not super high prio tho."

## The rule

An airport's O&D passengers a year over 10M, kept between 1 and 3 (m), multiply its decay (20 km)
and its reach (80 km); the reach is at most 150 km and fades over its last 40 km, as the 40-80 km
fade did. So 10M and under: today's 20 km and 40-80 km, unchanged bit for bit; 15M: 30 km, fade
80-120 km; 20M: 40 km, 110-150 km; 30M and over: 60 km, 110-150 km.

- Sim (`everyday.rs` `air_far`, `air_reach_m`, `fade_to`, `airport_multipliers`): an airport's
  size is its terminal cells' `airport_trips` (cells within 4 km of one another are one airport)
  plus the outings of its people outside the pack (`airport.sites[].outside_outings`, nearest
  terminal within 4 km), x 732 (two passengers an outing, 366 days). Each airport zone draws at its
  own decay (one decay table per length, `cdf_each`) and its trips fade at its own reach, both in
  the sampling and in the pair weight. Every other purpose keeps `DecayTable` and `local_share`
  untouched. `cdf_each` repeats `DecayTable::cdf`'s indexing: a change to the table's layout
  changes both.
- A pack without sites (built before T-2896) sizes a hub by its in-pack outings only: Stansted in
  `lon` counts 18.5M and gets x1.85 (real 29M).
- Offline, for the next rebuild: tier0's `airports` step weighs the pack share with the same decay,
  cut at the reach (GHSL pad = the reach; a big airport only, as `cross.rs` sums it, fading the
  trips after) and writes each site's `decay_m` and `reach_m`; `cross.rs`'s airport draw reads them
  (a site without them keeps 20 km and 80 km, so an old header's `outside_weight` still matches its
  kernel). Compiled and unit-tested only; never run on packs.
- No new data.

## Where it comes from (CAA 2023)

CAA Passenger Survey 2023, tables 4.1 (terminating scheduled passengers by planning region) and 5.x
(by county within the airport's own region):
https://caa.co.uk/media/k01hswm4/t04_2023.pdf, https://caa.co.uk/media/p0hj12jf/t05_2023.pdf
(2019's table 4.1 for comparison: https://www.caa.co.uk/media/tjvlhaq3/t04_2019.pdf). They give
counties and regions, not distances, so the 80-150 km share is my allocation of each county or
region by where its people live (main towns' straight-line distance from the airport):

| Airport | Total (000s) | Greater London | 80-150 km, allocated | Past 150 km |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heathrow | 57,598 | 52.0% | ~13% | ~15% |
| Gatwick | 33,688 | 42.3% | ~16% | ~9% |
| Stansted | 26,033 | 47.3% | ~17% | ~4.5% |
| Manchester | 24,743 | - | ~15% | ~3.5% |
| Birmingham | 9,550 | - | ~10% | small |

Heathrow's allocation (share of all its terminating passengers): Oxfordshire 2.8% x 35% (Banbury,
Bicester, Witney past 80 km), Hampshire 4.0% x 55% (Southampton, Portsmouth, Winchester), Isle of
Wight 0.14%, West Sussex 1.5% x 35%, East Sussex 0.9% x 55%, Kent 1.9% x 45% (Canterbury, Ashford,
Thanet, Dover), East of England 8.2% x 45% (Essex past Chelmsford, Cambridgeshire, Suffolk), South
West 7.1% x 35% (Swindon, Wiltshire, Dorset), East Midlands 3.3% x 30% (Northamptonshire), West
Midlands 3.1% x 20% (Coventry, Warwickshire); Greater London, Berkshire, Buckinghamshire and Surrey
all under 80 km. Gatwick, Stansted and Manchester the same way (Manchester: Lancaster and south
Cumbria, York, Harrogate and Hull, Birmingham and the Black Country, Nottingham and Leicester, north
Wales). Each figure is good to a few points; the done-when's factor of 1.5 is wider.

Counts, not shares of the near trips: the game's airport total is all O&D passengers, so its
trips from past 150 km (none exist) land nearer. Its 80-150 km count is total x its 80-150 share,
the published count total x the published share of all; so the game's share is compared with the
published share of all passengers.

Nothing usable found outside the UK: Incheon (Korean searches: nationality shares only), Pudong
(Chinese searches: none), Frankfurt (Statista's distance table refused, 403), Logan (Massport's
2016 ground-access survey exists; its environmental data report is over 10 MB, not read).

## Fitting (GHSL people x the kernel, 1 km blocks to 160 km)

Scratch scripts `catch.py`, `fit.py`, `london.py` (in the agent's scratchpad, not kept). Residents
only; the game's ends are half residents, a quarter jobs and a quarter leisure jobs.

80-150 km share of the weight (today's rule gives 0% everywhere):

| Rule | Heathrow | Gatwick | Stansted | Manchester | Birmingham |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| published (allocated) | ~13% | ~16% | ~17% | ~15% | ~10% |
| decay 40 km, fade 75-150 | 5.0% | 5.6% | 7.8% | 9.8% | 11.6% |
| decay 60 km, fade 120-150 | 12.0% | 11.7% | 16.2% | 19.7% | 30.7% |
| chosen (10M to 30M, x1 to x3) | 11.2% (x1.16) | 11.1% (x1.44) | 14.9% (x1.14) | 18.1% (x0.83) | 0.1% |

A decay that reaches 80-150 km with the old fade (the reach scaled with the decay, fade from half
the reach) moves the near trips out before any arrive past 80 km: the far share needs a long decay
and a late fade together. Greater London's share (a 22.4 km circle round Charing Cross, 9.3M GHSL
people): Heathrow today 70.9%, new 52.0% (CAA 52.0%); Gatwick 59.2%, new 49.8% (42.3%); Stansted
49.4%, new 46.4% (47.3%).

Other thresholds tried: 5M with the square root (fits Birmingham, changes every mid-size airport),
10M with the square root (Manchester x1.8, Stansted x2.4), 10M to the power 0.7 (Gatwick x1.54).

The chosen rule for the Asian and other hubs (no published figure): Pudong 17% from 80-150 km,
Hongqiao 11%, Incheon 7.6% (and 57% from 40-80 km, Seoul's band, against 34% today), Frankfurt
(O&D 0.5) 22%, JFK 7.4%.

Why it may not carry over: all four are UK airports, with few airports for their people; the
10-30M ramp is judgement (Birmingham, 12M, the only one in it, gets nothing past 80 km against a
published ~10%).

## What the far trips need (not done here)

`sha`, `seo` and `lon` were built before T-2896 and have no `airport.sites`, so their airports send
nothing across a zone line; even a pack with sites holds `outside_outings` weighed at 20 km. The
smallest way: at the next rebuild, rerun tier0's `airports` step for the packs holding an airport over
10M O&D passengers (it now pads GHSL to the reach and writes `decay_m`, `reach_m`), then
`cross_everyday` and `market_zones` for their units (Shanghai and Suzhou share a unit: one country, 50
km apart). The same rerun T-3485's cause 2 (border factor in the pack share) needs, so the two go
together. The hand-built US and Ontario packs (`enrich_airports.py`) send none across a line and are
not changed.
