# T-736: why the money graph stepped, and when money lands now

## Why it stepped, in plain words

Demand is solved for five periods (morning peak 06-10, midday 10-16, evening peak 16-20, evening
20-24, night 00-06) and two kinds of day (weekday, weekend). Money was booked straight from those
tables:

- **Fares**: each hour of a period was paid the period's fares divided by its hours. So 10:00 to
  15:00 earned exactly the same each hour, and 16:00 jumped to the evening peak's figure and stayed
  there until 20:00. The boarding account (the riders on platforms and trains) already spreads a
  period's riders over it with a peak in the middle (`sim/src/track/boarding.rs`, `SHAPE`); the
  money ignored that shape (this is T-294's point).
- **Running costs**: the timetable has three service levels (high, medium, low) tied to the same
  periods, and each hour was charged its level's car-km. So costs stepped at the same hours as
  fares, which is why the two lines moved in lockstep: both were keyed to the period, not to
  anything that varies inside it. Costs did not depend on demand, only on the timetable that
  changes at the same boundaries.
- **Weekday to weekend**: the hour's kind of day was the kind at its start, so Friday 23:00 earned a
  weekday hour and Saturday 00:00 a weekend hour, with nothing in between.
- **The day chart** steps between weekdays and weekend days because a weekend day really has
  different (fewer) riders; that is demand, not booking. With the change below a little of Friday
  night's fares lands on Saturday, so the edge softens slightly, but the days still differ.

Fares do follow riders closely, and should: every fare is a rider times the fare curve. What
looked wrong was the identical flat stretches and the shared cliffs.

## What changed (`app/src/game/moneyTiming.ts`, used by `workers/clock.worker.ts`)

- **Fares, rides and points per hour follow the boarding account's arrival shape**: the period's
  total spread over its hours by the same tent, falling or valley shape and busiest-hour factor the
  account uses (the factors come with the riders message, per kind of day).
- **Fares and points land when the ride ends**, rides count when they start: fares are spread over
  a trailing window as long as a typical one-way run of the network (each running line's one-way
  run at the medium level, weighted by car-km, at most 2 hours), so a
  ride boarded at 15:50 pays partly in the 16:00 hour, and a Friday 23:40 ride pays on Saturday.
- **Running costs follow trains running**: the timetable's car-km per hour is spread over the same
  window, since a train that leaves at 09:55 on the peak timetable keeps running into 10:00.
  Running costs do not use the demand shape.
- Totals: over any whole number of days with the same tables, fares, rides and running costs are
  unchanged (the window only moves money between neighbouring hours; `checks/t736-money-timing.mjs`
  checks a week to 1e-9). A single day can gain or lose the few minutes' worth that crosses
  midnight.
- Within an hour, fares still land as the hour's trains pick up riders (T-284's `hourShape`).
- The per-line fares taken today (`lineDaysAt`), region ledgers and the load replay of running costs
  use the same functions, so regions still add up to the network.

Not changed: the demand model, period tables, fare levels.

## Checks

- `checks/t736-money-timing.mjs`: a week of hours keeps fares, rides and running costs to 1e-9 at
  lags of 0 to 90 minutes. On a test table the largest hour-to-hour change in fares falls from 920
  to 620-800 (what remains is the rush-hour ramp inside the peak, which the riders have too) and in
  costs from 240 to 140-190.
- Real save (T-007-real-nyc.save), clock jumped to Saturday 20:00, Money tab, hours view, in
  `artifacts/trainworld/`: `t736-before-hours-jump.png` (flat runs, cliffs at 06, 10, 16, 20 and
  midnight, fares and costs stepping together) against `t736-after-hours-jump.png` (each period
  rises and falls, peaks at 08 and 18, the costs ramp over the window). Also `-hours-speed0`,
  `-hours-speed2` (after playing at the first and fastest speeds) and `-days`, `-days-after-run` for
  each. Friday's fares read $2.77B both ways; Saturday's running cost so far $742M before, $734M
  after (the evening peak's last trains now run, and cost, past 20:00, more than Friday night's
  moved into Saturday).
- The day chart still steps from weekday ($2.77B fares) to weekend ($1.85B): that is the weekend's
  demand, not timing.

Seen on the way: after importing the save, fares stayed at $0 for about 40 wall seconds of play
(the income table reaching the worker after the load); a clock jump made in that time books those
hours with no fares. Cause and fix in notes/T-742.md (the previous game's hold released the clock).
