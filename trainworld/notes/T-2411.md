# T-2411 Profit by stretch of track: a Profit map mode (design, agent profit, 2026-11-04)

Anita: "is there a way we can reasonably say like which stretch of track is profitable? would be cool to have a
"profitability" map mode lol."

## Recommendation

Yes, and it adds up exactly. A fifth track display, **Profit**, beside Line / Traffic / Level / Speed, colours each
stretch of track by its profit per km per day on the day shown, red for a loss, grey about even, blue for a profit.

**The rule, in two lines:** a stretch earns the fares for the km riders ride on it plus a share of the base fares
(half by those km, half by riders boarding at its two stations); it pays its trains' running cost by their km on it and
its upkeep (track by the trains a day of the lines on it, each station half to the stretch on each side).

Construction and trains bought stay out, as in the Cities tab's profit. No WASM change; about 350 lines of TypeScript.
On the real save it shows something no panel does: New Haven, Harlem and the NEC earn money as lines but lose it on most
of their km (the outer ends), and the loss is the trains' running cost, not the track.

**Built as T-2420 stage 1 (rail), tree .tw-trees/profit:** `game/stretchMoney.ts`, the Profit button, the stroke
shader's `PROFIT_STOPS` ramp by edge id (`u_profit`), the hover rows; `checks/t2411-profit-map.mjs` (each line's
edges sum to its weekday fares to 4e-16 and its upkeep share to $0; stretches $2,171.4M = the network). Decided:
the boarding half, blue, no key. **Later stage, not filed yet:** bus and ferry lines by the same rule per
stop-to-stop stretch of their route (upkeep per road km, ferries none), drawn as Traffic draws them (T-2274);
until then they draw as in the other views and their share of a station's upkeep is off the track.

## 1. The rule, and that it adds up

Per running line, then summed over the lines on each piece of track (`edge`):

| Part | Goes to the stretch by | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Per-km fares (rate x paid passenger-km, times the high-speed or ferry multiplier; demand's bent-curve extra `fareX`) | passenger-km on the stretch, both directions, each at its starting stop's fare scale | exactly how `incomeFor` charges it |
| The line's base fares (its T-2104 share, `baseTrips`) | half by passenger-km, half by boardings at the stops at its ends (a through stop half each side, an end stop all to its one stretch) | mirrors T-2104 inside the line: a busy station's base goes beside it |
| Running trains (`runningToday`'s train-km cost) | km of the line's path: every km carries the same trains | a line's trains run end to end |
| Track upkeep (`edgeUpkeep`) | the lines on the edge by trains a day, as `line_upkeep` (money.rs) already splits it | unchanged rule |
| Station upkeep | the lines stopping by trains a day, then half to each stretch beside it on that line | same, placed on track |
| Track no running line uses | its own upkeep, alone | shows red: unused track costs money |

**A stretch** (what the colour and hover cover) is the track between two stations or junctions, the colour its total
over its km, so one stretch is one colour. Upkeep alone varies along it (a tunnel costs more per km).

**Checked on the real save** (`analyze.py`, weekday demand, 109 lines, 1,647 edges):
- each line's stretch fares sum to its `incomeByDay` fares to 4e-16; its stretch upkeep to `line_upkeep` to $0; the
  path lengths to `lengthM` to 0 m. Network: fares $3,718M, running trains $1,309M, upkeep $237M, profit $2,171M a
  weekday, and the stretches sum to the same $2,171M.
- **The panels differ, by the day, not the rule.** The line panel's "Expected profit today" counts the past 24 h of the
  boarding account; the map counts the day shown, as Traffic's riders do. The save loads at 07:00 on a Monday, so its past
  24 h is mostly Sunday: panels' fares $3,068M against Sunday's $2,970M and a weekday's $3,718M (running and upkeep
  agree to the dollar). On a settled day of the same kind the two agree, as the worker pays each hour by `incomeByDay`.
- On the average day the rule runs on the average view's loads; the base share's cap is not linear, so its lines may
  differ slightly from 5/7 weekday + Saturday + Sunday (not measured).
- **Variant considered:** the whole base by passenger-km. 73 of the 1,647 edges change sign (terminal stubs mostly:
  with the boarding half, a terminus where many board keeps its stretch's base). I chose the boarding half for T-2104's
  sake; the other is one line simpler to explain.
- **What it cannot say:** what removing a stretch would do. A tail's riders pay their per-km fares on the core too, so
  cutting a red tail also loses blue fares downtown (Steve Munro: route profit figures give "meaningless numbers and bad
  policy decisions" if read that way). A later "what if" would need a demand solve without it; not proposed now.

## 2. Where it sits

- **Track display row**: a fifth button, `profit` in code. Like Level and Speed: uniform width, no line strokes, trains
  keep their line colours, blueprint middles mixed from the view's colour.
- **Colours** (continuous between stops in the stroke shader, as `SPEED_STOPS`): per km per day, −$300k #b2182b,
  −$100k #e0603f, −$20k #f4a582, 0 #bdbab3 (dark theme #6c747e), +$1M #92c5de, +$4M #4393c3, beyond #2166ac.
  Blue for profit, not green, so red-green colour blindness still reads it. Fixed money, not scaled by country: in a
  low-fare country most track reads red, which is true of its cash.
- **Hover** (track tag, every row the game's own tag): the stretch's profit on its colour chip, per km, then the three
  parts as the line panel's profit hover names them. Bare track: "No trains run here" and its upkeep.
- **Key**: none, as T-1499 decided for Level and Speed; the hover gives the numbers. Strings for one are below if wanted.
- **Buses and ferries** (none on the real save): the same rule per stop-to-stop stretch of the route, upkeep per road
  km (ferries none), drawn as Traffic draws them (T-2274). A second stage.

## 3. Mockup and what a player learns

`C:\Users\anita\projects\maps\artifacts\trainworld\t2411\profit-per-km.png` (the proposal, 7 bins standing in for the
continuous ramp); `fares-over-costs.png` beside it colours by fares over costs instead (farebox recovery), for
comparison. The real save's edges are straight station to station, so express edges (Harlem-125 St to Stamford, 46 km)
cut across. Scripts: `extract.mjs` (page dump), `analyze.py` (rule and checks), `draw.py`, same folder.

- **Downtown earns the most:** the L under 14 St, the 7 under 42 St, the 1 and 2 between Penn Station and Times Sq,
  $8-13M per km per day. The median stretch makes +$560k per km.
- **Losses are the outer ends:** 418 of 1,647 stretches, 1,944 of 4,499 km. Commuter tails: Harlem Line past Southeast
  (fares $0.11M, running trains $8.05M a day), the Montauk Branch east of Speonk, Port Jervis, the Waterbury branch, the
  Hudson Line past Croton-Harmon, the NEC from Princeton Jct to New Brunswick. Subway stubs: the 1 past 238 St, the 4 to
  Woodlawn, the 2 to Nereid Av, both ends of the Staten Island Railway (−$400k to −$500k per km).
- **The cause is trains, not track:** on the median losing stretch fares cover 71% of its running trains; upkeep is a
  quarter of its costs. The lever is fewer trains past the busy part (a line reversing partway, T-797) or a shorter line.
- **Profitable lines hide losing km:** New Haven +$24M a day with about 200 of its 242 km losing, Harlem +$28M with 147
  of 214, the NEC +$69M with 96 of 211. Lines losing overall: Montauk Branch −$29M, Port Jervis (MNBNP) −$14M, MNEG −$12M, Waterbury −$10M, New Canaan −$8M, Staten Island Railway −$7M, Danbury −$7M, Greenport −$6M.

## 5. Looking outward

No game found colours profit by stretch; they stop at the line or vehicle, and agencies at the route.
- OpenTTD: profit this year and last per vehicle; on transfers a feeder gets a "feeder share" credit, so a final leg can
  show negative income. The same allocation problem as ours, solved per vehicle.
  https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Manual/Negative%20income%20with%20feeder%20service ,
  https://wiki.openttd.org/en/Manual/Game%20Mechanics/Cargo%20income
- NIMBY Rails: base ticket per boarding plus a per-km price, accounting per line. https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/Line
- Cities: Skylines: the transport info view lists passengers per line. https://gameplay.tips/guides/6412-cities-skylines.html
- TTC route costing: revenue once by passenger-km, later by boardings; "short routes always make a profit" under
  boardings; costs by vehicle hours. https://stevemunro.ca/2014/01/30/allocating-transit-costs-and-revenues/
- Houston METRO: route farebox recovery, revenue by ridership and route average fare, costs by a nine-factor model.
  https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/83862 ; fewer than 1% of routes cover fully allocated costs:
  https://escholarship.org/content/qt5cc7395h/qt5cc7395h.pdf ; farebox recovery as Sound Transit defines it:
  https://www.soundtransit.org/sites/default/files/2016-fare-revenue-report.pdf
- Network Rail's Variable Usage Charge bills track wear per vehicle-mile, as our upkeep split by trains does.
  https://www.orr.gov.uk/access-charging-framework-use-network-rail-infrastructure-user-guide/variable-usage-charge-vuc
