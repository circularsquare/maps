# T-1168: a line diagram with related lines (design)

## For Anita

A button in the line panel's Stops heading turns the stops strip into a diagram of the line with
the lines related to it. The selected line runs straight down; lines that run beside it get their
own coloured column to the left or right, turning out to the side with their bullet where they leave;
stops are white dots that stretch into one oblong over every column stopping there, so a stop the
selected line skips shows as a dot on the other line's column only; lines that only cross are thin
horizontal bars through the row. Nothing is built yet.

## What the panel shows today

`ui/inspector/LineInspector.tsx`, `stopsBlock`; [docs/ui-inspectors.md](../docs/ui-inspectors.md)
"Lines tab and the line inspector". The Stops section (after Past 24 hours; at the top while stops
are edited) has the heading "Stops" with "boardings in past 24 h" on the right, then a `ul.stops`:
one 25 px row per stop, a 6 px bar in the line's colour down the left drawn by CSS (`.stops
li::before`), a white dot with a dark rim per stop (10 px at a transfer stop), then the name
(click: select the station), its time from the start (hover: speed and what limits it), a
reversal icon where trains reverse, the two busiest other lines at the complex as bullets with a
"+" for the rest, and the stop's boardings. Flat junctions and flat crossings on the route are
small marks on the bar between two stops (`lineMarks`, T-629). Nothing shows the other lines
except those bullets.

## The toggle

An icon button right after "Stops" in the heading: three strokes splitting from one, like the
mockups' header. Hover "Show related lines", and "Hide related lines" when on (`btn icon mini`,
`.on` tinted as other toggles). One setting for every line, off at the start and kept while the
page is open, as the Lines tab's scope toggle is (a `relatedLines` signal in `game/ui.ts`). Hidden
while stops are being edited: the editor keeps the plain list with its remove buttons.

## Classifying the other lines

Everything is measured along the selected line L's route (its path of track edges in order, with
distance s from the first stop) and along each other line O's route.

**Meetings.** O meets L where
1. they share a track edge (a run of shared edges is one meeting with a start and an end on each
   line), or
2. a station of O's route and a station of L's route are in one complex (`game/complexes.ts`:
   platforms within 50 m, centres within 100 m, or linked by the player). Stations passed count,
   not only stops, so an express passing a local's station meets the local there.

Flat crossings and flat junctions stay the existing marks on the spine; a line crossing over or
under with no station is not related.

**Groups.** Take O's meetings in O's order. Two consecutive meetings belong together when
- the order along L does not turn back (O does not go down L and then up it again), and
- O gets from one to the next on shared track, or O's track between them strays from L's track
  between them by at most max(100 m, 5% of O's run between them). The stray is the farthest point
  of O between the meetings from the nearest point of L between the same meetings, sampled at O's
  vertices and every 200 m.

**Kinds.** A group with shared track, or with two or more stations, is a line *beside* L over
that span; a group that is one station is a *crossing*. So:

| Anita's case | How it comes out |
|---|---|
| interlined with it | shared track, beside, branching off at each end it goes on past |
| branching off where they leave | the end of a group O goes on past turns out to its side |
| parallel, own track, many shared stations | consecutive shared stations with O's track close by: beside |
| crossing once | a lone shared station: crossing |
| meeting twice far apart | one gap: within 5% it is an express (beside the whole way, no dots in between), over 5% two crossings or two separate side spans |

A line can be beside L in two places with a gap between (the 5 beside the 2 in Brooklyn and the
Bronx, Manhattan between): two side spans, the first turning out where it leaves, the second
turning in where it comes back, and the hovers name each other.

**Why 5% and 100 m.** On the real save every gap between consecutive meetings was measured
(`notes/t1168/gaps.py`, 2458 gaps): 80% stray under 2.5% (locals and expresses on neighbouring
tracks), then a long tail. The cases that should stay separate sit above 5%: the B and D against
the 4 (Sixth Avenue against Lexington, 1.6 km off over 20 km, 8%), the 2 and 5 through Manhattan
(2.0 km over 18 km, 11%), the E and M (7%). Below 5% are the N and R against the 4 from Atlantic
Av to Union Sq (3 to 4%), which read fine as express routes beside it. The save's track is straight
stop-to-stop chords (every imported line has its own private track and stations), which
understates strays; real curved track only pushes borderline cases towards separate. The 100 m
floor keeps short gaps between neighbouring stations together whatever the ratio.

**Also left out:** a side span drawn over fewer than three rows is shown as crossings at its rows
(the J and Z beside the 6X for Chambers St and Canal St only).

## Layout

Not to scale, as today: rows, not distance.

**Rows.** L's stops; stations L passes where a drawn side line stops; and each side line's own
stops inside its span, placed at the nearest point of L's track (the 1's local stops between
the 2's express stops). Ordered along L. A row nobody drawn stops at is left out. L's own rows keep
everything they have today (time, reversal icon, boardings, name click); a row only a side line
stops at has its name in `--muted` and nothing else.

**Columns.** L is the spine, 6 px wide, in the middle of the drawing. Side lines take columns
10 px apart:
- a line that stops more often than L over its span (a local beside an express) goes right, one
  that stops as often or less (a sibling, an express) goes left;
- the more stops it shares with L, the nearer the spine; it takes the innermost column on its side
  free over its span plus half a row at each turn, so lines beside L in different places share a
  column; the other side when its own is full;
- at most 3 columns a side, and only as many as fit in 40% of the panel's width (2 a side at the
  narrowest dock). A side line with no room is drawn as crossings at its shared stations.

**Turns.** Where a side line goes on past its first or last meeting it turns out half a row beyond
that row, a rounded corner then straight out to the gutter on its side, ending at its bullet (16 px,
`Chip size="sm"`). A turn crossing an outer column is cased in `--bg` so it reads as passing over.
Lines turning at the same gap fan out 7 px apart, outermost first, so a bundle leaving together
(the three Lexington locals leaving the 4 at 125 St) never crosses itself; their bullets stack 17
px apart down the gutter. A side line that ends at a shared station just ends in the dot. A column
with no turn gets its bullet in its gutter level with its first row.

**Dots.** Per row, a white pill with the dark rim (`--net-ink`, as today) over each run of
neighbouring columns that stop there, the spine included: one oblong where L and the lines beside
it all stop, a round dot on one column where only that line stops, and two separate dots where a
column between them passes. 8 px tall, 10 px at a row with a crossing (today's transfer size).

**Crossings.** The other lines at a row's complex not drawn as a column there keep today's
bullets after the name (the busiest two by boardings, "+" with the rest). The first two also get a
3 px bar in their colour right across the drawing, behind the columns and dots (two bars 6 px
apart when there are two).

**Interaction.** A click on a side column or a bullet selects that line, so its own diagram opens;
a click on a name selects the station as today.

**Hovers** (every drawn thing; the game's tag, `data-tip` on the SVG shapes):
- a side column: "1 runs beside 2 from Chambers St to 96 St", then "6 stops in common", "12
  stops of its own", then "On the same track" for shared track; for each stretch it runs on its
  own track between two meetings: "8.9 km on its own track beside 8.4 km of 4" and "Up to 271 m
  from 4's track";
- a dot or oblong: "6, 6 and 6X stop at 23 St", then "4 and 5 pass";
- a turn's bullet: "4 goes on to Woodlawn", "14 more stops"; for a line that comes back, "5
  leaves here", "Back beside 2 at 149 St-Grand Concourse after 10 stops of its own" (and "5 joins
  here", "It left 2 at Borough Hall/Court St 10 stops back" at the other end);
- a crossing bar: "4 crosses at 149 St-Grand Concourse"; when the same line met L before and the
  gap was too wide to count as beside, also "It left 2 at Borough Hall/Court St 17.7 km back" and
  "Its track strays up to 2.0 km from 2's in between".

**Themes.** Line colours never change; dots stay white with the dark rim in both themes as they
are now; casings use `--bg`; muted names `--muted`. The mockups show both.

**Loops** (T-798): the spine is the stop list as today (first stop again at the end); a side span
crossing the seam splits into two.

## What is computed where, and the cost

**Clock worker, in Rust** (the track model has every line's route: `svc.lines[l].runs[0]` with its
nodes, distances, edges with direction and stop indices, and the edge geometry), as a new
`TrackApi.line_related(line, complex_of)` beside `line_marks`, asked through a clock query
`lineRelated` only while the toggle is on and the panel is open, again on each world version. The
clock worker builds the node to complex array with `buildComplexes` over all stations (blueprints
too, unlike the constructed-only one it builds for demand), cached by world version. Out, flat
numbers per group: the other line, kind, shared-track flag, its span on both lines, its meetings
(complex, distance on each line, who stops), each gap's stray, run and L distance, and the other
line's own stops inside the span with their distance along L.

**Main thread, in TS**: rows, columns, packing, turns, pills and hover texts in a pure
`game/relatedLayout.ts` (testable in Node), drawn by `ui/inspector/RelatedStops.tsx` as one SVG
beside the existing stop rows. The SVG reads each row's `offsetTop` after layout rather than
assuming 25 px.

**Cost.** The prototype (`notes/t1168/relate.py`, Python) classifies one line against all others in
21 ms on average on the real save (109 lines, 1756 stations; 112 ms at worst), and 15 ms on average
on the Japan save (`artifacts/trainworld/t737-japan.save`, 709 lines, 10,320 stations), 2.7 s at
worst for the Ou Main Line, a very long line meeting 23 others along long stretches. The time
is all in the stray test (every sample of O against every segment of L). Rust runs this kind of loop
50 to 100 times faster, so about 0.3 ms a line typically and 30 to 50 ms for the Ou line; with L's
segments in a 500 m grid for the nearest-point search that worst case drops to a few ms.
Candidates come from the complexes on L's route and the lines on its edges, so only lines that
actually meet L are measured. Layout on the main thread is well under 1 ms for 60 rows and 10
columns. Nothing runs while the toggle is off.

## Build stages

Each its own task id from the orchestrator, after Anita's look.

1. **Rust and query** (about 300 lines of Rust, 40 of TS). `line_related` with the grouping rule,
   stray with a segment grid, tests on small networks (a copied express sharing all track; a
   branch on shared track; a parallel line on its own track with shared stations; a line crossing
   once; a line meeting twice within and beyond 5%), the `lineRelated` query and the complex array
   in the clock worker. It changes the WASM interface, so it gets its own tree and builds the Rust
   before any app code calls it.
2. **Layout** (about 300 lines of TS). `game/relatedLayout.ts` and a Node check on the real save:
   the 2, 4 and 6X give the columns and rows the mockups show.
3. **Panel** (about 250 lines of TSX and CSS). Toggle, SVG strip, muted side rows, clicks, hovers,
   both themes; screenshots of the 2 and 6X light and dark; the ui-inspectors.md section and a
   DECISIONS line for the 5% rule. A layout task, so an Opus agent.

## Open questions for Anita

1. Lines on **the same track** and lines on **their own track** stopping at the same stations are
   drawn the same (the hover says "On the same track"). Default: the same. The other way would
   be a small gap between columns for own track.
2. **5%**: a line that leaves and comes back counts as running beside if its track stays within 5%
   of its run (100 m at least) of the selected line's. At 5% the 5 leaves the 2 at Borough Hall and
   comes back at 149 St; at 12% it would run beside the 2 the whole way, through Manhattan with no
   dots. Default: 5%.
3. **Twin lines** (the two 6s, identical through Manhattan) each get a column. Default: separate
   columns; merging lines with the same stops over the same stretch into one column with stacked
   bullets would save width but hides where they part.
