# T-2485: what holds the player's RAM

Agent memhunt, 2026-11-05, tree `.tw-trees/memhunt` (master b14e990f, after loadwait's landing).

## Short version

- The real save (`T-007-real-nyc.save`, 2 demand workers) **settles at 2.97 GB** of Chrome private bytes on the
  dev server (2.69 GB on 8800's production build) and **holds about 4.2 GB through its whole load**. T-2472's
  3.8 GB was a reading during the load: its `demandFinal` matched the new game's final before the import landed,
  so it measured with the focus pool's three workers still running (11 workers).
- **Biggest holder settled: the two solving demand workers' WASM memory, 367 + 328 MB** (heap at rest 233 and
  219 MB; the rest is the solve's high water, which WASM never gives back). **Biggest during the load: the focus
  pool, about 0.7 GB** (a load with `?perfOff=focus` peaks at 3.41 GB against 4.13).
- The page's own biggest piece was the market's corridor table, 188 MB for 5.87M place pairs, every pair of
  the world's 6,008 places with any trips (it does not depend on which zones are open).
- Built here, no figure changed: **settled 2.97 -> 2.87 GB, the load's peak 4.22 -> 3.97 GB** (two runs each).

## How it was measured

`scratchpad/memhunt/measure.mjs` (copied below in outline): headless Chrome through `checks/lib.mjs`, its own
profile, `?debug=1&new=1&paused=1&demandWorkers=2`, pinned to the cores the ledger granted (processor affinity
on every Chrome process of the profile, every 3 s), the real save imported and `demandFinal(..., { lines: 100 })`
waited for (the plain call returns on the new game's final, T-2472's slip).

- **All Chrome**: Windows `PrivateMemorySize64` of every `chrome.exe` with the profile in its command line,
  summed (as T-2472). The load's peak: the same, every 3 s from the import to the final.
- **Per target**: a raw CDP connection on `--remote-debugging-port`, `Target.setAutoAttach` (flattened) on the
  page, so every dedicated worker is a session: `Runtime.getHeapUsage` (JS heap, `backingStorageSize` = the
  ArrayBuffers outside the heap, which does **not** count WASM memory), and `Runtime.queryObjects` on
  `WebAssembly.Memory.prototype` for each instance's `buffer.byteLength`. The demand workers' own reports
  (`twDemand.client.memory()`, the focus pool's `fpool.mem`) agree with it.
- **Heap snapshots** (`HeapProfiler.takeHeapSnapshot`) of the page, the clock, shade and three demand workers
  after the final, summarised by `scratchpad/memhunt/snap.py` (self size by constructor, and for each
  ArrayBuffer the chain of named properties holding it).
- Querying objects in workers mid-load crashed the renderer once (the probe's doing: the mid-load reading
  now takes heap usage alone). One of the first runs, pinned to 1 core and with no probe running, also lost
  its renderer mid-load; not seen again in eight later loads at 2-3 cores.
- Figures: after each final, every kind of day's view, zones and market figures, keys sorted, hashed
  (`ca3bd7db94662f45` in every run, before and after, dev and 8800); and every corridor's figures through
  `game/demand.ts` `eachCorridor` (both ends, trips, rail, air, car, commutes, commute rail, each day), hashed
  per day with the table's key: the same seven hashes and key `5866836:1qqqm51` before and after.

**Workers**: 8 always (clock, shade, sea shade, MapLibre's, demand sums, 2 solving demand workers, the aux
demand worker), 11 while a save loads (the focus pool's 2 solving workers and its aux). The two solving
workers hold the same packs, one copy each (230 MB of WASM heap each at rest); sharing them needs a
SharedArrayBuffer, which needs cross-origin isolation, which GitHub Pages cannot send (architecture.md
"Threads talk by snapshots"). No worker is idle in the sense of holding nothing it uses.

**The corridor table** (188 MB, T-1008/T-2122): the market's place pairs with longer trips or commutes, 5,866,836
rows on this save, 32 bytes a row (from, to u32; longer, commutes f64; air, car f32). 5,842,589 rows (99.6%) have
no commute legs; the places number 6,008, so a place fits 16 bits. Each solving worker made the columns and
sent its own copy, so during a load the page and the workers held two or three at once (the mid-load page
buffers of 501 MB, each worker's 134).

Filed, with estimates:

- **T-2495 The focus pool during a load (~0.7 GB of the 4.2 GB peak).** Options, cheapest first: one solving
  worker in it instead of two (`FOCUS_WORKERS`; saves ~190 MB, the zone in view's figures come later; T-2045
  timed the pool); no aux of its own, its cell questions to the main pool's aux once that holds the zone
  (~60 MB); end it as soon as its last round is shown and the main pool's estimates cover the zone (the
  whole load's ~0.7 GB for the rest of the load). Timing is loadwait's area (T-2476).
- **T-2496 The demand WASM heap.** What a solving worker's 230 MB at rest is (packs, the composed market,
  the 34 MB corridor figures, the periods' state) needs Rust-side accounting (sizes per structure through a
  native test on the real save's packs); a solve's high water (+80 MB above rest, WASM never shrinks) may be
  trimmed by freeing per-period scratch between periods. Estimate 50-150 MB a solving worker if there is slack.
- **T-2136 (already open, updated)**: the page could keep no corridor table at all (118 MB now, and it grows
  with the square of the places: every pair of the world's places with trips): per-place totals and one
  place's rows asked of the worker that holds the market when a view needs them (city view, inspector,
  achievements, drive arcs read it today). This task did that task's "narrower columns" and "one worker sends".

Not filed (small, or someone else's): the clock worker's JS (138 MB, many small diff copies, no single big
holder); the GPU process (430 MB, MapLibre's); dev-only module source in every worker (production is ~10 MB a
worker).
