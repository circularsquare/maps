# T-290: Berlin from tier 0 with the Zensus grid and the Pendlerrechnung

2026-10-15. Berlin (`ber`) built by `pipeline/tier0.py` with three Germany upgrades from
`pipeline/germany.py`: Zensus 2022 100 m population scaled to each Gemeinde's current population
(the `population` hook), employed people by Gemeinde of work from the Länder's Pendlerrechnung
(the `jobs` hook), and a curve fitted on the Pendlerrechnung's published flows between Gemeinden
(`notes/t290_curve.py`). Registered, `market: -1`, `utcOffset` 1.

Scripts: `notes/t290_curve.py` (curve fit; makes the signals cache), `checks/city-browser.mjs ber`.
Logs and images in `../artifacts/trainworld/T-290-*`.

## Footprint

Twelve whole Kreise (BKG VG250-EW, land parts): Berlin, Potsdam, the eight Landkreise touching
Berlin (Potsdam-Mittelmark, Havelland, Oberhavel, Barnim, Märkisch-Oderland, Oder-Spree,
Dahme-Spreewald, Teltow-Fläming), Brandenburg an der Havel (enclosed by Havelland and
Potsdam-Mittelmark, so leaving it out makes a hole) and Frankfurt (Oder) (on the RE1 corridor,
between Oder-Spree and the Polish border; 57k people). One polygon, no holes
(`T-290-ber-outline.png`), 247 Gemeinden, about 180 x 170 km (12.14-14.76 E, 51.73-53.25 N).
Cottbus (inside Spree-Neiße, not touching the ring) is out. No unit overlaps another pack.

## Decisions

- **Jobs from the Pendlerrechnung, not the BA's insured employees** (the brief named the BA): the
  same atlas gives the flows, and it counts everyone employed. 2.94M in the pack; Berlin 2.14M.
- **Brandenburg's extract alone**: Geofabrik's "Brandenburg (mit Berlin)" covers Berlin, so the
  Berlin extract would have counted every OSM feature twice; it was not downloaded.
- **Population scaled to each Gemeinde's 31 December figure** (5,539,315) from the Zensus 2022
  grid (5,418,511 inside): the grid gives the shape, the Länder's current figure the totals.
- **Curve d^-1.4 exp(-d/28 km)**, the best on the published pairs (below).
- **Berlin's jobs inside the city are tier 0's proxy shape**: no district table turned up in one
  search (Statistik Berlin-Brandenburg publishes the city total only in the reports found).
- **BER's O&D share 1** (tier 0's default; few transfers).

## Curve: Pendlerrechnung 2024, published pairs (`notes/t290_curve.py`)

2,731 pairs with both ends inside, 2.60M workers (0.94M without Berlin's own 1.67M). 4,278 zones
of 2 km in the script (3,638 in the pack, which keeps only cells with people or jobs). Misplaced
= half the L1 distance between model and published counts over the published total.

| Paris's d^-1.3 exp(-d/20) | 8.4% | 15.2% | 9.6 |

Flat between powers 1.0 and 1.6. The model puts 6-12% more commuters into Berlin's own cell than
published: the 477k in-commuters to Berlin include many from outside the pack, whom the closed
model gives Berlin homes.

Not worth its cost: two water test points taken from the water cache but at a polygon's edge or
on a canal too thin for the mask (a second read_pack); one search for a Berlin district jobs table
that found nothing; guessing station coordinates was quick, but they are not checked against OSM.

Proposals:
1. **Water test points from the cache by polylabel** on the polygon clipped near the place, and
   none on water under about 50 m wide (now in adding-a-city.md's Berlin paragraph).
2. **Check Geofabrik's region page before downloading two extracts**: some regions contain others
   (Brandenburg includes Berlin); two overlapping extracts would double every OSM count.
3. **Interactive atlases often serve their data as plain files**: grep the JS bundle for `.csv` or
   `/data/` (the Pendleratlas took two calls this way).
4. **The point cache could keep railway stations** (`railway=station`), so a test line's stations
   come from OSM like Mumbai's did from its places.
5. **Brief**: right; the BA source it named was replaced by the atlas's for the reason above.
