## What made the stair steps

The coast was traced on the drawn grid (1.23 pitches a cell in New York, 3.2 on all Japan) and fitted with `fitOcti` at a
tolerance of at most 0.35 of the width of the water or land there. A piece 2 to 3 pitches wide at an angle off 0/45/90
(Bayonne's peninsulas, the Harlem River, creeks) could then stray about a pitch, so each shore followed the piece's own
wiggles in short 0/45 runs; the two shores were fitted apart, so nothing kept them parallel. Separately the least
tolerance (0.6 of a cell) was under the 0.71 of a cell a straight 45-degree edge steps on the grid, so even straight
coasts got one-cell nibs.

## What changed (part 1)

1. **Thin pieces as bands.** Water or land a disc 8 pitches across does not fit (four cells on a coarse grid) is a thin
   piece; each is thinned to its middle (Zhang and Suen 1984), spurs dropped, and each middle line drawn again as one
   band: median width (at least 1.5 pitches or 1.5 cells), middle fitted at 0/45/90 straying up to 0.4 of the way to the
   next piece of its kind (6 pitches at most, 0.85 of a cell at least; on water clear of pills). The two shores are now
   one fit. Guard: outside its own piece a band never comes within 1.7 cells of another piece of its kind, so no strait
   closes and no island joins (Governors Island merged with Brooklyn before the guard).
2. **Nibs and notches**: an opening by a 3x3 square, land then water, a cell flipping only where its 8 neighbours hold one
   run of each kind (so no shape splits or joins; this kept Buttermilk Channel open), never a pill or band cell.
3. **Coast tolerance two-sided**, as the flag-off sea: landward 0.35 of the land behind and clear of pills, seaward 0.35
   of the water ahead, both at least 0.85 of a cell where narrow enough.
4. **Narrow rivers as bands** (city maps, water on): round 3's rule (every arc of a crossed river, crossings moved onto
   their hops, pushed off stations, bridges where they cannot pass, drawn only where the grid's water is narrower than
   the band), at 3.5 pitches, stroked as vectors (painting them into a 3.2-pitch grid on Japan made blobs). The fit strays
   up to 6 pitches (at least 1.5), split round any station it would cover; stubs under twice the width crossing none of
   their hops dropped; the grid's own water under a band and along the river's uncorrected course, where narrower than
   the band, becomes land (else the Harlem showed twice, offset by the correction). The river finder runs at 25 m on a city
   map (round 3's; at 40 m the Harlem is a stub), via a new `fineM` argument; the flag-off water keeps 40 m.
5. **No narrow rivers on a country map** (T-2566 design 6 said so; tried on all Japan: 183 crossings drawn of 244, 235
   lines crossed where the river does not, and the cramped Tokyo core turned them into loops). Japan shows the water wide
   enough to be area, as before.

Tried and dropped: painting rivers into the grid (Governors Island erased by the wide river's band; coarse grids blobby);
wider river bands at the river's real width (same); erasing grid water near a band up to the band's width plus a cell
(ate the East River at the Brooklyn Bridge).
