# T-119: a faster demand solve (T-061)

Anita: demand bubbles took over 10 s to appear after a change. T-116 had measured 4.07 s to the
free-flow estimate and 15.9 s to crowding round three on the real 109-line New York network plus
one Philadelphia line (three workers). T-118 had doubled the small regional solve. Results are
unchanged (floating point noise only); the work is about where the time went.

## Where the time went

Native, one period, free flow or one crowding round, the real combined network (875 demand
stations, 5,039 New York subzones, 13.87M subzone pairs a period; `northeast_profile` below):

| | before | now |
|---|---|---|
| New York: strategy searches (`skims`) | 280 ms | 250-290 ms (shared) |
| Philadelphia: strategy searches | 280 ms | about 3 ms (2 destinations) |
| Regional market: strategy searches | 280 ms | 0 from round 1 (shared); 280 ms in round 0 |
| New York mode choice (13.87M pairs) | 810 ms | 770-810 ms |
| New York assignment, market's pair loop, rest | about 130 ms | about 130 ms |
| one period, all three populations | 1.75 s | 1.25 s (1.55 s in round 0) |
| five periods, four stages, one thread | 35.5 s | 22.2 s |

Three findings, in the order they mattered:

1. **The bubbles waited for the last round.** Free flow was published at 4.6 s but the bubbles
   appeared at 17.7 s, after round three. Cause: the bubbles ask each worker for its subzone sums
   (`sub_sums`) right after a round is published, when every worker has already started the next
   round; a worker only answers a question about round r while all its periods are at round r,
   which is never true by then. The question failed until the workers were idle. Fixed (below).
2. **Every population searched every station.** Philadelphia's 35 subzones are within walking
   distance of two stations of 875, yet its pass ran 875 destination searches, and so did the
   regional market's, on a graph identical to New York's. Three searches of 0.28 s per period and
   stage, two of them wasted.
3. **Three workers for five periods** is two period-stages in sequence on two of the workers: the
   solve is 2 stages long at round 0 and 8 over all rounds, whatever else is done. Splitting mode
   choice by origin zone over the pool (T-061's idea) would only cut that from 2 to 1.67 stage
   times (5 periods over 3 workers) and costs a reduce step per round; five workers cut it to 1.

## What was built

Rust (`sim/src/demand/kernel.rs`, `api.rs`, `intercity.rs`):
- `Skims` keeps a slot per searched destination, in separate vectors (adding one moves nothing),
  `skims_for`/`skims_extend`, and `egress_stations(sz)`: the stations in the access lists of
  subzones with jobs. Only those are searched towards (`want`, set in `set_network`).
- `with_skims(g, p, want, f)`: a thread-local cache of the two latest graphs, keyed by the graph's
  edges, frequencies and the boarding cost's parameters (exact comparison). The populations of
  one worker share the tables of a period and round: New York's search is used by the market
  from round 1 on, and extended by the few stations only the market needs. `release_skims()`
  (wasm export) clears it; the worker calls it after each period's stage so the tables (43 MB
  here) are not held between stages.
- Crowding rounds with nothing crowded are skipped: if the factors equal the ones the last pass
  used and every pass so far used them (free flow is all ones), the pass would repeat exactly, so
  the round count moves and nothing else (`Period::fac/uniform`). It never fired in the
  real-network runs (every period had at least one segment over its seats), so the saving is for
  small networks, which skip all three rounds (tested on the two-line network).
- `kernel::LEGACY` (test only, `cfg!(test)`): the solver as before this task, for comparison.

App:
- `demand.worker.ts`: `aux` role (a worker with no periods that only sets the network up; it never
  has a pass in progress); yields between populations, not only between periods, so a query or a
  newer network waits at most one population's pass (`roundsHeld[q] = -2` while a period is part
  way); subzone sums per city and round kept (`sumsByRound`) and sent unasked with the round,
  before its last result.
- `demandClient.ts`: pool of up to 5 solving workers plus the aux; `pushed` sums per version,
  round and city; `cellModes()` uses them (a question to the aux for the cell modes, none to the
  busy workers); `cityCells` also goes to the aux. `demandStep` (game/demand.ts) and the top bar
  say "Updating demand 2/4".
- `TopBar.tsx`, `style.css`: the step; `.demand-busy` no longer spills over the cash figure.

Tried and dropped:
- Splitting the pair loop into a gather pass, a straight arithmetic pass and a store pass, with
  wasm `simd128` on: no gain in Chrome (1.9 s against 1.8 s to free flow), 7% native. Reverted.
- Mode choice split by origin zone over the pool: see finding 3.
- Reusing periods an edit did not touch (a change to one demand level's trains leaves the other
  levels' periods identical): saves CPU, not time, once each period has a worker. T-124.

## Found, not changed

- **The regional market's free-flow estimate routes without the distance fare.**
  `IntercityApi::solve` never calls `set_crowding`, so its ride edges cost ride time alone in
  round 0 and ride time plus fare in rounds 1-3 (the local populations include it in all rounds).
  With the game's 0.1 a km the market's rail boardings are 16-20% higher in round 0, and the final
  average (round 0 is a quarter of it) about 17% above what a consistent run gives (tested: network
  loads moved by up to 0.3% of the largest). Fixing it would also let round 0 share New York's
  strategy tables (about 0.25 s of 1.7 s). It changes numbers Anita tuned the market against, so
  it is left (T-121).
- The regional market holds about 85 MB of WASM memory a worker (667,706 directed pairs at 40
  bytes, and the 3.3 MB header parsed into a JSON tree). With 5 + 1 workers that is the largest
  part of about 630 MB. T-123.
- Selection, station and longer-trip queries still need every period of a worker to be at the
  round asked about, so they are answered once the solve is done (as before). T-122.

## For the next agent on worker files (T-120, T-053)

- The pool is `computeN` solving workers plus the aux (last index); `ask()` defaults to the
  solving ones. A query that reads no period state can go to the aux.
- A worker's period is `roundsHeld[q] === -2` while a stage is part way; `live` in `query()` needs
  every one of its periods at the round asked.
- Sums for the bubbles come from `sumsByRound` / `pushed`, not from the period state.
- Coordinate frames: unchanged (stations are transformed per city in `run()`).
