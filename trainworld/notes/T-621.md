# T-621: the deep calibration (design, 2026-10-17)

The second pass of T-509's fit (notes/T-509.md sections 1-15), on the same harness (`calib.py run`,
`notes/t509s56/fit.py` (script removed 2026-10-24, in git history), cached runs) and the same cities: New York (half weight), London, Paris,
Seoul, Tokyo, Sendai fitted, Toronto held out. It does not need the world's cities: the constants
live in the sim, so packs built later take them with no rebuild.

## What it adds to T-509's fit

1. **Car ownership cost** (T-620, DECISIONS 2026-10-16 T-551): about $10 a day, charged per car
   trip as $10 over a driver's car trips a day (NHTS: about 3, so $3.33, about 10 perceived
   minutes at 3 a dollar). One worldwide figure in dollars, as fares are. T-551 measured it at
   +1.1 points of rail in New York and +1.7 in London before any refit.
2. **The walk weight against 1** (DECISIONS 2026-10-16): the fit runs twice, with the walk's 1.5
   and with 1; Anita picks, knowing her wish is 1 for clarity. (Waits are already plain minutes,
   T-585.)
3. **Walking fitted, not held**: `asc_walk` and `everyday_asc_walk` (held since T-558 to keep
   walking where it was) enter the fit on the travel surveys' walk shares: NHTS (US), NTS
   (England), EMP (France), Japan's person-trip surveys. Per country where a survey exists, a
   worldwide value elsewhere (as everyday rates do, T-450).
4. **Refit** with them: `asc_rail`, `everyday_asc_rail`, `other_asc`, the walking set
   (`walk_trip_w`, `asc_walk`, `everyday_asc_walk`; T-558 replaced `everyday_walk_d0_km`), and
   Japan's +0.5 rechecked. The search starts from the shipped values (`asc_rail` 0.4,
   `everyday_asc_rail` -1.1), not `fit.py`'s pre-fit 0 and 0. Leave one city out; Toronto's hold-out; every pack on the test cross
   (`notes/t509s7.py`); smoke.

Not in it: the long trips (T-528, a corridor fit of its own), crowding (`crowd_alpha`), bikes to
stations (T-529).

## Stage 2 (2026-10-17): ownership per trip and km, the fit; not applied

**Ownership, built** (main tree, defaults 0 so nothing moves). `Params::car_own_usd_trip` and
`car_own_usd_km` replace `car_own_usd_day` / `car_trips_day`. The per-trip part stays in
`park_parts`' fixed part (`car_own_min`); the per-km part is in the drive (`Params::car_drive` =
drive minutes x `w_drive` + road km x `car_own_km_min`), used by `NoRail::road`, `mode_choice`'s
lanes and `trip::pair_choice` (same expression order, so still bit-equal), the everyday no-rail
split and pass, `trip::everyday_choice` (T-586's), and the regional layer's commutes between zones
(`CPair::car`, road = km x `ROAD_DETOUR`). A drive to a station (`access_options`, home end) pays
both parts on its own road whether the rider parks or is dropped off; the far end pays none.
`market_params` sets both to 0, so the long trips and their station drives never pay (T-528).
Explorer slot 32 is the trip's ownership (both parts); slots 10 + 11 + 32 = 12. Test
`car_ownership_adds_dollars_per_trip_and_km`. At 0 the calibration runs equal stage 1's to the bit.

**The numbers: $1.50 a trip + $0.10 a km.** NHTS 2017 weekday: 2.9 vehicle trips per driver,
10.3 miles (16.6 km) each (Summary of Travel Trends, Table 30, adjusted lengths), so 2.9 x ($1.50
+ $1.66) = $9.20 a day. In the model: New York's commutes average 18.9 km straight (pack
gravity), about 26 km of road for the car trips, its everyday car trips about 7 km; a driver's day
of two commutes and an errand is 59 km, $10.40. London: 15.8 and 5.8 km, $8.20. A typical trip
pays $2.20-4.00. Same shape as the fare ($1.50 + $0.10 a km), easy to explain.

**Targets added.** Commuting on foot by home area from each census, scored like the rail share
(`calib.py` part `walk`; `walk_share` in each city's areas): ACS B08301 019 (New York), QS701EW
"on foot" (London), INSEE `P22_ACTOCC15P_MARCHE` (Paris), 2020 census tables 17-2 and 18 "walk
only" (Tokyo, Sendai), Census 2021 "walked" (Toronto); Seoul has none. The calib bin writes each
area's walks (`DemandApi::walk_by_cell`). Targets and area files rebuilt and checked byte-equal
apart from the walk shares (London and Paris from their pack copies). Everyday walking outside New
York (scored in fit621.py from the cached runs): London LTDS 2019/20 by purpose derived from
Travel in London 2024 (shopping and personal business 42.3%, leisure 41.0%, education 43.8%);
Ile-de-France EGT 2010 home-based (shopping 52%, personal business 36%, so errands 45%; leisure
41%; education 55%); Tokyo PT 2018 table c-1 (home to private 29.0%, home to school 49.4%;
bicycles separate, 18% of home-to-private). Sendai PT 2017 has no walk share by purpose, so Japan's
everyday constant is Tokyo's. Sources in the research report the fit's scratch folder kept.

**Method** (`notes/t509s56/fit621.py`, `surr621.py`, `show621.py`; cache
`artifacts/trainworld/t621s2/`): ownership fixed at $1.50 + $0.10; walk weight 1.5 held; the
calibration binary built from a copy of sim/ (HEAD plus this change). Parameters: `asc_rail`,
`everyday_asc_rail`, `other_asc`, Japan's `country_asc_rail`, and per country (US, GB, FR, JP) the
commute walking constant and the everyday layer's whole walking constant (`asc_walk` +
`everyday_asc_walk`, searched apart so the two do not drag each other); Seoul and Toronto take the
four countries' mean. New York at half weight; penalty 0.05 x (move / range)^2 weighted by the
fitted cities a parameter reaches. Coordinate search, three sweeps, from the shipped values; then
64 random points around the best (each parameter within 0.4 ranges) and per-city quadratic
surrogates (98-113 runs each, rms residual 0.001-0.003), the joint optimum and leave-one-out on
them; the optimum run for real agreed (6.751 against 6.755). About 700 runs, 2.5 hours.

**Fitted** (start -> fit): `asc_rail` 0.4 -> -0.48; `everyday_asc_rail` -1.1 -> -1.46 (everyday
total -0.7 -> -1.94); Japan +0.5 -> +1.19 (Japan's commute total 0.9 -> 0.71); `other_asc` 10 ->
8.6. Walking, commute / everyday: US 2.80 / 3.21 -> 2.44 / 3.78; GB 1.11 / 4.42; FR 1.27 / 5.41;
JP 0.24 / 3.73; the worldwide mean 1.26 / 4.34.

**Why it was not applied.**
- *Toronto, the hold-out, loses half its subway riders*: stations x0.99 -> x0.48, everyday rail
  4.7% -> 1.7%. A rail-only refit (walking held at today's constants) lands in the same place
  (`asc_rail` -0.16, `everyday_asc_rail` -1.79, Japan +1.375, `other_asc` 6.25; Toronto x0.53), so
  it is the rail level the fitted cities' counts ask for, not the walking. Toronto alone wants
  about +0.5 on `asc_rail` (x0.89) or +1.0 on the everyday constant (x0.72). T-509 already saw
  Toronto pull the other way (x1.00 -> x0.78).
- *Everyday rail halves everywhere* (London 12.6% -> 5.1%, Seoul 14.9% -> 6.2%): New York's NHTS
  shares (4.7% errands, 7.2% social) and the station counts of New York, London and Seoul agree
  with it, but it is a large change to the game.
- *Commute walking now matches the census, and the walkers go mostly to the car*: London's
  commutes 36.9% -> 46.2% car, Paris 46.3% -> 57.2% (census Ile-de-France 35.8% car, 48.1% public
  transport with the bus, 5.1% bike), Sendai 48% -> 60%. The game has no bike, and "other" is
  small for short trips (`other_wait`), so Japan's cyclists (Tokyo's home-to-private 18%) and
  Paris's bus riders cannot land anywhere but the car. Paris's stations fall to x0.72.
- *Everyday walking rises* to the surveys' 30-40% while the game has few short trips (T-558: 17%
  under 2 km against NHTS's 43%), so more of it is 2-4 km walks.
- Japan's commute walking constant near 0 (0.24) is the bike effect above, not a walking habit.
