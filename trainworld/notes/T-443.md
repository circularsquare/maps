# T-443: does Greater Tokyo really make more shopping and social trips than work trips?

2026-10-15. Anita: "tokyo seems to have way more shopping and errands and social/leisure than work.
is that real?" Measurement only; nothing in the rules or packs changed.

## How the game counts

- **Work**: one round trip a weekday per commuter (job holder not working at home), drawn as two
  legs. `tyo`: 17.74M jobs x (1 - 0.23 work at home) = 13.66M commuters (notes/T-420.md, stage 2
  table), 27.3M legs.
- **Shopping and errands, social and leisure, medical**: outings per resident a day, each an outward
  and a return leg (docs/demand.md 4.1; `sim/src/demand/everyday.rs` `RATE` = 0.3, 0.2, ..., 0.04).
  The rates are New York's everywhere (T-142), Japan's packs included.
- So work and the everyday purposes are counted the same way (round trips, two legs each, the
  return leg under the activity's purpose). There is no counting mismatch inside the game.
- **Weekends** change the mix a lot: a weekend day is work x0.30, shopping x1.22, social x1.60
  (docs/demand.md 4.2, from NHTS). On a weekend the everyday purposes outnumber work about 6 to 1 in
  any city. Not checked which day Anita was looking at.

## The real shares

**Tokyo Metropolitan Region Person Trip Survey, 6th (2018)**, weekday, residents aged 5 and over of
Tokyo, Kanagawa, Saitama, Chiba and southern Ibaraki. Trips by purpose (2018, the "genkyo" bars, read in units of 10,000 trips),
from the Tokyo Metropolitan Region Transportation Planning Council's summary of its 2021 future
study, figure 3-4, p. 11 (https://www.tokyo-pt.jp/static/hp/file/publicity/toshikoutsu_2.pdf,
linked from https://www.tokyo-pt.jp/special_6th):

| Purpose | Trips a day | Share | Per person 5+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commute (tsukin) | 15.15M | 21.4% | 0.43 |
| School (tsugaku) | 5.07M | 7.2% | 0.14 |
| Business (gyomu) | 1.73M | 2.4% | 0.05 |
| Escort (sogei) | 1.91M | 2.7% | 0.05 |
| Shopping (kaimono) | 5.15M | 7.3% | 0.15 |
| Private (shiji: eating out, social, leisure, hospital visits, other errands) | 10.64M | 15.1% | 0.30 |
| Return home (kitaku) | 31.02M | 43.9% | 0.88 |
| All | 70.66M | | 2.00 |

The per person rates check against MLIT's road statistics table "trips generated per person by
purpose" (Tokyo region weekday, 6th survey): to work or school 0.50, return home 0.84, business
0.09, other 0.56, total 2.00 (https://www.mlit.go.jp/road/toukei_chousa/road_db/pdf/2024/15-5.pdf,
p. 1). 70.66M / 2.00 gives the 35.3M people the survey covers.

**PT and return trips.** A PT trip's purpose is what the traveller does at the destination, and
going home is its own purpose (kitaku). So commute counts only the trip to work, shopping only the
trip to the shop, and all the trips home (44%) sit in return home. Commute and shopping are counted
on the same one-way basis, so their ratio compares directly with the game's round trips.

- **Tokyo, real**: shopping + private = 15.79M against commute 15.15M, **1.04** (1.17 with escort
  trips, which the game would count as errands). A trip chain (home, shop, shop, home) is two
  shopping trips in PT but one outing in the game, so the game's outings should if anything sit a
  little below PT's trips.
- **Tokyo, game**: **1.45** (shopping + social) or **1.57** with medical, which PT counts in private.

**NHTS 2017** (US, every day of the year), Summary of Travel Trends table 5c, p. 18, average annual
person trips per household (https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/policyinformation/documents/2017_nhts_summary_travel_trends.pdf):
to or from work 546, work business 51, shopping 580, family and personal errands 628, school and
church 341, social and recreation 866, other 128, all 3,140. NHTS's legacy purposes are "to and
from": the trip home takes the purpose of the activity, like the game's two legs. Shopping +
errands + social is **3.8x** work over the whole year; on a weekday alone (taking out weekends with
the game's own NHTS weekend factors) about **2.7x**. New York's game ratio, 1.36, is half of that:
T-066 chose 0.3 and 0.2 as partial budgets, not survey rates, so the US side is deliberately low.

## Verdict

**Mostly real, somewhat overstated.** In Tokyo people really do make about as many shopping and
private trips as commute trips on a weekday (15.8M against 15.2M), so a purpose view where the
everyday purposes together outnumber work is right. The game's 1.5x is about 40% too high, and the
cause is a **tier 0 rate problem**, not counting:

- **Shopping is about double**: 0.299 outings a resident against PT's 0.15 shopping trips a person
  (0.20 with escort trips). Japanese residents shop much less often by trip than New York's
  borrowed rate. Social and medical together (0.24) sit a little under PT's private 0.30, which
  also holds errands, so the excess is all on the shopping side.
- **Work is about right for now**: 0.344 commuters a resident against PT's 0.43 commute trips a
  person in 2018. The survey predates COVID; with the 0.23 work-at-home share (census 9.7% plus a
  2-days-in-5 telework share of MLIT's 37.5%) the 20% gap is about what post-2020 telework would
  take off (not checked against current ridership). Work at home 0.23 is not the problem.
- Not a counting difference: the game counts work and everyday outings both as round trips, so the
  ratio stands whether one counts legs or outings. Showing PT's numbers next to the game's legs
  would need halving (or PT's return-home trips spread back over the purposes).
