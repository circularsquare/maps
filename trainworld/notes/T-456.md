# T-456: how complete OSM's shops are, and a fallback that leans on jobs where they are thin

Measurement and proposal, 2026-10-16. No pipeline, pack or registry change. Scripts in `notes/t456/`
(they ran from a scratch folder; paths inside point there and at `data/raw/t456/`).

## Summary

- **OSM holds a quarter to three quarters of real shops in rich countries, and almost none in poor
  ones.** Against official establishment counts: Japan 24% of retail and personal-service
  establishments (eating places 37%), US Northeast 50% (65% counting banks, pharmacies, fuel; food
  64%) of employer establishments, England 71% (food 84%) of registered local units, Toronto roughly
  all employer locations. In Nigeria OSM has 0.07 shops per 1,000 people; Lagos 0.025 shops and 0.07
  errand places per 1,000, against tens of trading enterprises per 1,000 there: well under 1%.
- **Completeness varies inside a country** about as much as between rich countries: Japanese
  prefectures 9% (Kumamoto) to 67% (Miyagi), English regions 57% (London) to 93% (North East), US
  states 33% (Rhode Island) to 81% (DC). Density matters a little in Japan (21% rural, 32% in the
  densest), not in England or the US; income not at all at county level.
- **What predicts it is OSM's own density**: log OSM shops per resident correlates 0.91 with log
  completeness across Japanese municipalities and US counties (0.75 English districts), because
  real shops per resident vary much less (sd of log 0.2-0.36) than OSM's (0.43-0.83). Official
  establishments per job vary as much as per resident (Japan sd 0.40 against 0.36).
- **Thin OSM still helps if handled right**: fallback D as approved (geometric mean of jobs share
  and OSM share) beats jobs alone at Japan's 25% completeness (17 against 20% misplaced, 2 km
  cells), but collapses when OSM is thinned towards Lagos or Mumbai levels (58% misplaced at 1%
  kept), because a zone with jobs and no mapped shop gets nothing. The proposal (section 6) fixes
  that with one pseudo-place per zone, an OSM exponent that falls with OSM's density, and a
  quarter population in the errand base: Japan 14.2% misplaced at full OSM and 16.8% at 1% (jobs
  alone 20.3); London errands 18.3 / 21.7 (D 18.4 / 38.9). Never worse than jobs alone in either test.
- **Found on the way, for T-453 stage 2**: T-453's Japan mesh columns are off by one (section 8).

## 4. What predicts the gap where no official count exists

Across units with at least 5,000 people (correlation with log OSM / official):

| predictor | Japan (1,587 municipalities) | England (295 districts) | US NE (195 counties) |
|---|---|---|---|
| log OSM shops per resident | **0.91** | **0.75** | **0.91** |
| log OSM shops + food per resident | 0.88 | 0.67 | 0.85 |
| log residential density | 0.29 | -0.12 | -0.02 |
| food share of OSM shops + food | 0.24 | -0.43 | -0.52 |
| log official establishments per resident | -0.20 | -0.18 | -0.15 |
| sd of log OSM shops per resident | 0.83 | 0.43 | 0.47 |
| sd of log official per resident | 0.36 | 0.29 | 0.20 |

Real shops per resident vary little within a rich country (sd of log 0.2-0.36) and stay within
about 5-14 per 1,000 across Japan, England and the US Northeast (official counts, each with its own
coverage). OSM's own count per resident is therefore a usable completeness index: a pack at 5 or
more errand places per 1,000 (London, Paris, Berlin) is mapped well enough to trust its pattern; at
1-3 (Japan, Sao Paulo) partly; below 0.5 (Shanghai, Mumbai, Lagos) not at all. Buildings per shop
(taginfo) separates Africa and Indonesia (hundreds to over a thousand) from places with no building
imports, but Japan sits at 103 with 25% shops, so it is a weaker sign. OSM edit density was not
tested: it needs the full history file, and OSM per resident already explains most of the variation.

Informal trade is the limit: where most selling is in markets and on the street (Lagos, Mumbai),
no count of fixed shops, OSM or official, says where errands go. Population and market sites would.

## 6. Proposal: fallback D with a completeness factor

For each pack, each purpose (errands, leisure), over the pack's 2 km zones z:

    O_z   OSM places of the purpose in the zone (tier 0 counts, weight 1)
    J_z   the pack's jobs in the zone;  P_z  the pack's residents in the zone
    r     = 1000 * sum(O) / sum(P)                 OSM places per 1,000 residents, whole pack
    c     = min(1, r / R)                          R = 5 for errands, 3 for leisure
    b     = 0.25 for errands, 0 for leisure
    base_z = (1 - b) * J_z / sum(J) + b * P_z / sum(P)
    osm_z  = (O_z + 1) / sum(O + 1)                one pseudo-place in every zone of the pack
    w_z    = base_z ** (1 - c/2) * osm_z ** (c/2), normalised to sum 1

Inside a zone, w_z is spread over cells by the purpose's OSM places where the zone has any, otherwise
by jobs (the London test spread this way; spreading errands by the same jobs-residents mix is an
untested alternative). Then scaled to 15% and 8% of the
pack's jobs as now. Where a country publishes sector totals for larger units (France's communes),
those totals replace J_z's share as T-453 says, and the same formula applies.

At c = 1 this is T-453's D with the pseudo-place and, for errands, a quarter of the jobs side
moved to residents (London, Paris, Berlin, Sendai, Akita: c = 1); at c near 0 it is the base alone
(Lagos 0.018, Mumbai 0.04-0.06, Shanghai 0.07); Tokyo c = 0.56, Fukuoka 0.33, Sao Paulo 0.27. The
header's everyday source line should state c. Measured: never worse than jobs alone, 3-6 points
better than D in Japan and up to 17 points better than D where OSM is thin (section 5).
Inputs are all already in tier 0 (`osm_errand`, `osm_leisure`, jobs, pop, zone ids); no new data.

## 7. Plausibility check for a published sector source

A sector source S (jobs or establishments by unit, for a purpose) is used only if it passes both;
otherwise it is treated as no source, the fallback runs, and the header says why.

1. **Level**: S's total as a share of the pack's jobs lies within 0.08-0.20 for errands (retail +
   personal services) and 0.04-0.15 for leisure (food, accommodation, arts). Japan's retail +
   personal services is 15.5% of persons employed; with wholesale left in (I + N) 22%, which fails.
   (US LODES CNS07 + CNS19 is about 14% of jobs nationally, England's BRES 45 + 47 + 95 + 96 about
   11%; from published totals, not measured here.)
2. **Concentration**: the share of S lying in units where S's share is more than 3 times both the
   unit's share of residents and its share of the purpose's OSM places (+1 a unit) is under 5%.
   Japanese municipalities, retail only: 0% in every prefecture; with wholesale: 9.6% in Tokyo
   (Chiyoda, Chuo, Minato, where 79-81% of I is wholesale). Skip this test when c < 0.3 (OSM too thin
   to compare against); the level test still runs.

A source that splits the sector itself (9-1A/9-1B for Japan, BRES SIC divisions) passes both. France's
Flores GZ (all trade) would likely fail test 2 in central Paris; check it when built.

## 8. Found on the way: T-453's Japan mesh columns are off by one

e-Stat's header for T001162 (500 m mesh, economic census 2021): 001-021 are establishments (A-S, A-R,
C-E, C, D, E, F-S, F ... S), 022-042 persons employed in the same order. Checked by sums: 022 (16.41M
in first mesh 5339) equals the sum of the industry columns 025-042 less the F-S total (16.39M).
So persons employed are I = 032, M = 036, N = 037, and establishments I = 011, M = 015, N = 016.
T-453's `t453_tyo.py` and its note use 033 (J, finance), 037 (N) and 038 (O, education) for I, M and
N, so its Tokyo sector figures are wrong and stage 2 must use 032 / 036 / 037. Also `japan.py`'s
`census_jobs` uses 023, which is A-R (public service S left out, about 3-5% of jobs), and its comment
("001-022 count establishments") is off by one; 022 is all industries.
