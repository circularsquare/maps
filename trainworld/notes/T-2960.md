# T-2960: uneven gaps at a merge (diagnosis)

Anita, 2026-11-09: trains alternating through a merge go A, short wait, B, long wait (about 6:1), A.
Screenshot: `../artifacts/trainworld/t2960-anita-merge.png`.

Diagnosis only; no sim/ or app/ edits. Verdict: **two separate causes**, both measured. Which one
Anita's merge shows depends on whether its two lines are set by train count or by headway.

## How a merge's order is decided

Nothing queues trains at a merge. Each line runs its own timetable (`Service::run_deps`,
`service.rs` `lattice_deps_local` at 322: per stretch of one demand level, `start - lead + off +
phase + k * headway`). The merge's wait is Kingman's average (`capacity.rs` 489-505 builds the merge,
kind 12) baked into every train's profile as the same hold just short of the node, so the order and
gaps at the merge are just the two lines' departure patterns laid over each other. The one thing that
tries to keep them apart is departure spacing (`spacing.rs`, T-1925): one phase per line and level,
chosen when that line is edited, scored over every place it meets another line.

The drawn trains add the display guard (`app/src/map/trainGuard.ts` 1-9: "nor onto a merge another
train is on", 30 m gap): when B is due just after A, B is held until A clears. That is the "very
brief wait". The trains standing on both branches in the screenshot are the Kingman hold itself
(120-250 s per train at 90-96% in the runs below), drawn as each train stopping before the node.

## Measurement

Scratch copy of the crate with a test (`t2960_tests.rs`, kept in the agent's scratchpad, not in the
tree): a Y with a 3 km trunk T-J and two branches of different lengths, line A T-Br and line B T-M,
made in game order (A created and scheduled, then B). Times each train passes J towards T (and
leaves T the other way) over 9-10h, 13-14h and 17-18h on a weekday; "ratio" is the mean gap after
one line's trains over the mean gap after the other's. Five branch geometries, flat and flying, six
schedules. Outputs: `../artifacts/trainworld/t2960-measure-current.txt`,
`t2960-spacing-meets.txt` (what spacing weighs), `t2960-measure-final.txt` (after the change below,
which matches the trial).

## Cause 1: lines set by train count run different headways, so the gap slides all day

A count line's headway is its round trip over its trains (6.3 "Scheduling"). Two branches of
different lengths give two different headways, so one fixed phase cannot hold: the A-to-B offset
creeps by the headway difference every train, through the whole 06-20 stretch.

4 trains each, merge at 94-96%:

| geometry | headways A / B (s) | merge ratio 09h | 13h | 17h |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| flat, A 5.1 km B 6.0 km | 301.2 / 302.3 | 2.31 | 7.06 | 18.96 |
| flat, A 6.4 km B 6.0 km | 305.6 / 300.0 | 3.18 | 2.95 | 5.21 |
| flying, A 6.4 km B 6.0 km | 304.6 / 299.0 | 5.37 | 2.14 | 2.91 |
| flat, A 4.8 km B 7.0 km | 297.9 / 309.3 | 2.95 | 1.99 | 1.26 |

Anita's ~6:1 sits in this range; the ratio she sees depends on the hour she looks. With 6 trains
each the capacity cut leaves A at ~218 s and B at ~420 s, so A-A-B patterns. Headway-mode lines hit
this too at a busy merge: at 300 s and 90% the clamp (6.3 "Headway mode", `clamp_auto`) left A at
343.7 s against B's 300 s.

No spacing rule can fix this; only equal headways can.

## Cause 2: with equal headways, spacing trades the merge off against other meetings

Headway lines at 360-600 s run equal headways, and a phase that evens the merge exists in every
case (a sweep of B's phase finds a merge ratio of 1.01-1.08). But `spacing.rs` picked phases giving
up to **2.41:1** (420 s flying, A 4.8 km B 7.0 km) and 1.65-1.96:1 at 360 s flat. Why
(`t2960-spacing-meets.txt`):

- `crowding` (`spacing.rs` 38-68) scores every meeting the same way: per train, short of half the
  smaller headway, squared, weight 1. The phase is the least-squares compromise.
- **Flat junction crossing** (`spacing.rs` 89, kind 4): B's merging move crosses A's diverging move
  in the other direction. It is scored as if it needed half a headway, though a crossing is clear in
  about 90 s. At 360 s flat: merge wants B's phase at 178.9 s, leaving T wants 181.1 s, the crossing
  wants 50.7 s; chosen 135 s, so the merge runs 1.65:1 where 180 s would give 1.01:1 both ways.
- **The other direction.** One phase serves both runs (`service.rs` 923-924). A headway line's
  reverse departures leave `there + wait/2` after the forward ones (`service.rs` 904-919), so the
  two lines' trains reach J (towards T) and leave T (away from it) at offsets that differ by the
  branches' run-time difference. At 420 s flying, A 4.8 km B 7.0 km: merge wants 392 s, leaving T
  wants 221 s; chosen 315 s, both directions uneven (2.41 and 2.61).

## Trial fix for cause 2 (scratch only)

In the scratch copy: a merge meeting weighted 25 (close to "the merge first, the rest as tie-break"),
and a junction crossing scored only within 120 s instead of half a headway. Over the 30 headway-mode
cases (360, 420, 600 s; five geometries; flat and flying):

- merge ratio at most 1.20 everywhere, most 1.01-1.09 (was up to 2.41). Weight 4 instead of 25 was
  not enough: the quadratic compromise still shares the error (2.41 became 1.66).
- leaving T (the diverging direction, where trains do not queue) gets worse where the branches'
  run times differ a lot: up to 8:1 (420 s, A 4.8 km B 7.0 km). One phase per line cannot give both
  directions even gaps there.
- the existing spacing tests pass.

## What "equal" costs

- **Cause 2 fix: no throughput change.** Spacing moves departures within a headway; rates, holds,
  round trips and demand do not read it ("waits are by headway"). The merge's trains an hour and its
  Kingman wait stay the same. It moves the unevenness from the merge (visible: trains queue there) to
  the diverging direction on the trunk (no queue).
- **Cause 1 fix: lowers the faster line a little.** Equal gaps need one headway, which a count line
  only gets by standing at its terminals: the faster line runs at the slower one's headway, losing
  the difference (0.4% for 301.2 against 302.3 s; 11% for 290.8 against 324.1 s, so it needs a cap,
  say within 2-3%). That slows the fast one rather than bringing the slow one down; bringing the slow
  one down needs more trains, which is the player's call. The merge's throughput drops by about half
  the faster line's loss.

## Options

1. **Spacing puts the merge first** (cause 2). **Done**, below.
2. **Both directions even, for headway lines** (cause 2, fuller): a second phase for the reverse
   departures, taken out of the wait-out time a headway line already stands at its ends (free there;
   a count line has no slack and would pay in trains an hour). Touches `Lattice`, `spacing.rs`, the
   `LPH1` save block and the eras; a medium stage. Filed as **T-2972**.
3. **Count lines sharing a merge at nearly one rate run one headway** (cause 1): a design change to
   how count lines are timetabled (the faster stands the difference at its ends, within a cap). Touches
   the settle and `lattice`, changes rates demand reads a little; a medium stage and a call for
   Anita, since it means a count line no longer always runs at its round trip over its trains. Filed
   as **T-2973** (Anita's call).

Option 1 fixes headway lines at moderate load; it does nothing for count lines, which are the
default. Cause 1 is likely what Anita saw if her two lines are set by train count.

## What landed (option 1, worktree `tw/mergegap`)

`sim/src/track/spacing.rs`: each meeting carries a weight and a clear distance. A merge (wire kind 12)
weighs `MERGE_FIRST` = 25 against 1 for any other meeting, so the others only choose among phases
nearly as good at the merge (its error at most 1/26 of what they pull, plus the 24-step grid). A flat
junction crossing counts only while its trains are within the crossing's own time (`CONFLICT_S`,
90 s), not half a headway. A pure lexicographic order was not used: with continuous scores it
almost never ties, so the other meetings would never be heard; weight 4 was too weak (2.41 became
1.66). Test: `spacing::tests::trains_take_even_turns_at_a_merge` (360 s flat and 420 s flying Ys that
were 1.65:1 and 2.41:1; now under 1.25:1, turns alternating). Spec: docs/track-and-service.md 6.3
"Departures".

The 30 headway cases at 09h, before -> after (`../artifacts/trainworld/t2960-measure-final.txt`):

| headway | merge median | merge worst | leaving T median | leaving T worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 600 s | 1.08 -> 1.04 | 1.29 -> 1.09 | 1.08 -> 1.09 | 1.25 -> 1.48 |
| 420 s | 1.17 -> 1.05 | 2.41 -> 1.20 | 1.25 -> 1.34 | 4.16 -> 8.05 |
| 360 s | 1.40 -> 1.04 | 1.96 -> 1.16 | 1.44 -> 1.25 | 3.16 -> 2.09 |

All 30: merge cases over 1.25:1 went from 12 to 0; leaving T from 11 to 15. The worse ones leaving
T are geometries whose branch run times differ by most of a headway, which one phase per line cannot
serve both ways (T-2972). Throughput, rates and demand are unchanged. Lines placed before this keep
their phase until they are next edited, or a save without phases is loaded.
