# T-463: commutes between Japan's packs (with T-465's at-home fix)

2026-10-16. Rule: docs/zones.md 4 and docs/demand-worldwide.md (Japan). Script: `notes/t463_jp.py`
(`fit`, `commute`, `flows`). Logs and tables: `../artifacts/trainworld/T-463-*`. Backups of every
Japan pack (merged and res-9), its market file, the registry and `japan_zones.json` before the
rebuild: `data/work/pre-T463/`.

## What was built

1. **At home by municipality of work** (T-465's fix) in every Japan pack: work-end commuters = jobs x
   (1 - the census's at-home share of the cell's municipality, table 4) x (1 - telework).
   `japan.at_home_share`, the `at_home` and `telework` config keys (tier0.py uses them in place of a
   flat `wfh`). Telework stays each pack's own as before: 0.15 Greater Tokyo, 0.098 Keihanshin,
   0.078 Chukyo, 0.067 (`TELEWORK_JP`, MLIT's 16.8% outside the big three at 2 days in 5) for the
   regional metros and the 38 zones. The brief said 0.15 everywhere; that was taken to mean "telework
   unchanged", since only Tokyo has 0.15 and the others come from MLIT's regional shares.
2. **Curves.** The zone fit grid (`japan_zones.fit`'s rule) run on each pack's old and new margins
   (`t463_jp.py fit`; `T-463-fit-<id>.json`). The old margins reproduce every zone's shipped curve.
   On the new margins 17 of the 38 zones' choice moves, mostly through the tie rule (misplaced within
   0.3 points, own-municipality share nearest the census's); they take the new choice
   (`japan_zones.json`). The seven metros keep theirs: their curves were chosen by
   `t420_curve.py` with judgement, and on the new margins each shipped curve misplaces as few or
   fewer than the grid's choice (Tokyo 16.6% both, Keihanshin 17.4% against 17.6%, Chukyo 12.3%
   against 12.4%, Hiroshima 8.7% against 8.9%; Fukuoka, Sapporo and Sendai unchanged choices).
3. **One joint commute gravity over Japan's 45 packs** (`intercity.py --group jp`, the T-297 solve
   via `joint_gravity --packs`): its own group (Okinawa joins through Kagoshima's islands), distances
   in a conic at the packs' mean origin, cut at 150 km, a border factor between packs of 0.4
   (`borders.json` "JP", fitted below). The cross-pack part goes to
   `data/work/jp.joint_commutes.json` by 6 km square of each pack's own frame;
   `market_zones.py` (class `Groups`) matches the squares to each zone file's 6 km zones and writes
   `commute_*` into every Japan market pack.
4. Then `merge_cells.py` (2,500), `read_pack.py` OK on all 45, `routes.py --check` current on all 45
   (zones unchanged), `register.py <45 ids> --market --trips`. Only the 45 Japan registry rows changed
   (marketRow, localTrips; Tokyo 79.31M to 79.07M local trips).

Tier 0 headers now carry `"country"` (the config's iso2), which the joint gravity reads for border
factors; a tier 0 pack's single region "00" is keyed by its pack, not shared with every other
tier 0 pack.

## Commuters

At home by municipality barely moves totals (Tokyo 13.66M to 13.63M commuters, Keihanshin 6.40M to
6.40M; zones within 0.1%) but moves them toward office centres: per-cell at-home shares run 0.5% to
34% (Tokyo), 1.2% to 41% (Keihanshin).

Joint: 46.78M commuters, 46.04M within their pack, 0.74M (1.58%) to another, mean 43 km. Biggest
outflows: Gunma to Tokyo 36.5k, Tokyo to Gunma 34.5k, Shiga to Keihanshin 23.7k, Tochigi to Tokyo 22.9k.

## The border factor

Cross-pack commuters against the 2020 census's workers between the same prefectures (table 4;
residents of the home pack's prefectures working in the work pack's; Hokkaido's subprefecture packs
left out), dry solves at several factors (`T-463-trial-*.md`):

| Factor | Model between packs | Census | Ratio | Pair shares misplaced |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1,526,887 | 818,274 | 1.87 | 25.8% |
| 0.5 | 868,235 | 818,274 | 1.06 | 25.3% |
| 0.4 (shipped) | 718,571 | 818,274 | 0.88 | 25.2% |
| 0.3 | 560,499 | 818,274 | 0.68 | 25.2% |

The census counts every commuter; the model's are less telework (7-15%), so 0.88 is the level
match. The factor moves the level, not the spread over pairs (25% misplaced at every factor).

Nara to Osaka is inside Keihanshin's pack, so this task leaves it to the pack's own curve.

What a symmetric distance gravity cannot hold: the doubly constrained solve sends about as many each
way, where the census has one-way pulls (Shiga sends 64k into Keihanshin and takes 31k), and it
crosses mountains and straits by the crow (Shiga to Chukyo over the Suzuka range 3.4x, Kagawa to
Okayama over the Seto Inland Sea several times). A travel-time cost would fix both; not this task.

## The joint solve's residual

The balance stalls at a column error of about 8e-4 from step ~90 (T-297's Northeast reached 1e-8 in
38 steps). Every one of the worst columns is in Okinawa's outlying islands (the Daito and Sakishima
groups, 300-400 km from Naha), whose few hundred jobs reach almost no homes but their own island's
under Okinawa's short curve: a near-disconnected block whose level crawls. Their jobs are met to
0.08%; the solve keeps its best step and `intercity.py --group` stops at 150 steps (about 4 minutes
with 8 threads, plus 40 s for the flows). The mainland's columns are not separately measured; if it
matters, solving Okinawa apart (one commuter a day joins it to Kagoshima) would show it.
