- **Turn rules**: a line ending at a through station still holds its platform 4 minutes (15 an
  hour). A dead end pools its turns, now with a floor: max(turnaround + 60 s, 200 s) / platform
  tracks, so at most 18 an hour on one track and 36 on two however short the turnaround (it was
  unbounded below 140 s turnaround). Default 3 minutes: 15 and 30 as before.
- **Terminal**: arrival and departure platforms held dwell + 60 s each, two sidings pooled at the
  same floored rule (30 an hour at 3 minutes, 36 at 2), the 90 s crossing kept; reversing partway
  there 120 s a side (30 an hour). Price 1 km of track at the station's level (US$117M at +2).
- **Park and ride** (`kernel::ride_fixed`): `p_ride` 15 less 10 at that station, both ends of a
  trip (bus bays serve the far end too). P after the station's name (CSS badge on the label).
- **Waiting room** (`kernel::wait_factor`, `Graph::st_wait`): the half-headway part of boarding
  cost x 0.8, at first boarding and changes, in the strategy search and the trip legs.
- **Passage**: the links (`Network::links`) are passages: US$10M + US$90M a km of the straight
  line between the station nodes (ground metres), at most 1 km; paid at once between built
  stations, else it is in the blueprint quote and paid when the second station is constructed
  (`built_cost` counts a passage once both stations are). Demand: `Network::st_passage`, the walk
  edge between the pair costs d / 2 m/s instead of d x 1.3 / 1.2 m/s. Old saves' links load as
  passages and were never charged.
- **One edit for many stations**: `set_station_upgrade(nodes, bit, on)` takes a list (the panel
  passes one; checks/t1281-upgrades.mjs passes every station).

## Design (2026-10-25)

Design only (2026-10-25). Anita asked for a station upgrade to a terminal that turns 30 trains an
hour, and for other station infrastructure people like building (as in NIMBY Rails), decorative
but ideally with a function or a sign of something.

## For Anita

- **30 an hour is realistic for a proper terminal.** The Victoria line runs 36 trains an hour into
  two-platform termini at both ends (Brixton, Walthamstow Central; it was held to 24 beyond Seven
  Sisters until Walthamstow's crossover was rebuilt in 2015). Two-track Japanese commuter lines turn
  12-15 an hour on ordinary layouts and close to 30 with short tail tracks (Chuo Rapid at Tokyo).
- **What the game does now:** a line ending at a station that has track on past it (every station
  placed on open ground has a short stub beyond its platform) turns on its arrival platform, held
  for the turnaround plus a minute: **4 minutes, 15 an hour**. A line ending at a true dead end
  pools its turns over the platform tracks: **30 an hour** on double track at the default 3-minute
  turnaround. Reversing partway is held to 12 an hour a side.
- **Recommended short list**, each bought from the station panel like a flyover (blueprint, or paid
  at once on a built station):

  | Upgrade | Price | What it does |
  |---|---|---|
  | Terminal (turnback sidings) | 1 km of track at the station's elevation (US$45M at ground, US$99M at -2) | trains turn in two sidings past the platform: 30 an hour at a 3-minute turnaround, the platform free for other trains; reversing partway there 30 an hour too |
  | Park and ride (car park, bus bays, bike racks) | US$20M | riding to this station costs 10 perceived minutes less |
  | Passage (between two stations up to 1 km apart) | US$10M + US$90M a km | they become one complex; changing between them walks the straight line at 2 m/s |
  | Waiting room | US$5M | decorative: drawn beside the platform, the waiting crowd shown inside it |

- **Not now:** extra entrances (walks to stations come from tables built offline), depots (the game
  has no depot model, and a required one adds a chore), passing tracks (quad covers it), platform
  doors. Longer platforms already exist; T-751 adds the line-wide button.
- Park and ride and the passage change demand, so they are yours to approve (the demand rule in
  CLAUDE.md). The terminal and the waiting room do not.

Questions are at the end.

## Turning trains: the rules now

From `sim/src/track/capacity.rs` and docs/track-and-service.md 6.2, at the default turnaround of
180 s (`DEFAULT_TURNAROUND_S`):

| Where a line ends | Rule | Most an hour |
|---|---|---|
| A station with track past it (`built_ports > 1`), including the 10 m stub a station on open ground brings | arrival platform held for turnaround + 60 s; the turn crosses the other track (90 s) | 15 |
| A true dead end (one edge at the node) | (turnaround + 60 s) / platform tracks, pooled | 30 on double track |
| Reversing partway (T-797), at a stop or a bare track end | as above, plus the stopgap `REVERSAL_LIMIT_S` 300 s a side | 12 a side |

So the 15 an hour bites where most lines end, since a station drawn on open ground and extended from
one side keeps its stub on the other. Anita's T-657 note ("the first ten lines reach capacity at
once even at 4 minutes") is this rule.

## Real terminals

- Two-platform stub terminal with a scissors crossover in front: 24-30 an hour; 36 with automatic
  driving and crews stepping back (Victoria line). The crossover's speed is often the limit:
  Walthamstow Central's held the line to 24 an hour until it was rebuilt.
  https://railmagazine.com/news/network/2015/02/17/lu-promises-36-trains-per-hour-for-victoria-line
- Tail tracks (sidings past the platform): the train empties, runs into a siding, reverses there and
  comes back into the departure platform, so the platform is held only for the dwell. Near 30 an
  hour on two tracks (Pedestrian Observations on Japanese terminals:
  https://pedestrianobservations.com/2011/06/13/boston-south-stations-supposed-capacity-limit/).
- Turning on a single platform track, every train on the same one: 12-15 an hour. The game's 15
  is right for that.

## The upgrades

### 1. Terminal (turnback sidings)

- **Rule.** At a station with it, a line that turns there (ends there, or reverses partway there)
  holds its arrival platform for dwell + 60 s, then a siding for turnaround + 60 s pooled over two
  sidings, then its departure platform for dwell + 60 s, and crosses the other track once (90 s, the
  existing turnback conflict). At 180 s turnaround the sidings allow 30 an hour, the platforms 40,
  the crossing 40: **30 an hour**; 36 at a 2-minute turnaround. Through trains share the platforms
  at their usual dwell + 60 s, so a terminal can also be a through station for other lines.
  Reversing partway at a station with it takes `REVERSAL_LIMIT_S` down from 300 to 120 s (30 an
  hour a side).
- **Both ends.** One upgrade covers turns from either side (sidings drawn at each end where a line
  turns).
- **Dead ends** keep their pooled rule (they are a terminal by their shape already); the upgrade
  adds nothing there but the drawing. (See question 1.)
- **Price**: 1 km of double track at the station's elevation, 2x over water: US$45M at ground,
  US$90M at +1 or -1, US$99M at -2, US$108M at -3, US$117M at +2, US$162M at +3. No upkeep of its own.
- **Shown**: from zoom 14, where platforms are drawn, two short sidings past the platform's end in
  the track's style (blueprint while blueprint). The capacity markers' hovers and a line's held-trains
  alert already say what helps ("helped by ending the line at a dead-end terminus"): they would say
  "Build a terminal at Nerul Village". The line panel needs nothing new: its trains come back.
- **Work**: a station flag (spare bit in the station's saved flags), an undoable blueprint op and a
  paid-at-once op on built stations (the flyover's pattern), the capacity resources above in
  `build_nodes`, the sidings drawn, the panel row. About 300 lines; an Opus task (capacity rules);
  its own worktree (new WASM functions). This is T-657's "buildable ways to turn trains faster".

### 2. Park and ride

- **Rule**: a ride to this station (car, bus, bike or a lift, docs/demand-mode.md "Riding to a
  station") costs `p_ride` - 10 perceived minutes (15 to 5). In a suburb a ride to a station 3 km off
  goes from about 43 to 33 perceived minutes, so it draws riders from further out and from other
  stations. One per-station flag read in `access_options`; the regional layer's rides use the same
  function, so they follow.
- **Why not capacity or a parking price**: car parks are automatic and unlimited today, and parking
  at a suburban station already costs next to nothing by the parking rule, so a lot that only made
  parking free would do nothing where people build one. The fixed ride cost is what a real
  park-and-ride (a lot beside the entrance, buses timed to trains) cuts.
- **Price** US$20M, no upkeep.
- **Shown**: a small P badge beside the station's name from zoom 12; the lot as a pale rectangle by
  the platform from zoom 15. The station panel's "How they arrive" bar is the sign it works: its
  drove share rises.
- **Demand change**: a per-station input to access, not a fitted constant; New York's real save is
  unchanged (no station has it). Needs Anita's yes.

### 3. Passage

- **Rule**: a passage joins two stations up to 1 km apart (a link, T-197, made built) into one
  complex, and a change between them walks the straight line between the platforms' centres at
  2 m/s (moving walkways, no 1.3 detour) instead of the straight line x 1.3 at 1.2 m/s, plus the
  usual fixed 2 perceived minutes (T-717). 300 m: 325 s to 150 s; 600 m: 650 s to 300 s.
- **Price** US$10M + US$90M a km of the straight line (300 m: US$37M). No upkeep.
- **Shown**: a solid grey line between the two platforms from zoom 14 (links stay dashed), and the
  transfer bubbles (T-1124) walk it at the passage's pace, so the change is visibly quicker. The
  complex map draws it solid.
- **Demand change**: the walk between platforms is a demand input (T-717), so this needs Anita's yes.
- **Work**: a flag on `Network::links` entries (links are saved with the network, outside the undo
  history; a passage would be a blueprint like other building, so it needs its own op), the walk
  rule in the demand station builder, drawing. About 200 lines.

### 4. Waiting room

- **Decorative**, with a sign: a small building beside the platform from zoom 15, and the waiting
  crowd, drawn today as the platform filling (T-219), shown filling it as well. No rule changes.
- **Price** US$5M.
- A function it could have, if Anita wants one: waits at that station counted at 0.8 (a real
  effect of shelter and seating on perceived waits). It would be a new demand factor, so not by
  default.

### Not now

- **Entrances**: walks to stations come from precomputed walk tables per pack (T-1182); an entrance
  away from the station point would need its own walks, so reruns or a new runtime walk. Not worth
  it for the effect.
- **Depots**: trains appear and leave at line ends and cars wait in an abstract depot (6.4). A real
  depot means empty running to and from it and a rule for where it must be, a chore more than fun.
  A decorative yard where the trains held out of service stand overnight could come later.
- **Passing tracks / more platform tracks at a through station**: quad track and its crossovers
  already give overtaking and a second platform pair.
- **Platform doors, more doors**: the game has no dwell model to change.
- **Longer platforms**: exist (60-400 m, lengthening a built one paid at once); T-751 adds
  "Expand all platforms on this line".

## In the station panel

Station view (one station opened alone) gets one row of upgrade icons under its buttons (the
buttons-first rule, T-1134): terminal (two sidings), park and ride (P), waiting room (a bench), and,
in complex view or for a linked pair, passage. Each is a toggle: outlined when not built, dashed when
in the blueprint, filled when built; its hover gives its name, what it does in one line and its price
("Terminal. Trains turn in sidings past the platform, up to 30 an hour. US$45M."). A click adds it
to the blueprint or, on a built station, builds it at once for its price, as the junction
inspector's flyover does. Removing a built one refunds nothing, like track.
