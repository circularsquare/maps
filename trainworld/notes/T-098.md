# T-098 flows: where a set of cells' commuters go

Done 2026-10-09 by the demand agent, for the views agent's bubble view (T-097). Given cells at one
end of the commute (homes, or jobs), it answers with all their commuters, every mode, spread over
the cells at the other end. Click a home bubble to see where its people work, a job bubble to see
where its people live, or drag over several bubbles to see them together.

## Interface (`app/src/game/demand.ts`)

```ts
flows(end: "home" | "work", cells: Uint32Array /* pack cell ids at that end */): Promise<Flows | null>
interface Flows {
  version: number; end: "home" | "work";
  rail: number; walk: number; drive: number;            // the selected commuters a day
  cells: Uint32Array;                                    // far-end cells, pack order
  farRail: Float32Array; farWalk: Float32Array; farDrive: Float32Array;  // per far cell
  cutRail: number; cutWalk: number; cutDrive: number;    // far cells left out
}
```

- Units as `cellModes`: commuters a day (one person, a trip each way). Walk is the whole trip on
  foot; drive is everything else.
- `rail`, `walk`, `drive` equal `cellModes` summed over the selected cells (test `flows_add_up`;
  in New York to the commuter at every selection tried). Cells beyond station reach are included
  at both ends, with walk and drive only. A cell listed twice counts once.
- The far cells plus the cut totals add up to the totals exactly. The cut is the smallest far cells
  that together hold 1% of the selection's commuters (`FLOW_CUT` in `sim/src/demand/api.rs`).
- Null when there is no solve yet or the workers have moved on to a newer network, like the other
  queries. Asked on demand, never pushed. Debug: `window.twDemand.flows(end, cells)` with `?debug=1`.

## How it is worked out

Two steps, like `cellModes`: every worker computes the rail part for its own periods
(`DemandApi::flow_rail`), the main thread adds them up, and one worker spreads all modes
(`DemandApi::flows`). Messages `flowRail` and `flows` in `workers/demandProtocol.ts`.

- **Everyone**: the zone gravity's trips from the selected cells' zones (each cell's share of its
  zone's commuters) to every zone at the other end. Within a far zone they are spread over its
  cells by commuters at that end.
- **Rail**: the selection's rail trips at its own subzones and stations (exact, the solve's own
  numbers). They are then carried along the period's station pairs to the stations at the far end,
  and from each of those to the places around it. The assumption, as in `stationRiders`: riders
  boarding at a station spread over the stations they ride to like all its riders, and riders
  leaving at a station spread over the places they walk to like all of them. Exact in total and by
  far station, a little blurred per far place.
- **Walk and drive**: what the gravity sends to each far zone less its rail. The walk share of that
  is the zone pair's walk share, less the walkers rail took (in proportion to the zone's rail). The
  blur in rail can give a far cell more rail than the gravity sends it commuters (1.7% of the
  commuters at a line's end on the test network). Walk and drive are therefore scaled to the near
  end's exact totals.

## Cost (WASM in Node 22, New York pack, after a full solve)

| selection | flow_rail, all 5 periods | flows | far cells | answer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| homes: 1 cell to 1,800 cells (an 8 km drag) | 0.4-1.5 ms | 8-18 ms | 16-28k | 250-450 kB |
| jobs: 1 cell to 1,800 cells | 0.5-6.4 ms | 9-13 ms | 74-79k | about 1.2 MB |

Hand-made and real networks give about the same times. In the app each worker runs `flow_rail` for
its own one or two periods, so a query is about 10-20 ms of worker time, then one transfer. Where
people live is spread more thinly than where they work, so a job selection's homes need about
75k cells to hold 99% of them. The answer's buffer is transferred, not copied.
