# T-3938: warm-start crowding prototype (agent warmstart, 2026-11-28)

Her 11-28 question: is there anything left that would cut load time substantially? A load's crowding rounds started
from an earlier final's loads, instead of from free flow, behind `?warmStart=K` (off by default; nothing players see
changes). Measured on the real NYC save with one solving worker. Changing convergence stays hers: nothing is switched on.

## What a load waits for now (read from the code)

- A load's solve is a whole solve with the barrier: every period is a free-flow pass (round 0) and three crowding rounds,
  each round priced on every population's averaged loads, averaged in at 1/(r+1) (`DemandApi::crowd_in`, `msa`), so a
  period's figures are the mean of four passes, the free-flow one included. A round whose loads moved under 3% (T-434) or
  that crowds nothing (T-119) is skipped. The cap's riders who would come are the free-flow pass's boardings (T-1255).
- On the dev run below New York's later rounds cost about what the first does (10-12 s a pass); T-4343's 0.4 is not seen
  with one worker. Periods 1, 3 and 4 already skip 1, 2 and 3 of their rounds as settled or uncrowded (14 passes, not 20).
- **A desktop reload with the T-328 cache already waits for no trip calculation**: the cached final is shown at once and
  the load's hold goes (`checkHold`, `cacheDone`); the solve runs on in the background and only clicks wait for it. After
  an update the last build's result stands in the same way (T-4202). The loads a player waits on are the cache misses:
  phones and tablets (no cache, T-2794), another device or browser, a file or an account copy, a pack that changed. Those
  have no earlier loads unless they travel with the save, and a save is the player's inputs only (SPEC 11): her call.
  So nothing here is stored: the prototype keeps finals in the tab only, which a re-import of the same network uses.

## What was built (tree warmstart, behind `?warmStart=K`, K = 0-3)

- Rust: `solve_warm(q, total, rounds)` on the commutes (`DemandApi`), the everyday layer and the market: the free-flow
  solve's code with its first pass priced on `total` (laid out as `crowd_with_external` takes it), then at most
  `rounds` crowding rounds; each `Period` has `rounds_left` (no limit in a cold solve), and a round past it is skipped as
  a settled one is. A warm pass on loads that crowd nothing is the free-flow solve to the bit and its rounds are skipped
  (T-119); its `seen` is the warm loads, so round 1 is skipped when the warm pass already reproduces them (T-434). The
  market's `solve_crowded` (T-1735) is unchanged. Tests: `a_warm_solve_runs_only_its_rounds` (api and everyday).
- Worker (`step`, the whole solve): round 0 of a period with a record calls `solve_warm` for every population (falling
  back to `solve`, noted), and each population's free-flow boardings come from the record, so the cap reads true free
  flow and a chain of warm loads keeps it. At each period's final (one solving worker, side "all") it sends `warmLoads`:
  the loads before the cap, each population's free-flow boardings, how the period started and its passes' ms by round.
  A shared period (two workers) never starts warm.
- Page (`demandClient`): with the flag, every solve asks for `warmLoads`; a final with all five periods is kept by
  network (`keyOf`, two at most); a load's barrier solve of a kept network posts it to the solving workers first.
  `loadAgain(fresh)` (for measuring) makes the next load of the network in hand solve again as in a new tab: a load
  of the same network is otherwise the solve in hand (`submitNetwork`'s `lastKey`), and the workers keep every
  cluster they hold (`dirty`), so the solve gets `again` and `fresh`.
- perf_bench (tree only): `PERF_WORKERS` (the demand workers, default 3) and `PERF_WARM=1` (after the first import, the
  save imported again with no final kept, the control, then warm; marks, CPU and figures of each in the run's record).

Not built: edits from the last solve's loads (an edit's whole solve is not a barrier solve; its local solve is other
code). An edit after a warm load crowds on the warm solve's trail. NA not measured (the brief named NYC).

(Two earlier cold imports, 167 and 155 s on 3 cores, gave the same 12,672,577.) In the second warm load two periods
skipped round 1 as settled.

**Why the figures move**: the cold figures are the mean of four passes, one of them free flow (weight 1/4, the
fastest lines' riders with no crowding). A warm start drops that pass: its figures are the mean of the pass priced on
the last final and the round after it. So even a perfect warm start would not land on the cold figures, and the lines
that move are the ones crowding pushes riders off: PATH, HBLR and the NEC, where a full train sends riders to a parallel
line. A second warm load moves them again (PATH 32 -9.6% then +26.4%): two passes from a warm start do not settle.

## What it would take, and the call

- **Time**: about 38% off a one-worker NYC load's trip calculation with K=1 (two passes a period), more on the second
  load of the same network (94 s against 178 s on the dev server).
- **Drift**: far past the 1% aim: 15 of 92 big lines past 5% (NEC -19%, PATH +15%, HBLR +14%), total rail trips -0.3%,
  and each later load moves them again. It is structural (the cold figures average in free flow, a warm start cannot),
  so more rounds narrow it but do not end it, and they take back the time. Figures would also depend on how many times a
  game was loaded, where today a network's figures are the same to the bit however it was reached (T-392).
- **Where the loads come from**: nowhere a waiting player has them. A desktop reload with the cache already shows its
  figures at once; the loads that wait (phones, files, another device, changed packs) have no earlier loads unless 0.46
  MB of them went into the save, which SPEC 11 keeps to the player's inputs.
- Recommendation: do not pursue. The prototype stays behind the flag for the record (or is dropped at landing).

Edits from the last solve's loads were not built: their whole solves are not barrier solves and their local solves are
other code; the drift above would apply to them too.
