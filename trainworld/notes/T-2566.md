maps-17's framing (reply to Anita): today each station carries its own local stretch and neighbours disagree (New
York: 1 in 5 hops over 2x), and the water is drawn by asking the nearest stations' stretches, so it jumps where the
nearest station changes (Hakodate's spit, the Sound as lakes then 3.75x wide, squeezed Manhattan rivers). One shared,
continuous warp removes the jumps. Known technique: focus+context metro maps (Wang and Chi 2011) deform a grid to
magnify dense areas smoothly with the background drawn through it; population cartograms likewise.
