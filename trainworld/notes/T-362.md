# T-362: a worldwide baseline for long trips between zones (design)

2026-10-14, design only, no code. Background: research/intercity-review.md, docs/regional.md 5.1,
notes/T-276.md. Experiment scripts in `notes/T-362/`, outputs in `../artifacts/trainworld/t362/`.

## Decisions for Anita

1. **One world market with a long-haul tail on the curve** (below), so every zone pair gets long
   trips. London - New York goes from 58 trips a day today to a few thousand (the target is the
   real figure, about 17,000 air seats a day of which perhaps 10,000 travel only between the two).
2. **Air as a third mode the player does not build**: every zone with a scheduled airport links to
   every other, priced door to door. Rail wins air trips only where it is quicker door to door
   (roughly under 4 hours on the train); London - New York stays at 0% rail but its bubble shows.
3. **Income**: trip rate and attraction scale with each country's GDP per head, to the power 0.7
   (China about 0.45 of the US rate, India 0.23). The alternative is one rate everywhere for now.
4. **The Northeast moves a little**: in the trial, New York - Boston and New York - Washington rise
   1.3-1.4x, Boston - Washington 1.7x (all toward NEC FUTURE), New York - Philadelphia falls ~10%.
   Accept, or pin the Northeast with a regional factor?
5. **No car between landmasses** unless a fixed link joins them (a short hand-kept list: the Channel
   Tunnel, Øresund, Japan's bridges and tunnels); ferries left out for now.

## The problem

The market is one offline pack for the Northeast's 13 zones, fitted to NHTS 2017 Northeast ground
trips; Toronto and London have `market: -1`, so they make no long trips and have no far bubbles.
Hundreds of metros are planned, so the market must cover every pair of zones from global sources,
cost nothing extra per new city, and stay cheap as pairs grow as n². The present curve also fails
far out: it was fitted to ground trips (air excluded) and decays as d^-2.37, so run over the whole
world it gives London - New York 58 trips a day, about 1/300 of the air segment figure.

## The model

**Departures.** Each resident of site *i* in country *c* makes `R_c = 0.107 x (y_c / y_US)^0.7`
departures a weekday of 25 km and up, *y* GDP per head at PPP (World Bank, global), capped at the US
value. 0.107 is the present NHTS fit. 0.7: the income elasticity of long trips is 0.5-1.0 by distance
band in Great Britain's NTS (Dargay and Clark 2012) and 0.74 across countries for international trips
(Davoine 2024). The rate stays production constrained, as now.

**Destination choice** over **the whole world**: weight `a_j x b_ij x f(d_ij)`, where

- `a_j` is attraction: inside a pack its `population + 2 x jobs` rescaled so the pack's total equals
  its population; elsewhere Kontur population; both times `(y_j / y_US)^0.7`, so long trips favour
  rich destinations as a GDP gravity does (it also curbs the pull of Asia's billions on everyone);
- `b_ij` is 1 inside a country and **0.4** between countries (flights pairs of 1,000-2,500 km: the
  international median carries 0.38-0.41 of the domestic one against the same gravity). It replaces
  the 0.5 guess for the US-Canada border;
- `f(d) = on(d) x [ max(d,25)^-2.37 + k x max(d,D1)^-G x u_j ]`: the present NHTS ground curve and
  onset (0 at 25 km, 1 at 50 km), plus a **long-haul tail**. `u_j = (cell people / 100,000)^TH` on
  Kontur H3 r5 cells weights the tail toward dense places, because long trips go to cities
  (airports, business, sights), not evenly over farmland. Trial values: k 1e-4, G 1.0, D1 300 km,
  TH 0.5 (Evidence); stage 2 fits them.

The normaliser `N_i = Σ a_k b_ik f(d_ik)` runs over a **fixed world field** (Kontur H3 r5 within
300 km, r4 to 1,000 km, r3 beyond, each cell with its country and GDP), never over the zone list. So
`N_i` depends only on where the site is; adding a zone changes no other zone's figures, only moves
trips that went to "elsewhere" into the new pack (the property of T-276's outside world, worldwide).

**Fit (stage 2)**: k, G, D1, TH and the border factor jointly to NHTS 2017 all-mode non-commute
trips by band up to 5,000 km (`research/intercity/nhts_long.json` already holds them, air included),
the flights project's segment passengers between 48 world metros over 1,000 km (times about 0.6 for
connecting passengers, judgement), and the review's NEC FUTURE pairs. Checks: MLIT's 2015 survey
(Japan: car under 300 km, rail 300-700, air beyond), US outbound trips per head, China's passenger
statistics.

**Modes**: rail, car and air in one logit at 0.025 per perceived minute (the present coefficient; in
a hand check it gives rail about 70% of rail plus air at 3 hours on the train and half near 4, the
European rule of thumb: Steer Davies Gleave 2006, Coogan 2012; stage 4 checks it properly).

- *Car*: as now (time, 15 c a km, parking, two occupants); only within one landmass (Natural Earth
  land plus the fixed links). Coach stays folded into car.
- *Air*: between zones each with an airport of scheduled service (OurAirports, public domain,
  global; packs hold airports since T-142). Door to door: the T-295 drive to the airport, 90 min at
  the airports, the flight at 750 km/h on the great circle plus 30 min, 60 min more with no direct
  route, fare $60 + 9 c a km (judgements, fitted in stage 4). Not built, no capacity, not drawn.
  The flights data are used offline only (FR24's terms forbid passing on the raw data).
- *Rail*: the player's network, as now.

No induced travel (unchanged).

## How it is computed and stored

- **Per zone pack, offline** (`pipeline/intercity.py` per pack, seconds): each site's country, rate
  and normaliser against the world field. Stored in the pack, not a shared market pack. A new zone
  needs only its own pack built; nothing else is rebuilt. The world field (~400k cells, ~6 MB) is a
  pipeline input, never shipped.
- **Near pairs** (zone centres under 300 km apart): 6 km zone to 6 km zone as now, built only
  between served zones. A zone has a handful of such neighbours, so these grow with n, not n².
- **Far pairs** (300 km and over): zone to zone. A far pair's trips are
  `b_IJ f(d_IJ) a_J Σ_{i in I} O_i / N_i`, so one number per zone (`Σ O_i/N_i`) and the centres
  give every far pair. Inside each end the trips spread over its 6 km zones by departures and
  attraction share (error of order zone width over distance, under 10% at 300 km).
- **Registry row per zone**: `Σ O/N`, attraction, centre, country, airport flag (~50 bytes; 1,000
  zones are 50 KB), so the far-zoomed bubbles and the inspector compute any zone's row of all-mode
  trips (n multiplications) with nothing opened.
- **Scaling**: far zone pairs are n²/2 (500k at 1,000 zones) but the solve builds them only between
  served zones (200 served zones: 20k pairs, a few ms a period). Air and car costs of a far pair do
  not depend on the player, so they are computed once when the pair is first served and kept.

## What changes for the Northeast

Near pairs barely move: the tail is small under 300 km and the normaliser grows a few percent. Long
pairs rise toward NEC FUTURE (Evidence). T-299's seam trips (New York - Philadelphia 380k against
NEC FUTURE's 57k) are mostly a definition gap, 25-80 km trips across a pack seam, which this does not
change; its comparison is stage 5. T-214's commuter corridors are T-276's commutes plus T-295.

## Park-and-ride (T-295), the assumed interface

T-295 supplies, for a site and a station, an access cost in perceived minutes (walk, or drive plus
a parking penalty, whichever is cheaper, flattening with distance) and the stations a site may use
(within the drive catchment). This model uses the same function for rail access at both ends and
for the drive to an airport, and needs nothing else. Far pairs use it per 6 km zone, so one station
in a city catches a share of the whole city's long trips.

## What the player sees

- Zoomed far out every zone has its bubble, Toronto and London included; a click on London draws a
  bubble per other zone with its long trips, New York among them, coloured by train share.
- The inspector's "Inter-region trips" gains the mode split (train, car, plane icons with hovers)
  per place and an "Elsewhere" row for trips to places with no zone. No new panels.

## Stages (each its own task, T-375 to T-379, proposed)

1. World field: Kontur r5/r4/r3 with country ids (geoBoundaries ADM0), GDP per head, landmass ids
   and fixed links. Small, half a day.
2. Fit k, G, D1, TH and the border factor (the scripts here are the start). Medium, a day.
3. Per-pack normalisers and far pairs in the kernel and pipeline; Toronto and London join the
   market. Large, two days; replaces the Northeast pack's derived block.
4. Air as a mode, constants checked on the rail-air share curve; the inspector's mode split. Medium.
5. Northeast checks: T-299's comparison over 80 km centre to centre, the review table re-run. Small.

## Risks

- **Segments are not trips**: hubs carry connecting passengers; the 0.6 is a guess, and pairs with
  no direct flight drop out of the fit.
- **The tail sends trips across oceans easily**: with G 0.5 most departures went over 5,000 km.
  Income-weighted attraction and the border factor should hold it; stage 2 must check outbound
  trips per head (US about 0.3 international air trips a person a year).
- **Income elasticity** comes from rich countries; density stands in for "city" (GHSL urban centres
  if too coarse); production constrained means an isolated city travels as much as any. Tier 0.

- Today's curve: Northeast residents' trips over 1,000 km are 0.0012 a day against NHTS all-mode
  0.0030 (1,000-5,000 km); air segments over 1,000 km run a median 14x the model on domestic pairs
  (1,000-2,500 km) and 100x at 2,500-6,000 km.
- Trial tail: NHTS bands 500-700 / 700-1,000 / 1,000-1,500 / 1,500-2,500 / 2,500-5,000 km come out
  0.0020 / 0.0010 / 0.0009 / 0.0009 / 0.0024 against 0.0026 / 0.0018 / 0.0010 / 0.0011 / 0.0009;
  domestic air pairs over 1,000 km still 5.9x the model (median), so big cities need more pull.
- A flatter tail (G 0.5) matches the long air pairs better but sends 50-90% of all departures over
  1,000 km, mostly over 5,000 km: rejected. Hence income-weighted attraction and the border factor.

## Anita's decisions (2026-10-14)

1. **One world market** with the long-haul tail.
2. **Very far pairs** (no rail can compete with air) are shown for completeness but cost little: computed coarsely and never in the crowding rounds.
3. **No driving between landmasses**, but find a precompiled source of fixed links rather than a hand-kept list (for example road connectivity from OSM's routable network, or an existing dataset of bridges and tunnels between islands); T-375 looks first.
4. Order: London stage b (T-360) first, then this, then the crossover tool.
6. (2026-10-15) **A pair is served** only when both ends reach stations on one connected network:
   actual track joining them. Unserved pairs' trips are fixed offline (a row per zone in the
   registry) and never enter the solve. **Clusters merge only through track**, never through the
   market's trips alone, so the world never becomes one cluster (T-326). The near-pair cutoff
   does not depend on density (only distance changes how people travel) and is **250 km**
   everywhere (Anita, 2026-10-15; from 300). **Shared trains between long trips and local riders
   (direction agreed, details deferred):** cities stay separate solves, coupled loosely. Each city
   takes the long-trip riders on its trains as a background load from the last long-trip solve,
   and a neighbour re-solves only when that load moves past a few percent. Anita's refinement: the
   long-trip riders will mostly crowd one or two intercity lines, while every other line sees a
   few percent, so only lines where long trips are a large share of the load might be solved
   jointly, and the rest take the background. Stage 3 works this out and measures it against a
   fully joint solve.
5. Not answered, decided by the orchestrator on the agent's picks: air as a third mode the player does not build; trip rate and destination pull scaled by GDP per head^0.7; the Northeast's long pairs allowed to rise 1.3-1.7x (towards NEC FUTURE) rather than pinned.
