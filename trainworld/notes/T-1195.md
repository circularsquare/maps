# T-1195 The loading bar in stages (with T-1194, T-1196, T-1197)

Anita, about the fifth time: the bar's last few percent take a large share of the wait; "why don't we
just make it bigger?". Measured 2026-10-24 on my Vite (6146), headless Chrome, a fresh profile per
save, the save put in a slot and picked from the start menu: the first load (nothing remembered) and
a second pick of the same slot (the slot's last load remembered, packs in the HTTP cache). The card's
title, step and fill read every 100 ms. The machine was busy with other agents' builds and pages:
every load here takes two to three times what it takes on a quiet desktop (the real New York save
85 s against T-884's 30 s), so read the shapes, not the seconds. "Before" is the last commit's
loading code, served from a copy of the app with the files this work changed taken from HEAD
(scratchpad `before-app.mjs`); "after" is the main tree.

## Why the earlier fixes did not stick

T-243, T-793 and T-884 each tuned one formula on one or two saves. T-884's bar was the time so far
against the time so far plus the time still expected; it is only as good as "the time still
expected", which came from a step model whose solve rounds were weighted 1, 1.1, 1.05, 0.55 (New
York's rounds in October). Today every save's first round takes three to five times each later one
(New York 30.5 / 10.8 / 9.0 / 8.1 s; the world save 44.7 / 13.6 / 13.2 / 15.2; China 71 / 14.4 /
14.2 / 15.0), so while round 0 ran the expectation kept growing and the bar stood still (New York 37
s at 54%), then the later rounds jumped it. On China the step model put the 169 packs' opening at
a sixth of its real length: the bar sat near 50% for five minutes, jumped to 95% when the solve
started, and stood there 115 s, a quarter of the wait above 90%. A remembered wait helped only
loads of the same slot. And the share measured (time above 90%) passed bars that were wrong
elsewhere.

## What changed

- **Stages** (`game/loadBar.ts`, `workers/demandClient.ts` `jobProgress`): the game (pick to the
  network reaching demand), the city data (downloads and every pack open in every worker) and the
  solve (to the zone in view's final). Each gets the share of the bar its expected time gives it, and
  the stage in hand moves inside its share only: by what has landed (bytes; each pack opened in each
  solving worker, now reported one by one, `packOpened`; solve rounds and periods) or by its time
  against its expectation, creeping when late, never past its share until done. A late stage slows
  the bar where it is, instead of the last stage waiting near full.
- **Expected times**: the slot's last load, stage by stage (`anitabuilder-load-wait`), when the
  network is within a fifth of its stops; otherwise the model, recalibrated here: rounds 1, 0.28,
  0.28, 0.28; each city pack 450 ms plus 220 ms per MB (market packs as before, 20 ms per MB and
  1.5 s for the load's composition);
  the first estimate 1 s plus 3.5 ms per stop, so the solve's share against the data's matches the
  three saves; the data time scaled by how this browser's last load compared with the model
  (`anitabuilder-load-pace`). Before the network is in: 6/24/70.
- **The card** (T-1194) says the stage: "Loading", "Loading city data", "Calculating demand". No
  count: the packs open in one batch per worker, so "(0/32)" never moved until the end.
