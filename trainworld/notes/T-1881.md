# T-1881, T-1889, T-1944: building hills in the hillshade and the price; T-1856 hexes (written up)

Anita 2026-10-30: "tokyo and nagoya seem to have some hills in suspiciously building dense areas... also
shanghai"; T-1889 (East River at 42nd St priced as hills and mountains), T-1944 (Midtown 6th Ave and
45th St priced as mountains). Anita 2026-11-02: "we should definitely attempt to fix hills".

## Causes

Two surface models, two problems.

- **The price** (T-1889, T-1944): GEDTM30, sold as bare earth, still carries tower blocks in some
  centres. Midtown Manhattan is one blob 1-1.5 km wide standing 50-100 m above its streets (its 240 m
  file too); the Loop and Lower Manhattan the same. The T-1854 cap (the slope between 1/240 degree
  mean elevations) failed because those means are tower-inflated too. Other centres are milder:
  Sydney, Canary Wharf, Yeouido, LA, Toronto 10-50 m; Shanghai's Lujiazui, Dubai, Bangkok, Nagoya, Tokyo
  under 15 m, which the cap already held flat. The 42nd St East River case was the UN and Tudor City
  blocks on the Manhattan bank, not the bank itself: once the towers go, it is flat.
- **The hillshade** (T-1881): the public Terrain Tiles are 3DEP (bare lidar) in the US, so Midtown's
  shade was clean; in Asia they are SRTM, a radar surface model: speckle everywhere and bumps on
  every tower cluster and raised expressway (Nagoya's loop shows as lines). The classes there were
  already flat.

## Fix, the price: tower fields cut from the 30 m surface (terrain_fine.py `tower_ground`)

Per 1 degree block, before the slope and elevation planes, read with 64 px (1.9 km) of margin:
built-up (GHSL GHS-BUILT-V E2020 at 30 arc seconds, gross building height = volume / cell area at
least 5 m) AND rough (rms of the surface about its 60 m smoothing, over 150 m: ramp 5.5-8.5 m) AND
gentle (the low envelope's own slope, ramp 3-6 degrees), spread 300 m and blurred 150 m; the cut
pixel takes the envelope (grey opening of 600 m half-width, smoothed, never above the surface).

Measured roughness at 30 m, centre 1 km (p50 / p90): Manhattan 8.0 / 11.8, Chicago 6.5 / 10.1,
Sydney 3.9 / 6.9, Canary Wharf 2.5 / 6.3, London 4.1 / 5.1, Tokyo 3.0 / 3.5, Shinjuku 3.5 / 4.4,
SF 2.5 / 4.4, Seoul 3.8 / 6.0, HK 5.9 / 8.4, Wellington 3.5 / 4.8.

What did not work (scratch tests, cap2.py, g30.py):
- A cap ground of mean elevation minus k x GHSL height (gross or ANBH net height), envelope at 1.4 km:
  GHSL's heights are far too low in Midtown (gross 8-16 m at 460 m against 50-90 m of excess) and
  no k tells Midtown from built hills; at k = 3 on ANBH, Midtown went 98% flat but SF's centre went
  from 28% to 57% flat and Seoul's from 35% to 79%.
- Rough + built with a 500 m-blurred built mask: hit Namsan and Wellington's hills (mountains 36% ->
  19%). The 1 km GHSL mask and the gentle-envelope gate fixed most of that.
- GEDTM30's std layer (`gedtm_rf_std_30m`, 566 GB): high on hills too (Seoul centre p50 3.8).
- FABDEM (Copernicus minus buildings and trees): CC BY-NC-SA, and the project ships no non-commercial
  data (GADM and NUTS polygons were ruled out the same way). Copernicus GLO-30: a surface model.

The lie: a rough built-up patch on gentle ground narrower than about 1.2 km is cut to its
surroundings, so a small rocky knoll inside a dense district (none found) would price flat.

## Fix, the hillshade: drawn flat where the price is flat (map/demFlat.ts)

From tile zoom 10, a Terrain Tiles tile that meets loaded class grids goes to a worker with its 8
neighbours and a mask of how flat its ground is (each class cell the share of flat cells within two
cells, bilinear between centres); heights where flat are blurred (sigma 400 m, three box passes, the
sea floor held at 0 so shores are not dragged down). An opening first was tried and dropped: its flat
tops left blotches and lone hilly cells showed as dots. Tiles wait up to 1.5 s for their zones'
grids (realms in the game only) and are drawn again (`map.refreshTiles`) when a late grid lands.

## The world rebuild (for the orchestrator, after the Philippines run reports)

The tower filter changes the intermediate, so the 30 m pass runs again on AWS (same as T-1854 section 6).
1. `trainworld\pipeline\terrain_fine.py blocks <scratch>\blocks.json` (all blocks; the margin adds tiles)
2. a code tgz with `trainworld/pipeline/terrain_fine.py` and `blocks.json` at its root, as t1854-terrain-1
3. `trainworld\tools\aws\launch.py t1881-terrain-1 --kind terrain --type m7a.4xlarge --hours 4 --disk 40
   --file code=<tgz> --file reqs=trainworld\tools\aws\requirements.txt --put manifest --put part0 ...
   --put part7` (the machine fetches GHSL's 200 MB zip itself)
4. `bucket.py get` the manifest and parts; back up `data/work/terrain-fine/` and `data/terrain/` to
   `data/work/pre-T1881/`; put the new parts and manifest in `data/work/terrain-fine/`
5. `zone_terrain.py build --force --jobs 8` (about 4 minutes), `zone_terrain.py mix` (expect the
   world mix within a few tenths of a point of 59.8 / 14.3 / 10.7 / 15.1), smoke, rebuild 8800
6. delete the backups once checked; delete the GHSL file (`data/raw/ghsl/`) if nothing else uses it
Time: about 40 minutes on the machine (fetch-bound, 80 GB plus the margin), under US$1.

## T-1856, hexagons: design (not built)

- Lattice: pointy-top hexes in degree space, the same cell area as today's 1/960 squares (side
  0.6204/960 degree; rows 1.5 x side apart, so about 1,032 rows a degree; centres √3 x side apart,
  odd rows offset half a cell). Like today's squares they narrow with latitude; one world lattice, so
  overlapping zones agree.
- File: `TWT2`, the same header plus the lattice kind; rows of run-lengths as now (a hex row is a
  row of cells), so file sizes and decode cost stay the same.
- Lookup (sim `ClassGrid::at`, app `classAt`): the point's two candidate rows, then the nearest of at
  most three centres: a few float operations, no table.
- Classes: each hex's mean slope sampled from the fine 115 m plane (the mean of the fine cells whose
  centres fall in it), the majority filter over the 6 neighbours plus itself (ties keep its own), the
  coarse cap as now.
- Tint (`terrainTint.ts`): the same per-pixel loop with the hex lookup.
- About a day: pipeline, sim decode and lookup (no WASM interface change), app decode and tint, a check.
  Prices stay what the squares give within a cell's edge; build it after the tower rebuild lands so a
  shape change is not mixed with the tower change.
