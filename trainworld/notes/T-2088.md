## How each side is counted

- **Game trips** = `view.railTripsPerDay`, a journey once however many lines it uses. That sets against the
  CSV's *entries* and *linked* rows.
- **Real total** = the city's CSV rows summed. Unlinked rows on a metro, light rail or tram (every boarding
  counted, a change counted again) are divided by 1.5 to make them entries-equivalent, since the CSV puts unlinked
  at 1.3-1.9 times entries; unlinked commuter rail rows stay as listed (a railway trip is one boarding). Shares of the
  real total that were unlinked: New York 21% (LIRR, Metro-North, NJ Transit, PATH, NJ light rail), Seoul 70%
  (Seoul Metro, Incheon), Toronto 87% (TTC subway and streetcars); the others none. As listed with no division the
  ratios are New York 2.37, Seoul 1.06, Toronto 0.83.
- **Tokyo's real rows** are MLIT S12 operator entries (FY2019, before telework): a change between operators counts
  twice, which T-1742 put at about 1.25 times journeys. Left as listed, so 0.36 is a floor; on journeys it is about 0.45.
  Osaka, Nagoya and the other Japanese cities are read from the jpn7 save, below.
- **Riders per km**: the real row is weekday over `length_km`. The game side is the metro lines' riders over the km of
  track those lines use, shared track once and parallel edges within about 30 m once (`uniqueKm`; the saves
  set lines side by side on shared real track, so a plain sum of edge lengths is 17-30% higher). Against an
  entries or linked row (New York, London, Paris, Tokyo) the riders are the metro lines' boardings turned into
  journeys at the network's journeys per boarding (0.48-0.54, 0.79 in Sendai); against an unlinked row (Toronto) they are
  the boardings themselves. The game's line boardings run high by the way the saves cut one real line into pieces (T-1742).
- **Which game lines are the metro**: New York's lettered and numbered lines (30), the Tube's eleven names (29 pieces),
  Paris's numbered lines (20), Tokyo Metro plus Toei's Oedo, Asakusa and Mita (13), Seoul's "Line n" (21 pieces),
  Toronto's 1, 2 and 4, Sendai's two subway lines. Game metro km: New York 331 (table 399), London 357 (402), Paris 195
  (246), Tokyo 246 (304), Toronto 67 (70.5), Sendai 28.

## Where the game is high or low

- **New York is high by about 2.4 times** on trips (0.53 a resident against 0.22) and 2.7 times on the subway's
  load per km (25,100 against 9,400). Its subway carries about 8.3M journeys in the game against the MTA's 3.7M entries. This matches T-1742
  (2.4-2.7).
- **Tokyo is low by about 0.36-0.45 times** on trips (0.41 against 1.13 on operator entries) and 0.45 on the metro
  per km (15,200 against 33,600). T-1742 had 0.38-0.47 on the same sources.
- **London, Paris, Toronto, Seoul and Sendai are inside 0.68-1.39.** London 1.08 on trips, Paris 0.82, Toronto 1.16
  (0.83 as listed), Seoul 1.39 (1.06 as listed), Sendai 0.68. London's real total includes ORR's whole London and South East sector, which
  reaches past the pack, so its ratio is somewhat low. Paris and London metro loads per km are 1.24 and 1.33
  times the table; Paris's real km (246) is more than the game's metro track (195), so its per-km ratio is flattered.
- **The game spans 0.22-0.80 trips a resident across these seven while real cities span 0.19-1.13**: the same narrow band
  as T-1742 and `research/ridership.md`. New York at the bottom of real use for its network size is the high end of the
  ratios, Tokyo at the top of real use is the low end.
- **Run against the earlier numbers**: New York 11.13M rail trips (T-1742 12.39M), London 9.03M (9.79M), Paris 8.62M (9.05M),
  Tokyo 15.12M (16.11M), Seoul 11.87M (13.03M), Toronto 1.38M (1.55M), Sendai 0.37M (0.44M). Each is 5-16% below,
  from demand changes since 2026-10-28 (both read a weekday; I did not look for which change). The ratios here are
  therefore a little lower than T-1742's.

## Limits

- The CSV's own: years 2019-2025, some Seoul and Korail rows with no year, weekday estimated as annual / 300 or / 340
  where no weekday is published, and per-resident figures good to about a factor of 1.5 (ridership.md "Caveats").
- Row coverage: the game saves include lines the CSV has no row for (Staten Island Railway) and the CSV rows can cover
  more than the pack (NJ Transit rail, ORR's sector). The ratio moves by those shares and no more.
- The 1.5 divisor for unlinked metro rows is a mid-point of a 1.3-1.9 range; Seoul and Toronto move from 1.39 to 1.06 and from 1.16 to
  0.83 between the two readings, so their placement inside the band does not depend on it.
- Riders per km uses one metro row a city. Commuter rail per km (the CSV gives few lengths) and the new car-centred
  US save the task asked for are not here.

## The Japanese cities (T-2093 part 1, 2026-11-03)

`node trainworld/checks/t2088-ridership.mjs jpn7` (the five cities plus Tokyo and Sendai out of the same run; json in
`../artifacts/trainworld/t2088/`: one file a city, `summary-jpn7.json`, and `jpn7-stations.json` with every station's
weekday and average-day boardings and transfers). The save is `data/work/realnet/jpn7_real.save` (491 lines, 5,992
stations in the game); the solve took 6.5 minutes (weekday final, round 3), 18.5M rail trips over all seven packs.

**How the circle is matched.** The CSV's Japanese rows are MLIT S12 sums of station entries (boardings plus alightings
halved, FY2019) inside a circle round the main station: Osaka 40 km, Nagoya 30, Tokyo 50, the others 20. The check
sums the game's stations inside the same circle (same centre, same radius, great-circle distance from each station's
lng/lat). I re-summed the S12 file with those circles and got the CSV's totals (Osaka 10.97M, Nagoya 3.10M, Fukuoka
1.06M, Sapporo 0.90M, Hiroshima 0.46M). The game's entries at a station are its boardings minus the riders changing
line there, which is a journey once (summed over a whole network it is within 1% of `railTripsPerDay`). S12 counts
each operator's gate entries, so a change between operators counts twice in S12: the ratio is a floor, as Tokyo's is.
The game's station boardings (every change counted) are the ceiling. Every S12 station in each circle has a game
station within about 1 km (100% by entries), so the pack cut does not drop stations. Population is the CSV's, both sides.
S12 is an average day of the year; the main column is the weekday like the other cities and the average day is beside it
(the game's average day is 8% under its weekday). No riders per km: the CSV has no length for these rows.

NOTE lines the check printed: every row but Hiroshima and Sendai on the weekday, which are just inside 0.5.

**What they show.** All five are low, 0.35-0.52, the same side as Tokyo (0.36) and Sendai (0.68): in Japan the
game carries about 40-50% of the station entries the S12 file counts, and about 60-85% even if every change of
line is counted as a trip. The cities with the most operators (Osaka, Nagoya) are lowest, where S12's double
counting of changes between operators is also biggest; the smaller cities sit at about 0.5.
**The jpn7 network is itself lower than the dedicated saves**: Tokyo out of this run is 0.26 against 0.36 from `tyo.save`
(10.85M entries against 15.12M journeys), Sendai 0.51 against 0.68, a factor of 0.72-0.75 both times. The jpn7 lines are
N02 with services from MLIT counts (T-737, "rough"), not the timetable-built saves. Scaled by that factor Osaka would be about
0.48, Nagoya 0.58, Fukuoka 0.67, Sapporo 0.59, Hiroshima 0.70, so the ratios for the five are best read as
0.35-0.52 as run and about 0.5-0.7 on a network built like Tokyo's. I did not look into why jpn7 is lower.
