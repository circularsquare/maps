## Simple prices

The game's rule that fares follow a base plus per km stays the default: a game nobody changes keeps it exactly.
Points, Steps and fare zones are things a player opts into, the departure from that rule she asked for here.

## The good news first

The demand side already takes any fare curve: the sim is handed the curve as a list of points of any length, and
it prices each station pair at its trip length on that curve (since T-883). Only the game's own tidying holds the
curve to three points today. So curve points and steps need no sim change. Fare zones do need one, but T-3578 (the
line multiple on the base) built exactly the kind of per-station-pair price they need, so that is a copy of a
known pattern.

## 1. Advanced fare panel (T-3739)

**Opening it.** An "Advanced" button beside the Fare heading in the Money tab opens a panel in the middle of the
screen, the same kind as the detailed schedule (closed by its x, Esc or a click outside). The Money tab's fare
controls stay as they are.

**What's in it**, top to bottom:

1. The fare graph, bigger, the same zoom and drag rules as the Money tab's. A country picker above it ("Fares in
   Japan") says whose fares the graph and rows show, starting on the Money tab's country.
2. A shape switch: **Lines** (straight between points, as now) or **Steps** (each distance band has one fare).
3. One row per point, as the Money tab's rows, each with a remove icon, and an "Add point" button under them.
4. High speed, Ferry, Bus and Transfer fare rows, the same as the Money tab's.
5. **Fares by country**: every country with a built station, the reference country first, each with its multiple
   stepper, anchor (make it the reference) and undo icon, all as the Money tab's one row does now, plus the base
   fare charged there. A "Show all countries" switch lists the rest.
6. Fare zones (T-3740), once built.

**Points on the curve.**

- Add: click the line on the graph where there is no point (a tag says "Click the line to add a point"), or press
  "Add point", which puts one halfway along the longest stretch (for touch).
- Move: drag, the same one-way drags as now (sideways for distance, up or down for fare).
- Remove: the row's remove icon, or right-click the point. The base fare point can't be removed.
- 1 to 12 points. Fares never fall with distance, as now.
- Every change is one fare edit: one demand recalculation on release, and Ctrl+Z takes it back.

**Steps.** In Steps each point starts a band: the fare is flat from it to the next point, then jumps. So Tokyo
Metro's fares are five points: up to 6 km $1.80, 6-11 km $2.10, 11-19 km $2.60 and so on. Past the last point the
fare stays flat unless the "Past 40 km" row's per-km rate is set above $0.00. One point in Steps is a flat fare for
every trip. Switching shape keeps the points; under Steps each point charges its own fare to the next one.

How steps reach the sim: each jump is sent as two points 0.1 km apart (the sim needs distances to rise), with a
closing point far out so the curve doesn't carry on at the jump's slope. The first band is always flat, so route
search, which charges the first stretch's slope on every ride, charges no per-km fare at all under Steps.

Lines has the same trap today: a second point 0.1 km out with a big jump puts a huge per-km rate on every ride.
Route search's rate becomes the first stretch's slope but no steeper than the curve's average slope over its first
10 km; what riders pay stays exact, since each pair's extra makes up the difference. The default straight line is
unchanged by this. A minimum fare is a Steps job.

Every new typed number is held to `FARE_MAX` (US$1,000) as the fares are since T-3303: each point, the past-the-last
rate (through its far-out closing point), and later each zone price.

**What nothing-changes means.** A game whose player never opens the panel keeps its three points, Lines, no new
save fields. The sim gets the same input as now and the real save's trips a day are the same to the trip. That is
the check the builder runs.

**How demand and the trip explorer read it.** Unchanged: each station pair pays the curve at its trip length (km of
track ridden), the trip explorer shows each route's fare at its own length, and income pays lines the same way.
Two things to know:

- A station pair whose riders split between routes is priced at their average length, so a pair whose routes are
  5.8 and 6.3 km long, with a step at 6 km, pays the fare at about 6.05 km. A player could only see it in the
  trip explorer's figures for that one pair.
- The fare estimate while dragging ("Riders a day -6%") reads each line's average ride, which is too blunt for
  steps: moving a step across that average would jump and moving it anywhere else would show nothing. It gets
  averaged over a spread of lengths around each line's average (an hour of work, in stage F1).

**Saves.** The points as placed (1 to 12), plus `shape: "steps"` and the past-the-last rate only when set. An older
build loading a save with other than three points turns it into a straight line through its first and last
point; saves are hers, so nothing more.

**The Money tab once a curve is custom** (more or fewer than three points, or Steps): the graph still shows and
drags it; the rows under it give way to one line, "Set in Advanced fares", with the Advanced button. Going back
is in the panel: "Use the simple curve" asks first ("Go back to three points at 0, 20 and 100 km? The other
points are dropped."), keeping the fares at those distances. The same rule as the schedule panel: moving up keeps
everything, moving down asks.

## 2. Fare zones (T-3740)

The word "zone" already means one of the game's city areas in player text ("new zones!"), so these are always
**fare zones**, and a set of them belonging together is a **fare area** ("Tokyo fare zones").

### Ways to draw them

- **A. Paint stations.** A Fare zones tool: pick a fare area and a zone number, then click stations or drag a
  loop around many to give them that zone (tap on touch; two fingers still pan). The map tints the ground around
  each painted station in its zone's colour with the number on it. Exact and cheap; shapes are as free as the
  player likes; Cities in Motion 2 does this (painted, up to four zones). What it lacks: new stations have no zone
  until painted (their panel says so, with a stepper to set one); the tint is a coloured patch around each station,
  not a zone outline, since the game has no geometry library to merge them; and the closest game, Subway Builder,
  ships drawn zones instead. Each click, loop or rings press is one edit, so one recalculation of trips.
- **B. Rings.** Pick a centre station and a ring width (5 km); every station gets its zone from its distance:
  London and Hamburg in one click. New stations get their ring by themselves. Can't do odd shapes.
- **C. Drawn outlines.** Draw each zone's outline on the map like a lasso; stations take the zone they sit in, new
  ones too. The most like a real zone map and what "draw" sounds like, but the most work: editing outlines, shared
  borders between neighbours, touch. Subway Builder added drawn zones in v1.7 (how they charge: not found).

All three give the sim the same thing, each station's area and zone number, so the charging works the same
whichever is built and they can be added one after another.

### How a trip's zone count is worked out

- **i. Start and end zones**: zones counted = from the lower number to the higher, so zone 1 to zone 3 is 3 zones,
  2 to 2 is 1. Priced per station pair, which is exactly what the sim can already do.
- **ii. Zones the route passes**: a trip from zone 2 north to zone 2 south through zone 1 pays for 2 zones
  (London's rule). Needs a pass over each route like T-3578's, and is fuzzy where riders split between routes.
- **iii. A table per zone pair**: any price for any two zones. Most flexible (honeycomb systems, odd pairs), but a
  10-zone area is 55 prices.

### What a trip pays

The zone price takes the distance curve's place in today's formula, and nothing else changes:

    fare = base x M + (zone price - base) x (1 + (m - 1) x share of km on high-speed lines)

M is the largest own multiple among the lines ridden, m the high-speed multiplier (ferry and bus the same way),
plus the transfer fare at each change, times the starting country's scale. Each area has its own price list, one
price per zone count ("1 zone $2.80, 2 zones $3.40 ..."), never falling, at most `FARE_MAX`, and never below the
base fare, held when read as the transfer fare is (a base raised later lifts them). A new area
starts its prices at the curve's fares at 5, 10, 15 km and so on, so painting zones doesn't jump fares.

So high-speed and line multiples, the transfer fare, the base's share between lines and the never-below-zero
income rule all keep working. In the sim the pair's price moves by (zone price - curve at its trip length), the
same slot T-3578 uses; income pays it to the lines by km as the curve's extra is paid (the `fareX` channel), so
each line's cover, and so its points (T-3657), count zone fares like any other. The lie: inside an area,
riders choosing between routes still lean a little toward fewer track-km, since route search charges per km
on each ride; what they pay is exact.

### Across cities

- A trip with both ends in **one fare area** pays by zones; any other trip (one end outside, or two areas) pays
  the distance curve for the whole trip, as real systems do (a Verbund ticket stops at its edge, long trips go
  by distance on DB or JR).
- A fare area is the player's, not tied to a game zone, so one area can cover Buenos Aires and La Plata, or the
  whole Ruhr, and two cities can have one each. Its default name is the city of its first painted station.
- That means zone fares must also reach the regional market's trips between game zones inside one area. T-3578's
  term reached the market through the same pair prices, so this is planned in; if it turns out costly, the
  fallback is local trips only, with market trips paying distance (a few hours less).

### Recommended

**A (paint stations) with B's rings as a one-click helper, counted by i (start and end zones), charged as the zone
price in the curve's place.** Reasons: painting is the cheapest real drawing, works the same at any size and on
touch, and rings give London or Hamburg at once; i is what the sim already prices per station pair, so it costs
the least and is exact for any trip that doesn't dip toward the centre and back out; putting the zone price in the
curve's place keeps every multiplier and income rule with no special cases. C (drawn outlines), ii (via the
centre) and iii (a pair table) can follow on the same engine if players want them.

   jump switch on every point, and twelve points of Lines can already get close to any shape.

## 4. Stages and hours

per-line multiple (T-3567) under 2 hours, the line multiple on the base (T-3578, the same kind of sim change zones
