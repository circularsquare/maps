# T-1270 Terrain: design and cost

Design 2026-10-25; Anita approved it as designed with every default the same day ("this terrain
approach makes sense, we can do it"). Stages 1-3 are built (below, "Built"; stage 3's tint,
2026-10-26, is in docs/ui-map.md); 4 (the mountain speed cap) stays open.

## Built (stages 1-2, 2026-10-25, tree t1270)

- **Prices**: `TERRAIN_FACTOR` [1, 1.25, 1.6, 2.5] in `sim/src/track/params.rs`, applied in
  `cost::track_cost_parts_t` to levels -1 and up, never over water; ramps charged at the dearer end
  with the factor counted. Per-edge spans `Network::edge_terrain`; `TrackApi::add_terrain` reprices
  the edges inside a grid's box. The blueprint breakdown gets kind 6 rows (the level field holds the
  class), the tool's preview `preview_ground` (metres per class) for the tag's chip.
- **Data**: `pipeline/zone_terrain.py` reads GEDTM30 240 m (1/450 degree pixels, float32 metres,
  2048 px deflate tiles) tile by tile, falling back to a Zenodo range request for a tile the local
  file does not have yet. Lattice 1/240 degree, thresholds 2 / 5 / 10 degrees of mean slope, 3 x 3
  majority. Built for all 1,770 zones in 33 minutes on one core: 56 MB in all, median 13 KB a zone,
  90th percentile 58 KB, largest 1.9 MB (`cea`); cells 52% flat, 16% rolling, 13% hilly, 20%
  mountain. The 10 GB download (MD5 checked against Zenodo's) took about two hours at 0.6-3 MB/s;
  Zenodo answered Python's urllib with 403 but curl with ranges throughout.
  Probes within 8 km (flat / rolling / hilly / mountain): Tokyo 100/0/0/0, Amsterdam 100/0/0/0,
  Copenhagen 100/0/0/0, Denver 97/3/0/0, San Francisco 62/24/10/3, Seoul 50/26/16/7, Zurich
  35/28/32/6, Pittsburgh 12/60/28/1, Hong Kong 42/8/12/38 (the flat share is mostly sea), Oslo
  (10 km) 28/41/27/4, Bergen 18/17/23/42; within 6 km of Midtown 86/10/4/0 (the Palisades and upper
  Manhattan's ridges). The real New York save's built value rises a little with that rolling
  ground; nothing already paid changes.
- **Runtime**: the clock worker asks for `terrain/<id>.terrain` whenever it asks for a mask, installs it
  between messages, and sends the state when the blueprint quote moved; Construct (all, a line, a
  piece) waits for the grids under the blueprint first. Vite serves `data/terrain` at `/terrain` (a
  missing file is a 404) and a build copies the registry zones' files into `dist/terrain/`.
- **Hillshade**: Settings > Show > Terrain, off by default; MapLibre `hillshade` over the land
  cover, the water drawn again above it. Tiles: **Terrain Tiles on AWS Open Data** (Terrarium PNG,
  z0-12 fetched). Mapterhorn's hosted tiles were not used: its site, README and attribution page
  state no terms for an app's use of `tiles.mapterhorn.com`. **R2 fallback, planned not built**: cut
  a Terrarium PNG pyramid z0-10 from the same GEDTM30 file (about 1-3 GB at a guess, land only),
  put it on R2 `anitamaps` beside the published game, and point `TERRAIN_TILES` at it before
  publishing if AWS's bucket ever goes or its terms change.
- **Check**: `checks/t1270-terrain.mjs` (flat 45, mountain ground 112.5, tunnel 99 US$M a km;
  Construct charges the quote; screenshots). Anita asked how doable an elevation map is: a rough ruggedness that makes
track dearer, an optional topographic layer, the price perhaps applied after placing, and perhaps
tunnels through mountains.

## For Anita

- **Doable, and small.** The smallest useful version is a hillshade you can switch on (a day's work,
  no data of ours) plus a terrain class per half-kilometre (flat, rolling, hilly, mountain) that
  multiplies the price of track at or near the ground: 1x, 1.25x, 1.6x, 2.5x. (Built: a median of
  13 KB a zone, 56 MB in all, from one global bare-earth elevation file in 33 minutes; the design
  guessed 2-6 KB, but rough ground makes shorter runs.)
- **Tunnels through mountains need nothing new.** Bored tunnels (elevation -2 and -3) take no
  terrain factor, so in the mountain class a tunnel (US$99M a km) is cheaper than track on the
  ground (US$112M) or a viaduct (US$225M), and players will tunnel through mountains on their own.
  In flat, rolling and hilly ground the surface stays cheaper, as it is in life.
- **Building never waits.** The class rides along with the water mask, so it is nearly always
  loaded before the cursor gets there; when it is not, the blueprint is priced flat and repriced
  when it lands. Blueprints are free, so only Construct needs it, and Construct waits for it.
- **Not recommended:** real heights (track at absolute heights, gradients from the ground,
  automatic tunnels and bridges). That is a different track model, the reason NIMBY Rails never did
  it, and the cost model above gets most of the play.

Questions for Anita are at the end.

## What the game does now

- Ground is flat (docs/track-and-service.md 6.1). Elevations -3 to +3 are 8 m levels relative to the
  local ground, so a -2 tunnel always lies 16 m under the surface wherever the surface is.
- Track price = US$90M a km x the level's multiplier (ground 0.5, +1 1.0, -2 1.1 ...) x 2 over
  water x the track type (`track_cost_parts`, `sim/src/track/cost.rs`). Water is a run-length raster
  per zone at 25 m (`RunMask`), sampled every 12.5 m along each edge into wet spans; the price cuts
  each edge at vertices and span ends.
- Water masks are fetched by the clock worker when the view comes within 20 km of a zone's box or
  track comes within 2 km of it (docs/zones.md 6); a mask that arrives under drawn track reworks the
  price of the track within its box only (T-1039). Edits that make track wait for a missing mask
  because water decides whether ground track is allowed at all.

## Other games

- **NIMBY Rails**: no terrain in the track model. The developer, January 2021: elevation "does not
  [affect track], and it is not planned to", since it needs a 3D or 2.5D view; a gradient-based cost
  "could be workable", a low-priority note. Its map has SRTM shading at low zoom only. Track heights
  are set by the player, relative to flat ground, as here.
  https://steamcommunity.com/app/1134710/discussions/0/3112518479597864319
- **Subway Builder**: depth multipliers only (deep bore 4.5x, tunnel 2.0x, cut and cover 1.0x,
  elevated 0.8x, at grade 0.3x; research/competitors.md); no terrain.
- **Transport Fever 2, Cities: Skylines**: full 3D terrain, track at absolute heights, slopes limited
  and tunnels and bridges made where the track leaves the ground. That is the "real heights" model
  ruled out below.

So a priced ruggedness with tunnels as the cheap way through mountains would be more than NIMBY or
Subway Builder have, at a fraction of the 3D games' cost.

## Recommended design

### Ruggedness classes (the price)

- **Measure**: mean slope in degrees from GEDTM30 240 m, over 480 m cells (2 x 2 source pixels),
  then a 3 x 3 majority filter so the classes come in patches of a kilometre or more and a route's
  price does not flicker from cell to cell.
- **Four classes** (thresholds to be set at build time by looking at the Netherlands, New York,
  Pennsylvania, Seoul, Hong Kong and the Alps; starting values): flat under 2 degrees, rolling 2-5,
  hilly 5-10, mountain 10 and over.
- **Multiplier**, one per class, on every level except the bored ones (-2, -3), which are 1x
  everywhere:

  | Class | Factor | Ground (0) | +1 viaduct | -1 cut and cover | -2 bored |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Flat | 1.0 | 45 | 90 | 90 | 99 |
  | Rolling | 1.25 | 56 | 112 | 112 | 99 |
  | Hilly | 1.6 | 72 | 144 | 144 | 99 |
  | Mountain | 2.5 | 112 | 225 | 225 | 99 |

  (US$M a km of double track.) The factors are chosen so that ground stays the cheapest choice
  until the mountain class, where the tunnel wins: the real pattern (rolling-country lines in cuts
  and fills, base tunnels through ranges). It is one more factor in the existing product (level x
  water x track type x terrain), so the Build tab and the track tag explain it the same way.
- **Water cells** are flat (the model is 0 slope over water), so a bridge over a fjord costs the
  water's 2x, not a terrain factor too.
- **Ramps** take the dearer of their two levels after terrain, as now.
- **Left alone** (defaults, easy to change): stations, junctions and flyovers take no terrain
  factor; upkeep stays flat (`track_factor` only); nothing is refused for being steep.

### Data and sizes

- One sibling file per zone, `<zone>.terrain` beside its pack, in the zone's own equirectangular
  frame like the water mask (so `GeoMask`'s warp lattice reads it unchanged): a header (origin,
  cell 480 m, width, height) and the classes run-length encoded per row, two bits a class.
- **Size**: the 1,733 zones' boxes cover 43M km² (with overlap and sea), 189M cells at 480 m: 47 MB
  raw at two bits, far less run-length encoded since sea and plains are long runs. Median zone
  (11,000 km²) about 12 KB raw, about 2-6 KB on disk; the largest (795,000 km²) under 1 MB raw.
  For comparison a zone's water mask is about 280 KB (notes/T-303.md: 14 zones, 3.9 MB).
- **Not in the packs**, so no pack rebuild, no join re-gate, no T-1182-style rerun: a new pipeline
  script (`pipeline/zone_terrain.py`, about 150 lines) reads each zone's box out of the GEDTM30 COG
  (one 10 GB download, or HTTP range reads), computes slope with the latitude's metres a degree,
  classes, filter, runs, writes. Under an hour on one core for every zone; a new zone runs it with
  its other files.

### Runtime

- **Fetch** with the water mask, by the same rule (view within 20 km of the box, track within 2 km):
  one more small request per zone.
- **Sim**: a `TerrainMask` beside `WaterMask` (class at a point; none = flat), `terrain_spans`
  sampled every 100 m along each edge (cells are 480 m, so 100 m finds every patch of the filtered
  field) into class spans, and `track_cost_parts` cutting at their ends as it does at water spans.
  Cached per edge like the wet spans; a terrain file landing reprices only the track inside its box
  (T-1039's path). Cost: a few hundred array reads per km of edge, nothing next to the 12.5 m water
  sampling.
- **Applied after placing**: edits do **not** wait for a terrain file (it decides price only, never
  whether track is allowed). The ghost, the click and the blueprint quote use what is loaded,
  missing cells priced flat; when the file lands, blueprint prices update. **Construct** waits for
  the files under what it builds (a fraction of a second, and almost always already in), so nothing
  is paid on a flat guess. Money already paid is in the save's cash, so a later class file never
  changes the past.
- **Shown**: the track tool's tag already gives the average cost multiplier "level and water over
  its length"; terrain folds into it, and the hover and the track inspector's breakdown get a row
  ("Mountain 2.5x over 1.2 km"). The WASM interface gains a function (installing a terrain file), so
  the build agent needs its own worktree (CLAUDE.md).

### The topographic layer

- **Hillshade**: a MapLibre `raster-dem` source (Terrarium encoding) and a `hillshade` layer in the
  basemap style, under roads and labels, its shadow and highlight colours set for the light and dark
  themes. Off by default, one switch (Settings or the map display buttons). Cost: when on, about
  6-12 tiles of 512 px a view at 50-150 KB each; the GPU prepares each tile once and then draws it
  like any raster, under a millisecond a frame. Off, nothing is fetched.
- **The classes as a tint** (later stage): with a build tool active and the layer on, the four
  classes drawn as faint brown washes (none, light, medium, strong), from the same files the price
  reads, so what is shown is what is charged. The main thread fetches the same few KB; each zone's
  grid goes on the map as an image source split into strips of a few km of latitude, since an
  equirectangular grid is not a mercator quad (one quad over a 1-degree box is off by about 270 m
  at 40 degrees north).
- **Attribution**: GEDTM30 (CC BY) and whichever hillshade source, in the map's attribution.
- **Hosting**: Mapterhorn's hosted tiles are the quickest to try. Before the game is published,
  either confirm Mapterhorn's terms for an app's use or put our own Terrarium pyramid on R2 (from
  GEDTM30 at 240 m, z0-9, a few GB as I estimate it, unmeasured; softer close up than Mapterhorn's
  30 m).

### Mountain tunnels

Nothing beyond pricing. Levels are relative to the ground, so a -2 tunnel under a mountain is drawn
and priced as any tunnel; that it "climbs" with the surface 16 m beneath it matters only if grades
change speed, which they do not. A tunnel's real cost per km depends little on how much rock is
above it. Portals need no rule: the ramp from ground to -2 is 400 m at 4%, priced at the dearer
level as now.

### Gradients and speed (optional, not recommended first)

- Grades from a DEM profile are not meaningful while track follows the ground at a fixed level: a
  ground track up a 20% hillside would read as a 20% grade.
- If mountains should also slow trains, the simple form is a speed cap by class: track at levels -1
  to +3 in the mountain class runs at most 120 km/h (curves and grades), bored tunnels not capped. One
  more reason to tunnel, in the same predictable terms. Its cost: the class enters the run-time
  profile as one more speed limit per span, and the track tag's speed row names it.

### Ruled out: real heights

Track at absolute heights, the ground deciding where it is a bridge or a tunnel, gradients limiting
where track can go: that is the Transport Fever model. It would rewrite levels (relative today, used
by crossings, ramps, stations, quad, bridges, the Elevation display and saves), need the DEM at
fine resolution under every edit (so building would wait), and a 2.5D view to read. Not worth it for
this game.

## Open questions, with my defaults

1. The four factors 1 / 1.25 / 1.6 / 2.5, set so the tunnel wins only in the mountain class.
   Default as given.
2. Should stations take the terrain factor? Default no (a station is short; keeps station prices
   one table).
3. Should upkeep follow terrain? Default no.
4. The mountain speed cap (stage 4)? Default not now.
5. Hillshade: off by default, a switch in Settings. Default as given; with a build tool the tint
   shows only while it is on.
