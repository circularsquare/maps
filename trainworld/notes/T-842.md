# T-842: riders expect the service each stop really gets

Anita, 2026-10-21: "if passenger expectations differ from train schedule perhaps we should be mostly
shifting the passenger expectations though", and on the direction: "yeah demand should know the
schedule, roughly". Design note; nothing built. Measurements are on `T-007-real-nyc.save` with
T-835's midpoint rule as committed (its no-service exception included).

## Where demand prices the wait today

- `period_net` (demand/api.rs) gives each line one headway per period from its trains an hour at
  that period's level. `build_graph` (kernel.rs) turns it into the boarding edge's cost (half the
  headway plus the change cost) and `Graph::rn_tph`, one figure per route node (line, direction,
  stop), all of a line's nodes alike.
- The strategy search (step 4) pools common lines by `rn_tph` (`join_line`: wait = 30 / summed
  trains an hour) and loading splits riders between them by the same figure. Each boarding also
  adds `rn_left`, the extra wait for full trains (step 7), per route node.
- So the search already works per (line, stop, direction, period): only the inputs are per line.
  Giving each route node its own figures changes no code path in the search, no table and no
  memory figure.

## How far demand and the schedule disagree now

Method: a one-off script (not kept; `T-835-analyse.mjs`'s loader) with a fresh capture
of demand's riders (10,279,724 rail trips, round 3). For every route node and period it takes the
game's drawn departures passing the stop on a steady day and the account's arrival shape, and works
out each rider's wait for the next train. "No service" is a rider whose next train is more than
2 hours away (4 hours in brackets). Classes as in T-835: frequent (at least 2.5 trains an hour at
every level), frequent with no night, and the rest (T-835's 80 sparse lines, split here into those
running at most 2 an hour at the peak and the others).

| Lines | Boardings a day | Demand's wait, min | Drawn wait, riders in service | Riders with no train due |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Frequent (21) | 12.48M | 3.69 | 3.71 | 0 |
| Frequent, no night (7) | 2.15M | 4.75 | 4.76 | 0.1% (0.1%) |
| Middling (24) | 2.77M | 8.44 | 8.66 | 0.4% (0.1%) |
| Sparse, at most 2 an hour (57) | 1.16M | 34.5 | 33.5 | 11.4% (4.2%) |

First-train wait, rider-hours a day: the account (riders spread over the whole period) 2,262,935;
demand's half headway 1,993,735; +13.5%, as T-835's "As built" table found. Of the 269k gap, about
230k is the sparse lines, and nearly all of that is riders arriving in a gap with no train for hours
(after a peak's last train on a peak-only line, overnight before the first). Riders who do have a
train coming wait what demand assumes, within 3% in every class and period except the evening and
night on middling lines (13.6 against 11.9 min, 27.6 against 38).

So after T-835 the disagreement is a service-span problem, not a headway one.

A stop's own headway shifted by its place on the line (the period in force when its trains pass,
half a run either side of the middle) was measured too: 3.69, 4.75, 8.45 and 33.4 minutes, the same
as demand's today. The midpoint rule already lines the periods up, and what is left at the two ends
is early at one end and late at the other, so it averages out over a period. Not worth building.

## The proposal: service span per stop

1. **The figure.** For each line, direction, stop and period, `phi` = the share of the period's
   riders (by the account's arrival shape, at the stop's own clock) who arrive while a train is due
   within 2 hours. 1 on every line with service through the period; below 1 where the stop has a
   gap with no train: before its first train, after its last, between a peak-only line's peaks.
2. **Where it is computed.** The clock worker's track model, which owns the departures
   (`Service::run_deps` on a steady day, no eras, plus `stop_departures` offsets), beside the
   boarding account and with its per-line input hash, so an edit redoes only the lines whose
   departures changed. A closed-form integral of the shape between consecutive passing trains;
   a few ms for the whole real save in WASM (estimate; the account's own pass over the same
   departures is 40-60 ms and does far more).
3. **How it reaches demand.** One more array in the service snapshot, beside the trains an hour:
   route nodes x 5 periods. Real save: 3,512 route nodes, 17,560 floats, 70 kB per snapshot to each
   worker. `set_network` treats a period whose `phi` moved by 0.01 or more like one whose trains an
   hour changed (results forgotten, period solved again). `phi` only moves when departures do (trains,
   headways, run times, turnaround), which already wake demand, so no new wakes.
4. **How demand uses it.** Boarding a line where only `phi` of riders have a train costs
   -ln(`phi`) / 0.10 perceived minutes more: the logit's own form for an option open to `phi` of
   the people (0.10 is demand's scale per minute). `phi` 0.9 adds 1.1 min, 0.5 adds 6.9, 0.1 adds
   23, 0.01 adds 46. It goes into `rn_left` (the per-boarding extra already there for full trains),
   so the search, the common-line pooling and the loading need no change. The trip explorer
   (`trip.rs`) adds the same figure to the boarding's wait and says so in its hover.
5. **The boarding account** spreads a stop's period boardings only over the times a train is due
   (the same 2 hour rule), keeping the period's total, as T-680 already does for a new line's first
   train ("nobody who would have arrived earlier waits for it"). Then the account and demand price
   the same riders the same way.

The wait inside service stays half the period headway. A per-stop wait (the drawn mean, as an
effective trains an hour per route node in `rn_tph`) would cost the search nothing either, but the
table above says it would change little on the real save. It stays a later stage, for long intercity
lines where T-835's rule leaves the far ends 75 min out (London - Paris).

## Cost of the search

Measured natively (a scratch binary calling the sim crate's `build_graph` and `skims` on the real
New York network, `nyc_real_track.json`, 873 demand stations, 4,385 nodes, every station searched
towards): 342-424 ms a period now, 329-441 ms with a different trains an hour on every route node.
The same within noise, as the code says it must be: no change in time, memory (the skims tables do
not store the figures), clusters (each cluster's sub-network carries its lines' route nodes, so
`phi` is sliced with them like trains an hour), or graph sharing between populations (`phi` is part
of the graph). The demand cache (T-328) keys on a hash of the network demand reads, so `phi` must go
into that hash. The market shares the local search, so riders between cities see the same `phi`.

## What changes in figures (estimates)

From the captured riders, each node and period's boardings moved by the binary logit at demand's
0.10 scale and a rail share of 0.5 (0.3-0.7 brackets the range), holding everything else still:

| Lines | Boardings lost, 2 h rule | 4 h rule |
|---|---:|---:|
| Frequent | 0 | 0 |
| Frequent, no night | 1,100 | 1,100 |
| Middling | 7,400 | 1,400 |
| Sparse | 118,000 (10% of theirs) | 33,000 (3%) |
| Whole network | 127,000 of 18.55M (0.7%) | 36,000 (0.2%) |

- Rail trips a day: at 1.8 boardings a trip, about 70,000 fewer of 10.28M (0.7%) with the 2 h rule,
  20,000 (0.2%) with 4 h. An upper figure: some riders move to another line rather than off rail,
  and trips over an hour are damped (4.3).
- Riders at first and last trains: frequent lines unchanged; the C and the other no-night lines lose
  their few riders after midnight's last train. On peak-only and sparse lines, riders who today
  arrive after the last peak train and wait hours mostly go by car instead.
- The account against demand: the 13.5% gap in first-train wait (2.26M against 1.99M rider-hours)
  falls to the in-service difference, about 1% over the network (+0.7% frequent lines, +2-3%
  middling, -3% sparse). The longest waits in "Trains today" stop showing riders waiting 3-8 hours
  for a peak-only line's next train.
- Money: fares follow demand, so about 0.7% fewer fares, almost all on LIRR and NJT branches. Running
  costs unchanged.

## Simpler alternatives considered

- **Only first and last trains of the day** (the span ends, not gaps inside the day): on the real
  save that misses the peak-only lines' midday gap, which is most of the sparse lines' 11%.
- **A per-stop correction factor per period** (drawn wait over half headway): the same table as a
  per-stop wait, and it does nothing for riders with no train at all, who are the gap.
- **Pricing a rider in a gap at the wait to the next train, capped**: one figure per node, but the
  mean of a 5 minute wait and a 3 hour one, put through the logit, loses far more riders than are
  actually without a train, and a 60 minute cap made sparse lines look better than today (28.6
  against 34.5 minutes), since half a 2.5 h headway is already over it.
- **The period headway shifted to the stop**: measured above, the same as today.

## Recommendation and stages

Build the service span. It is the part of "demand knows the schedule" that is wrong by a lot today;
the rest agrees within a few percent.

- **T-842a, demand side** (small to medium, one session): `phi` in the track model, the snapshot
  array, `set_network` and the cache hash, the `rn_left` term, the trip explorer, tests (a peak-only
  line's midday `phi` is 0 and nobody boards it at noon; a line with service all period is unchanged
  to the bit). Docs: demand-kernel step 4, demand-plain's wait line, demand-worldwide (the rule
  holds anywhere: it reads only the player's timetable).
- **T-842b, the account** (small): arrivals only while a train is due, period totals kept.
  boarding.rs's cumulative shape gets the same gaps as a mask.
- **Later, only if a long line shows it**: a per-stop wait in `rn_tph`. Free in the search; the left
  behind rule (step 7) would keep the line's own trains an hour rather than read `rn_tph`.

**T-835's midpoint rule stays.** With only the span built, demand still assumes the period's
headway inside service, and the midpoint rule is what makes that true at mid-line stops (the 06h
gap on frequent lines went from +7,383 to +781 rider-hours). It also matches what the player sees
in the panel. With a per-stop wait built later it would no longer be needed by demand, but it is
built, costs 1% car-km, and keeps the map honest, so it would still stay.

## Questions for Anita

1. How long a wait counts as "no train for me": 2 hours (sparse lines lose about 10% of their
   riders) or 4 hours (about 3%)? Default 2 hours.
2. Riders between cities share the local search, so they get the same rule. Real long-distance
   travellers plan around a timetable rather than turn up; is the same rule fine for them, or should
   the market keep half the headway? Default: the same rule, one search.
