### Three modes

A line's schedule has three modes, picked at the top of a new **schedule panel** in the middle of the screen (like
Community), opened from a new icon beside the line panel's schedule. A player who never opens it sees nothing new.

1. **Simple**: today's three rows (High 6-20, Medium 20-24, Low 0-6), the same every day. The line panel works as now.
2. **Detailed**: rows by days and hours ("Mon-Fri 6-9 / 12 trains"), a colour per day of the week, whole hours, up
   to 6 rows a day. Run on: Every day, Weekdays or Weekends. Weekends: Same as weekdays, Scaled (a share like 75%,
   trains rounded up; the weekend rows show greyed and locked) or Custom. Holidays run as weekends, the busy holiday
   travel days as weekdays.
3. **Exact**: every train of the day is its own. Click a train on the chart (or in the train table) to select it;
   arrows nudge it 15 seconds at a time, Remove train takes it out, Add train places a new one where you click. The
   rows stay as a summary, their headway now an average ("Average headway"). The time-distance chart sits in the
   panel, with the big train table (each train a column, each station a row, small numbers) under it behind a "Show
   train table" toggle.

The panel's right side has the timetable in every mode: a station's departures board (hour rows with minutes,
Japanese style) and the chart. The train table only appears in Exact.

Moving up a mode keeps everything (Simple's three rows become Detailed's first rows; Detailed's rows become Exact's
trains). Moving down asks first, since it drops what the lower mode can't hold.

### When a moved or added train runs into another

The game works it out, the same way for every train: **first come, first served**. Wherever two trains need the
same piece of track, platform or crossing, the one that would get there first goes, and the other waits at its stop
before until it's clear (never between stations). That wait goes into the timetable, so the drawn trains, the
riders and the money all follow it. So a nudged train can hold up another line's train, or be held up by it.

It shows: the waiting train gets a red ring on the chart where it waits, with a hover ("3 train waits 1m20s at
Elm"), the selected train's time there is red in the table, and the panel says the line's total ("Other lines wait
4m10s/day for this line"). An added train that would make some train wait more than 20 minutes is refused.

I'd have the game resolve it rather than only report it: a report alone leaves the map drawing trains through each
other or holding them for no reason the timetable shows, the problem T-3264 is fixing. The other choice is that the
train you moved always gives way, which never hurts another line but feels less real; I'd keep first come.

## How the game schedules now (for the agent who builds it)

Rules: docs/track-and-service.md 6.2 "Delay and slower trains" and 6.3 "Scheduling", "Trains on a running line";
wiki/read/scheduling.md, slower-trains.md, line-capacity.md, rush-hours.md.

**The player's inputs.** Per line, per demand level (`HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, `LOW`, `LEVELS = 3`, sim/src/track/params.rs):
a train count (`Network::line_trains`) or a headway (`LineData::auto`, the `LAU1` block); the settle turns either into
trains an hour (`line_tph`). Levels map onto demand's five periods by `PERIOD_LEVEL = [HIGH, HIGH, HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW]`
over `PERIOD_HOURS` (6-10, 10-16, 16-20, 20-24, 0-6); the app's copy is game/periods.ts. Every kind of day uses the
same map (T-2335). Dwell and turnaround are per line kind (`line_dwell`, `line_turn`), with no player control. Train
kind, length and speed limit are per line.

**Departures** (sim/src/track/service.rs). A line's pattern is a `Lattice`: tph, reverse offset `off`, `lead`
(half a run, so a train belongs to the level in force at the line's middle), local-time `shift` and a `phase` per
level. `lattice_deps_local` lays departures per stretch of one level (`level_stretches`), `start - lead + phase +
off + k * headway`, dropping one closer than half a headway after the last stretch's (`MIN_GAP_SHARE`). Pure
function of the pattern: any window agrees. `run_deps` adds eras (the patterns before an edit, `Era`, T-206) and
`hand_over` chains trips into trains at the terminals. `Trip { line, run, level, dep, draw, end, hw, next }`.

**Profiles: one per (line, run, level).** `Run::prof[level]`, built by `profile_for` from the run's free hops, the
capacity holds `Run::holds[level]` (Kingman queueing delays and following losses, run off as a slower approach or a
stand before the resource) and `Run::stop_holds[level]` (extra dwell per stop). **Every train of a level on a run
has the same stop times.** The renderer indexes profiles as `(line * 2 + run) * 3 + level`
(sim/src/track/wasm/service.rs `trips`, `line_trips`): the stride of 3 is baked in.

**Capacity and the settle.** `capacity::build`, `solve`, `holds_for` per level; `Capacity::fill` caps fleets at
100% (trains past it wait at the ends); `TrackWorld::settle_train_counts` (world.rs:203) iterates counts and rates,
`pace_merges` (world.rs:222) keeps count lines at a merge in step (T-2973), `place_departures` (world.rs:161) calls
`spacing::place`. A count edit's settle is 63-128 ms in WASM on the real save, 320-570 ms on shared New York
(notes/T-1407.md); levels whose fleets match are not stepped (T-415).

**Spacing between lines** (sim/src/track/spacing.rs, T-1925, T-2960, T-2972). One phase per line per level, chosen
from 24 per headway so the line's trains meet each other line's as far apart as they can, merges weighted 25x;
headway lines also choose where their spare time stands (`split`). Greedy, only the lines an edit touched. Saved in
`LPH1` and `LSP1`. The player cannot set it.

**Following and overtakes.** Behind one slower line with no passing tracks the faster line is timetabled `s` before
the next slower train and loses `max(0, D + 2s - H)` (`capacity::follow_best`, T-2419); behind several, a random
meeting (`follow_loss`). With passing tracks (sim/src/track/overtake.rs, T-2231) `Plan::sweep` runs one cycle of
departures train by train (up to 12 slower headways or 2 hours) and gets **each slower train's own wait** at each
passing point, at the best of 24 relative phases (`Plan::best`), capped by T-3238. Then only the **mean** wait per
point goes into `stop_holds` (kind 13), so every local stands the average. `Plan::trains_at` returns the per-train
waits, used only by checks. Each direction picks its own best phase, but the faster line has one phase for both
runs (T-2239), so in one direction the holds and the drawn trains disagree.

**Single track.** Capacity counts a section as run time + 240 s for both directions; nothing schedules where trains
meet. The display guard keeps opposite trains off a stretch (T-3202, `guard_singles`), so a train is drawn waiting
where the timetable has none.

**What is drawn vs what is computed.** The clock worker sends each hour's trips (`TrackApi.trips(epoch)`, the page
holds this hour's and the next, renderer `tripsBy`, map/renderer.ts) and the profiles; the GPU draws
`profile(t - draw)`. On the main thread `TrainGuard` (map/trainGuard.ts) adds a per-trip lag each frame: follow 30 m
behind, flat crossings, single-track turns, places at line ends (notes/T-3264.md has the table). The boarding
account (sim/src/track/boarding.rs) and the timetable chart (ui/inspector/MareyChart.tsx, `line_trips`) use the
timetable, not the lag. Demand (sim/src/demand/api.rs `period_net`) reads each period's level's stop-to-stop times
and the period's headway (`svc.tph[l][PERIOD_LEVEL[p]]`) and the per-stop service span (sim/src/track/span.rs, from a
steady day's departures). Weekend and holiday riders are the weekday solve scaled by purpose and period
(sim/src/demand/days.rs). Demand solves each of the five periods on its own after every edit that changes its inputs
(api.rs, the per-period "same" mask), so the number of solves is five whatever the levels are.

## The design, in stages

Each stage is its own task. Player text comes from the table above; a building agent lists any change for her. A
line in Simple mode runs and reads exactly as today at every stage.

### The model: modes, rows, period slots, departure lists

- **Mode** per line: Simple, Detailed or Exact (a new save block; absent is Simple).
- **Rows** (Detailed, and Exact's summary): per line and per kind of schedule (weekdays, weekends), up to 6 rows
  tiling 0-24 in whole hours, each a train count or a headway (the line's Trains/Headway switch). Simple's three
  levels are the rows "Every day 6-20, 20-24, 0-6"; a save without rows loads as those, figures unchanged to the bit.
- **Period slots**: the sim keeps capacity, holds, profiles and fleets per demand period instead of per level: 5
  weekday slots, plus 5 weekend slots for a line whose weekend differs. `LEVELS` becomes the slot count. Slots whose
  every line has the same rates as another slot are copied, not solved (today's High is three periods with one
  solve, so a game of Simple lines costs what it does now).
- **A slot's rate** is the time-weighted rate of the rows (or, in Exact, the departures) inside its period: trains
  in the period over its hours. Capacity, delays and fleets settle on it. A line's fleet is the most any row needs;
  in Exact, what the day's chained trips need (`hand_over` already chains them).
- **Demand's headway for a period** is the rider-weighted mean headway of the service inside it (riders arrive by the
  boarding account's shape, `PEAK_HOUR_FACTOR`), so the mean wait is right. Riders in an hour with no trains are
  already dropped by the service span (span.rs reads real departures). Exact mode's "Average headway" is this figure
  per row. To keep a nudge from re-solving demand every click, a period re-solves only when its mean headway moves
  by more than a few seconds or its span changes past the existing tolerance (as `DEMAND_TIME_EPS_S` does for stop
  times).
- **Departures**: Simple and Detailed from the rows (`level_stretches` becomes the line's rows, a stretch per row);
  Exact from the line's **departure lists**: per kind of day and direction, sorted times in the line's local day in
  15 s steps (`u16`), a pure lookup by time so any window agrees, eras as for any service edit (T-206). Each
  departure takes the profile of the slot in force when it reaches the line's middle (the existing `lead`).
- **Capacity per row**: a row can't run more often than its period's limit (`Capacity::line_limits`, the T-679 /
  T-3165 clamp, per row), and a cheap per-row utilisation check catches a burst that would overfill shared track
  while the period's average fits.
- **What is averaged** (DECISIONS when built): riders' choice and the statistical track delays inside a period. Not
  averaged: the trains, their crowding, riders left behind, fares, running cost, and in Exact the conflict waits.

### Stage 2 as built (agent tt2, 2026-11-12, tree tw/tt2, not landed)

**What runs now.** After every edit has settled and its departures are placed (commit, reload, bus rework,
allocation, a time-zone change), `timing::retime` works out each train's own timetable:

- **At passing stations**: per slower run and level, the day's departures (the level's stretch, `day_deps`)
  go through `overtake::train_waits` against the faster line's trains as they really run (its placed
  departures and its timetable at each point), by `Plan::sweep`'s rule and T-3238's caps. The sweep takes
  both lines' departures as plain lists, so Exact mode can feed a day's real departures.
- **Timed behind a slower line with nowhere to pass** (`FOLLOW_TIMED`, one slower run on the stretch):
  per faster train, `follow_one`: no loss where it never comes within the spacing of the slower train
  ahead; else slower running from the node before the first place it would, losing just enough to keep
  the spacing at every node (and a stand at the stretch's start if it reaches it too close).
- **Variants**: at most 8 per run and level (`MAX_VARS`), waits and losses rounded up to 2 s, merged
  past 8 by the pair that adds the least waiting. `Run::vars`, `Run::pattern` (by the departure's place
  in its stretch, the 4th field of `service::Dep`), `Trip::var` set only in `Service::run_deps`; every
  reader takes `Trip::var` (renderer, boarding, the chart, the train's stops list, edit continuity).
- **Renderer index**: `((line * 2 + run) * 3 + level) * 9 + var` (`timing::PROFILE_VARS`; app
  `map/profiles.ts`, checked against `TrackApi.profile_vars` at each state build). `window.tw` has
  `profileLine/Run/Level/Of` for checks.
- **Trip ends**: `trip_len_var` = the level's trip length, or the variant's run plus 60 s where that is
  longer (`TURN_MIN_S`): a train that waited longer stands less at its last stop.
- **Edits**: an era's trains keep the variant they ran at the change (`Era::vars`, not saved); in
  `hand_over` a train standing across a change never takes a departure already running at it, and one
  that takes none leaves service 1 s after the change, not at it (T-1071's
  `a_change_to_a_following_pair_on_full_track_moves_no_train` caught both).
- **Cost**: profiles memoised across retimes (`Service::timing_memo`), and retime skipped when nothing it
  reads moved (`timing_key`).
- **Unchanged**: demand, round trips, fleets, delays and every line figure keep the plan's averages.

### Stage 6: Exact mode (24-34 h, or 20-28 after T-3264 B; two tasks)

**6a, sim (12-18 h; 10-14 with T-3264 B's engine):**
- Entering Exact materializes the line's departure lists from its rows for each kind of day that differs (Scaled
  weekends become Custom). Leaving drops them (the app asks).
- Ops, each one edit with undo and the edit lead (T-1071): move departures by a number of 15 s steps, remove
  departures, add a departure at a time and direction. Trains in service when an edit lands carry on (eras).
- Fleet: from chaining the day's trips at the terminals (`hand_over`); a departure no arriving train can make after
  its turnaround brings a train in, so a move or add can buy a train (cars bought as any fleet growth; refused if
  the cash is short, as a count edit is).
- **Conflicts, first come first served**: a day's sweep over the exact line and the lines sharing a resource with it
  (blocks of track in one direction between stops and joins, platforms, flat crossings and junction moves,
  single-track stretches, places at line ends; the resources T-3264 B lists), trains in the order they would reach
  each resource by their own timetable; a train that would come within the resource's clearance (the track's `s`,
  dwell + 60 s on a platform, 120 s at a crossing) waits at its previous stop until clear. The waits become per-trip
  holds (stage 2's variants, addressed per departure through `Trip.var`); on a non-exact line held by an exact one,
  a sparse table of (departure, extra holds) for the day. At resources an exact line shares, the deterministic waits
  replace the Kingman queue between it and the lines it meets, so a delay isn't counted twice; the queue stays among
  the other lines. Anything making a train wait over 20 minutes (T-3264's valve) is refused.
- Reports: per trip, where it waits, how long and for which train; per line, the waits it causes other lines a day.
- Saves: the mode, the lists (2 bytes a departure a direction a kind of day), nothing for the waits (derived).
- Done when: on a test line sharing a merge with another, a nudge into the other's path holds whichever train comes
  second, the drawn trains and the boarding account follow, the other line's held train shows its wait, a removed and
  an added train change the fleet and running cost as expected, a save round trip keeps every departure, and a day's
  sweep on the real save with one exact line takes under 50 ms in WASM.

- Conflict marks: red ring where a train waits, the hovers in the text table, the line's total in the toolbar.
- Done when: pictures of the panel in Exact light and dark, judged; a check that a click on a table column and on its

## How others do it

- **NIMBY Rails** builds the full timetable of every run, recalculated whenever a line, station or train changes
  (not on track or speed edits), repeating weekly; shown as a line timetable, a train timetable, a station board in
  the station's Platforms view, and a passenger route planner. A line is stops with times relative to the first; a
  train runs it "auto" (evenly spaced to fit one loop, or on a fixed interval, the same every day, 24/7) or by manual
  orders ("depart exactly at", "arrive exactly at", "arrive no later than", "loop until next order"); night depot
  runs need manual orders. 1.14 moved schedules to their own editor ("deserve a highlighted and central spot ...
  rather than being a half hidden tab"), with shifts (one train each, can turn orders off or offset them) and groups
  that offset start times by hand or automatically. Pax ignore a schedule until a shift is set up. Players report a
  single line's timetable taking over an hour (research/player-voices.md). Signals decide who waits, by path
  reservation (research/passing-tracks.md).
- **OpenTTD**: a timetable per vehicle's orders, travel and wait times per order; autofill learns them from a run;
  a start date per vehicle, spread automatically across vehicles sharing orders; newer versions' Automate with "use
  timetable to ensure vehicles separation". Players add slack to wait times so lateness doesn't build up.
- **Transport Fever 2**: no base-game timetable; the Timetables mod gives each station arrival/departure slot
  pairs, a train takes the slot whose arrival is nearest, and it controls only departures (holds early trains). Used
  to make locals wait for expresses at a passing station. Reloads have lost settings.
- **Simutrans**: per-stop "wait for load" with a max wait, and per-line "spacing" (departures per month spread over
  the convoys) with a shift; a forum dev notes true timetables don't fit how time passes in Simutrans.
- **A-Train** (Hajimaru Kanko Keikaku, the Switch game): per train, departure conditions by time ("first
  departure", "repeat interval", "number of departures"), set at any station; a late train skips to its next slot
  rather than leaving late; the player works out the cycle time by hand; copying a plan and offsetting it adds
  trains. Single-track meets are hand-timed with waiting points and get hard past about three trains. A-Train 9's
  weekday/holiday split: not confirmed.
- **Subway Builder**: trains per hour per route by time of day (since v1.7, from research/competitors.md); a TPH cap
  per train type and at grade crossings; stop and turnaround seconds per train type. No timetable view found.
- **Cities: Skylines II**: per line, the number of vehicles, the ticket price and the operating hours (day, night or
  both). No timetable.
- **Mini Metro**: lines get trains and carriages; no timetable or frequency setting (general knowledge of the game;
  no source found, as research/passing-tracks.md also notes).
- **Real operators.** Japanese station timetables split weekdays from Saturdays and holidays, list hours with the
  minutes under each, mark train types (no mark for a local, 特 for a limited express) and destinations by a single
  character, and mark trains that start there. Working timetables (Network Rail) carry platforms and running lines
  where there's a choice, and three kinds of padding: engineering, pathing and performance allowances. Swiss
  clock-face timetables repeat the same minutes every hour and line connections up at hub stations, which forces
  run times between hubs to fit the pattern (28 or 58 minutes).

What this suggests: every game that offers hand-made timetables pays in tedium and broken saves; the ones players
find easy give a count or interval per line, by time of day at most. Hence three modes, the hand-made one last and
opt-in, and a game that works out conflicts rather than asking the player to. Japanese timetables' weekday versus
Saturday-and-holiday split is the weekend grouping proposed here, and the train-by-station table is their big
timetable book's layout.
