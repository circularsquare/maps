## For Anita

### What changes for players

Nothing, unless they open the new panel. The line panel stays as it is today: three rows (High 6-20, Medium 20-24,
Low 0-6), the same every day. A new icon beside the schedule's copy, paste and chart opens a **schedule panel** in the
middle of the screen, like Community. In it:

- **Rows down the left**, each a set of days and hours with a train count or a headway: "Mon-Fri 6-9 / 12 trains".
  Each day of the week has its own colour (a little strip of seven squares before the label). Rows split and merge;
  their hours are typed in whole hours. While weekends run the same as weekdays the rows read "Every day 6-9".
- **At the top**, three choices for people who don't want rows:
  - Run on: Every day, Weekdays, Weekends (one click for weekday-only or weekend-only lines).
  - Weekends: Same as weekdays, Scaled (a share like 75%, train counts rounded up), or Own rows. When scaled, the
    weekend rows show under the weekday ones, greyed and locked.
  - Trains or Headway, as now.
- **On the right, the timetable**: a station's departures board (hour rows with the minutes listed, Japanese style)
  and the timetable chart, bigger than the one at the dock's foot.
- Holidays run as weekends; the busy holiday travel days (Golden Week, Spring Festival and the like) run as
  weekdays, as before.

The line panel shows a line's rows for today's kind of day once it has rows of its own (labelled by hours, "6-9",
same steppers), and the three named rows while it has not.

### Your boundary question

**Rows don't have to line up with demand's five periods, and they don't make trip calculation slower.** Demand keeps
its five fixed periods (6-10, 10-16, 16-20, 20-24, 0-6). Each period sees the service of the rows inside it, weighted
by when riders arrive: your "40 min from 15-20" makes 10-16 see one hour of 40-minute service in six, and 16-20 see
four hours of it. Demand already solves all five periods after an edit, so the number of solves doesn't change.

- **What counts as a boundary**: any whole hour where one of a line's rows ends and the next begins. Demand's own
  period edges never show and rows cross them freely. Rows always cover the whole day (a row can have no trains).
- **The lie**: inside one period, riders choose as if the service were spread evenly over it. Someone running
  trains every 3 minutes from 7 to 8 only, and every 10 minutes the rest of the morning, gets riders who see the
  morning's average wait. The trains themselves run exactly as set, and the crowding, full trains and riders left
  behind are worked out train by train, so those stay right; only the decision to take the train is averaged. Delays
  at busy track are also the period's average. I don't think a player could tell.
- **The cost** is in edits, not trip calculation. The game works out track capacity and delays once per distinct
  schedule: 3 today, at most 5 for weekdays and 5 for weekends. Changing a row costs about what a train count change
  costs now (63-128 ms on the real save). A track edit costs more for each distinct schedule the game's lines use,
  up to about 3 times today's in a game where every period differs on many lines. It runs in the background and
  never blocks the map.
- **So no warning is needed.** The other way (each new boundary a demand period of its own) would add about 20%
  solve time per boundary, and since every line's boundaries add up across the whole game, a cap per line wouldn't
  cap it. Not recommended.
- **Cap**: 6 rows per kind of day per line (early morning, morning peak, midday, evening peak, evening, night), so 12
  with own weekend rows. The cap is for reading the panel; the cost doesn't depend on it.

### Weekend riders (assumed)

I've assumed riders don't react to weekend service: weekend riders stay the weekday's scaled to the day, while
crowding, running cost, fares and the drawn trains follow the weekend trains. Making them react would mean solving
Saturday and Sunday on their own, about three times the demand work after every edit.

## How the game schedules now (for the agent who builds it)

Rules: docs/track-and-service.md 6.2 "Delay and slower trains" and 6.3 "Scheduling", "Trains on a running line";
wiki/read/scheduling.md, slower-trains.md, line-capacity.md, rush-hours.md.

**The player's inputs.** Per line, per demand level (`HIGH`, `MEDIUM`, `LOW`, `LEVELS = 3`, sim/src/track/params.rs):
a train count (`Network::line_trains`) or a headway (`LineData::auto`, the `LAU1` block); the settle turns either into
trains an hour (`line_tph`). Levels map onto demand's five periods by `PERIOD_LEVEL = [HIGH, HIGH, HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW]`
over `PERIOD_HOURS` (6-10, 10-16, 16-20, 20-24, 0-6); the app's copy is game/periods.ts. Every kind of day uses the
same map (T-2335). Dwell and turnaround are per line kind (`line_dwell`, `line_turn`), with no player control. Train
kind, length and speed limit are per line.

**Departures** (sim/src/track/service.rs). A line's pattern is a `Lattice`: tph, reverse offset `off`, `lead`
(half a run, so a train belongs to the level in force at the line's middle), local-time `shift` and a `phase` per
level. `lattice_deps_local` lays departures per stretch of one level (`level_stretches`), `start - lead + phase +
off + k * headway`, dropping one closer than half a headway after the last stretch's (`MIN_GAP_SHARE`). Pure
function of the pattern: any window agrees. `run_deps` adds eras (the patterns before an edit, `Era`, T-206) and
`hand_over` chains trips into trains at the terminals. `Trip { line, run, level, dep, draw, end, hw, next }`.

**Profiles: one per (line, run, level).** `Run::prof[level]`, built by `profile_for` from the run's free hops, the
capacity holds `Run::holds[level]` (Kingman queueing delays and following losses, run off as a slower approach or a
stand before the resource) and `Run::stop_holds[level]` (extra dwell per stop). **Every train of a level on a run
has the same stop times.** The renderer indexes profiles as `(line * 2 + run) * 3 + level`
(sim/src/track/wasm/service.rs `trips`, `line_trips`): the stride of 3 is baked in.

**Capacity and the settle.** `capacity::build`, `solve`, `holds_for` per level; `Capacity::fill` caps fleets at
100% (trains past it wait at the ends); `TrackWorld::settle_train_counts` (world.rs:203) iterates counts and rates,
`pace_merges` (world.rs:222) keeps count lines at a merge in step (T-2973), `place_departures` (world.rs:161) calls
`spacing::place`. A count edit's settle is 63-128 ms in WASM on the real save, 320-570 ms on shared New York
(notes/T-1407.md); levels whose fleets match are not stepped (T-415).

**Spacing between lines** (sim/src/track/spacing.rs, T-1925, T-2960, T-2972). One phase per line per level, chosen
from 24 per headway so the line's trains meet each other line's as far apart as they can, merges weighted 25x;
headway lines also choose where their spare time stands (`split`). Greedy, only the lines an edit touched. Saved in
`LPH1` and `LSP1`. The player cannot set it.

**Following and overtakes.** Behind one slower line with no passing tracks the faster line is timetabled `s` before
the next slower train and loses `max(0, D + 2s - H)` (`capacity::follow_best`, T-2419); behind several, a random
meeting (`follow_loss`). With passing tracks (sim/src/track/overtake.rs, T-2231) `Plan::sweep` runs one cycle of
departures train by train (up to 12 slower headways or 2 hours) and gets **each slower train's own wait** at each
passing point, at the best of 24 relative phases (`Plan::best`), capped by T-3238. Then only the **mean** wait per
point goes into `stop_holds` (kind 13), so every local stands the average. `Plan::trains_at` returns the per-train
waits, used only by checks. Each direction picks its own best phase, but the faster line has one phase for both
runs (T-2239), so in one direction the holds and the drawn trains disagree.

**Single track.** Capacity counts a section as run time + 240 s for both directions; nothing schedules where trains
meet. The display guard keeps opposite trains off a stretch (T-3202, `guard_singles`), so a train is drawn waiting
where the timetable has none.

**What is drawn vs what is computed.** The clock worker sends each hour's trips (`TrackApi.trips(epoch)`, the page
holds this hour's and the next, renderer `tripsBy`, map/renderer.ts) and the profiles; the GPU draws
`profile(t - draw)`. On the main thread `TrainGuard` (map/trainGuard.ts) adds a per-trip lag each frame: follow 30 m
behind, flat crossings, single-track turns, places at line ends (notes/T-3264.md has the table). The boarding
account (sim/src/track/boarding.rs) and the timetable chart (ui/inspector/MareyChart.tsx, `line_trips`) use the
timetable, not the lag. Demand (sim/src/demand/api.rs `period_net`) reads each period's level's stop-to-stop times
and the period's headway (`svc.tph[l][PERIOD_LEVEL[p]]`) and the per-stop service span (sim/src/track/span.rs, from a
steady day's departures). Weekend and holiday riders are the weekday solve scaled by purpose and period
(sim/src/demand/days.rs). Demand solves each of the five periods on its own after every edit that changes its inputs
(api.rs, the per-period "same" mask), so the number of solves is five whatever the levels are.

## The design, in stages

Each stage is its own task. Player text is drafted by the building agent from the table above and listed for her.
New players' experience is unchanged at every stage: nothing moves out of the line panel, and a line with no rows
of its own runs and reads exactly as today.

### The model: rows, period slots, and the averaging

- **Rows** (stage 3 on): per line and per kind of schedule (weekdays, weekends), a list of up to 6 rows tiling 0-24
  in whole hours, each a train count or a headway (the line's Trains/Headway switch, as now). Today's three levels
  are the default rows, "Every day 6-20, 20-24, 0-6"; a save without rows loads as those, figures unchanged to the bit.
- **Period slots**: the sim keeps capacity, holds, profiles and fleets per demand period instead of per level: 5
  weekday slots, plus 5 weekend slots for a line whose weekend differs. `LEVELS` becomes the slot count. Slots whose
  every line has the same rates as another slot are copied, not solved (today's High is three periods with one
  solve, so a game with no rows costs what it does now).
- **A slot's rate** is the time-weighted rate of the rows inside its period: trains run in the period over its hours.
  Capacity, delays and fleets settle on it. The fleet for a line is the most any of its rows needs.
- **Demand's headway for a period** is the rider-weighted mean headway of the rows with trains inside it (riders
  arrive by the boarding account's shape, `PEAK_HOUR_FACTOR`), so the mean wait is right. Riders in an hour with no
  trains are already dropped by the service span (span.rs reads the real departures), so a gap inside a period is
  honest without new code.
- **Departures** come from the rows themselves: `level_stretches` becomes the line's rows (a stretch per row), each
  departure taking the profile of the slot in force when it reaches the line's middle (the existing `lead`).
- **Capacity per row**: a row can't run more often than its period's limit (`Capacity::line_limits`, the T-679 /
  T-3165 clamp, applied per row); and a cheap per-row utilisation check (rates times occupancy, no solve) catches a
  short burst that would overfill shared track while the period's average fits, clamping that row.
- **What is averaged** (DECISIONS when built): riders' choice and track delays inside a period. Not averaged: the
  trains, their crowding, riders left behind, fares and running cost.

### Stage 1: the panel and the timetable (8-12 h)

- The schedule panel, centred like Community (`saves-panel` frame, about 1000 px wide), opened from a fourth icon by
  the line panel's schedule (hover "Detailed schedule"), closed by its close button, Escape or the icon. Left: the
  line's three rows as "Every day 6-20 / 20-24 / 0-6" with the line panel's steppers and dots (the same component).
  Right: Departures and Chart tabs.
- **Departures board**: a station picker (the line's stops), one block per direction ("to Gum"), hour rows in the
  line's local time with the minutes of each hour listed, Japanese style; only trains that stop there. Sim: one
  query, `TrackApi.station_departures(line, node, t0, t1)`, from `Service::line_trips` over a day, each trip's time
  at the stop (`draw + prof.dep[k]`). Weekday and Weekend tabs appear in stage 4.
- **Chart**: the existing timetable chart component, at the panel's width. The dock-foot chart stays.
- **Group chart**: the group view gets the same panel with the group's lines on one chart, the group's longest
  line's stops as the axis and the others mapped through shared track (T-2660's `pathTable`).
- Done when: the board's minutes match the chart's trips at three stations of the real save (a check); a group chart
  on a group sharing track; pictures light and dark, judged; her look; line panel unchanged for a player who never
  opens the panel.

### Stage 2: each train its own waits (10-14 h; closes T-3343 and T-2239, makes T-3271's case drawable)

- **Variants.** Per (line, run, slot), a short list of variants (`Run::var[slot]`: per stop extra holds and its
  profile) and a pattern: departure `k` of the cycle takes variant `pattern[k mod K]`, the cycle anchored on the
  departure lattice so it is a pure function of the departure time. `Trip` gains `var`. Lines with no passing
  waits have one variant, as now.
- **Plans at the phase the trains really run.** Sweep each overtake pair at the relative phase the lattices give in
  that direction (both directions, from the faster line's one phase), not at each direction's own best phase. Keep
  `Plan::best` only to choose the phase (`follow_targets`). The per-train waits (`trains_at`, today checks only)
  become the variants; keep T-3238's caps.
- **Following with nowhere to pass**: the same machinery can time each faster train exactly behind its slower one
  instead of the averaged slower running (call 5); measure first.
- **What reads it**: the renderer's profile index becomes a table (line, run, slot, variant) instead of the stride
  of 3; boarding uses the trip's variant for its stop times; the chart and the board draw variants for free; the
  stops list shows the plain variant's times, its hover naming the waits. **Demand keeps the mean** stop-to-stop
  time per slot, what `stop_holds` gives now, so riders move only where the real phase differs from the best one.
- Saves: nothing new (variants are derived). Performance: profiles grow only on lines with passing waits, at most 12
  per run and slot; on the 180 km test line at 10/10 that is 3. Measure the settle on the real save and a Japan save
  with passing tracks.
- Done when: on the 180 km test line (`passing_tracks_let_the_express_by`) at 10/10, 30/10 and 10/30 every local
  leaves each passing station at least `PASS_CLEAR_S` after the last express passes, both directions, in the
  timetable and on the map (t2233-passing-map plus a timetable-only Rust test); t1965-follow 1+1 passes or is down to
  the one-place-end frames T-3231 owns; the real save's figures unchanged to the bit and the Japan save's riders
  within 1% or explained.

Stage 2 is independent of the panel and can run beside stage 1.

### Stage 3: rows with hours (12-16 h)

- Sim: the model above for weekdays (one kind of schedule, every day): rows per line (a new save block, the
  default rows when absent), slots per period with the copy-not-solve rule, slot rates and demand headways by the
  averaging, departures from rows, the per-row clamp. Ops: set a row's trains or headway, split a row at an hour,
  remove a row (its hours join the row above, or below for the first), move a boundary. One undo each.
- App: the panel's rows editable (hours box, steppers, split and remove icons, cap 6); copy and paste carry rows;
  the line panel shows a line's own rows for today ("Hours" column head) and the three named rows otherwise;
  profit estimate (game/profitEstimate.ts) and game/periods.ts by slot.
- Done when: a line at "6-9 12 trains, 9-16 6, 16-19 12, 19-24 4, 0-6 2" draws exactly those departures, its
  running cost, crowding dots and fares follow, demand's per-period headways equal the rider-weighted means (a Rust
  test), the real save loads and settles to the bit, a row edit's settle on the real save is within today's 63-128
  ms, and a track edit's time with 5 distinct weekday slots is measured and written down.

### Stage 4: weekends (6-9 h)

- Sim: a second kind of schedule per line, weekend slots (at most 10 slots in all), each day's kind from the line's
  calendar (T-1720's `Service::cal` and `levelOn` are in git history, removed by T-2335): public holidays run the
  weekend rows, a superholiday's days the weekday rows. `lattice_deps_local` takes the day's rows. The boarding
  account already works a weekend day out on that day's trains.
- Run on: Weekdays sets the weekend rows to no trains; Weekends sets the weekday rows to no trains and the weekend to
  own rows (from the weekday rows as they were). Scaled: each weekend row is its weekday row times the share, counts
  rounded up, a headway row's headway divided by the share and rounded down to a whole minute (so trains round up);
  a stepper from 10% to 200% in steps of 5%. Own rows: edited as weekdays.
- Riders: weekday solve scaled, as now (assumed, above).
- App: the top settings, the locked weekend rows (greyed, a lock, "Follows weekdays at 75%"), "Every day" rows when
  the same, the board's Weekdays and Weekends tabs, the line panel's rows for today's kind of day.
- Done when: a line scaled to 50% runs, draws, charges and boards half its trains on a Saturday and on a public
  holiday, its weekdays and a superholiday day unchanged; weekends-only and weekdays-only lines run on the right days;
  old saves unchanged.

### Stage 5: nudging a line (3-5 h; after stage 2)

- In the panel's chart, above it, "Leaves Ash at :07, :22, :37, :52" (the row in force) with two arrow buttons that
  move every departure of the line a minute earlier or later. A moved line is pinned: `spacing::place` never moves it
  again, other lines still place against it; a pinned line can go back to automatic.
- Sim: a pinned flag per line beside the phase (a new `LPN1` block), an op setting the phase in whole seconds.
  Stage 2's sweep at the real phase keeps passing waits right whatever the player picks.
- Done when: a nudge on a shared-track line moves only that line's trains, survives an edit elsewhere and a reload,
  and a nudged express on the passing test line still has every local wait for it.

### Stage 6: single-track meets (8-12 h; T-3219, after stage 2)

- For a line on single track with passing points (stations on double track or with passing tracks), plan where each
  pair of opposite trains meets over one cycle and hold the first to arrive there, as stage 2's variants. Capacity
  of those sections comes from the plan instead of run time + 240 s, so it rises (2 trains on 2 km from 5.1 to
  about 6.4 an hour). Several lines on one single track: first come first served in the sweep.
- Done when: t3202-single-meet shows trains waiting only at stations, the guard's single-track rule never holding a
  train in steady service; capacity on the test line as computed; her OK on the figure change (call 4).

### After these: the drawn hold plan (T-3264 B, its own 10-14 h)

T-3264's plan in the clock worker stays the safety net for what a timetable can't promise: queues at near-full
merges, flat crossings, one-place ends. With stages 2 and 6 its valve fires far less. Its geometry half (D) is
independent and can go any time.

### Not proposed

- Rows as demand periods of their own (about 20% more solve time per boundary, summed over every line's boundaries).
- Saturday apart from Sunday, and half-hour rows: later if asked.
- Dwell and turnaround per line or per stop: a small control (2-3 h), only if players ask.
- Patterns inside one line (skip-stop, short turns): lines in a group do this already and read better.
- A hand-written timetable (NIMBY Rails style).

## How others do it

- **NIMBY Rails** builds the full timetable of every run, recalculated whenever a line, station or train changes
  (not on track or speed edits), repeating weekly; shown as a line timetable, a train timetable, a station board in
  the station's Platforms view, and a passenger route planner. A line is stops with times relative to the first; a
  train runs it "auto" (evenly spaced to fit one loop, or on a fixed interval, the same every day, 24/7) or by manual
  orders ("depart exactly at", "arrive exactly at", "arrive no later than", "loop until next order"); night depot
  runs need manual orders. 1.14 moved schedules to their own editor ("deserve a highlighted and central spot ...
  rather than being a half hidden tab"), with shifts (one train each, can turn orders off or offset them) and groups
  that offset start times by hand or automatically. Pax ignore a schedule until a shift is set up. Players report a
  single line's timetable taking over an hour (research/player-voices.md). Signals decide who waits, by path
  reservation (research/passing-tracks.md).
- **OpenTTD**: a timetable per vehicle's orders, travel and wait times per order; autofill learns them from a run;
  a start date per vehicle, spread automatically across vehicles sharing orders; newer versions' Automate with "use
  timetable to ensure vehicles separation". Players add slack to wait times so lateness doesn't build up.
- **Transport Fever 2**: no base-game timetable; the Timetables mod gives each station arrival/departure slot
  pairs, a train takes the slot whose arrival is nearest, and it controls only departures (holds early trains). Used
  to make locals wait for expresses at a passing station. Reloads have lost settings.
- **Simutrans**: per-stop "wait for load" with a max wait, and per-line "spacing" (departures per month spread over
  the convoys) with a shift; a forum dev notes true timetables don't fit how time passes in Simutrans.
- **A-Train** (Hajimaru Kanko Keikaku, the Switch game): per train, departure conditions by time ("first
  departure", "repeat interval", "number of departures"), set at any station; a late train skips to its next slot
  rather than leaving late; the player works out the cycle time by hand; copying a plan and offsetting it adds
  trains. Single-track meets are hand-timed with waiting points and get hard past about three trains. A-Train 9's
  weekday/holiday split: not confirmed.
- **Subway Builder**: trains per hour per route by time of day (since v1.7, from research/competitors.md); a TPH cap
  per train type and at grade crossings; stop and turnaround seconds per train type. No timetable view found.
- **Cities: Skylines II**: per line, the number of vehicles, the ticket price and the operating hours (day, night or
  both). No timetable.
- **Mini Metro**: lines get trains and carriages; no timetable or frequency setting (general knowledge of the game;
  no source found, as research/passing-tracks.md also notes).
- **Real operators.** Japanese station timetables split weekdays from Saturdays and holidays, list hours with the
  minutes under each, mark train types (no mark for a local, 特 for a limited express) and destinations by a single
  character, and mark trains that start there. Working timetables (Network Rail) carry platforms and running lines
  where there's a choice, and three kinds of padding: engineering, pathing and performance allowances. Swiss
  clock-face timetables repeat the same minutes every hour and line connections up at hub stations, which forces
  run times between hubs to fit the pattern (28 or 58 minutes).

What this suggests: every game that offers hand-made timetables pays in tedium and broken saves; the ones players
find easy give a count or interval per line, by time of day at most. Players get most from reading the timetable
(boards, diagrams) and trusting that trains do what it says. A nudge (an offset, as NIMBY's groups and OpenTTD's
start dates do) covers "shift trains slightly" without per-train editing. Japanese timetables' weekday versus
Saturday-and-holiday split is the same weekend grouping proposed here.
