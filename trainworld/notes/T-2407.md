# T-2407: the network diagram's city blocks (stage 1 of large-scale distortion)

Design: notes/T-2406.md, candidate 1 ("cluster then connect"). Rule: docs/ui-diagram.md ("The layout": A, Blocks,
The blocks' stage, A rebuild, Exposed). Code: `app/src/diagram/blocks.ts`, called from `layout.ts`' `layoutDiagram`
(and `warmStart`'s fallback, T-2417). Check: `checks/t2407-blocks.mjs` (the coast-to-coast mock, moved from
diagscale's `build.mjs`), pictures by `checks/t2407-draw.py`.

## What it does

- **Blocks** are cities by geography: `cityParts` cities joined within 15 km, 10 stations or more. Groups of other
  stations (joined by their hops) reaching one block are taken in (branches), lone groups within 15 km of a block
  are taken in (Staten Island's railway), lone groups far from any block are blocks of their own (islands, small
  networks alone), the rest are stations between blocks.
- **One block: nothing changes.** The real New York save is one block; its layout hashes the same as master's
  (1329633967 both, after diagpar's landing).
- **Two or more**: A lays out each block as if alone (hops between blocks left out of A; A's rounds by the biggest
  block). Then one least squares solve over a translation per block and a place per station between blocks: each
  hop between them runs its real bearing for its target plus, at a block end, the way out past the block's
  stations within 16 pitches of the bearing, plus half the 16-pitch gap. Blocks no track joins get links (the
  shortest set joining all, at a lonely hop's target). Anchors are tiny and weighted by the root of a block's size,
  so the big city stays where the hops leave a choice. Then rounds pushing boxes apart (grown by a fifth, padded by
  the gap). The hops between blocks keep the placed length as their target in B and the drawing; a station between
  blocks on a hop 4 median hops or longer ends a segment (else the routing slid the stops of a long bent piece into
  Los Angeles' box). After B, boxes B brought within 12 pitches are moved apart again.
- **Rebuilds**: the hops between blocks and the links the last layout had (by node keys, `LastPlaces.blocks`) are
  held at their drawn vectors; only new ones get targets, so an edit moves only what it changed. With the same set
  and no overlapping boxes the stage does not run. Each block's last places, B and drawn, move by its move.

## Tried and dropped

- **Boxes from the city stations only** (branches outside the box): branches then overlapped other blocks.
- **Every station farther than 15 km from a city left between blocks** (branches placed by the solve): the mock's
  New York branches overlapped Chicago's box, and all Japan's stretches got longer (Tatsuno-Shimoji 1,096 pitches,
  7 stretches over the block).
- **Exits to the box's edge** (diagscale's mock): a hop out of a sparse corner of a sprawling block (Kyoto-Osaka with
  Shiga) crossed empty box; replaced by the way past the block's own stations near the bearing.
- **Stations between blocks pushed out of boxes**: B and the drawing put some back inside, and every rebuild then
  pushed them out again, dragging the chain (blocks moved 20 to 46 pitches on an edit inside New York).
- **Re-solving from scratch on a rebuild** (the targets, not the drawn vectors, for hops that existed): undid B's
  own drift each time, moving Chicago 9 to 65 pitches against New York when one stop was added near Denver.

## For later stages: borders (T-2168) and water (T-2169)

`layout.blocks` (posted with the layout): `of` per node (block index, -1 between blocks), `core` per node (1 for the
city's own stations), and per block `box` (drawn), `move` (the stage's translation, layout units) and `fit`, an affine
map from real places (graph metres, `g.x`, `g.y`) to drawn ones, least squares over the block's stations.

- **City borders inside blocks** (T-2168): take each zone's real outline in graph metres and map each vertex. The
  affine `fit` is the coarse answer (a block is A's layout of its real places, roughly a scaled copy); for borders
  that follow where things are built, warp by the stations themselves: each vertex moved by an inverse-distance
  weighted blend of nearby stations' (drawn minus fitted) residuals, added to the block's fit. Keep vertices far from
  any station on the fit alone, so the outline stays the city's shape.
- **Water across blocks** (T-2169): a coastline must be continuous through blocks and the gaps between them, so no
  single block's fit will do. Warp each vertex by a blend of blocks: weights falling off with the real distance from
  the vertex to each block's stations (e.g. 1/d^2 to the block's nearest station, normalised), each block giving
  its `fit` of the vertex, plus the stations between blocks as control points with their own drawn places
  (moving-least-squares with all stations as controls is the general form; T-2146 named the complexes'
  triangulation). Inside a block its own fit dominates; in a gap between two blocks the vertex blends their moves,
  so the coast bends smoothly across the gap. A vertex far from all stations (open sea) takes the blend of the
  nearest few blocks, so oceans keep their side. Rivers as bands: warp the centreline the same way, then draw the
  band at a fixed width. The blocks' moves are rigid translations, so a coast crossing two blocks is only stretched
  in the gap, where the hops themselves are drawn short.
