# T-809: a gentler border for the EU, Schengen and the UK

Anita, 2026-10-21: the market's one border rule (0.26 x origin income^1.5, T-376) cut London - Paris
to about 9,600 trips a day; in play she saw about 10k through the Channel Tunnel.

## The rule

One open-border area: the EU 27, Norway, Iceland, Switzerland, Liechtenstein and the UK. A long
trip between two of its countries takes a border factor of **0.5**, whatever the origin's income
(`OPEN_AREA_BORDER`, `border_factor` in `sim/src/demand/intercity.rs`); a pair with an end outside
keeps 0.26 x income^1.5. Within one country there is no factor, as before.

Why a flat 0.5 and not the general rule multiplied up: what an open border removes (passports,
roaming, mostly one currency, through trains) does not scale with the origin's income, and a
multiplier would leave Bulgaria (general 0.08) or Romania far harsher than the Netherlands for no
reason the corridors show. The trip rate itself already scales with income (s = inc^0.7). The max
with the general rule keeps the area from ever being harsher than outside it (never true today: the
general rule tops out at 0.26). The general factor inside the area was 0.15-0.24 by country.

Countries come from each row's `country`. Melilla is still registered as "MA" (T-783), so it stays
outside the area: fixing it is a registry and rows change, left to T-783.

## Sources (open, average days)

- **London - Paris**: Eurostar carried almost 8M between London and France in 2023 (company figures,
  Rail Business Daily, "exceptional growth in 2023"), and London - Paris grew by another 280,000 in
  2024 (Business Traveller, 2025-01-24): about 8.3M a year, **22.7k a day by rail** both ways
  (some of it Lille, Disneyland and the Alps). Rail holds about 70-80% of rail + air (T-712's
  sources: Eurostar "over 70%", Bloomberg 2022 "80%"), so air about 6-10k a day; cars through
  the tunnel or on the ferries add little to this pair (the model sends 2% of its non-rail trips by
  car). **About 30k a day in all**, about 26k a weekday (the game's weekday is the average day over 1.17).
  Anita's "30,000 by Eurostar alone" is nearer all of Eurostar's London traffic.
- **Paris - Brussels**: Thalys carried 7.5M in 2018 on its whole network (TourMaG), about half of
  them on Paris - Brussels (Thalys: half of its first 50M, 2006): about 3.5-4M a year, plus OUIGO's
  1M in its first year from December 2024 (Railmarket, BX1): **about 12-13k a day by rail**. Rail
  held 43% of the corridor in 1998 (Railway Gazette); with car and coach the corridor is
  plausibly 20-25k a day.
- **Amsterdam - Brussels** (a land pair): Eurocity Direct (Amsterdam) and the EuroCity (Rotterdam)
  carried 3.5M in their first year from December 2024 (Railmarket, BX1, Treinreiziger), all of
  Brussels to the Netherlands with the stops between; with Eurostar's Brussels - Amsterdam part,
  **perhaps 4-7k a day by rail** between the two places, more with the car.
- Vienna - Bratislava and Copenhagen - Malmö: neither Bratislava nor Malmö is a registered zone yet,
  so no land pair across a Schengen inner border at under 100 km has two places to measure; Lille -
  Brussels (89 km) and Antwerp - Rotterdam (88 km) stand in, with no published total found.

What it says:
- London - Paris lands at the published level (about 1.1x). The land pairs stay under it (Paris -
  Brussels about two thirds of the rail figure alone, Amsterdam - Brussels about half): one constant
  cannot fit an island pair and a land pair at once, and a factor of 1 (no border at all) would put
  London - Paris at about twice the published total. 0.5 is the compromise, set on the corridor
  Anita sees in play. Domestic pairs of the same length were not checked against published
  totals here, so whether part of the land pairs' gap is the ground curve rather than the border is
  open.
- Pairs outside the area do not move now. After the rebuild they fall about a fifth: the long-haul
  tail's departures are shared over a normaliser that the open area raises 1.6-2.6x for European
  places, so some of a European's long trips move from New York or Moscow to Europe. Total trips
  per resident are unchanged (the fit's trips abroad stay as T-376 fitted them).

## Commutes between zones

The area does not apply to commutes. Commutes across a national border take `pipeline/borders.json`'s
`national_default` 0.05 (judgement on Windsor - Detroit), fixed offline by the joint gravity. An open
border plainly matters there too (Luxembourg, Geneva, Basel draw large cross-border workforces), but
a commute factor measures a different thing from a long-trip factor (a labour market split by tax,
law and language, not a propensity to travel), so 0.5 has no claim on it; it wants its own value
fitted on cross-border commuter counts, and it is a pipeline change with a joint gravity rebuild.
Filed as a task.
