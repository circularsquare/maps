## #7, peanutmow: scratch buffers for profile builds

- What: `sim/src/track/profile.rs` only. The profile builder allocated four vectors per hop; they are now kept per
  thread (`with_scratch`), and duration-only builds (`hop_time`) record no phases. The arithmetic and its order
  are unchanged, so results should be identical to the bit. The author reports a 240-line network loading in 7.1 s
  instead of 18.9 s, `lines_info` identical on the real save and theirs.
- Applies: cleanly to current master.
- Bears on: T-3388 (London edits about 4 s; T-3387's notes found a settle round's cost is "the cap clone and the
  profiles"), T-1936 (run profiles with many holds slow). Not the settle revision T-3387 waits for: #2 was closed
  2026-11-14, so T-3387's "PR is open for a revision" is stale. The PR links the 240-line save our #2 reply asked
  for (`big240.zip`), which is a useful bench for T-3388.
- Risk: low. No figures, saves or state change if bit-identical. One leftover: four unused `thread_local!` vectors
  (CUTS, IVS, FWD, BKD) that clippy will flag (maps lints clean since T-3443).
- Recommendation: **take**, with the dead thread-locals removed. Check before landing: `cargo test --release`, the
  T-3387 harness (`pr2cmp.rs`) or `fig_dump` on the real save, London and big240 identical to the bit, and London's
  edit time before and after. About 1-2 h.

## #1, Renkon: bus routes follow one-way streets

- What: the road graph keeps OpenMapTiles' `oneway` per edge; the bus search (`RoadGraph.path`) runs a one-way
  street only its way and goes around the block otherwise; a free end is searched forward instead of found backward
  and reversed. Arrows on one-way streets while the bus tool is on, from zoom 14. Trams and rail snapping are
  unchanged (`turnPath` ignores it). A unit test file comes with it.
- Applies: cleanly.
- Limits: a line set to both ways, or a loop, still runs the drawn road in reverse, so a two-way bus line on a
  one-way street still drives against it half the time. That makes it a half-measure for realism.
- Anita (19:08): "i dont really wanna do bus routes on one-way streets except as an optional thing".
- Recommendation: **leave**, or take as an option that is off by default. What an option costs: one setting read in
  `busRoads.ts` (the search treats every edge as two-way when off, which is today's behaviour exactly), the arrows
  only when on, the setting row and its string, a re-run of the test; existing routes keep their drawn points. About
  2-3 h with checks. Draft label for her review, if wanted: "Keep buses to one-way streets" (Settings, under Game).
  Her call (changes what players build).

## #13, GabrielMoraez: weekend and holiday train counts per line

What it does: each line gets its own trains per level for weekend days and for holidays. The line panel's schedule
table stacks three sections (Weekdays, Weekend, Holidays) with steppers; today's section is bold by the line's
country calendar. In the sim: `LineData::rest` saved in an `LDT1` block (save level 19); a per-line calendar window
(`service::Cal`, 12 days, sent by the clock worker); `Lattice::levels_on(day)` picks each day's slot; each kind of
day whose counts differ gets its own settle; crowding per kind of day on its own trains (`Boarding::kind_row`,
`HOLIDAY_ACCT`); running cost per kind of day; fleets sized to the busiest day; quad-track twins handled. Demand stays
the weekday solve scaled. 579 track tests pass.

Applies: 4 files conflict (holidayCalendar.ts, clock.worker.ts, t1071_tests.rs, twin_tests.rs); small.

## T-3373: demand workers share one copy of the zone data

Where it stands (notes/T-3071.md, notes/T-3329.md): every solving worker opens every reached zone, every market pack
and sets up each network; only the periods are split. A period is 15-25 MB; the rest, 85-95% of a worker, is copied
per worker: about 0.45 GB a worker on NYC, 1.3-1.4 GB on all-China. A 12-thread machine gets 3 solving workers.
T-3072 halves the pool only after an out-of-memory; Anita kept "a reported 8 GB keeps its workers".

**First, 2-3 h: the long-session check the goal asks for.** Her 7.4 GB in Firefox after 43 minutes is not explained
by the copies alone (NYC at 3 workers settles at 3.3 GB). A 45-minute session in Firefox and Chrome with edits and
pans, sampling `twDemand.client.memory()` and process memory, says whether the growth is the copies, WASM high water
that never shrinks, closed zones still held (T-1367), or the aux worker after pans (T-325). That decides which option
fixes her case.

Which PR pieces help: #12 part 3 (CPU speedups) makes option A cheaper in time; #12 part 4 (WebGPU) could in
principle make one worker enough, at the costs above. #12's chunked Furness passes are the shape a threaded build
(option C) would use. #7, #13 and #1 don't bear on it.
