# T-2238 and T-2230: an edit that cut a line's edge, and road tiles on big saves

Measured with `checks/t2227-roads-lag.mjs` on the real save (`RUNS=real:on`, and `real:off` at the end),
one demand worker, Vite on 6188 in tree t2238, other agents loading the machine (runs of one case vary by
about 50 ms). JSON in `artifacts/trainworld/t2226/t2227-t2238-*.json`.

## T-2238: why the fifth placement took 1.7 s

The fifth placement (a segment from -73.855, 40.672, Ozone Park) starts on line 11's track, so it cuts
that edge in two and line 11's path changes. Every other placement leaves every path alone, and
`lineBands` hands back its last answer. Here the worker's buffers (`networkFromState`) took 1.5 s:

- `lineBands` worked out every line's bands again (700 ms): any path change meant a whole pass.
- Then 90 of 109 lines' strokes were made again (750 ms), though their bands draw the same: the knots
  carry Traffic recipe ids, numbered across the whole network in line order, so a ribbon gained by line
  11 renumbered every line after it, and `networkFromState` compared the profiles bit for bit.

## What changed

- `network.ts` `sameBand`: profiles compare equal but for the recipe ids, which `bandPoints` reads only
  as none or the same as the next knot's. 92 lines rebuilt became 2.
- `lineBands.ts` `BandMemo`: each line's bands are kept with what they were made from (the edges it read,
  ribbon by ribbon; the boxes it looked in to hold a ribbon across a gap; its ribbon changes for T-2140's
  lining up). An edit works out again only the lines new, on another path, reading an edge that changed
  (its points, samples, lines or their directions) or holding a ribbon near one; then any line kept with
  a ribbon change linked, change to change within reach, to one that is new, gone or moved. Ribbons at
  samples are kept from call to call and hold until an edge they were made from changes. Recipes are
  numbered per line; the network's table and numbers are laid over them at the end, in the order a whole
  pass numbers them (a ribbon is one number by its lines and edges, where a pass gave two alike shapes two).
  The network-wide pair rule (T-2058) is worked out every call; if any pair's answer changes, every line
  is worked out again.
- `corridors.ts`: an edge's samples (own records and mirrors) are made again only where its records or
  those of an edge seeing it were, and stay the same object when the content is the same, which is how
  `lineBands` tells an edge's samples changed. The mirror check keeps a set per sample (it looked through
  the sample's list for each mirror, quadratic in a bundle).
- `?patch=check` also works out each build's bands whole and logs `T-2238 check:` on any difference
  (orders, profiles and Traffic records, recipes compared as what they are, numbers to 1e-4).

The lie: lines kept beside a line worked out are lined up with its changes as they were, so where a path's
metres move in the last bits (an edge cut in two upstream), a kept line can be off a whole pass by about
1e-9 m. Checked: eleven path-changing edits on the real save (the branch, undo, redo, stations on the three
busiest long edges, a line removed, undos) gave no difference against a whole pass, every time.

## T-2230: road tiles

`add_road_tile` went through every edge's pieces and worked each reached edge's crossings out again over
its whole length from every tile. Now (`Network::add_roads`, `roads::crossings_in_tile`): the edges near
the tile come from the 1 km edge grid (`Grid::in_box`); only the new tile's crossings are looked for (each
crossing is found in one tile), on the chords of the stretch reaching the tile, sampled where a whole pass
samples them, and merged into the edge's own (a cargo test checks the result equals a whole pass to the
bit); an edge with none new keeps its price; crossovers and track wholly half a level or more off the
ground keep no crossings (they can never be charged; `derive` skips them too). No `repriced`: only edge
prices change, and no line's rows hold one.

Install times on the real save (ghost pass, 26 tiles): before 0.9-6.5 ms (median 2.4); after 0.0-0.9 ms
(median 0.2). The placements' tiles: mostly 0.1-0.3, the worst 2.3 ms (a tile with long ground edges
right across it: every chord in the tile is still sampled).
