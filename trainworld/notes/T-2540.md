## Built (diagislands, 2026-11-05, tree `.tw-trees/diagislands`)

The rule as built is docs/ui-diagram.md "Water" (the far grid, land masses, the sea in the drawing, straits).

- **What was wrong.** The drawn sea was decided by rays: each station looked 16 ways on the ground for the sea within
  three mean hops, and a cell past the sea on that ray was sea, past no sea land. So far from the stations the drawing
  was wedges of land and sea running to the box's edge (the Pacific off Shikoku land, Hokkaido's seas in strips), and
  the coast between cities was made up. Islands drawn close together merged: all of Japan was one piece of land.
- **The sea sampled** (`waterSea.ts`): each voter carries the cell's offset back to the ground through its local map
  and asks whether that place is sea (`waterReal.ts` `isSea`). A far grid knows the sea past the coarse tiles (the
  masks' wide water, else the nearest known cell's), so open sea is sea however far from the stations. The ray code is
  gone.
- **Land masses** (`waterReal.ts` `massAt`): land connected round the sea and round water at least about 600 m wide
  everywhere across, over the tiles and the far grid. On all Japan: Honshu 7,147 stations, Kyushu 1,009, Shikoku 488,
  Hokkaido 429, Okinawa 19, Kobe's Port Island 8 (the Kanmon strait, 650 m at its narrowest, parts Kyushu).
- **Straits** (`waterSea.ts`): after smoothing, a land cell is sea where it meets a cell nearest a station on another
  land mass, or nearest a station with 2 km of sea between them on the ground (and 30% of the way) where the drawing
  squeezes them (drawn nearer than half their ground distance at their maps' scale: Tokyo Bay). Only a pill and half a
  pitch and half a cell round it stays land. A strait cut off inside the land (pills in its way) is dropped as small
  sea, so no slits.
- **The coast's fit**: seaward it strays at most 0.35 of the way across the sea straight out from it (so it never
  closes a bay or strait the grid has open; before, two coasts 6 pitches apart each strayed 12 and met), and within a
  cell of a strait half a pitch or 0.4 of a cell. `fitOcti` (`borders.ts`) now takes an outward tolerance smaller
  than the inward one (only the sea passes one; the zone borders' outward one is always the larger, so they are
  unchanged).
- **Mouths** (`water.ts`): a river end at the sea whose own sea is not drawn near and no way by water reaches one goes
  into whatever drawn sea is near (a drawn region's sea is voted by the places its cells are carried back to, which can
  be a neighbouring sea's), so no river end is left dry.
- **A raster fix**: the drawn sea rasterised back to the grid (`wet`) flipped whole rows where a ring's vertex sat on a
  row's middle and float rounding dropped the row from one segment's range (horizontal stripes of land across the sea
  in the regions the rivers meet).
- **The checks' frame** (`checks/diagram-saves.mjs`): the saves' frames were hard-coded (Japan at 139.7, 35.7) while
  Japan's saves are at 139.7671, 35.6812, so every place sat 6 km west of its water in the Node checks (Matsuyama's
  tram in the sea; T-2497/T-2516's Japan and Tokyo figures were measured so). `openSave` now returns the save's own
  `frameSpec`. The game was never affected (`view.ts` `zoneFrame` uses the stations' own lng, lat).
  `t2464-borders.mjs`, `t2438-city-names.mjs` and `t2289-diagram-intercity.mjs` still hard-code it (T-2542).
- **Checks** (`t2169-water.mjs`): a new PASS line, no two land masses of 10 or more stations drawn as one piece of
  land; and per land mass its pieces, which others share them, and its overlap with its land on the ground carried
  into the drawing by the similarity that best fits its stations (IoU: cells in both over cells in either). With
  `--png`, `<city>-islands.png` (the drawn land coloured by land mass) and the ground picture coloured by land mass.

## How others draw coasts on schematic maps

From memory and two searches (no map pages fetched):
- **JR group maps** (JR East's and JR Hokkaido's route maps, the JR timetable's national map): the four islands are
  always drawn apart, with the straits as plain gaps the Seikan and Kanmon lines and the Seto-Ohashi line cross; the
  coast is heavily simplified, often only a pale land shape behind the lines, and the islands' outlines are kept so
  that Japan reads at a glance even where the lines are drawn straight.
- **Tokyo Metro / Toei map**: Tokyo Bay drawn as one flat shape at 45 and 90 degrees with the Sumida and Arakawa;
  land past the network is cropped.
- **Hong Kong MTR map**: Victoria Harbour and the sea round Lantau kept as water with octilinear edges; Hong Kong
  Island and Lantau keep their rough outlines, so the islands read.
- Research: Bast, Brosi and Storandt (Metro Maps on Octilinear Grid Graphs, 2020) treat coastlines as obstacles the
  lines are routed around; an ICA 2026 abstract on automated Berlin diagrams notes that optimising local segments
  loses global shapes (the Ringbahn's circle). Both point the same way as here: a recognisable outline has to be held
  by the layout, which the water can only follow.
- What this took: islands always drawn apart (straits never closed), the coast from the real one carried into the
  drawing rather than made up, and few bends.
