# T-3906: plain bus speed floor at 20, 25, 30 and 35 km/h

Agent busfloor, tree `.tw-trees/busfloor` (measurement only, nothing landed). Diff: `notes/T-3906.patch`.

## For Anita (3 sentences)

A lower floor makes buses a little worse everywhere and never changes who wins: a 5 km bus in a dense city makes 36.2, 37.1, 37.8 and 38.2 US$M a day at floors 20, 25, 30 and 35, always 45 to 48% of the street tram's 79.8, and buses beat trams or rail only where nothing else pays. Bus trip share on a 2 km trip is 31.9%, 32.9%, 34.5% and 35.8% against the street tram's 42.7%, and rail riders move by under 0.1% from floor 20 to 35 (NYC 23.32M to 23.31M). BRT at 35 on top of a 30 floor would add only about 1.5 to 3 points of share and 8% shorter trips, while on top of a 20 floor it adds 4 to 9 points and 27% shorter trips (45.5 to 33.0 minutes round trip on the dense 5 km line), so 20 leaves the most room for BRT and 30 leaves little.

The trunk cap (1.5x, from Anita's second message) matters more than the floor on roads marked trunk: with every stop on a trunk road, today's uncapped bus does the dense 5 km round trip in 23.8 minutes and the cap puts it at 35.6, 31.8, 29.1 and 27.2 minutes for floors 20 to 35, which costs a dense bus 3 to 8% of its profit and takes the thin 5 and 10 km buses from +0.04 and +0.09 to about 0.

## What was run

- Constant: `BUS_LANE_KMH` in `sim/src/track/streets.rs` only. Demand has no copy of it (grep of `sim/src` and `app/src`: it is used in `bus_kmh` and tests only).
- Trunk cap: `trunk_bus_kmh(street_car) = min(0.85 x 88, 1.5 x bus_kmh(street_car))` in `sim/src/track/bus.rs`, used for trunk-flagged road in both `street_bus_kmh` and `main_bus_kmh`. Today a trunk stretch is `bus_kmh(max(car, 88))`, about 75 km/h. In a dense centre the cap is 1.5 x the floor: 30, 37.5, 45 and 52.5 km/h at floors 20 to 35.
- One WASM build per floor (the cap code is in all of them; it only acts on routes with the trunk bit). The baseline (30, no cap) is a separate build of today's code.
- Tools: plainbus's `tools/frontier.py` (`sweep --only <corridor>:<km>:bus`, run twice so the S service runs, `shares --modes bus --spacings 400 --offsets 0`), the same twelve NYC and London test routes (`addbuses_nyc.js`, `addbuses_lon.js`, headway default), real saves `data/packs/real/nyc.save` and `lon.save`, one calculation after adding the 12 buses. Chicago ("cic"), sandbox.
- Trunk measurement needed a small tool change: the fixtures draw straight routes with no trunk bit, so none of the 12 test buses nor any frontier bus uses a trunk road as built, and the cap changes nothing for them (the 20/25/35 tables 1a and 2 to 3 are the same with or without the cap). To see the cap, `TW_TRUNK=1` sets the trunk bit on every route point of the frontier buses (table 1b, an upper bound: every road is a trunk road). I did not run the 12 NYC and London buses with trunk bits, so "how many of the 12 use trunk roads" is none as built, and the real figure depends on how a player draws the route.

## Real-world yardsticks

- NYC local bus: 7.8 mph (12.6 km/h) citywide, 2020-2024 ([NYC Comptroller, Behind Schedule](https://comptroller.nyc.gov/wp-content/uploads/documents/Behind-Schedule-Bus-Report.pdf)); 8.18 mph for the first ten months of 2023, 6.28 in Manhattan ([amNY](https://www.amny.com/nyc-transit/nyc-buses-running-slowest-speeds-since-2019/)).
- London: 9.3 mph (15 km/h) network average in 2023/24, 7 mph in the City of London ([TfL via London Assembly](https://www.london.gov.uk/who-we-are/what-london-assembly-does/questions-mayor/find-an-answer/average-london-bus-network-speed-fy-201718-202324)). I saw the 15 km/h as a conversion in a search summary, not quoted by TfL.
- BRT: ITDP's BRT Standard gives full marks at a corridor average of 20 km/h; its guide suggests above 20 km/h with frequent intersections and 25 where traffic interference is low; real systems: TransMilenio about 27 km/h, Lahore 26 ([ITDP BRT Standard](https://africa.itdp.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/06/BRT2016-REV7.75.pdf), [BRT Guide](https://brtguide.itdp.org/branch/master/guide/system-speed-and-capacity/design-objectives)). Search-summary figures, not checked against the pages.
- Against these: floor 30 gives a city bus 18.9 km/h with stops, above London's 15 and NYC's 12.6 and about where a real BRT line starts; floor 20 gives 14.4, London's figure; floor 25 gives 16.8; floor 35 gives 20.8, the ITDP corridor benchmark.
