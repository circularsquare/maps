# T-2660 / T-2665 timetable chart: other lines, flat junctions, span and zoom — notes

Agent marey, 2026-11-06. The rules are in docs/ui-inspectors.md "The timetable chart".

## References looked at (flat junctions and shared track)

- Bildfahrplan / graphical timetable practice (Ibry 1846 onward; RailSys, Viriato, OpenTrack, FBS draw
  one): junctions ("Abzw", Abzweigstelle) sit on the distance axis as a rule like a station; a train of
  a route that leaves the line ends at that rule, one that joins starts there. Conflicts are read from
  the blocking time stairs (Sperrzeitentreppe): each train's occupation of each block section, with
  margins, drawn as boxes along its path; two trains' boxes overlapping is a conflict.
  https://railwaynews.net/wiki/the-pulse-of-the-network-mastering-railway-timetabling,
  https://www.via.rwth-aachen.de/english/program_family.php?x=18 (time-route graphs and blocking time
  stairs before simulation), https://ep.liu.se/ecp/069/071/ecp19069071.pdf (blocking stairway, conflicts
  where they overlap), https://journals.open.tudelft.nl/ejtir/article/download/4331/4229 (junction
  running and buffer times).
- TrenoLive's graphic timetable: trains coloured by line; conflicts marked where they arise, colour by
  severity (red: two trains on the same infrastructure element), shown only after a conflict check run;
  blocking times drawable as "Calculated" or "Planned".
  https://docs.trenolab.com/docs/getting-started/trenolive/timetabling/graphic_timetable,
  https://docs.trenolab.com/docs/getting-started/trenolive/timetabling/conflicts
- PTV Visum's graphical timetable: per-line graphic parameters (colour, width) for the lines drawn.
  https://cgi.ptvgroup.com/vision-help/VISUM_2021_ENG/Content/2_Daten_tabellarisch_grafisch_darstellen/Grafikparameter_fuer_den_grafischen_Fahrplan_einstellen.htm
- NIMBY Rails: no time-distance chart found in the game (its timetable is per stop times;
  https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/Timetable). JMRI's TimeTable tool draws a train graph per segment:
  https://jmri.org/help/en/html/tools/TimeTable.shtml.

Taken: the junction as a rule across the chart (dashed, so it reads apart from the stops' grey rules),
with the line panel's diamond or cross at the right end and its hover; other routes' trains in their
line colour, thinner and fainter, ending at the junction rule where they leave; and, in place of full
blocking stairs (too costly per redraw, and the game's guard has no block sections), a dot where each
train of a conflicting move passes the junction or crossing, so the player sees which of the line's
trains must take turns with it. Not taken: blocking boxes, conflict colouring by severity.

## Which saves show it

- T-007-real-nyc.save has no two lines on one track edge (0 of 109 in the path table) and no flat
  crossing or crossing junction move (the guard's conflicts are empty), so on it the chart has no other
  lines, no toggle and no rules. data/packs/real/lon.save (the London real network) shares heavily:
  163 of 171 lines share an edge; Thameslink (line 165) draws 1,131 other trains over 4 hours
  (Southern, Southeastern, Great Northern, the other Thameslinks), 4,087 over a day. It has no flat
  conflicts either (real networks' junctions are flyovers). The flat crossing rule, its hover and the
  dots were checked on two lines built crossing at grade in a new game (scratch check measure2.mjs).
