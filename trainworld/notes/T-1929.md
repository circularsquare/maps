Design: notes/T-1897.md section 2. Rule as built: docs/track-and-service.md 6.4 "Road crossings".

Against the viaduct's US$75M a km: ground through Manhattan, Tokyo and London is 20-35% dearer;
rural ground (26-27) stays far the cheapest; suburbs land near the viaduct (58-87). Paris's centre
(wide blocks, many roads on bridges or one-way pairs counted once) stays a little under. US$6M meets
the aim, so it stands. The T-1897 estimates (suburb 4, centre 10) were low: suburbs measure 6-11.
Japanese farmland is the outlier: its many paved farm roads are `minor` in OpenMapTiles, so ground
there costs about a viaduct's price.

## How it is built

The brief had the main thread count the crossings and send them with each edit. Built instead
like terrain, in the clock worker: many edits get their shape from the track model itself (a node
drag, a reshape, a bridge over water, a split), so the main thread cannot know the polyline before
the edit. The worker fetches the tiles (the page sends the basemap's URL template) and gives them
to the track model, which works the crossings out per edge whenever its shape is derived. Money
stays right because a tile landing reworks every edge it reaches at once, outside any edit, so an
edit's charge (the change in what is constructed) never picks up a tile's arrival. Constructed
track in an old save gains its crossings in the figures when a tile under it lands (the built
total moves; nothing is charged).
