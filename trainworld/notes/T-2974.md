# T-2974: train flow at merges and terminals (design)

Agent trainflow, 2026-11-09. Designed, then built 2026-11-10 in tree `tw/terminus`: stage A as T-2980, B as T-2972, C as T-2973 (sections at the end). Sources and other games:
`research/train-flow.md`. Measurements: scratch tests on a copy of the crate (kept as
`../artifacts/trainworld/t2974_tests.rs.txt` and `t2974_tflow_tests.rs.txt`), outputs
`../artifacts/trainworld/t2974-y-terminal-merge.txt`, `t2974-both-directions.txt` and
`t2974-nyc-save.txt`.

## In short

**The terminus queue is the drawing, not the timetable.** The game's capacity sums already treat a
two-platform terminus as two platforms, and a terminal's sidings as two places to turn. But the
drawn trains always stand on one spot, the end of the arrival track. When a second train is due
while the first is still standing, the drawing holds it outside, on the approach, and the delay is
passed on to its next trip. The sidings are drawn as track but no train ever goes into them. So the
player is right: "even with a siding, only one train can stop at the terminus". In a test with two
lines into one terminus at 5 minutes each, about half the arrivals queue there for a minute and a
half; with lines set by headway, which stand their spare minutes at the terminus, nearly all do.
Drawn with both platforms in use, none queue.

**At a merge, two things keep trains from taking even turns.** Lines set by train count run
"round trip divided by trains", so two branches of different lengths run very slightly different
headways (say 5:01 and 5:02) and slide past each other through the day: fine at 9, bunched by 5pm.
Lines set by headway now take even turns at the merge (tonight's fix), but only towards the trunk:
the other direction can come out uneven, because each line stands its spare minutes half at each
end, wherever that leaves its trains.

**What real railways do.** Lines that share track run headways from one family (the same, or
double), offset so they alternate: two branches every 10 minutes, 5 minutes apart, give a train
every 5 on the trunk. Each line stands its spare minutes at its ends (recovery time), and planners
use that slack to fit each direction into its slot. Busy shared terminals turn trains fast on two
platforms or sidings; the long waits happen at the quiet outer ends.

**The useful finding.** Where the spare minutes are stood is the knob that times the other
direction. Standing all of a line's spare time at the end it doesn't share (the far end of its
branch) instead of half at each end, a test of 30 two-branch layouts with headway lines came out
even both ways (the worst gap ratio 1.14:1 towards the trunk and 1.14:1 away from it, where away
from it was up to 8:1), and the shared terminus emptied out (arrivals queuing there went from half
or nearly all to none at 10 and 7 minute headways). No train is added or removed, and riders' waits don't move.

**Count vs headway, made the same.** Yes, make them behave as if a headway is set. Under the hood
a count line already is a headway line whose headway is its round trip over its trains, with no
spare time. What makes them differ at a merge is that two count lines get slightly different
headways. So: count lines meeting at a merge whose headways are within 3% of each other all run the
longest of them, the faster ones standing the difference at their far end, like a headway line.
In the test, 5:01 and 5:02 lines went from 1.6:1 at 9 and 30:1 by 5pm to 1.02:1 all day, for
0.4% fewer trains on the trunk.

**Recommendation, in order:**

1. Draw terminus trains on both platforms, and into the sidings at a terminal. Fixes the player's
   post and the "terminal should hold 2 trains" note. Drawing only.
2. Let the spacing choose where each line stands its spare time (which end, or how it is split).
   Both directions even for headway lines, the shared terminus quiet. Sim, small to medium.
3. Count lines through a merge share one headway (within 3%), and a full merge is shared out
   evenly. Then count and headway lines behave the same. Sim, medium.

Leave out: timetable slots as a thing the player edits, and train-by-train dispatch (each train
simulated against the one ahead). Both cost far more, and 2 and 3 get the same picture from the
repeating timetables the game already has. Optional, your call: round count lines' headways up to
a whole 15 seconds, so the panel shows tidy numbers (about 1% fewer trains an hour on average on
the New York save, up to 7% on the most frequent lines).

## 1. What the game does today

### At a merge

Each line runs a repeating timetable of its own (`service.rs` `lattice_deps_local`: departures at
`start - lead + phase + k x headway` per stretch of one demand level). Nothing queues trains at a
merge: its wait is an average (Kingman, `capacity.rs`) baked into each train's run as a hold just
short of the junction, the same for every train. So the order and the gaps at the merge are just
the lines' departure patterns laid over each other. Two things set those:

- **Headways.** A headway line keeps the headway set. A count line runs round trip / trains, so
  two count lines on branches of different length differ by a second or so (301.2 s and 302.3 s
  in the test), and the gap between their trains creeps by that every train, all day (T-2960:
  2.3:1 at 9h, 7:1 at 13h, 19:1 at 17h).
- **Phase.** `spacing.rs` gives each line one phase per demand level, picked when the line is edited
  so its trains meet other lines' as far apart as possible, the merge weighing 25 times any other
  meeting (T-2960, landed tonight). One phase moves both directions together. A headway line's
  reverse departures leave the far end after its run, its turnaround and half its spare time
  (`Service::lattice`, `off = there + 0.5 x wait`), so the other direction's gaps are whatever
  that leaves; up to 8:1 leaving the trunk where branch run times differ by most of a headway.

### At a terminus

- **The sim.** A dead end holds each turning train for `max(turnaround + 60 s, 200 s)`, pooled over
  its platform tracks (30 an hour on two at a 3 minute turnaround); a terminal's sidings the same
  (kind 10); a line ending at a through station without one holds its arrival platform for the
  turnaround plus 60 s (15 an hour, kind 6). The wait there is an average hold before the station,
  shown as a dot from 75%. One line alone gets no wait until it is over the limit. A headway line's
  spare minutes are stood at the platform and are not counted as occupancy.
- **The drawing.** A train finishing a run stands at the end of that run, on its own side's track,
  until its next departure; the next trip starts on the other side's track. There is one standing
  place per line end. The turn-back scissors and the terminal's sidings are drawn
  (`network.ts` `turnbackStrokes`, `terminalSidings`) but no drawn train uses them.
- **The guard** (`trainGuard.ts`, display only): on lines that share any track with another line,
  a train may come no closer than 30 m behind the back of the train ahead on the same track, and a
  held train's lateness carries into its next departure. A train standing at the end of its run
  counts as "ahead". So a second train due while the first stands is held on the approach,
  and the queue passes on down the line. On a line that shares no track, the two are drawn one on
  top of the other instead.

So the player's queue happens when a terminal end's standing time is longer than the gap between
arrivals there:

- a count line stands its turnaround (3 minutes by default), so one line queues at headways under 3
  minutes, and two lines into one terminus at under 6 minutes each;
- a headway line stands its turnaround plus half its spare time, up to half a headway more, so it
  queues at much longer headways.

Measured (Y: 3 km trunk into terminus T, branches 5.1 and 6.0 km, both lines ending at T, 9h-13h;
`t2974-y-terminal-merge.txt`):

| schedule (each line) | stand at T | arrivals queuing, one spot (as drawn) | mean wait | two spots (both platforms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 trains (5:27) | 180 s | 49% | 85 s | 0% |
| 4 trains (5:01/5:02) | 180 s | 51% | 82 s | 0% |
| headway 10 min | 396 s | 98% | 96 s | 0% |
| headway 7 min | 192 s | 0% | 0 | 0% |
| headway 6 min | 281 s | 99% | 101 s | 0% |
| headway 5 min | 224 s | 67% | 117 s | 0% |

## 2. Real railways and other games

Detail and links: `research/train-flow.md`. In short:

- Branches onto a trunk run clock-face headways from one family, offset to alternate (Munich: 20
  minutes per branch, seven lines through one tunnel; Stuttgart: 2.5 minutes on the trunk; Elizabeth
  line: 24 an hour in the core from four branches).
- Each line carries recovery time at its ends; that slack lets the planner time each direction into
  its slot on the shared section. Padding runs about 7% (Switzerland) to 25% (Germany).
- Busy terminals use two platforms with a scissors, or sidings beyond the platform; arriving trains
  take whichever is free.
- Games: NIMBY Rails and OpenTTD simulate every train against signals and leave the timetable and
  the spacing to the player (OpenTTD spreads a shared order list evenly by start date; auto-
  separation and scheduled dispatch are patch-pack features). Transport Fever 2 has no timetables
  without a mod, and added alternative terminals so a line's train can use another platform. Nothing
  found for Subway Builder or Mini Metro.

The game already has what clock-face timetabling needs (every line a repeating pattern), and none of
the per-train cost; what to copy from the other games is the free-platform choice at terminals.

## 3. Count mode and headway mode, the same

Anita: "ideally the behavior between set number of trains vs set headways should basically be
identical ... maybe we make it behave like headways are set?"

How they differ now:

| | count line | headway line |
|---|---|---|
| headway run | round trip / trains, to the bit | as set (up to 0.5% over) |
| spare time at the ends | none | up to a headway, half at each end |
| at a merge with a like line | headways differ slightly, gaps drift all day | even towards the trunk (T-2960) |
| the other direction | fixed by the phase | whatever half the spare time leaves |

Proposal: every line is timetabled as a headway line. For a count line the headway is the shortest
its trains can keep, as today, so on its own nothing changes. The two rules that make them alike
where it matters:

- **Through a merge, one headway** (option C). Count lines meeting at a merge (and, through other
  merges, each other) whose headways are within 3% of the longest of them all run that longest one.
  The faster ones now have spare time, stood like a headway line's. Lines further apart keep their
  own headways: forcing them together would cost real trains (6 trains each on a full flat
  junction gave 3:39 and 7:02; the longer for both would cut the trunk by a third).
- **Spare time stood where it evens the other direction** (option B), for every line that has some.

A headway line set by the player keeps its headway: the panel's promise "the headway set is the
headway run" stays. Two headway lines set to 5:00 and 5:10 at one merge stay uneven (a question
below).

**A full merge shared out evenly** (part of C): today a merge at capacity cuts lines in proportion
to what their fleets would give, and in the test that left two identical 6-train lines at 3:39 and
7:02 (`t2974-y-terminal-merge.txt`; T-2960 saw the same). With one shared headway the merge's trains
an hour would be split evenly instead, about 4:48 each, the same total. Worth a look at
`Capacity::fill` while doing C; which line takes the queue at a flat junction is the likely cause.

What the player sees: count lines through a merge take even turns all day; the headway in their
panel may rise by a few seconds (5:01 to 5:02); their trains wait a little at the far end. Costs,
measured on the Y with 3 or 4 trains a line: two lines 0.4-0.5% apart lost 0.4% of the trunk's
trains an hour; 4.6% apart (outside the 3%), 2.4%; 14% apart, 6.6%. A fleet never grows. Demand
re-solves for those lines, as for any rate change.

**Rounding count headways (option D, optional).** "Behave like headways are set" could also mean
the panel shows a tidy headway. Rounding round trip / trains up to a step, on the New York save
(102 count lines, `t2974-nyc-save.txt`): to 15 s, 1.1% fewer trains an hour on average, worst 6.8%,
2 trains freed; to 30 s, 1.9% and 14.5%; to a clock-face family (2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7.5, 10 ...
minutes), 10.9% and 32%: too costly. It doesn't by itself fix merges (5:01 and 5:02 both become
5:15, but 4:59 and 5:01 don't), so C is still needed; and the settle needs hysteresis so a round
trip sitting on a step does not flip. I'd leave it out unless tidy numbers matter to you.

## 4. Getting close to the real picture

### B: where the spare time stands (the reverse direction's timing)

A line's reverse departures leave the far end after its run, its turnaround and a share `x` of its
spare time `wait` (`Service::lattice`, now `x = 0.5`). That share is the reverse direction's phase:
it can be anything in `[0, wait]` without a train more or less, and the round trip stays whole
headways. So T-2972's "second phase for the reverse departures" is exactly this share, with the
spare time as its limit.

Measured (T-2960's 30 headway layouts: 10, 7 and 6 minutes, five branch geometries, flat and
flying, 9h; `x = 1`, all spare time at the end the lines don't share, against `x = 0.5`):

| headway | towards the trunk, worst | away from it, worst | arrivals queuing at T (one spot), median |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 min | 1.09 -> 1.08 | 1.48 -> 1.08 | 98% -> 0% |
| 7 min | 1.20 -> 1.07 | 8.05 -> 1.12 | 49% -> 0% |
| 6 min | 1.16 -> 1.14 | 2.09 -> 1.14 | 99% -> 50% |

Why it works on a Y into a shared terminus: the two lines' trains leave T in some order and come
back to the merge after their branch round trips plus the spare time stood out there; with all of
it at the branch end, each line's cycle is whole headways from T to T, so the order leaving T is
the order coming back. Elsewhere (a trunk in the middle with branches at both ends, a line sharing
both ends) the right share isn't always 0 or 1, so the stage should let `spacing::place` choose
`x` with the phase: about 9 values of `x` for each of its 24 phases, scored on run 1's meetings
(the merge first, as now). Lines with no spare time (count lines on their own) keep `x` as it is.

Player sees: both directions even; trains waiting out their spare minutes at the outer ends rather
than at the shared terminus, as real lines do. Throughput, rates and demand: unchanged (waits are by
headway). Speed: on edits only, 9 x 24 crowding sums instead of 24. Save: `x` per line and level in
a new trailing block beside `LPH1`; without it, 0.5 as now. Eras already store the reverse offset
(`ERA2` writes `lat.off`), so they need nothing new. Size: one sim stage (service.rs `lattice`,
spacing.rs, save.rs, tests), Opus since scoring both directions together has open choices.

### C: one headway through a merge

As in section 3. Where: the settle (`TrackWorld::settle_train_counts`, `Service::lattice`, the
rates demand reads), grouping lines by the merges (wire kind 12) they share; and `Capacity::fill`
for the even split. Old saves' settled rates come out different for grouped lines and settle again
on load. Size: one sim stage, Opus (it touches the settle's convergence; T-1283 and T-1787 show how
touchy that is). Do it after B so the faster lines' new spare time goes where B puts it.

### E and F, left out

- **Slots on the shared section** (a merge holds N slots an hour, lines take slots): this is how a
  planner works, and it would show the player why a line can't fit. But with every line already a
  repeating pattern, B and C give the same timings without a new idea for the player to manage.
  Worth revisiting only if players ask to place lines by hand.
- **Train-by-train dispatch** (NIMBY Rails, OpenTTD): trains really wait for the one ahead and delays
  spread. It needs every train simulated as events in every city, which is the expensive end for a
  game meant for hundreds of cities; and boarding, demand and the drawn trains all rely on trains
  being a pure function of the timetable, so it would touch everything. The guard already draws the
  local waits that a player can see.

## 5. The terminus: holding two trains

The sums need nothing for a dead end or a terminal: they already count both platforms (or both
sidings). The work is the drawing (option A):

- **Dead end with two platform tracks.** Each arriving train takes the platform that frees first:
  one on the arrival side stays on its track; one on the other side crosses the scissors on the way
  in and leaves straight out. Drawn as a lane change over the approach, the way passing stations
  draw a stopping train on the outer track (T-2233, `PASS_OUTER`). The guard treats each platform as
  its own standing place.
- **Through station with a terminal.** The train stops at the arrival platform for its dwell, is
  drawn running forward into one of the two sidings, stands its turnaround there, and comes out to
  the departure platform for its dwell. The sidings already have strokes (`terminalSidings`).
- **Through station without a terminal, a single-track dead end.** One place to turn; a queue there
  is real (15 or 18 an hour), and the capacity dot and the line's alert already point at building a
  terminal.
- **Picking the platform the same in every window.** Drawn trips are a pure function of the
  timetable. The pick should be too: assign platforms by arrival order over the trips of all lines
  ending at that node (first free, started a fixed time before the window), done where the trips
  are made, so a reload or another window draws the same.
- **When both are taken.** A third train waits outside, as now. With B, spare time moves to the
  quiet ends, so this gets rare. A follow-up could count stood spare time as terminus occupancy so
  the dot warns when it fills a terminus; not needed first.

Size: one app stage (network.ts lanes at line ends, trainGuard.ts places per platform, the trips'
platform pick), Opus for the determinism and the guard, own tree since trainGuard.ts is shared.
Nothing in sim/, no save change, no demand effect.

## 6. Recommendation

1. **A, the terminus drawing**, first: it is what players see and post about, it is display only,
   and it covers T-2898.
2. **B, where the spare time stands**, alongside A (sim versus app, no shared files): both
   directions even for headway lines at no cost. Reshapes T-2972.
3. **C, one headway through a merge**, after B: count lines behave as headway lines. T-2973.

Left out, and why: D unless you want tidy headways (costs trains for looks); E (a new idea to manage
for the same timings); F (the per-train cost the game avoids on purpose). This fits "sensible and
fun over realistic" and "simple, predictable": no new controls, and the rule a player could learn
is one line: lines that share a merge keep in step, and trains wait out spare minutes at the far end.

What stays short of the real picture: lines at unrelated headways (5 and 7 minutes) can't
interweave, as on a real railway; delays never spread from one train to the next; and a count line
on its own has no spare time, so a line with no merge partner keeps its single phase.

## Stage A built (T-2980): trains use both platforms and the sidings

What changed (app only, no sim, no save):

- `app/src/map/terminalBays.ts`: `terminalEnds` finds each line end's places (a double-track dead
  end: two platforms; a station with a terminal: two sidings; a through platform without one:
  one place, but the next trip leaves over the diagonal behind it instead of jumping across).
  `profileTimes` reads, per profile, when a trip reaches its last stop (the turnaround is inside a
  trip's seconds) and when it passes a point on the way out (holds at the platform end included).
  `assignBays` gives each arriving train the place that frees first, its own track on a tie, wanted
  from 30 s before it stops; a place is taken until the train's next trip is 250 m past the
  platform's end. Lines arriving along the same track share the places.
- The clock worker runs `assignBays` for each hour sent, with the hour before as lookback, and sends
  four numbers per trip with the trips (`bays`). Deterministic: a function of the timetable.
- Train shader: `acrossAt` moves a train across to the other track over the 50 m crossover (into a
  dead end's other platform, or out of the arrival side's), `sidingMove` runs a terminal's train
  forward into its siding and back to the other platform during its stand (30 s, 40 s, stand, 40 s,
  30 s; squeezed below 140 s), `lineAtX` carries the line on straight past its end for the siding.
  `tripAt` (clicks, the train panel) does the same on the CPU.
- Train guard: a train standing at a two-place end holds no one on its track; one due at a place
  still taken (standing there, or leaving and not yet off the platform) stands short of the crossover.
- `?perfOff=bays` gives the old drawing, for A/B checks.

Measured.

The Y's timetables from the scratch Rust dump through `assignBays` (9h-13h, both lines ending at
T; "queue" is an arrival finding its place still taken):

| 3 trains | 49% queue, 85 s | 1%, 115 s |
| 4 trains | 51%, 82 s | 0% |

## Stage B built (T-2972): where the spare time stands

`Network::line_split` (per line and level, the share of `Service::spare` stood at the last stop,
0.5 by default) sets the reverse offset in `Service::lattice`. `spacing::place` searches it with the
phase (24 x 9), seeds it at the end the line does not share (`seed_split`), then moves the met
lines' shares (split only) and places the edit's lines once more. A bug found on the way: the
placement reads other lines' times from lattices built while `split` (like `phase`) is out of the
network, so their offsets are corrected by hand (`base`). Saved in `LSP1` (level 12; agent oneway's
`LOW1` takes 11). Test: `spacing::tests::trains_take_even_turns_at_a_merge` now also checks the
direction leaving T and the save round trip.

T-2960's 30 headway layouts at 09h (merge worst / leaving T worst), before (T-2960 landed) -> after:

| headway | towards the trunk | leaving T | arrivals at T sharing one spot (median) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 min | 1.09 -> 1.08 | 1.48 -> 1.08 | 98% -> 0% |
| 7 min | 1.20 -> 1.07 | 8.05 -> 1.14 | 49% -> 0% |
| 6 min | 1.16 -> 1.06 | 2.09 -> 1.38 | 99% -> 49% |

The 6 minute worst is a flat junction, whose crossing (90 s) the spacing also weighs. Medians are
1.04 both ways in all three. Outputs: `../artifacts/trainworld/t2972-*`.

## Stage C built (T-2973): count lines in step at a merge

`TrackWorld::pace_merges`, after every settle (three rounds at most): count lines meeting at merges
are grouped; within a group, per level, lines whose round trip over trains is within 3% of the
longest run that headway plus 0.2% (all of them, the slowest too); a headway line counts with its set
headway and is never moved. A merge at 98% or more is shared out evenly between its count lines.
The pace is `Network::line_pace`; `Network::running_trains` and `Service::spare` treat a paced
line as a headway line, so its spare time is stood where stage B puts it. Derived, not saved. Test:
`spacing::tests::count_lines_at_a_merge_run_in_step`.

The Y (3 km trunk into T, flat junction), count lines, before -> after (`../artifacts/trainworld/t2973-y-after.txt`):

|---|---|---|---|
