## What changed

- Rust (service.rs): `note_moved` also records, per moved run, where it moved (`Span`): from the first entry unlike
  the old run's (or on an edge the edit touched) to the start of the alike end, whose entries moved along by the run's
  length change alone (to 1e-6 m); the length changes add up as `drift`. Several rebuilds before a take make one span
  over all (the earlier one widened by the later drift), none known (a global pass, a bus or ferry line, a line that
  stops being ok) makes the run whole. Taken with `take_moved`, put back with `moved` after a trial.
- Rust (wasm): `take_moved_spans` (`[line, run, lo, hi, drift, len]` each), `run_polyline_range` and
  `run_bends_range` (`bends::bends_in`), which give the whole run's numbers bit for bit there.
- Worker (clockMain.ts `forget`, `spliceRun0`): a moved line's run 0 with kept samples is sampled again only from one
  sample before its span to one after it, its bends for every interval with a sample sampled again, the rest kept; it is
  named in `touched.paths` only where they differ (T-4280's rule). Whole as before when nothing is kept, no span is
  known, the count of samples changed, its summed drift would pass 5 cm (`DRIFT_MAX_M`), or under `?patch=walk`.
  `?patch=check`'s sample check compares a drifted line within its drift (ground m) and its bends not at all.
- Test: changed_tests.rs `a_station_on_a_lines_track_moves_its_runs_only_around_it` (a station on a curved line's
  track, then two before one take: spans for both runs, the stations inside, samples outside within the drift of a
  whole resample, bends outside alike; the ranged calls equal the whole run's slices bit for bit).

### What a later click waited behind, from the code

The worker takes a message only between tasks. After an edit, its follow-up (the boarding steps, the services owed,
then the state: the rows, the network in pieces, the post with the trips) goes a piece a task, and an edit that
comes between the pieces goes ahead of it (`goesAhead`, `standAside`). But `processMessage` sent every other
message, every question but the delete question's among them, to `guardedFollowUp`, which ran what was left of the
follow-up whole (`runFollowUp`), and `sendTrips` did the same from a timer (`tripsPrelude`). The page asks `today`
on every new world and `blueprintQuote` a quarter second after it, so in a click run a question sat in the queue
behind most clicks, and the next click waited the whole follow-up.

### Why the post and the rows were not made local here

Every state carries whole row lists (`ClockState.edges`, `nodes`, `lines`), and both the drawn network
(`edgeDiff`, `sameRefs`) and the post (`rowsDelta`, `sameBytes` on the network's arrays) find what changed by
comparing all of them. The post's compares cannot cost what the edit touched while their inputs are whole lists and
fresh arrays: `rowsDelta` needs the rows to hand over what they read again since the last post (a delta), and
`pSame` needs the network build to pass an array it did not rebuild as the same object (map/network.ts, netcut's).
The rows themselves (`begin`, the edge and node reads, the names folded over every station) copy and compare every
record. One change covers them all: the rows built from the touched list as a delta, which the network and the post
take as it is. Filed as T-4291.

whole), samples equal to the model's bit for bit, no check error.

From the code, every state the worker builds comes after that piece or after a commit that settles first:
- a follow-up's state: `followSteps` settles first; an edit going ahead first settles in Rust (`commit_recorded`,
  undo and redo, a trial with services all call `settle_owed` first, its moved lines taken by that edit's `forget`);
- every other message runs the waiting follow-up whole before it (clock.worker.ts `processMessage`), the
  fare/link/building edits included; questions wait for its state; a state from fetched ground or streets runs it
  first unless held, and a held follow-up never carries owed services (a station or delete is never held);
- a save settles first (`saveBytes`, `saveParts`), autosave waits (`tick`).
And the only lines whose runs are stale in that window run no trains: running no line's trains is what lets an edit
owe its services. So nothing drawn is ever wrong; the netfix8 error was the check's own order.

### Why it cost a whole read (from the code)

- sim boarding.rs `window` (T-4149 stage 1) kept each line's block with what it read, the time included
  (`day_key`'s first entry was `t`'s bits). While the clock runs the time differs at every question, so no block was
  ever used again: every question read every line's whole day (every departure at every stop, both days' accounts,
  twice over for the trains, plus the account's crowding by period).
- The page asks `today` every 250 ms while a panel showing it is open (game/today.ts `useToday`), paced by the clock
  client to at most a third of the worker (`POLL_REST`). In her 1.7-hour session it was asked 590 times, at most 264 ms
  each (at most about 2.5% of the session); how much of a click's wait it made is not measured.
- The worker's side (workers/clockMoney.ts `pastDays`) then worked out each line's 49 hours of fares again
  (`fareHour`, 6 samples each), about 2 ms of 4 on Asia once the model's part was kept.

### What changed

- sim `boarding/day.rs` (new; `window` moved there from boarding.rs): each line's block is kept as `DayKept`, keyed by
  its accounts, service parts and local day, and moved along (`advance`) while those stand and the clock goes forward:
  - per stop and part (yesterday's rest, today so far), the window's bounds in the stop's departure order (`lo`,
    `done`) and its sums: today's take in the departures the clock passed, yesterday's let them go; riders gone and left
    are summed per stop now (today's from the window's first departure, yesterday's from the day's last, kept at each
    departure that has any), so a bound that moves costs the departures it passed;
  - per trip, its stops in the window and the first of them (by stop) with the most riders aboard: today's take in a
    stop (the first by stop wins a tie, as a pass over the stops in order does), yesterday's let it go and look again
    only when the one that leaves was the fullest;
  - trains in service now from kept per-trip spans and full flags; the crowding by period and the busiest stretch kept
    per account (they read no time).
  - A whole read when the key changes (an edit's lines, new riders, the local day), when the clock goes back, or while
    an opening (`open`, `stop_open`) is within a day of the window (a new line's first two days).
- Two definitions moved to rounding, both in the whole read too: riders gone and left are summed per stop before they
  are taken off (was one running sum), and a trip's stop times are `RunAccount::leaves` (the stop order's own) where the
  trains' pass used `(deps + o).rem_euclid(DAY)`; the two can differ in the last bit, so at a departure's exact time a
  train can count on the other side of it.
- workers/clockMoney.ts `lineFares`: each line's fares in the past 24 hours and its local day kept while the hour, the
  local day and what `fareHour` reads for the line stand (its income array, its kind-of-day function, the shapes
  `peakC`, the lag `moneyLag`); summed with the same weights in the same order as before, so to the bit.
  `?patch=check` compares the kept with a whole read ("T-4281 check: ...").
- Tests (boarding/tests.rs): `old_window`, the read before this change as an oracle; `past_day_moved_along_equals_a_whole_read`
  (a line that leaves riders behind and turns riders away, every departure at every stop and a bit either side, every
  train going out of service, the periods' starts and midnight, Friday into Saturday's account, the clock going back, the
  service changing ahead of its account and the account worked out again) and
  `past_day_moved_along_through_local_midnight_and_an_opening` (a line on London's time through its midnight; T-848's
  opening line with full trains at every stop, days 2-5). Kept against whole to the bit at every time; against the oracle
  midway between changes, to 1e-9. Planted faults both caught: the fullest stop leaving yesterday's part not looked for
