# T-1082: a lighter build for countryside zones (measured 2026-10-23)

## In plain words

Anita asked whether countryside rows, where people are unlikely to build much, could be built
more cheaply. A countryside row takes about 2.4 minutes of one core today (63 built US zones: build
55 s, routes 80 s, the rest 11 s), plus about half a minute in the gate. I profiled four kinds of
countryside row (a farm and coast zone, a ring round Atlanta, the sparse west of Kansas, Florida's
reserves), then built each one again in scratch with one change at a time and ran a test day of
the game on both packs.

Most of the saving turned out not to need any loss of accuracy. Four changes give the same packs
as today (byte for byte, or the same to 1e-15), so they cost the game nothing:

1. **Parks on big reserves** (T-1042): buffer each piece of a reserve made of many pieces on its own,
   then join them. Hernando's parks went from 424 s to 17 s, St. Lucie's from 142 to 11, Alachua's
   from 159 to 57 (25 with the few huge reserves simplified by 25 m first, which moves one reserve's
   draw by 0.07%). Buffered whole, one marsh reserve of 963 islands used over 16 GB of memory.
2. **The gravity check by table**: `read_pack.check_gravity` works out the decay for every pair of
   zones, 16 times a pair where there are 4 km blocks, and it runs three times a row (blocks, merge,
   gate) and twice more at the join. Read from a table of the decay by grid offset, the sums come
   out bit for bit the same and 14 times faster: 87 s to 6 s a call in west Kansas, 4-10 s to under
   1 s in ordinary rows.
3. **The build's first gravity solve stopped at once**, since the 4 km blocks solve it again straight
   after: the same pack; 75 s saved a row in west Kansas (17,000 zones), 3-7 s elsewhere.
4. **The GHSL population read only inside the outline** (plus 1 km), not over its whole box: the
   same cells; 7 s on an ordinary zone, 30 s on a ring round a metro whose box takes in the city.

Small extras in the same spirit (row 5): caching Wikidata's airport answers (a cold query waits up to 8 s
an airport), building a row's county outline once instead of four times (about 4 s), and dropping
the unread `nocar` step (2-6 s, already T-878).

Two changes that do move the game a little:

- **Coarser far drive tables** (row 6 below; car detours beyond 12 km looked up by 16 km squares instead of
   8 km): 11 s a row on an ordinary zone, about 2-2.5 minutes on a ring or a sparse western zone.
   On a test day commuter rail moved by 0.2-0.75% and the busier stations by under 1% on average,
   one rural stop by 7% (115 riders to 123).
- **No walking-around-water search inside 4 km blocks** (row 8): 7-40 s a row; walking moved by under
   0.1%, rail not at all.

Not worth it: searching near car routes only to 30 km (6-30 s a row, no change on the test lines,
but it understates the car's detour round a bay, exactly where a rail bridge should win); one
population point a pixel instead of four (3 s, and stations moved up to 4%); checking only some
countryside rows in the gate's browser test (about 4 s a row, and that test caught a real fault in
the pilot).

**Recommendation**: do 1-4 (T-1083); they need no decision about accuracy and help metros too. They
save about half a minute on an ordinary countryside row (of about 2.9 minutes with the gate), about
5 minutes on a sparse western row like west Kansas (of about 11.5), and part of that falls in the
gate and the join, which run one row at a time. Adding rows 6 and 8 (T-1084) saves another 20 s
to 3 minutes a row for the small changes above; that one is Anita's call. Little else is worth a
separate countryside mode: once 1-4 are in, what remains in a countryside row is mostly the drive
and walk tables, and rows 6 and 8 are already the countryside versions of those.

## 2. Experiments

Each change was built in a scratch tree (world_run's `TW_RUN_ROOT` redirect, the sim copy of HEAD,
one thread), from the same pre-routes snapshot for the routes changes, then compared with the
baseline built the same way: `packdiff.py` (every array), and `game.py`, a test day on calib's
kernel (commutes and everyday purposes, game fares, 2 crowding rounds) over a fixed test network:
line A through the zone's six biggest towns with a stop every ~5 km between them (the rural stops),
line B a 7-stop town line through the biggest town. Seconds are cProfile-free where stated, else
under one-thread scratch runs (comparable between variants of a row).

The routes changes 6 and 7 leave a zone's trips, its mean commute and its far-zoom bubble exactly as
they were (those come from the gravity, which they do not touch), and 8 moves them by under 0.01%
(the merge joins a few more cells); only rail, walking and a station's riders can move. Test-day baselines: Lowndes 383,641 commutes a day, 1.02% by rail on the two test
lines; Bibb 701,116, 0.54%; west Kansas 928,704, 0.98%.

Seconds saved a row (one thread) on Lowndes (low), Bibb (bio), west Kansas (sed) and Hernando or
Alachua (heo, aac), and what moved:

Notes on the rows:
- **1**: the slow part was GEOS buffering a multipolygon of hundreds of marsh islands 2 km in one
  call (Crystal River Preserve, 963 pieces): its offset curves are all noded together. A buffer
  distributes over a union, so piece by piece then a union gives the same set; it took 7.7 s for
  Crystal River where the whole buffer passed 16 GB of memory and several minutes before I stopped
  it (the pilot's 424 s). What is left in Alachua is single big reserves (Big Bend WMA, 49,443
  vertices, 26 s), which 1b's light simplification cuts to 10 s.
- **2**: `check_gravity` runs in coarse_zones, in merge_cells (`read_pack.check`), in the gate's
  figures, and again in the join's merge and re-gate. It evaluates exp and pow for every zone pair
  and, with 4 km blocks, for 16 sub-square pairs each. The decay depends only on the integer square
  offset, so four tables (small or block at each end, sub-squares folded in) and one gather a pair
  give the same sums: sed 87 s to 6.2 s, bio 10.6 to 0.8, low 4.5 to 0.3. It grows with the square
  of the zone count, so the West's biggest zones gain most.
- **3**: with `--coarse` (every new row since T-852) coarse_zones re-solves the gravity on the mixed
  grid straight after the build; the build's own 2 km solve is used only for its zone ids (the
  everyday step and the enrichments read `zone`, not the factors). On sed it ran 17,014 zones,
  about 81 s.
- **6, 7**: the far table is a full Dijkstra over the road graph from each supercell's heaviest
  zone; its cost is supercells x graph size, so it is largest for big sparse zones and for rings round
  a metro, whose road graph takes in the metro. 6b barely helps: wherever a 2 km zone sits among
  blocks it keeps an 8 km supercell. Item 7's capped pairs are detours round water, which is where a
  rail bridge would beat the car, so the test lines (which cross no bay) do not show what it costs.
- **8**: `pack_walk` searches only where its window holds a barrier (water), so its cost depends on
  water; blocks are where walking between zones barely happens.

## 3. Time saved per wave and for the world

A row's own seconds, then what that means at the scale of a wave. "Ordinary" is a row like
Lowndes (near the US median, 106 s a row); "ring" like Bibb; "sparse" like west Kansas, which the
West's zones (Montana, Wyoming, Nevada, New Mexico's north) resemble or exceed, since the gravity
solve, the check and the far table all grow with the zone count and the area.

| | Ordinary | Ring | Sparse |
|---|---|---|---|
| A row today (build steps, one core) | about 145 s | about 410 s | about 630 s |
| Exact fixes 1-5 in the build steps | about 25 s, plus the parks tail (fleet mean 12 s) | about 60 s | about 4 min |
| ... and in the gate and the join (the check's later calls) | 4 s, 8 s | 10 s, 20 s | 80 s, 160 s |
| Rows 6 and 8 on top (build steps) | about 22 s | about 2 min | about 3 min |

- **The US**: 72 countryside zones are still to build after wave 2 (whose builds started before
  any change): the West's 37 and wave 4's 35, plus reruns. With 1-5 the West alone saves roughly 2
  hours of build worker time if half its zones are sparse like west Kansas, about 12 minutes of wall
  at 10 builds. More usefully it shortens the slowest rows (a sparse row is 10-11 minutes now, the
  tail of a run) and the serial parts: each sparse row's gate figures lose about 80 s, which is wall
  time because a region's gate is one process (20 sparse rows: about 25 minutes), and the join's
  merge and re-gate lose twice that. Rows 6 and 8 would add about another hour of worker time for
  the West.
- **Per 100 countryside rows anywhere** (one core): 1-5 save about 1 hour if ordinary, about 13
  hours if sparse (4 of them in the serial gate and join); rows 6 and 8 add about 35 minutes
  ordinary, 5 hours sparse. The world's remaining countryside (Africa, South America, Russia,
  Australia, Canada's north) will have many sparse zones of the west Kansas kind or larger.
- **Hernando-type rows** (reserves mapped as hundreds of pieces; likely among the West's national
  forests and wildlife areas) lose minutes each with fix 1, and stop risking a build process of
  16 GB beside 26 GB extract reads on this PC.

## 4. Recommendation

Ranked by time saved against loss:

1. **Do now, they cost nothing (T-1083)**: the parks buffer by piece (1, with 1b's simplification
   of the few huge reserves: a 0.07% move in one reserve's draw is far inside what anyone could see),
   `check_gravity` by table (2), the first gravity solve stopped when the blocks follow (3), the GHSL
   read inside the outline (4). Each is a few lines in one file (`enrich_parks.py`, `read_pack.py`,
   `tier0.py`/`build_city.py`, `tier0.py`), checked by building a row both ways as here. Item 5's
   pieces are small and general: the Wikidata cache and the outline built once are worth folding in;
   `nocar` is T-878. These help metros too, not only countryside, and need no decision about accuracy.
2. **Anita's call (T-1084)**: coarser far drive tables (6) give the most of the lossy ones,
   2-2.5 minutes on a ring or sparse row, for a commuter rail change of half a percent and a few
   riders at a rural stop. The walk skip in 4 km blocks (8) is small and almost free (walking moves
   by a tenth of a percent). Worth doing together before the West if Anita accepts that kind of
   change; the game would not look different.
3. **Not worth it**: the 30 km near limit (7): 6-30 s a row, no change on the test lines, but it
   is exactly the car's detour round a bay or lake that it understates, where a rail bridge is the
   point of building one. One GHSL point a pixel (9): 3 s for visible shifts of cells and riders.
   The gate's browser check sampled (10): about 4 s a row, and it is the check that caught a real
   fault in the pilot. A separate "countryside mode" in general: once 1-4 are in, what is left in a
   countryside row is mostly the drive and walk tables, and 6 and 8 are already the countryside
   versions of those.

Also seen, outside this task: `pack_walk` and the far Dijkstras run one thread each (T-970 has the
walk on threads); the two gravity solves (Rust) on a sparse row's 7,500-17,000 zones are 30-80 s
each.
