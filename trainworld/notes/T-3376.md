## 1. Jurong Island's workforce

- **About 26,000.** Singapore government's SG101 case study: "more than 100 companies employing some 26,000 people"
  (undated, https://www.sg101.gov.sg/economy/case-studies/jurong2/).
- EDB, 27 October 2025: "The E&C sector provides employment for more than 27,000" (the whole energy and chemicals
  sector, not the island; https://www.edb.gov.sg/en/news-and-insights/industry-news/jurong-island-marks-twenty-five-years-of-low-carbon-innovation).
  JTC's own page gives only "over 100 companies" (https://www.jtc.gov.sg/jurongisland). A LinkedIn post says 18,000
  workers; not used.
- Island: about 32 km2 in the pack's outline (Wikipedia: "over 3,500 hectares" after 2009). So about 800 jobs a km2.

The game's 128,007 is the whole **Western Islands** subzone (45.5 km2: Jurong Island plus Pulau Bukom's Shell
refinery, Busing, Seraya, Sebarok). Jurong Island alone holds about 101,800 (its 231 res-9 cells, `jurong_cells.py`):
3.9x the published figure.

## 2. Why the game puts 128,000 there

Singapore's jobs are tier 0's proxy (`tier0.job_shares`): 3.53M jobs, 30% by GHSL non-residential volume x
height^2, 50% by OSM job places, 20% by population. Western Islands by part (`decompose.py`):

| Part | Jobs | What carries it |
|---|---|---|
| OSM job places | 113,906 | 2,798 land places (one per 5,000 m2 of OSM industrial land), 12 points |
| GHSL non-residential volume | 13,676 | 15.0 Mm3 at a mean height of 8 m |
| Population | 425 | |

So the T-3287 guess ("tanks read as building volume") is wrong: GHSL gives 11%. The cause is **OSM industrial land**.
Mappers drew Jurong as 78 separate `landuse=industrial` polygons, one per plant (ExxonMobil Chemical, Singapore
Refining Company, Shell Seraya, Vopak, Oiltanking, the power stations), 15.2 km2 after the 1 km2 cap; only five reach
the cap. In sin one place is worth 0.5 x 3.53M / 43,487 = **40.5 jobs**, so mapped industrial land carries about
**8,100 jobs a km2**, ten times the island's real density.

This is the Lagos refinery again (T-459, notes/archive/t0001-0499/T-460.md). The 1 km2 cap per area fixed one 23 km2
polygon, and only works when mappers draw one big polygon: Yeosu's 31.6 km2 complex is one relation, capped at 200
places, while Jurong's per-plant polygons pass it untouched. The cap was measured on London and Toronto only, where
few areas reach it (the bar was "no worse").

Jobs per place (`measure.py`, as built) vary with how much OSM a city has against its jobs: Singapore 40.5,
Tokyo 25.2, London 20.8, Houston 14.2, the English metros 10-18.

## 3. The measurement: packs with real job counts

Where the game already holds a real small-area count, the tier 0 proxy can be rebuilt from the pack's own signals and
OSM rows and compared: Houston (LODES 2023 by block), Tokyo, Osaka, Nagoya (2021 economic census, 500 m mesh), the
nine English BRES packs (London, Liverpool, Manchester, Birmingham, Leeds, Sheffield, Newcastle, Nottingham,
Portsmouth: BRES 2024 by LSOA), and 12 Mexican packs (DENUE establishments; its top band 251+ counts 900, so big
plants are low). Measure: jobs misplaced at H3 res 7 (5.2 km2), half the L1 distance between shares, as T-460's.
Each land row's true area: rows of an area at the cap count the 0.003 degree grid square each, others their weight.
Caveat: the Japan and London caches predate T-460 (each area at one point, uncapped), so the land share per cell is
lumpy there and the effect of the rule is understated.

**Real jobs a km2 of mapped land** (land-dominated res-8 hexes, all jobs in them over the land): Houston 430-2,800,
Japan 1,400-5,400, England 350-10,000 (most estates 3,000-8,000), Mexico 1,300-15,000. Heavy industry sits at the
bottom: Houston's ship channel 440, Kashima 1,030, Tokai 1,190, Yeosu about 1,200 a km2 of land, Jurong 1,870.

**The proxy today on those hexes**: 2.4-3.4x the real jobs in Houston and Japan, 1.5-2.8x in Mexico, 0.8-1.5x in
England. Both building parts overshoot on heavy industry: on the Houston ship channel the proxy gives 202,000 against
LODES's 52,000, of it 126,000 from GHSL volume and 74,000 from OSM land. Dropping the land alone still leaves 131,000.

- Land use split (only England's and Georgia's `areas.fgb` caches survive): 72-77% of the English land weight is
  `landuse=industrial`, the rest commercial and retail; Atlanta's only 38% (55% commercial). With the factor on
  industrial land only, England's mean goes 0.196 to 0.191 (London 0.253 to 0.246, Nottingham 0.215 to 0.192,
  Birmingham 0.208 to 0.191; small losses in Leeds +0.005, Newcastle +0.004, Portsmouth +0.006), and Atlanta (LODES)
  0.296 to 0.283, better than the type-blind 0.285. Industrial-only, the factor is flat from 0.25 to 0.5 in England
  (0.1911, 0.1903, 0.1904) and best at 0.35 in Atlanta (0.281; 0.25 gives 0.283). With commercial land in the factor,
  dense town centres lose (Marina Centre in Singapore, -14,000). The other US, Japan and Mexico caches have no land use
  column, so their figures above are for all mapped land, and 0.25 is their type-blind best region (0.15-0.25).
- England's land-dominated hexes go from 1.20x their BRES jobs to 0.85x at the median under the industrial-only rule
  (Leeds 0.66, Newcastle 0.55, Manchester 0.74): dense estates end up a little under.
- Roof share (GHSL non-residential surface over the land) does separate tank farms from estates in Singapore (Western
  Islands 0.09, Pioneer 0.73), but not across countries: Trafford Park 0.23, the Japanese coasts 0.17-0.27, the
  Houston ship channel 0.14, with real densities from 440 to 6,200. Dropped.
- OSM's `industrial=*` value: 90% of England's 1,327 km2 of industrial land has none (port 2.4%, oil 1.0%, refinery
  0.9%). A heavy/general split by tag can't work yet. Dropped.
- Sending the jobs taken off to where people live (the caps' rule) is worse everywhere but in Japan and some Mexican
  zones. Dropped.
- The factor's value: type-blind, the English packs do best at 0.5 and the others at 0.15-0.25 (0.15: 0.204);
  industrial-only, England and Atlanta are flat from 0.25 to 0.5. Without the 1 km2 cap the same factor is worse
  (land x0.25: 0.222 against 0.211 over all 25 packs).

## 4. The proposed rule

**On OSM `landuse=industrial`, the jobs proxy counts the land and the GHSL non-residential volume on it at a quarter
of today's weight. Commercial and retail land, OSM points and population are unchanged; the 1 km2 cap stays.**

- Deciding number: `INDUSTRIAL_JOBS = 0.25`, from section 3: industrial-only in England (0.196 to 0.191) and Atlanta
  (0.296 to 0.283), flat from 0.25 to 0.5 there; type-blind elsewhere, since those caches have no land use (0.255 to
  0.205 over Houston, Japan and Mexico, best between 0.15 and 0.25). 0.25 is where the two meet.
- In code: `canada.osm_places` keeps each land row's land use in the cache (a `use` column); `tier0.signals` adds a
  per-cell industrial land share (rows' true area over the cell); `job_shares` takes the factor on the industrial
  land weight and, by that share, on the cell's NRES weight. Both parts are then normalised as today, so the jobs
  go to the pack's other buildings and places. A cache without the column (packs not rebuilt) keeps today's weights.
- No new factor by country or city kind. It replaces nothing: the cap per area stays, since the factor alone without
  it was worse.

What it does not fix:
- **Zones where industry is most of the buildings** (sne: Yeosu, Gwangyang's steelworks): 61% of sne's NRES weight
  sits on mapped land (Singapore 42%, Houston 42%, Rotterdam 33%), so the factor shrinks most of the zone's buildings
  together and normalising gives much of it back. Yeosu stays at 1.7x. Fixing that needs an absolute density for
  industrial land, a second rule; not proposed.
- **Refinery belts stay about 2x** (Houston's ship channel 1.9x, Yeosu 1.7x, Coatzacoalcos 1.8x) and **heavy industry
  on the Japanese coasts 3.5-6.5x** (understated by the old caches, but still the worst case).
- **Dense estates lose**: Trafford Park goes from 1.2x to 0.7x, and England's industrial hexes from 1.20x to 0.85x at
  the median (Leeds 0.66, Newcastle 0.55), though every English pack's fit overall is as good or better but three
  small losses (Leeds, Newcastle, Portsmouth, all under 0.01).

## 6. Side effects (gate, players)

`gate_scan.py` over 300 random shipped proxy packs, type-blind (all mapped land takes the factor, so an upper bound):
- jobs that change cell: median 11.6% of a pack's jobs, 90th percentile 19%, most 23% (Singapore 16.5%). They go to
  town centres: in sin Rochor +32,800, Outram +23,400, Kallang +20,800, Orchard +19,700; Western Islands -77,500,
  Tuas View Extension -31,400. A player sees smaller job bubbles on ports and refineries and bigger ones downtown.
- top zone's jobs share (the gate fails over 0.2): 14 packs over 0.2 before, 13 after; 3 newly over (pup 0.193 to
  0.207, jlp 0.187 to 0.214, mrp 0.182 to 0.217) and 4 newly under (blr, ras, dki, sdk); over 0.1 (a warning), 9 newly
  over and 21 newly under. Median change +0.001, largest +0.043 (mnc 0.125 to 0.168, bbv 0.208 to 0.245). So about
  1% of rebuilt rows would newly park on it (40 or so in a world rebuild), the one-town zones the playbook already
  relaxes (docs/adding-a-country.md 6), and about as many stop parking (`gate_cross.py`).
- top cell against size (fails over 600): 5 packs over before and after; the cell cap is unchanged.
- Packs on real counts (US, England's metros, Japan, Mexico, France, Germany, sao, sha, tor) keep their totals by
  small area; only the spread inside each area moves a little.

## 7. What a rebuild costs

- **Code**: `canada.osm_places` (the `use` column), `tier0.signals` (industrial share per cell), `tier0.job_shares`
  and `jobs_from` (the factor), `INDUSTRIAL_JOBS` with its test, docs/demand-worldwide.md's tier 0 "Jobs" entry
  and the data register. About 2-3 h of agent time. No wiki page states the job proxy.
- **Machine**: none of its own if it rides along with the world rebuild (T-3245) **in its full re-read form** (a
  light pass that reads no extract cannot carry it, since the land use comes from the extract): a row's rebuild deletes its
  `osm_jobs_<id>.csv.gz` and `tier0_signals.npz` (`world_run.ROW_CACHES`) and reads the extract again, so the
  new column costs nothing, and the jobs step is seconds a row. Measured afterwards with this kit's scripts, about
  10 min.
- **Alone, without the rebuild**: the cached rows have no land use, so only the type-blind version could run, which
  hurts town centres mapped as commercial land. Every proxy pack's jobs and all that follows them (commuters,
  gravity, everyday trips, merge, market, every country's join) would be redone from caches: about 1 core-minute a
  row for the build steps (notes/T-3244.md), so about 65-70 core-hours for 4,000 rows, plus the joins (par-fr alone
  66 min). Not recommended.
