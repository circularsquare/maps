# T-4052: riders waiting at bus stops (agent buswait, 2026-11-24)

Status: a bus stop's waiting cap landed in the tree (boarding account only); the issues tab's number for an overloaded
bus line is still large. Follow-up options at the end are the orchestrator's call.

## What the player sees

Bus lines have no drawn platform crowd and no "Waiting now" rows (`Boarding::hour` skips them, T-1464). The only
waiting figure is the issues list's "<line> crowding" row (person icon) and the line panel's "Riders left at the
stop": `tally`'s left behind over the past 24 hours, summed per departure as `min(arrived since the last bus,
queue after it)` (`a.min(q(j))`), where "arrived" is everyone who came, those turned away included.

## Why bus numbers ran bigger than stations'

1. **The stop held a platform's 5,000.** As each vehicle leaves, a stop side holds what it and the next 30 minutes'
   vehicles take (`room x (1 + floor(1800 / gap))`), at most `WAITING_CAP` = 5,000 (a 300 m platform side). That
   cap binds a long train at about two trainloads; an 80-rider bus never reaches it (63 busloads), so a bus stop held
   up to 15 busloads at a 2-minute headway, 41 at the 43 s of the Hong Kong save's Bus 1. Fixed: a bus stop holds
   what a bus's length of sidewalk holds by the same area rule (12 m x 5 m / 0.3 m^2 = 200), a BRT station its 40 m
   platform's (667); stations and ports keep 5,000 (`LineSvc::waiting_cap`).
2. **The count takes in riders turned away.** When a stop is full, `a.min(q(j))` counts everyone who came since the
   last bus up to the queue left behind, so the queue's older riders are counted again at every bus and the turned
   away count as left behind. Per full stop that is up to (queue after / room) per rider carried: about 1.08 at a
   10-car train's platform (5,000 cap), 1.5 at a bus stop now, up to 40 before. Not changed (see below).
3. **The rest is a real day's total.** An overloaded bus line in a dense city really turns away tens of thousands
   a day; the row shows that 24-hour total as a bare person count.

## Measured

- Rust test `a_crowded_bus_stop_holds_a_bus_stops_crowd` (a bus every 2 min, three times its room coming all
  morning, riders boarding at the first stop): most waiting as a bus leaves 1,200 -> 120; left behind 28,767 ->
  14,403 a day against 12,263 -> 11,183 carried (account boardings -8.8%: the morning's backlog no longer boards
  after the peak).
- Game, new NYC sandbox game, two bus lines on default buses (14th St, 9 stops, 4 buses; a Broadway-ish line, 12
  stops, 5 buses), one game day: Bus 1 6,219 left / 24,898 carried both before and after (its stops never held 200);
  Bus 2 28,433 -> 23,218 left (-18%), 30,660 -> 30,521 carried (-0.5%). Arrivals at the stops identical in both runs:
  demand, trips and fares do not move. Rail lines' accounts are untouched (their cap is still 5,000).

## Lower bounds and follow-ups

- A corrected count (riders who joined the queue and missed the first bus: `min(arrived - turned away, queue after)`)
  would take the game's lines to about 4.3k (Bus 1) and 14.5k (Bus 2) a day (per period, `left_h - gone`). It changes
  every station's figure a little (about -8% on a full 10-car platform). Exact needs the turned-away riders kept per
  departure apart from the 90-minute give-ups (`Queues` keeps them summed in `gone`): a memory change. Subtracting
  `gone` instead under-counts where riders give up (`riders_left_behind_too_long_go_another_way`).
- Presentation: the issues row shows a 24-hour total beside a person icon, which reads as people waiting now. The line
  panel already shows it as left/all. Options: the same left/all on the row, a share, or the most waiting at one stop
  (a crowd, 200 at most at a bus stop).
- Demand's queue (`kernel::capacity_fit`) has no stop cap, for stations (5,000) or bus stops (200): adding one is a
  demand rule change.
