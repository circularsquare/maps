## Method

- **Field** as the prototype (sheet3.mjs): each station's log scale (its room at the dial's 0.5, `gap` 4, `core` 1.8,
  2.1 on a country map) splatted with a Gaussian, blended to a floor, bounded by sweeps (doubling at most per 40
  drawn pitches, at most 1.6 times across a finest cell; the same sweeps, kept to the dirty nodes). Land masses of at
  most 300 km2 with a station: one size all over (the 0.75 point of what they ask, times 2).
- **Ground**: a raster of row land runs (50 m on a city map; on a country map max(200 m, extent/6000, half a finest
  cell)), from the zones' water masks where they cover and the ground's sea (`groundSea`, waterReal.ts) elsewhere, and
  land masses by union-find. On a country map a mask's water run counts only if the ground's sea agrees at its middle
  (lakes and rivers under the cell size are land there).
- **Lattice**: a quadtree with 2^7 roots on the finest grid (512 finest cells along a city map's longer side, 1024 on a
  country map), split where the field changes by more than 1.15 times across a cell (1.4 on a country map) unless the
  cell is drawn under 12 pitches, at most 16 finest cells a side, coasts near lines at most 1 (4 on a country map);
  2:1 balanced; hanging nodes tied to their edge's ends. Cells' weights are their finest cells' means (summed-area
  tables). Two triangles a cell.
- **Energy** per triangle (as round 3): uneven stretch (weight a by kind: land near lines 4, other land 2, water 0.1,
  small islands 80) and size and turn toward the planned size at the current turn (weight s: near lines 1 times the
  cell's stations over the median cell's, 0.3 to 6; other land and water 0.1). A cell near folding (uneven over even
  past 0.4, or under 0.3 of its size) gets a hold: weight up to 10 on both.
- **Solver**: the quadtree cut at each depth is a level; prolongation by the cells' corners; coarser matrices by
  Galerkin (P^T A P, 2x2 blocks); block Gauss-Seidel V-cycles as the preconditioner of conjugate gradients; the
  coarsest level by dense Cholesky.
- **Schedule** (`e-2:10:0.3,raise,e-2:6:0.3,e0:5:0.3,s12`): 10 rounds two levels coarse with each hold moving 30% of
  the way to what the stretch asks (a running mean: the prototype's 38 rounds of "the larger of now and 0.9 of the
  last" never settle, below), the cores raised where they came out small (as the prototype), 6 more coarse rounds, 5 on
  the finest, then up to 12 rounds with the holds frozen, a hold only tightening where a cell came nearer folding,
  until the stations' p90 move is under 0.5 pitch. Each round's turn is taken out (unturn), as the prototype.

## Speed

In the game (`?sheet=1`, this tree's Vite, the machine loaded by other agents' runs):

| | first open | again |
|---|---|---|
| New York: masks / ground / sheet | 27 / 266 / 1,322 ms | 0 / 0 / 1,202 ms |
| Japan (jpn_real.save, 9,075 complexes) | 259 / 2,759 / 3,020 ms | 0 / 1 / 1,943 ms |

Of New York's sheet: field 60-80, raster 70-80, quadtree 65-85, matrix patterns 120-145, rounds 820-900 ms. Japan's
rounds are 1.2-1.9 s: about 30 ms a round on the finest level (assemble 10, Galerkin 10, the solve 10, with one CG
iteration), and the cascade saves less there because the quadtree is mostly coarse cells already (level -2 has 6,800
free vertices of 11,900). The ground's sea (2.8 s for Japan) is the water's own step and is kept between graphs.

What was tried for speed: solver tolerance 1e-4 to 1e-3 in the frozen rounds (taken: the look unchanged, New York's
frozen rounds 560 to 230 ms in Node); Galerkin only every 3rd tightening round (5 folds in Japan, dropped); Anderson
mixing of the frozen rounds (diverges, folds); Japan on a 512 finest lattice (vertices halved, rounds only 37% faster,
look not checked). The time is spread over ten steps with no hot spot; the block sweeps run at about 9 ns a 2x2 block,
near what JavaScript does.

Options for under 1 s, for the orchestrator and Anita:
1. The rounds (assemble, Galerkin, V-cycle) in Rust/WASM: an estimated 1.5-2x on those, which alone does not reach 1 s
   for Japan; with SIMD and on an unloaded machine it might. Needs a WASM interface change, which the brief ruled out
   without asking.
2. A coarser country lattice (512) and fewer rounds, retuned so Japan keeps its measures: perhaps 2x, at some cost in
   the cores' detail (the cramped Tokyo core is what the fine lattice was for).
3. Solve once per network and keep it (the sheet changes little with an edit; a rebuild could start from the last
   sheet and run only frozen rounds, a few hundred ms), so only the first open is slow.
The sheet currently runs before the layout in the worker, so with the flag on the diagram waits for it.

## Decisions

- Off-by-default switch: the URL flag `?sheet=1` (the water's button is separate; the sheet is not drawn yet).
- Settled schedule: running-mean holds coarse to fine, then frozen holds that only tighten, to a p90 move of 0.5 pitch,
  in place of the prototype's 38 rounds (which never settle). Without the frozen tightening rounds cells fold (49);
  holds that forget at once fold (551); Anderson mixing with tightening diverges (109 folds).
- Country map settings retuned on the settled solver so Kyushu keeps round 2's size: `coreCountry` 2.1, `floorCountry`
  1.2, `flatCountry` 1.4, `coastCountry` 4, `refN` 256 (the field's radius and the near-lines reach at least 3/4 of a
  256 lattice's cell, as the approved Japan's lattice; no effect on New York).
- Japan's test network: `data/work/realnet/jpn_real.save` (9,106 complexes from the saves, 9,075 in the game).
- Tolerance: within the prototype's own drift (median under 9, p90 under 20 pitches with similarity taken out).
- Options kept in `SheetOptions` for comparing (uniform lattice, Anderson `aa`, `galEvery`, `drift`, `nearMax`,
  schedule stages `f`, `h`, `u`), all off by default.
