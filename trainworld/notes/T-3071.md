# T-3071: handling low memory better (exploration, 2026-11-10, agent lowmem)

Anita, 2026-11-10: "lets start another explration for handling low-memory situations better", after a player with an
8 GB laptop was told "This save needs about 3.2 GB" and asked "Maybe implement lazy loading save or something to save
memory?". Builds on notes/T-361.md, T-1205.md, T-2485.md, T-2794.md, T-2867.md. Read-only: no app or sim code changed.

## Short version

- **The save itself is nothing** (75 KB). The memory is the demand model each solving worker builds from the packs of
  every zone the network reaches, plus the intercity market. "Lazy loading" is mostly there already: zones no
  station reaches never open in a solving worker (bare zones go to the aux worker and close after 60 s), and zones not
  looked at are held coarse.
- **Every solving worker holds the same thing.** On the real save one worker holding all five periods has 378 MB of
  WASM; each of three workers holding one or two periods also has 378. A period costs about 15 MB of heap (NYC) to
  25 MB (all-China); the other 85-95% of a worker is copied in every worker. So N workers pay N copies.
- **The player's 3.2 GB** is the device warning, which shows only when Chrome reports 4 GB or less. Such a device
  already gets 1 solving worker and the phone's lean ways, so for them the copies are not the problem: at 1 worker the
  solving worker is 21% (NYC) to 36% (all-China) of the game. The estimate's fixed part (2.2 GB before any zone) is
  most of their 3.2; the game measures 1.13 GB at a new game and 2.1 GB settled on the real save.
- **Where a reported 8 GB machine pays most**: 3 solving workers on a 12-thread laptop. The real save then settles at
  3.3 GB and peaks at 3.9 GB, against 2.1 and 2.4 at 1 worker. `memBudget` takes a reported 8 as 8 GB free for the
  game, so nothing trims it on an 8 GB laptop (Chrome reports 8 for 8 GB and for 64 GB alike).

## What scales with what

- **Workers**: everything in a solving worker except its periods. Each worker opens every reached zone, every market
  pack, composes the market (with the 34 MB corridor figures, T-1204) and sets up each network. NYC: about 450 MB a
  worker; all-China: about 1.4 GB.
- **Periods**: 15-25 MB of heap each, split among the workers. WASM grows in 32 MB steps and keeps its high water,
  so the WASM figure barely moves with the periods held.
- **Zones reached**: a zone natively is 5-25 MB fine (New York 80, Shanghai 25; T-1206), 25-35% less coarse. On NYC
  `?lod=coarse` freed 23 MB of heap and no WASM; all-China already holds 80 of its 171 zones coarse.
- **The market**: on NYC 185 MB of a worker's WASM and 151 MB of the page, about 0.4 GB of 2.1 at 1 worker. Its
  corridor table on the page grows with the square of the world's places (T-2136).
- **Stations and lines**: the network's set-up (subzones, access, sample pairs) grows with stops times the samples
  they reach; on NYC it is most of what is not zones or market (T-361: about 180 MB at the first network).
- **Fixed**: about 1.1 GB for a new game (GPU 0.3, the page and workers' isolates, the clock worker).

## Elsewhere

- **Budget and fall back to lower detail.** CesiumJS keeps loaded tiles under `cacheBytes` (512 MB by default), drops
  the least recently used, unloads tiles out of view, and when a view needs more than the budget uses lower levels
  of detail ([Cesium3DTileset](https://cesium.com/learn/cesiumjs/ref-doc/Cesium3DTileset.html)). The game's coarse
  zones and its closing of spare zones are the same idea; D carries it to solved networks.
- **Warn early, in proportion to the real limit.** Figma shows a memory alert at 60% of the browser's limit
  ([Figma help](https://help.figma.com/hc/articles/360040528173)) and moved big buffers out of the WASM heap to avoid
  fragmentation and fit 32-bit browsers
  ([Evan Wallace](https://madebyevan.com/figma/building-a-professional-design-tool-on-the-web/)).
- **Device memory is coarse.** Chrome reports a power of two, at most 8, so 8 covers 8 GB laptops and 64 GB desktops
  ([Chrome](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/device-memory),
  [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/Navigator/deviceMemory)); sources disagree whether it rounds down
  or to the nearest, so an "8 GB" laptop with some memory reserved can report 4.
- **Shared memory needs isolation.** WASM threads share one `WebAssembly.Memory` over a SharedArrayBuffer
  ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/webassembly-threads)), which needs COOP/COEP headers; GitHub Pages cannot set
  them ([discussion](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/13309)); the usual workaround is
  [coi-serviceworker](https://github.com/gzuidhof/coi-serviceworker), which adds them from a service worker after one
  reload. Chrome 137 added a one-header route,
  [Document-Isolation-Policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/document-isolation-policy), Chromium only, still a
  header.

## Recommendation

1. **T-3072, pool by memory (option A) with an honest estimate.** Biggest saving for the cost on the machines that
   pay most (reported 8 GB, 2-3 workers), no change to any figure, no WASM change. Proposal: refit the estimate (a
   worker about 0.1 GB plus its WASM; WASM about 0.2 GB with the market plus a per-trip rate fitted on NYC and
   all-China; the page about 1.6 GB plus about 0.005 GB a zone), choose the pool at load so the total stays under
   5 GB where Chrome reports 8 ("Use more cores" and `?demandWorkers` still override), and skip the focus pool when
   capped. Done when: the estimate is within 15% of the measured peak in the four runs above; all-China on a
   reported 8 loads with 1 worker and peaks under 4.6 GB (dev) instead of 6.1; the real save keeps its 3 workers on a
   reported 8 and its 1-worker estimate is at most 2.5 GB.
2. **T-3073, lighter load at the warning (option B).** For the player's case (1 worker), the one cheap cut left: the
   device warning offers "Load without intercity trips" beside "Load anyway". Done when: the real save loaded that way
   at dm4 settles under 1.8 GB (dev) and the warning's text and buttons have been through Anita's review.

After those: D (park solved clusters) for big multi-city saves, once a count shows how many clusters such saves have;
E and F as already filed. G only if the game moves host or adopts the shim for another reason.
