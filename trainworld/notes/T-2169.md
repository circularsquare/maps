## From T-2491 (diagshape, 2026-11-05)

- **The drawing's scale now varies more by place.** Hops between two local stops have 0.4 of their target (at least 3
  pitches), so runs of plain stops (south Brooklyn, most Japanese branch lines) are drawn about twice as tight as
  interchange-rich cores. Any warp of water from real places into the drawing has to follow the drawn stations locally
  (a triangulation or per-hop placement), never one scale per block: a coast placed by a block's affine fit
  (`layout.blocks`) will miss its stations by more than before.
- **Zone outlines now swallow bays.** The region's closing is 50 pitches (gaps and bays under about 100 pitches fill),
  so the harbour, Jamaica Bay and similar water inside the network sit inside a zone's outline. Where a coast is drawn,
  the outline's outer edge should probably follow it rather than cross the water; borders are drawn as lines only
  (no fill), so water under them is not hidden.
- **A fit for coasts.** `fitOcti` in `diagram/borders.ts` now takes an outer tolerance (`outer`): a run may stray
  further to one side (outward) than the other. A coast fitted the same way, more freely seaward than landward, keeps
  clear of the lines while losing small notches.

### How it works, and why this way

- **Source**: the packs' water masks (25 m, every zone has one; TIGER AREAWATER in the US, OSM-based elsewhere). Not the
  basemap tiles (remote, so no Node checks) nor `outlines.json` (too coarse for rivers). A river is water at least
  three 40 m cells across (about 120 to 160 m: the Sumida, the Harlem River pass; Newtown Creek, the Don and the Humber
  do not); the sea is water at least 2 km across everywhere (so the Hudson at Manhattan, 1.3 km, is a river, and the
  Tappan Zee, Lake Ontario and Tokyo Bay are sea).
- **Rivers**: thinned (Zhang and Suen) on the fine grid near the tracks, traced as arcs, spurs dropped; crossings are
  where a hop's real track crosses a centre line. In the drawing each crossing is a run square to the bundle and the
  band is routed between crossings on T-2488's router, but with **no crossing fallback**: where no clean way exists the
  band has a gap, and a crossing run that would cross another line is left out. So the check's two rules hold by
  construction. The warped course matters little (W_DEV 0.1): routes mostly take the cleanest way between crossings.
- **Sea**: T-2488's warp failed at coasts because it carries places; this carries *directions*. Each station looks
  along 16 rays on the ground for the sea within 3 mean hops (at least 3 km); a drawn cell is sea when its nearest
  drawn stations, mapping its offset back through their local maps, find it beyond the sea on that ray. Every pill
  plus 2.5 pitches is land. Earlier tries: sampling the ground at the mapped point (real offsets near stations are
  under 1 km, so nearly everything was land), and flooding cells far from stations from the near ones (land rays ran
  out from line ends); both dropped.
