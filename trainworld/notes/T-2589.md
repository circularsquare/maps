# T-2589 / T-2592: the network diagram's rebuild on the rubber sheet, faster (agent sheetspeed, 2026-11-06)

Stage 1 done: the same arithmetic, done faster. Every change keeps each float operation and its order, so the
diagram is bit for bit what it was (checked below). The aim (all Japan well under 15 s, New York under 4 s) is not met;
what is left is at the end.

Code (all in `app/src/diagram/`):

- `sheetLines.ts`, the carry: both axes solved at once, vectors interleaved (x, y per node), so each pass over the
  matrix serves both; the matrix product and Gauss-Seidel loops with no tests inside the lattice; nodes with no corner
  terms (on the finest lattice all but the stations' nodes) skip them (a term of 0 adds nothing); the residual folded
  into the product; restriction and prolongation read only a node's used parents. The axes' solves follow the old
  order where the multigrid goes stale (x first; if x does not settle in 20 iterations the multigrid is remade and y
  solved from its start with the new one). The first version ran both under the old multigrid and drew a few coast
  cells of all Japan differently in the page; fixed.
- `sheetLines.ts`, the map: the 3 by 3 majority counts with small arrays (it built a Map per cell); `dropSmall`'s
  flood and fill without an array per cell. `sheetThin.ts` `fillZones` the same.
- `layout.ts`: the separation's sweeps (Hildreth and the projections) over terms packed into flat arrays (`packRows`;
  the corrections copied back after each settle); `spread`'s crowd test does the distance test before the Set lookup.
- `lsqr.ts`: A^T u gathered by column from a transpose made once per solve (the same order the scatter added in);
  each step's vector update, norm and scaling in one loop.

## Tried and dropped

- Rounds that find stations off their land solved to 1e-3, the last to 1e-4: a third fewer iterations (New York carry
  2.0 to 1.2 s) but the carried map's water ended up to 1.6 pitches from the converged solution (the old code: 0.36 at
  most, 0.035 at the 99th percentile on coasts), 3 rings fewer in New York. The residual test lets the water's slow
  modes stand; the old path is accurate because its later rounds add iterations.
- Two smoothing sweeps a level: 61 iterations to 41, carry 2.0 to 1.9 s, slightly less accurate. Not worth it.
- The multigrid made again every round: iterations 117 to 93, no faster.
- The map step cropped to the network (T-2592): the drawn grid is only a little larger than the stations' box in New
  York (2,598 by 2,387 pitches against 2,021 by 1,752), and the whole view shows past the box on the screen's long
  side, so a crop that cannot change the whole view saves little; all Japan's islands fill its box diagonally.

## Stage 2 (agent sheetwasm, 2026-11-06): the carry's solve in WASM

The solve (`pcg9`, the multigrid's making and V-cycle) is `sim/src/diagram.rs` (`CarrySolver`, feature `diagram`, on by
default so in the track module); `sheetLines.ts` calls it through a small interface (`CarrySolver`, `setCarrySolver`),
the JavaScript one kept as it was; `diagram/carryWasm.ts` wraps the module's class; the diagram worker loads the track
module with the first sheet and sets it, unless the page's `?perfOff=carryWasm` (or the module fails to load: a warning,
and the JavaScript one). `carryMatrix`, the right side and the reweighting stay in JavaScript; the matrix is copied in once
and a reweighting sends the entries it changed.

Same bits: every float operation and its order as the JavaScript's, the two axes one f64x2 (lane-wise add, multiply,
divide; WASM has no fused multiply-add and LLVM does not contract), JavaScript's truthiness where it tests a number
(`rx || ry`, NaN false), `Math.max`'s NaN. What changed is only the memory traffic and the order of independent work:

- the matrix kept as 5 cross terms a node and, apart, the 4 corner terms read only at flagged nodes (40 bytes a node a
  pass, not 72);
- the residual and its restriction done row by row just behind the forward sweep, the prolongation row by row just
  ahead of the backward one (the rows' matrix still in cache); the restriction keeps each coarse node's sum in a
  register over the three fine nodes of a row that feed it (the same additions in the same order); a node's coarse
  parents worked out from its place, not read;
- the product p·q summed in the product's own pass (node order is the sum's order); level 0's right side read from r
  and z left in level 0's x (no copies);
- the Gauss-Seidel sweeps 4 rows at once as a wavefront, each 2 nodes behind the row before: a node reads exactly what
  it does in the plain order. Natively this took a sweep from 6.0 to 4.0 ms on New York's finest level; in V8 it made no
  measurable difference either way (kept for the native gain).

Checked: the carried lattice (every node), layout places, sea, rivers, borders and sheet value by value against the
JavaScript in Node, New York and all Japan from the saves: identical. In the page (headless, water on), WASM against
`?perfOff=carryWasm` at the four cameras (New York whole and lower Manhattan, all Japan whole and Kyushu): the only
differing pixels are the debug draw-time figures at the top right (box 1196,64 to 1373,147).
`artifacts/trainworld/sheetwasm/{js,wasm}-*.png`, `side-jpn-kyushu.png`, `side-nyc-whole.png`.

The carry is about twice as fast, not the 3-4 times hoped. One solve iteration on New York's finest lattice (215,460
nodes) costs about 18 ms in V8 at best and about 10 ms natively (x86, same code without SIMD): memory bound, and the
Gauss-Seidel sweep a chain of dependent subtractions and a division per node. The JavaScript's: about 45 ms.

Memory: WASM memory never shrinks, so the diagram worker keeps its peak after the first build (all Japan 228 MB, New
York well under), reused by the next build.

Tried and dropped: V8's baseline compiler is not the cause (`--no-liftoff` no faster).

## What is left

- The aim (all Japan well under 15 s, New York under 4 s) is not met and the carry cannot meet it alone: all Japan's
  page wall time is about 41 s with the carry at 11; the layout (about 13 s), the map (5.8 s), the ground (2.6 s) and
  the sheet (2.4 s) are the rest. The layout is T-2589 stage B's (below), now waiting on the smoothing work in
  `layout.ts`.
- The layout on a country map: the separation runs its full 60 sweeps every settle (it never meets its tolerance), 33
  settles; LSQR runs to its 400 and 600 iterations. Any change of either changes the drawing a little; a faster
  layout needs a decision on how much drawing change is acceptable, or WASM too.
- The sheet itself (1.1 s New York, 1.7-2.1 s Japan) not touched.
- Found: on the sheet, a "zones" message redraws the borders through `sheetMap` but posts the water from the first
  map again (`diagram.worker.ts` line ~103), so water and borders can come from maps made with different zone outlines
when the outlines arrive after the layout. Not checked whether that ever shows.
