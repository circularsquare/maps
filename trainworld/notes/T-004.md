# T-004 notes: city pack format 1

The contract between the data pipeline (T-004, T-019) and the demand kernel (T-005). Change it
only by editing this file and telling whoever reads it; bump `format` when you do.

Format 1 (T-019, 2026-10-08) added the 2 km zone gravity: a zone id per cell, per-zone arrays and
the `gravity` header block, so opening a city no longer solves it. Nothing reads format 0.

Two files per city in `data/packs/` (generated, gitignored):

- `<city>.json`, the header:

```json
{
  "format": 1,
  "city": "nyc",
  "built": "2026-10-08",
  "origin": { "lon": -73.985, "lat": 40.758 },
  "h3_res": 9,
  "cells": 74920,
  "arrays": [
    { "name": "h3",       "dtype": "u64", "per": "cell", "offset": 0,       "count": 74920 },
    { "name": "zone_row", "dtype": "f32", "per": "zone", "offset": 2120608, "count": 5710 }
  ],
  "totals": { "pop": 21736575, "jobs": 10176424 },
  "sources": [ { "what": "population", "name": "2020 Census, TIGER/Line TABBLOCK20 POP20", "url": "..." } ],
  "zones": 5710,
  "gravity": {
    "zone_m": 2000.0,
    "grid_origin_m": [-101749.27, -137466.84],
    "grid": [140, 142],
    "decay": "pow_exp",
    "decay_km": 29.0,
    "decay_pow": 1.0,
    "intrazonal_km": 1.04,
    "workers": "population scaled to total jobs",
    "iterations": 60,
    "max_error": 9.8e-05,
    "mean_trip_km": 15.35,
    "trips": 10176424.0,
    "solver": "trainworld-sim kernel::gravity (sim/src/bin/pack_gravity.rs)"
  }
}
```

- `<city>.bin`: the arrays back to back, little-endian, each starting on an 8-byte boundary, at
  the offsets the header gives (bytes).

Each array says whether it has one entry per cell (`"per": "cell"`, `count` = `cells`) or per
zone (`"per": "zone"`, `count` = `zones`). Cells are sorted by h3 id; only cells with pop > 0 or
jobs > 0 are included. Zones are numbered in the order their first cell appears.

| name | per | dtype | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `h3` | cell | u64 | H3 cell id at `h3_res` |
| `x_m`, `y_m` | cell | f32 | cell centre in metres east and north of `origin` (equirectangular: x = R·Δlon·cos(lat0), y = R·Δlat, R = 6371008.8) |
| `pop` | cell | f32 | residents |
| `jobs` | cell | f32 | jobs at place of work |
| `zone` | cell | u32 | the cell's gravity zone |
| `zone_gx`, `zone_gy` | zone | u16 | the zone's column and row in the zone grid |
| `zone_row` | zone | f32 | row factor A_I·O_I (O_I = the zone's home-end weight, `commute_home` or else `pop`, scaled to the work-end total) |
| `zone_col` | zone | f32 | column factor B_J·D_J (D_J = the zone's `commute_work`, or else `jobs`) |

**Zone trips** (home zone I to work zone J, all day) are `zone_row[I] · zone_col[J] · f(d)`, with
f(d) = `d^-decay_pow · exp(-d / decay_km)` (`"decay": "pow_exp"`, T-006) or `exp(-d / decay_km)`
(`"decay": "exp"`, the same with decay_pow 0),
with d = `zone_m`/1000 · sqrt(Δgx² + Δgy²) km between zone centres and `intrazonal_km` within a
zone. A cell is in grid square ((x_m − grid_origin_m[0]) / zone_m, (y_m − grid_origin_m[1]) /
zone_m), truncated, computed in f32. The factors balance both constraints: row sums equal O_I and
column sums equal D_J to within `max_error` (Furness, relative). Zone population and jobs are not
shipped; they are the sums of the cells (of `commute_home`/`commute_work` when present). `trips` and `mean_trip_km` are for checks and display.
A reader that does not implement `decay` (`"exp"` and `"pow_exp"` exist; `pow_exp` since T-006,
2026-10-09, no format bump) must refuse the gravity.

The gravity block and zone arrays are absent from the pipeline's intermediate
`data/work/<city>.cells.*`, which is otherwise a format-1 pack; the Rust reader then solves the
gravity itself, as it does when the game's `zone_m`, `decay_km` or `decay_pow` differ from the pack's.

Readers must ignore arrays they do not know (by name, or a `per` they do not know), so new arrays
(road speeds, attractors) can be added without a format bump.

### Commuters (T-042; still format 1)

Added 2026-10-09 without a format bump: two optional per-cell arrays and a `commute` header block.

| name | per | dtype | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `commute_home` | cell | f32 | residents who commute: `pop` x (1 - the tract's ACS work-from-home share) |
| `commute_work` | cell | f32 | jobs worked away from home: per LODES sector, jobs x (1 - the industry's ACS work-from-home share) |

**When both are present, the gravity balances on them instead of `pop` and `jobs`**: zone
workers O_I are the zone's `commute_home` scaled to the total of `commute_work`, and D_J the
zone's `commute_work`; subzones split zone trips by the same arrays. The Rust reader
(`pack::parse`) puts them in `City.pop`/`City.jobs`, which are the kernel's demand weights; a
pack with only one of the two is an error. `pop` and `jobs` stay the true counts (display, the
other-trips layer). `totals` gains `commute_home` and `commute_work`; the `commute` block says how
they were made and carries the industry shares.

### Water mask (T-030; still format 1)

Added 2026-10-08 without a format bump: two arrays with `"per": "water"` (older readers skip them,
the Rust demand reader included) and a `water` header block. Absent in a pack without a mask; a
reader that needs water and finds none should treat everything as land.

```json
"water": {
  "cell_m": 25.0,
  "origin_m": [-104675.0, -142075.0],
  "size": [11410, 11647],
  "encoding": "row runs: ...",
  "water_share": 0.4022,
  "source": "TIGER/Line 2020 AREAWATER per county + open sea outside TIGER 2020 states",
  "counties": 77, "areawater_polygons": 30237, "min_width_m": 50.0
}
```

| name | per | dtype | count | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `water_row` | water | u32 | H + 1 | offsets into `water_x`; row r's entries are `water_x[water_row[r] .. water_row[r+1]]` |
| `water_x` | water | u16 | 2 x runs | per row, pairs (start, end): columns start..end-1 are water |

- **Grid**: `size` = [W, H] pixels of `cell_m` metres, in the pack's local frame (metres east and
  north of `origin`, the same equirectangular frame as `x_m`/`y_m`). Pixel (c, r) covers
  x in [x0 + c·cell, x0 + (c+1)·cell) and y in [y0 + r·cell, y0 + (r+1)·cell), with
  (x0, y0) = `origin_m`, the south-west corner. **Row 0 is the southernmost row, column 0 the
  westernmost.** A pixel is water when its centre was inside water.
- **Runs**: within a row, the bounds are strictly increasing: runs are sorted, disjoint and never
  touch (a gap of at least one land pixel between runs). `end` may equal W. Each row has an even
  number of entries; an empty row has none.
- **Lookup** of a point (x, y) in metres:

  ```
  c = floor((x - x0) / cell); r = floor((y - y0) / cell)
  if c < 0 or c >= W or r < 0 or r >= H: land          (outside the grid is land)
  row = water_x[water_row[r] .. water_row[r+1]]
  k = number of entries in row that are <= c           (binary search: partition_point(|v| v <= c))
  water = k is odd
  ```

  In Rust, with `row: &[u16]`: `row.partition_point(|&v| (v as i64) <= c) % 2 == 1`.
- **Along a segment** (build cost, level-0 validity): either sample every `cell_m / 2` metres with
  the lookup, or walk the rows the segment crosses and intersect its x span in each row with that
  row's runs; the second gives exact wet length at pixel resolution. A straight or arc piece is
  "over water" for pricing by the share of its length whose samples are water.
- Size for New York: 266,401 runs, 1.11 MB raw. `pipeline/read_pack.py` has a reference lookup
  (`is_water`) and checks 18 named places; `pipeline/look_water.py` draws `T-030.png`.

The boundary goes alongside as `<city>.boundary.geojson` (one polygon or multipolygon, WGS84).

## Build results (2026-10-08, New York)

The boundary file is a FeatureCollection with one Feature whose geometry is the MultiPolygon
(properties: city, county list, source).

`python pipeline/build_city.py nyc` (maps venv), then `python pipeline/read_pack.py nyc` to check
the pack independently of the writer (it re-checks the gravity factors against both constraints
in numpy), and `python pipeline/look_pack.py nyc` for `T-004.png`. The gravity step runs the sim
crate with cargo (notes/T-019.md), so Rust must be installed; it adds ~5 s. 15 s from scratch
before T-019, 13 s with downloads cached and the Rust bin built. The block tables come out of the
TIGER zips by HTTP range requests (only the .dbf member, 71 MB instead of 510 MB of zips);
`data/raw/` holds 83 MB.

How (as first built): each 2020 block's internal point (INTPTLAT20/INTPTLON20) picked its res-9
cell; POP20 and LODES 8 WAC 2023 C000 were summed per cell. The table below is that first build.
Since T-013 each block is spread over its polygon by land area (202,337 cells, notes/T-013.md),
and since T-014 341k LODES jobs reported at head offices are moved (notes/T-014.md).
