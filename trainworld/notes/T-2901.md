## Stage 3 done (2026-11-09, tree `terrainworld`)

- Sim: `Terrain` is a map of blocks by `cost::block_key`, with `BlockIndex`; a point in a listed block not installed reads
  flat and marks the block wanted (`take_terrain_wanted`), so the worker's fetch set comes from what was actually priced at
  the model's own 25 m samples, not a separate walk. `terrain_blocks_under` gives the Construct gate its exact block list,
  `drop_terrain` frees a block no edge reaches into. `rework_ground` now sets `repriced` only when a price changed, since a
  view brings 30 blocks. WASM additions: `set_terrain_index`, `drop_terrain`, `take_terrain_wanted`, `terrain_blocks_under`.
- Worker (`clock.worker.ts`, terrain section): index first, blocks by need (wanted) and by view, Construct gate by block,
  `openNetwork` waits at most 15 s for the blocks under a loaded game (no pre-load walk; the rework after the load costs
  only each edge's terrain pass). A listed block that 404s or holds bad bytes is a failure, retried, never flat. The worker
  keeps no bytes; the page gets the view's blocks (at most 200 kept), the model at most 300 beyond those an edge stands on.
- Page: `terrainGrids` by block key, `hexClass`, tint and `demFlat` by key, tint `MIN_ZOOM` 9. Vite serves
  `/packs/terrain-world/`; a build holds hard links in `dist/packs/terrain-world/` so 8800 serves them; `deploy.py` leaves them
  out of a release, `--terrain-world` uploads them to the fixed prefix (stage 5), a release is refused until it is complete.
- Numbers: Atlas segment 262.5 US$M a km (mountain) in a new game; NYC real save Built 769,549,339,791 before and after
  (identical, edge total 540,941,939,791). Stage 4 (zone files) untouched: the `/packs/terrain` middleware and the build's copy of
  `data/terrain/*.terrain` stay until then.

## Answer in short

- Rebuild the fine intermediate for all land in one AWS run (about 1 hour, about US$1.50), cut it into one class file
  per 1 degree block at the same 1/960 lattice (0.6-0.8 GB for the whole world), serve those from R2, and let them
  replace the per-zone files. The world at full resolution is about the size of today's zone files (616 MB).
- **A bug found on the way**: 553 of the 3,785 zones have over 10% of their land priced flat only because a tile
  was never fetched, 103 of them over half. The rebuild fixes it. See "Partial blocks".

### Partial blocks (the bug)

`terrain_fine.make_blocks` lists a block but fetches only the tiles under the boxes known that day; `run` then skips
any block already in the manifest. So a block once half fetched counts as done forever, and a later zone over its
missing half reads no data, which `classes_of` treats as flat. Measured: **3,785 registry zones, 823 with over 1% of
their land in no-data cells, 553 over 10%, 103 over 50%; 3.1% of the land cells in all zone boxes** (0.35 bn of 11.1
bn, boxes counted with overlaps). Salto (sbv): 28% of its land, Khamis Mushayt (khh): 26%, Wadi Ad Dawasir (waw): 20%,
every one of those cells flat in its `.terrain` file (checked: 100% flat over no-data land, 58-84% flat over the rest).

So today's "flat" in much of Argentina, Saudi Arabia, Peru, Russia and elsewhere is a data gap, not flat ground.
A clean rebuild fixes these zones as a side effect. Second small bug in the same place: `Fine.__init__` sorts part
tars as strings, so `part10.tar` reads before `part2.tar` and a re-done block in a later part would lose to the old
copy. A clean rebuild avoids it; anything incremental must fix it.

the partial tracking; the old parts are deleted once the new set checks out. Keeps her T-1854 rule ("don't have to
redownload later"): the intermediate stays, and thresholds or lattices can change from it without another read.

## 2. How it reaches the game

**Size, measured** on every 10th block with a file (1,006 blocks), classes made as `zone_terrain.classes_of`:

| Encoding | Mean KB (full land) | p50 | p90 | max | World, 18,828 blocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| TWT1 runs, gzip, 1/960 (today's format) | 42.5 | 39 | 87 | 125 | **0.6-0.8 GB** |
| same, brotli | 41.4 | 38 | 85 | 123 | saves 3%, not worth a second code path |
| 1/480 lattice (mode of 2 x 2) | 17.9 | 17 | 35 | 47 | 0.26-0.33 GB |
| 1/240 lattice | 7.0 | 7 | 13 | 17 | 0.10-0.13 GB |

Projected over all land by source tile bytes a block (a rough match, r = 0.4); the low end is a bucketed projection
(32 KB a mean land block), the high end the mean above times 18,828.

**One lattice, one file per block.** A coarser lattice for no-zone land saves 0.4 GB of R2 storage (about half a cent
a month) and costs two prices across every zone edge plus a second code path. Not worth it. So `<la>_<lo>.terrain`
files, 18-20k of them, exactly the existing `.terrain` format with `w = h = 960`, so `ClassGrid::decode` and the app's
decoder are unchanged. Blocks come from a new `pipeline/world_terrain.py` reading the intermediate with one cell of
neighbour (the 3 x 3 filter and the coarse cap need it across block edges). About 0.25 core-seconds a block, measured
(the sample run did three encodes a block), under 10 minutes on 8 cores for everything.

**Replace the zone files.** Today every zone has a `data/terrain/<id>.terrain` box and `world_run.py` copies them; zone
boxes overlap, and by construction their edge rows can differ (filter at `mode="nearest"`), so `Terrain::class_at`'s
`find_map` depends on which grid landed first near a box edge. With blocks a point belongs to exactly one file, which
removes that too. Zones stop needing a terrain step at all: a new country needs no `zone_terrain.py` run and no
`build_inputs` check row. `data/terrain/*.terrain` (616 MB, in every release's R2 prefix) goes away.

**Fetching.** Files on R2 under a fixed prefix, `r2:anitamaps/trainworld/terrain-world/v1/` (not the release prefix:
`tools/deploy.py` uploads each release to a new versioned prefix, and 19k files must not go up every release). Immutable,
cached a year. Add a `terrain-world/index.bin` bit per block for which blocks have a file (360 x 150 bits, 7 KB, fetched
at start beside `world.water`). Dev server and 8800 serve the same files from a local folder.

**Who loads what.** Two consumers, different needs:

- The track model loads blocks under what it prices: live edges, the blueprint, previews. It never evicts a block under a
  live edge (an evicted block would read flat at the next re-derive, and charges are the change in the built total).
- The tint loads blocks the camera reaches, as today's view fetch (view under `VIEW_SPAN_M` 400 km plus 20 km margin; wider
  views load only near the centre). Keeps at most about 200 blocks (LRU), held by a `Map` keyed on block, looked up by key.
  `hexClass` and the tint today scan all grids per hex; that scan must become a key lookup.

**Memory**: 230 KB a block (two bits a cell), in the sim, and the same again in the page's tint copy. A view is up to
about 30 blocks (4.7 degrees x 3 at 40 N plus margin): about 7 MB. A 1,000 km route crosses 10-25 blocks. A very big
network under about 100 blocks is 23 MB of WASM, less than today's biggest single zone at 60 MB. No limit trouble.

**Network**: mean 35-43 KB a file, so a 30-block view is about 1.2 MB and a 1000 km line 0.5-1 MB. R2: 0.7 GB is about
$0.01 a month, 19k uploads are free-tier, reads free up to 10M a month. Decode is RLE, milliseconds a block.

**The sim** (`sim/src/track/cost.rs`): `Terrain` gets a `HashMap<(i16, i16), Arc<ClassGrid>>` by block beside (later in
place of) the `Vec`. `class_at` takes the hex's centre (unchanged, which is why blocks don't change a single hex's
sample), picks the block by floor(lon), floor(lat), reads the cell. `add_terrain` and the rework logic are unchanged:
a block's bounds in the network frame, rework only the edges reaching in. WASM interface unchanged.

**Determinism.** A price must depend only on the point and the data version, never on what happened to load. Rules:

1. A block not listed in `index.bin` is sea, known flat, needs no file. A listed block that 404s or fails is a failure
   (retried, status tag), never flat. This closes today's "404 = flat" gap, which would otherwise make a partial upload
   silently change prices.
2. Construct waits for every block under the blueprint (the T-2613 gate, `groundForBlueprint`, moved from zones to blocks,
   walking along each polyline so a long straight edge counts every block it crosses) and is refused with `TerrainMissing`
   if any cannot be had. Nothing is paid on a guess.
3. Previews do not wait, as today: priced flat until the block lands, then repriced. The tool tag follows. Same behaviour
   and timing as a zone grid today.
4. Load: fetch blocks under the save first, like `masksBeforeLoad` does for water, so the save derives once and the Built
   figure doesn't jump. A failed block leaves that stretch flat until it lands; Built moves then, no cash.
5. Charges stay safe because `charge = built_after - built_before` inside one edit, both under the same blocks, and
   blocks install between queued messages.

**When a block is not loaded yet at the moment of building**: the blueprint's quote may read flat for a moment, then
updates. Pressing Construct waits (a second or two) and then charges the true price, or refuses if the block cannot be
fetched after retries. No save-time data is stored, so save and reload yield the same price.

## 3. What it changes for players

- **Zones**: same data, same prices, except the zones the rebuild fixes (above), which get real hills where they had
  flat by accident.
- **No-zone land**: real prices. Ground track on mountains goes from 0.5x to 3.5x of base (7 times, the mountain surcharge
  is +3.0x), hills 2x to 3.4x. A trunk across the Atlas will cost what a Rocky Mountain one costs. That is the point.
- **Built track on no-data land**: repriced at the next load, like T-2890, no save level needed. Built track is re-derived
  on load from its geometry, so its Built figure on the Money tab rises (for Morocco's Atlas track, possibly several times).
  No cash is paid or refunded: charges come from the change in the built total within an edit, removal refunds nothing
  (`track-and-service.md`, T-096), and upkeep is by level, not terrain. So the only visible effect is the Built number.
  Leaving old track at the old flat price would need a per-edge mark in the save and a second sampler, and would charge
  phantom money on edits to it, which is why T-2890 rejected the same idea. Recommendation: reprice, one DECISIONS line,
  no save level. T-2890's -0.002% was a resampling; this one is real new data on land that had none, and will be bigger
  on a mountainous network, but it is the true price of what they built.
- **Oceans**: no file, no change. Water surcharge decides as today; a class never adds to water. Coast cells are flat
  where GEDTM30 has no data.
- **Polar**: GEDTM30 covers 85 N to 65 S. North of 85 N there is no land in world.water at all. South of 65 S is
  Antarctica (6,933 land blocks with no data at all): stays flat and untinted, as it is now. Not worth another source.
- **The tint**: no zone needed any more; it draws where blocks are loaded. It stays faint and hex-based. At zoom 7-8 a view
  is wider than 400 km and only a patch near the centre loads, as today with zones, so a 1 degree square of tint would be
  visible; I'd raise the tint's `MIN_ZOOM` from 7 to 9 (T-1854 recommended this too). Visible, so it goes in her review.
- **Hillshade flatten** (`map/demFlat.ts`) also keys on zones; move it to the blocks so flat ground is flat everywhere.

## How much to trust the numbers

- Land counts use `world.water` at 60 cells a degree (1.8 km). Tiny islands are missed; the block list uses the source's
  own non-empty tiles (20,198), and the run records empty ones as null.
- Partial detection counts land from the same 60-a-degree mask, so a few percent either way; the three zone files
  checked by hand confirm it.
- File sizes are measured on zone blocks, biased to populated land. Mountainous Siberia will run high, the Sahara low.
  0.6-0.8 GB is the honest range; even double is a few cents a month.
- Time on AWS assumes the server's 60-76 MB/s again. I found no published rate limit and no complaint in the two runs.
  One connection attempt timed out from home today and two 5 MB tiles took 7 s and 22 s, so home runs are unattractive.
