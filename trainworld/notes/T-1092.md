# T-1092: central destinations for errands and social outings (built 2026-10-24)

Option 2 of notes/T-1090.md section 5, the aggregate form of Anita's "a POI per 10k people
skewed to dense centres". A runtime rule in the everyday sampler; no pack rebuild.

## As built

- `everyday::CENTRAL_SHARE` 0.2 and `CENTRAL_DECAY_M` 6000 (`Params::everyday_central_share`,
  `everyday_central_m`; `set_extra` names `central_share`, `central_m`). Errands and social only
  (`has_central`).
- Per origin zone the draw is a mixture: 80% the local draw (attraction x exp(-d / scale)), 20%
  the central draw (attraction x attraction per km² of land, x exp(-d / 6 km)). The cdf runs over
  the zones twice, local then central (`mix_central`), so a cell's stratified samples split
  between the parts in proportion. Within the zone the cell is drawn by attraction for both. Land
  is `ncell` x 0.1053 km², at most the square, as parking measures it. Sample weights are
  unchanged, so a cell's outings are conserved (less the 150 m and 40-80 km cuts).
- The local scales for errands and social go from 1.3 and 1.4 km to 1.0 km (`DECAY`), as in the
  scratch test, so the short end stays where it was while the central part makes the tail.
- Share 0 is the old code path exactly (no second cdf). The WASM interface is unchanged.
- Tests: `central_mix_shares`, `central_destinations_reach_a_dense_centre`.
- Harness: `notes/T-1092/t1092_meas.rs` (included into everyday.rs for a run, like
  `notes/T-1056/t1056_ceiling.rs`) walks every destination sample: length bands, mean, no-rail
  walk share, the share ending in the job core (the densest zones by jobs per km² of land holding
  the first 10% of the pack's jobs), and the five busiest destination zones. `run.py` drives it,
  the T-1056 ceilings and `calib` on the real networks. "Before" is `central_share=0,
  decay_errands=1300, decay_social=1400` on the same binary.

## Calls made

- **Local scale 1.0 km for both purposes** (T-1090 section 5 left "slightly shorter" open). The
  scratch test used 1.0 for both; one number, and it keeps under 2 km where it was (errands
  37.1% to 37.3% in New York) instead of losing a fifth of the short trips.
- **Density is the purpose's own attraction per km² of land, power 1** (the scratch's "g2"). The
  squared version sent 17% of New York's errands to the core against 8.9%, with no evidence for it.
- **Only errands and social.** School, university, medical and airports have their own sites and
  scales; medical (2 km) and university (3.5 km) already run long.
- **No OSM high-order tags** (option 3 as an ingredient): the packs do not carry them, and the
  brief is no pack rebuild. The attraction density alone puts the busiest central zones on the
  real centres (below).
- **No cap on one zone's pull.** The busiest zone takes 5-6% of a big city's errands (Midtown,
  Oxford Street), which is real; a lump in a thin tier 0 attraction proxy would be squared, so the
  worldwide entry names it as the check.
