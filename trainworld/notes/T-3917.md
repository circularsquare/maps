# T-3917: where big saves get slow and run out of memory

Agent lagsurvey, 2026-11-21. Measure only: no game code changed. Anita, 11-21: "general lag complaints are picking up.
lots of people have 2000 station saves it seems, and they're having trouble continuing to play."

## Method

- Saves: her `artifacts/trainworld/t3600-asianita-1852.save` (221 lines, 2,647 stations, 160 zones opened) and
  `artifacts/trainworld/saves/na-day-491.save` (175 lines, 2,240 stations, 163 zones opened). For phones the shipped
  real-network starts `data/packs/real/tyo.save` (Tokyo, 1,933 stations) and `osa.save` (Kansai, 1,438), and a new
  Tokyo game.
- My Vite on 6175 (dev server; production is 0.1-0.3 GB lower, T-2485). Headless Chrome with its own profile, pinned
  to 4 claimed cores (phones 2), `?debug=1&new=1&paused=0&demandWorkers=auto`, so the page's own pool rule runs: 16
  threads and a reported 8 GB ask for 3 solving workers, and T-3373 A's share cuts both saves to **1 solving worker**
  at load. The `dw2` arms force 2 (`demandWorkers=2`): what a 6-11 thread machine got before T-3373 A (11-16); a
  16-thread machine had 3, so the 2-worker gains are a floor of what A cost a big machine.
- Every load here is **cold** (the save imported as a file). A slot load with a cached result shows its trips at
  about 22 s and then solves the whole network again for 4-5 minutes (T-3868). Each arm ran **once**; other agents'
  checks shared the machine (ledger 8-10 of 10 cores most of the time), so compare arms roughly.
- Driver: scratchpad `lagsurvey/measure.mjs` (from T-3472's `session.mjs`; every evaluate returns plain data, so the
  T-2802 `jumpTo` artifact is gone), `pagelib.js` (the edits, with the ops the tools send), `profile.mjs` (a CPU
  profile of the page), `procs.ps1`, per-run `run-*.json` and `.log`, summaries `mem.py`, `edits.py`, `tree.py`.
- Memory: Windows private bytes of every Chrome process of the profile; per target (page, each worker) CDP heap and
  ArrayBuffers, each target's `WebAssembly.Memory`, and `twDemand.client.memory()`.
- "Shows up": the edit's answer, the world changing and two frames drawn. "Trips calculated": the demand final after
  it. The status tag's text and bar were recorded each second on the NA runs.
- Main thread: long tasks (script holding the page over 50 ms), frame gaps, long animation frames with their scripts;
  clock at speed 2. Headless frames on shared pinned cores are not a player's frame rate, and the 4-5 s frame gaps
  with no script in them are the headless GPU process, so stalls are counted as long tasks.

## Short version, ranked by what a player waits for

1. **Trips after an edit take 5-7.5 minutes.** One solving worker: NA 296-320 s for a train count change, a new
   station or a 1.5 km extension; asianita 303-450 s for the first three edits, 98-147 s for two later train count
   edits. The tag says "Calculating trips after your change..." with a bar that moves; the totals and every line's
   riders change only at the final (the zone in view's own figures and bubbles were not recorded). A second edit made
   60 s into a calculation waits for it, then starts again: NA 560 s from the first edit to trips calculated (499 s
   after the second), asianita 208 s.
2. **A cold load takes 5.5-7.6 minutes before the clock runs.** The map and network are drawn at 31-35 s, but no
   trips at all show until 188 s (asianita) or 324 s (NA), and the loading card stays until the final (455 s, 333 s).
   Every release discards every player's cached result (the cache key carries the build stamp), so the first open
   after each update is this cold load.
3. **Memory: 4.2-4.3 GB settled, 4.9-5.5 GB at the load's peak, 5.3-6.5 GB after 15 minutes of play.** Biggest
   holder: the one solving worker's WASM, 1.33-1.49 GB. What grows while playing is the GPU process (0.43 to 1.0 GB,
   map tiles while flying between cities) and the page's ArrayBuffers (0.26 to 0.6-1.2 GB, 0.63-0.67 held after a
   collection).
4. **Phones: the Tokyo and Kansai real-network starts peak at 1.9-2.0 GB in the tab** on an emulated iPhone (dev
   server), against iOS's kill at about 1.4-2 GB. A new Tokyo game with nothing built is 0.8 GB.
5. **Main-thread stalls in play: 6-11 long tasks a minute over 200 ms, worst 1.4 s.** In a 2-minute profile with
   one edit, each new world state took the renderer 0.44-0.54 s (`setNetwork`, the whole drawn network redone);
   the rest of the clock client's 15-24 s per 15 minutes is not broken down.
6. **The edit itself shows up in 0.4-1.0 s (train count), 2.6-4.2 s (a station on a line), 5.0-5.5 s (an
   extension)**: each step of a multi-step edit takes 0.6-2.1 s.

T-3373 A (one solving worker on these saves) cost them, at least: on NA two workers load in 226 s instead of 333 s
(32% less) and calculate the matched train edit in 188 s instead of 296 s (36% less), for 1.3 GB more settled and
1.65 GB more at the load's peak. Asianita's pair (248 s against 455 s, 45% less, +1.4 GB) straddles a landing and a
Vite restart: indicative only.

- asianita at 1 worker: the network reaches demand at 30 s, packs are open and the solve sent at 67 s, set-up 14 s,
  then 4 rounds of 5 periods at 13-28 s a period (rounds published at 188, 277, 344, 449 s). NA publishes all four
  rounds in the last 2 s (324-326 s), as T-3868 found.
- The cache key (`demandClient.ts` `keyBase`) carries `BUILD`, which is `Date.now()` at build time
  (`vite.config.ts`), and `sameKey` rejects any other build. So each published version makes the next open of every
  save a cold load. Releases this week changed demand (boarding minutes, ferries, trams), so most would have needed
  it anyway; a key on the demand code alone would only help releases that leave demand untouched.

## Recommended next

1. **T-2624: the lines in view and the edited zone's figures update within seconds of an edit.** Gain: the lines the
   player is looking at get riders in seconds instead of 296-450 s, on every edit of a 2,000-station save, at no
   memory cost. The seconds are from small zones only (T-2605: Salt Lake City's local refine 1.4-1.7 s; T-3868:
   Mexico City's local stage 4.7-6.9 s); for New York or Seoul it is not measured. The biggest felt change for
   "trouble continuing to play".
2. **New, small: a newer edit replaces a whole calculation that is under half done** (T-3868 option C). Gain on NA:
   two edits 60 s apart finish about 340 s after the first instead of 560; three, about 400 instead of about 840.
   About 2-4 hours; it gives up part of T-1734's promise that some final lands while the player keeps building.
   "Recalculate trips while paused" already covers a player who pauses to edit.
3. **T-3373 B (zones split between workers), with its gain stated as speed.** On these saves the pool is already one
   worker, so B frees no memory here; it lets 2-3 workers run in about one worker's memory. The 2-worker arm (periods
   split, so a proxy) gives the size: on NA 32% off the load and 36% off an edit's trips (asianita's load 45%,
   indicative). B's memory for a second worker is not measured (each worker would hold half the zones plus the
   market and set-up). 25-40 h (T-3373). Meanwhile, the hours-sized version of the same trade is T-3868's option A
   (two workers on saves this size, +1.3-1.6 GB): Anita's call.

For phones, T-3160 is the task; the crashes would be easier to place if the died-load report named the game that was
loading (stations, real-network or not). That is a few lines in `loading.ts`' flag, not filed.
