# T-727: snap track to the real network (stages a-d built)

Anita, 2026-10-19: "fast build tool for snapping rail placement to corridors? If this is not enabled
lets draw the existing rail corridors at like 0.15 opacity. If its enabled we can draw them as we
draw them now. To snap: if we start the rail placement near a real rail, and we end the placement
over another point nearish a real rail, and we can path along the real rail between them, we snap
the whole segment. So we should be able to click once near nyc, once near san francisco, and bang
place the whole thing along the ~california zephyr or whatever is shorter." Also, from the long list:
"few click fast reconstruct the entire route from tokyo to osaka". This is T-682's stage 2.

Everything below was measured with a prototype on noritetsu's data (scripts in `notes/t727/`,
outputs and pictures in `../artifacts/trainworld/t727/` and `T-727-fit*.png`). Nothing in the app
changed.

## The short version

- **It works, and the track model takes it as it is.** Two clicks, Tokyo Station to Shin-Osaka:
  the route found is the Tokaido Shinkansen, 515.1 km (the real figure is 515.4), fitted to our
  straights and arcs with 452 corners, on average 4 m from the real track and never more than 22 m.
  Two clicks, Penn Station to Emeryville: 5,410 km along the Lake Shore Limited and the California
  Zephyr, 5,649 corners. Both went into the real track model as **one** `add_route` edit (native
  build): 5 ms and 54 ms, undo 1 ms and 10 ms, $6.95B and $72.9B at ground level.
- **The routing graph is a small file per region**, built by a trainworld pipeline script from
  noritetsu's own extract (it does not touch noritetsu): 1.3-2.4 MB gzipped for the network the
  faint layer draws (Japan 1.8, US 1.6, Germany 2.4). The map tiles cannot be routed over.
- **What it follows is what the layer draws**: passenger lines, so New York to San Francisco is the
  Zephyr (her guess), not the 8% shorter freight route over Wyoming.
- **Three things the build has to fix**, all found by the prototype: a click must look at every
  track near it, not the nearest (at Tokyo Station the nearest is a conventional line, and the
  Shinkansen is not joined to it); the fit must check its own curves (one 95 m curve in 5,410 km made
  the edit refuse the whole route); and track drawn through corners is not bridged over water yet
  (T-667), which a route out of Penn Station under the Hudson needs.

## Where the routing graph comes from

The tiles are display data: below zoom 10 they hold merged chains with no way ids, the junction
vertex can be simplified away, and every line is cut at tile edges. A cross-country route would need
thousands of tiles and its topology rebuilt from coordinates. So the graph is its own file.

**Source**: noritetsu's extract per region, `noritetsu/data/proc/<cc>/ways.pkl` and `coords.npz`
(every railway way with its OSM node ids, which is what gives the junctions), filtered the way
`build_tiles.py` filters the tiles (main and branch track plus anything a passenger route runs
over), then to the ways some line runs over (`dist/data/<cc>/ways.json`, the tiles' `n` property).
That is the faint layer's own set, so a player can only snap to what she sees. Junctions are OSM
nodes shared by two ways; chains run junction to junction.

**Size** (`notes/t727/size.py`; vertices thinned to 3 m, 1e-5 degree steps, delta coded, gzip):

| region | drawn network (the layer) | all track incl. freight and yards | without yards |
|---|---|---|---|
| Japan | 99k chains, 1.8 MB | 2.7 MB | 2.0 MB |
| Great Britain | 71k, 1.3 MB | 2.1 MB | 1.5 MB |
| France | 80k, 1.6 MB | 3.3 MB | 1.9 MB |
| Germany | 131k, 2.4 MB | 5.2 MB | 3.0 MB |
| United States | 80k, 1.6 MB | 12.1 MB | 6.0 MB |

**Loading**: fetched from R2 beside noritetsu's tiles, for the regions in view, when the snap switch
is first turned on; the regions a route reaches are fetched as the search reaches their border.
About 300k vertices a region, so parsing should be tens of milliseconds (not measured).

**Borders**: Geofabrik's extracts overlap, so neighbouring regions share OSM nodes where track
crosses. France and Germany share 115 chain ends at 7 crossings (Basel, Strasbourg-Kehl,
Lauterbourg, Saarbrücken, Perl), so files join by OSM node id; each file keeps the ids of its chain
ends near its outline.

**Licence**: the file holds only OSM geometry (the registers are used only to choose which ways
count), so it is ODbL: the attribution the layer already carries, and the file offered under ODbL
where it is published. Registers' terms do not travel with it. T-682's licence question stands as it
was (only matters if the game is sold).

## Pathfinding

- **Shortest by length** over the chains, Dijkstra on directed chains (A* in the build). A route may
  not turn more than 60 degrees where two chains meet, so it cannot run into a turnout's one branch
  and out of the other. Measured: Tokyo-Osaka 30k states, 0.2 s in Python; New York-San Francisco
  48k states, 0.25 s. Over all track (freight and yards too) the US search grows to 890k states, 5.6 s.
- **Snapping looks at every chain within the snap radius** (prototype 400 m; in the build a screen
  distance, 12 px) and the search starts and ends on whichever is best, each candidate's distance
  from the click counted twice. With only the nearest track, Tokyo-Osaka found no route at all:
  Tokyo Station's nearest track is a conventional line, and the Shinkansen (another gauge) joins the
  conventional network nowhere on the way. With every track near the click it found the Shinkansen.
- **Which track counts**: what the layer draws, metro, main line and other passenger rail. Metro and
  main line are two families: a route keeps to the family of the track nearest its first click at
  face value and pays three times the length on the other, so a click on a subway line follows the
  subway and an intercity route does not cut through subway tunnels. Trams, light rail, heritage,
  funiculars and track no line runs over are out. The alternative, all main-line track including
  freight-only: New York-San Francisco 4,974 km over the Union Pacific's Overland Route (Omaha,
  Cheyenne, Ogden), 8% shorter than the Zephyr route but on track the layer does not draw. Yards and
  sidings would only be needed as connectors, and with every track near a click they are not.
- **Cap**: no length cap (New York-San Francisco is the use case); the search stops after about
  2 seconds or a few million states, and says no route was found.

## Fitting the route to our track

The fitted track is what the drawing tool already makes: corners (PIs) with straights between,
each corner cut by a circular arc, auto radius (the largest fitting half of each leg, capped at
1,796 m, at least 100 m). The prototype (`notes/t727/route_fit.py`) thins the real line with
Douglas-Peucker at 0.6 times the tolerance, fits the arcs, measures every 10 m how far the real
track lies from the fitted one, adds a corner where it is out of tolerance, then removes corners
whose curve would be under 100 m (where the route changes between two parallel tracks) if the track
stays within twice the tolerance there.

| route | km | tolerance | corners | per km | mean / 99% / worst offset (m) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tokaido Shinkansen, Tokyo-Shin-Osaka | 515 | 10 m | 710 | 1.38 | 1.9 / 5.3 / 9.5 |
| | | **25 m** | **452** | **0.88** | **3.8 / 12.9 / 21.7** |
| | | 50 m | 315 | 0.61 | 7.9 / 26.7 / 30.0 |
| | | 100 m | 222 | 0.43 | 15.2 / 53.0 / 76.7 |
| Northeast Corridor, New York-Washington | 361 | 25 m | 351 | 0.97 | 3.8 / 12.7 / 18.4 |
| Lake Shore Ltd + California Zephyr, New York-Emeryville | 5,410 | 10 m | 9,084 | 1.68 | 1.9 / 5.4 / 127 |
| | | **25 m** | **5,649** | **1.04** | **4.2 / 13.6 / 58.9** |
| | | 50 m | 3,979 | 0.74 | 7.8 / 28.5 / 165 |

The worst offsets on the US route are single spots where the prototype had to drop a corner to keep
a curve at 100 m. Corners per km follow the terrain: 0.1-0.5 on the plains, 4 over Donner Pass
(217 in 50 km). Curves come out about as tight as the real ones: at the Shinkansen's 24 curves under
1,010 m (all between Tokyo and Shin-Yokohama, and one at Shin-Osaka) the fitted radius was mostly
within a fifth of the real track's own radius over 300 m, a few up to a third tighter. So a line's
speed limits follow the real line's, capped at 160 km/h.

**Recommended tolerance 25 m**: about one corner per km of intercity line, 4 m off on average, which
is a pixel from zoom 14 (3.7 m a pixel at New York's latitude).

**What the build's fitter does better than the prototype**: set each corner's radius itself (the
edit takes a radius per corner) instead of the half-leg auto radius, so a tight real curve beside a
short leg does not come out too tight, and check every curve with the track model's own fit before
sending. The prototype's first New York-San Francisco edit was refused whole ("The curve is too
tight") by one 95 m curve. The fitter belongs in the sim crate (`track/trace.rs`) beside `geom::fit`,
called by the clock worker; in Python it took 1 s for Tokyo-Osaka and 15 s for New York-San
Francisco, almost all of it the offset check, which in Rust is a direct distance per leg.

**The edit**: `add_route` with the clicked corners (`pis`) as it stands, one piece per corner as
T-244 has it. Measured in a release build on an empty Mercator world (`notes/t727/edit_test.rs`):

| | corners | preview | add_route | undo | save grows by | price, ground, double |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tokyo-Shin-Osaka | 452 | 10 ms | 5 ms | 1 ms | 9.5 KB | $6.95B (514.7 km) |
| New York-Emeryville | 5,649 | 94 ms | 54 ms | 10 ms | 118 KB | $72.9B (5,399.9 km) |

A new game starts with $3B, so these go down as blueprint, total in red, as any big blueprint does.
One edit is one undo step. Crossings of the player's own track are whatever the edit makes of them
for a drawn segment (refused where it is refused now).

**Water, bridges, tunnels**: the ground is flat, so the real line's tunnels and viaducts (69 km and
180 km of the Shinkansen) are ignored and the route goes at the elevation selected. Water is the
one thing that matters, and only inside zones (the mask comes with the packs): a route through
corners is not bridged today (T-667), so the build makes `bridge.rs` raise each piece over water
like a drawn segment. Penn Station's route under the Hudson needs it.

## Interface

- **The switch**: an icon toggle in the Build tab beside keep placing ("Snap to real track" on its
  hover), remembered per browser like the other build settings. It changes only the track tool.
- **The layer**: off, the real network is drawn at 0.15 opacity; on, at today's 0.4. Turning the
  switch on shows the layer even if Settings > Real network is off, since there is nothing to snap to
  that cannot be seen.
- **The cursor**: within 12 px of a drawn real line, a ring sits on the real line at the snap point.
  Away from real track the tool draws as it does now, so the switch never stops ordinary drawing.
- **After the first click**: while the pointer rests near real track (120 ms), the route is searched
  and the real line it follows is drawn as the ghost, then the fitted track replaces it with the
  usual tag (length, cost multiplier, price; elevations; lowest speed limit). A search over half a
  second shows the small spinner in the tag. The second click places it at once, one undo step,
  as any segment; Shift or keep placing carries on from its end.
- **No real route between the clicks**: when both clicks are near real track but nothing joins them
  (two islands, two networks that never meet, or the search ran out), the ghost is red and the tag
  says "No real track joins these points"; the click does nothing. A second click away from real
  track is an ordinary segment.
- **From the player's own track**: a first click on her own track end or node starts there as now,
  and the snapped route begins from the nearest real track, joined to her end like any segment.
