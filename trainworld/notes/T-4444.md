# T-4444 Track capacity updated by what an edit touched (agent capupdate, 2026-11-27)

Stage C2 of T-4267. Both capacity updates (weekdays, then the weekend copy) of a click's train work, by index.

## What changed (sim/src/track)

- `capacity/places.rs` (new): each resource listed by the edges it covers (sections' keys, crossings' two edges), the node
  it sits at, the lines that use it, and the line a following loss or wait is tagged with. Built on first use, kept by
  `update` the way B1's index is (`places_attach`, `places_drop` with the swap_remove rename).
- `Capacity::update`: the touched edges and nodes are lists as well as flags, so nothing goes over every edge, node or
  resource; the drop pass looks resources up by the hot edges and nodes (and the tagged ones of lines flagged or no longer
  running); the lines to rebuild come from the lists, not a scan of the edges; the re-solve for rescheduled lines goes through
  the line index. Kill order, build order and every figure are as before.
- `holds_with` (when only some lines are wanted) goes over those lines' resources in resource order, not every resource.
- `Service::update_scoped`'s "lines sharing a resource with a changed line" goes through the line index.
- `overtake::Plan::same_as` and, in `update`, a rebuilt overtake plan equal to one just dropped takes the old plan, so
  its worked-out outcomes are kept (exact: an outcome depends only on the plan and the two lines' rates). Matters where many
  lines sit on the hot edges and few of them moved (edge 1818's 150 lines); on 1482 none matched (0 of 12).

## Exactness

- Rust: all suites pass (834 + 1071); the existing random-edit test (`same_as_global`) compares `update` with a whole
  build, and `update` now also asserts in tests that the index equals a fresh one.
- Real NYC save (`update_on_the_real_save`, ignored; `T4444_TRACK`): 3 lines' paths touched (61 edges, 64 nodes) and their rates
  moved: the updated capacity (3,512 resources, users, utilisation, delays) and every line's holds equal a whole build to the
  bit, `holds_for` of the affected lines too. Figure shift: none (total rail trips and lines unchanged by construction).
  The update took 1.1 ms there.

## Asia, T-4441's driver (edge 1482, 4 clicks; ms per click, later clicks, node, a busy machine)

Timers inside `update` (temporary, not kept):

| part | ms (both updates) |
|---|---|
| ensure index, flags, lookup of what to drop, drop | about 0.4 |
| sections, nodes, crossings built again | about 2.3 |
| following losses built again | about 1.5 |
| **solve the new resources** | **30-36** |
| rescheduled re-solve | 0 |
| `update_scoped` as a whole (cap update 34-36, line profiles 14-16, rebuild lines 4) | about 55 (62 in T-4441) |

The loops over every edge, node and resource were about 6 of the 40 ms and are gone. The rest of `Capacity::update` is
`Plan::best`: 8 following losses solved, each over its plans' outcomes (24 relative phases, each a sweep of a cycle of trains
through every point), 44 `best` calls a click at about 0.8 ms; 35 of them are the update's and the rest retime's
`passing_waits`, which find them in the plans' memo. The 3 lines' plans change because the new stations sit inside the shared
stretch, so no exact index skips them. Weekday and weekend plans share half their plans, but a plan's memo is by rates and the
weekend's rates differ, so that saved nothing measurable (tried, not kept).

So the goal (both updates under 25 ms in T-4441's run) is not met by the index: 62 -> about 55. Getting under needs
`Plan::best` cheaper (T-4504).
