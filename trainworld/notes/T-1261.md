## What is out of step

`merge_cells.py` keeps each merged pack's res-9 version in `data/work/res9/<id>.json/.bin` and a
stamp of the merged pack it wrote (`<id>.merged.json`, the sha256 of its .json and .bin). A step that
changes a merged pack is meant to run on the res-9 pack (`--undo`, the step, merge again);
`everyday_rates`, `cross_everyday` and `nocar` instead write both copies and refresh the stamp.
T-1182 ran `routes.walk_stage` on the merged packs only, so their res-9 copies kept the old
`walk_part` and `walk_zone`. A join's solve starts with `merge_cells --undo`, which copies the res-9
pack back without looking at the stamp, so the join's re-merge rolls the walk tables back and the
re-gate parks every new pack (`other_packs_unchanged ... header, walk_part, walk_zone`, T-1238).

The audit (`t1261_probe.py audit`) over T-1182's 992 packs: all registered, all merged, all with a
res-9 copy; **991 have a stale stamp** (only seo is in step, repaired in T-1238). No other pack is
stale. The stamp is a reliable test of the fault.

## The repair

For each of the 991 packs, merge_cells' own order:

    world_run.py step merge_cells <id> --undo
    world_run.py step routes <id> --stage walk          (pack_walk on the res-9 pack)
    world_run.py step merge_cells <id>                  (copies the new res-9 pack to data/work/res9, merges, re-stamps)

as T-1238's `seowalk.cmd` did for seo, with the sim copy of a kit (`TW_SIM_DIR`) so `pack_walk` has
T-1164's water cost. Before: back up the 991 merged packs, their res-9 copies and stamps and their
market packs to `data/work/pre-T1261/` (about 4.4 GB: 1.07 merged, 3.02 res-9, 0.30 market), and
the registry and `market-rows.json`. Nothing else may write data/packs meanwhile (no join, no
China batch): it is a data batch of its own.

So most packs with water lose 0.1-1.4% of their merged cells; packs with no water split are
unchanged. The walk tables themselves come out slightly different from T-1182's (on the res-9 pack
more zones split by water: ven 17 against 10, nyc 176 against 121), which is the intended rule: the
build pipeline walks the res-9 pack.

Zones, zone sums and every per-cell weight's total are unchanged (merge sums cells within a zone
part). What reads cells, and so has to be redone after:

- **Market packs** (`<id>.market.json`): their sites are the pack's cells in 600 m squares, so a
  changed merge moves sites and weights a little. `register.py <ids> --market --trips` over the
  repaired ids, as a join's market step does: market packs, then market rows (every row, since
  nearly every place changed; `plan_rows` reaches all of them anyway), then local trips. A pack whose
  cells did not change writes an identical market pack.
- **Registry rows**: from that step (`market` rows, `localTrips`); `box` is unchanged.
- **Joint commutes and everyday unit files** (`data/work/*.joint_commutes.json`,
  `*.cross_everyday.json`) are keyed by 6 km squares and zones, not cells: unchanged.
- **Gates**: no full re-gate. merge_cells checks `read_pack` before it replaces a pack. Add a check
  script against the backup: per pack, the zones and every array's per-zone sums equal, only
  `walk_part`, `walk_zone`, the cells and the merge block differ; then `stale.py` 0, smoke, the real
  save's rail trips a day (13,014,633 at T-1238), and `pack_gate.py ven nyc --no-browser` as a sample.
- Then `zone_outlines`, `realms`, `zone_aliases` need nothing (outlines are not cells), and a
  `freeze.py` for publishing.

## Time at 8 cores

T-1182's walk stage on the merged packs took 3.12 worker-hours (log `t1182-walk.log`, 992 packs,
median 7 s, longest mic 118-145 s). On the res-9 pack the walk search costs about the same (seo
14.4 s against 16 s), plus undo and merge (seo 2 s) and three process starts a pack: about 4
worker-hours, **about 35 minutes on 8 workers**. The market step runs in one process under the
registry lock: T-1238's join made 24 market packs, every touched row and the local trips in 9
minutes, so 991 market packs at a few seconds each is **about an hour**. Checks 15 minutes. **About
2 hours in all**, one evening, after the China batch.

## A runner change so it cannot happen again

1. **`merge_cells.undo` refuses a stale stamp** (the merged pack was changed after merging, so the
   res-9 copy lacks that change), as `res9_copy` already does for a merge: exit naming the pack and
   the fix (its stage rerun on the res-9 pack), unless `--force`. And `world_run`'s `solve` checks
   every member's stamp before the solve and raises a RunFault naming the stale packs: the run stops
   and parks nothing, instead of parking every new pack of a batch for a fault of an old one.
2. **Pack-rewriting steps refuse a merged pack.** `routes.write_arrays` (every routes stage writes
   through it, walk included) exits when the header holds `merged`, with the order to use, as
   `coarse_zones` does; the same in the enrichments (`enrich_all`). T-1182's script imported
   `routes.walk_stage` directly, so the guard belongs in the function, not the command line.
3. **One command for a stage rerun on finished packs**: `merge_cells.py <ids> --through "<module>
   {id} <args>" [--workers N]`, which per id does undo, the step, merge, and stops at the first
   failure leaving that pack's res-9 version in data/packs. The T-1261 repair is then
   `merge_cells.py <991 ids> --through "routes {id} --stage walk" --workers 8`.

Item 1 alone would have turned T-1238's parked batch into a stop with the cause named; items 2 and 3
keep a rerun from making the fault at all. The audit (stamp in step) is worth running before any
join that touches packs built before 2026-10-25.
