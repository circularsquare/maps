## The mechanism

All on the page (`demandClient.ts`, `firm`); the worker is unchanged, since a worker never sees a
newer network while it should go on.

- A solve sent without the barrier (after an edit, `Solve.edit`) is firm from when it is sent until
  its final is shown. Meanwhile `flush` holds any newer network (`waiting`): an edit, a zone opened
  or at another level, a rough solve for the zone in view. The final's `publish` flushes it, and the
  workers, whose populations now hold that final's network solved, solve the touched zones locally
  on top (T-1733) and then whole.
- **The gap.** From the local result shown until `whole` is sent (`EDIT_SETTLE_MS`, 8 s) a network
  that arrives goes at once, as before: a line built right after its stations is solved locally too.
  A network held since before the gap (`heldFrom` before `localShownAt`) waits for the final instead:
  on all of Japan the local solve takes about 2 minutes, so a player building every minute always
  has one held, and letting it in would restart the local solve for ever, never starting a whole one.
- A worker replaced meanwhile (`reopened`) still sends at once (`flush(true)`): the firm solve cannot
  end without it. A solve with the barrier (a load, a zone just opened) is not firm.
- Ordering: every worker finishes the firm solve before it gets the next network, so no local result
  newer than a final can be shown before that final; the shown view's version never goes back.
- `?perfOff=firm` restores the old behaviour (a newer network cancels the running solve).

## What a player sees

Building steadily, a whole solve lands about every local solve plus whole solve. An edit made while
one runs shows its figures (the T-1733 local result) only once that solve has landed and the local
solve on top of it is done: up to about 14 minutes on all of Japan, about a minute on China, where
before a newer edit cancelled the running solve and so, building every minute on Japan, nothing past
the first edit ever showed. The final that lands is for the network it started on: lines built since
show "constructing..." until the local result on top, as they did before it landed.

## A local solve in between the whole solve's rounds (asked 2026-10-29, not built)

The orchestrator asked whether a held network's local solve could run inside the whole solve (pause
it, solve the touched zones on the newest network, show, resume). What it would need, and why it
is not done here:

- **The touched populations' state.** Mid-solve they hold the whole solve's network and its rounds
  so far. Putting them on the newest network (`set_network`) drops that, so the whole solve could
  not resume. It needs either a Rust stash/restore of a population's state (a new WASM function,
  plus `set_network` back and forth, seconds each for Tokyo), or a second copy of the touched
  populations (on all of Japan each worker already holds about 1.25 GB of WASM, and Tokyo's two
  populations would add hundreds of MB).
- **The rest's state.** The untouched populations are mid-solve as well: their trail and loads are
  the running solve's partial rounds, not the last landing's. The local solve would need the last
  landing's trail kept apart (about 10 MB a worker), and its figures (`figures` reads every
  population's loads, summaries, purpose parts, crush, fares and transfers) would have to be the
  landed result's with the touched populations' landed share taken out and their new one put in.
  That is new bookkeeping for each figure kind.
- **The time doesn't add up on all of Japan while building every minute.** The local solve alone
  takes about 2 minutes on the slowest worker (T-1733). Each pause makes the whole solve longer by
  that, and the whole solve has about 11.5 minutes of its own work. Landing within 20 minutes leaves
  about 8 minutes of pauses, so 3-4 pauses per whole solve, about one every 5 minutes. An edit then
  waits on average about 2.5 minutes for its pause plus 2 for the solve, about 4.5 minutes, and up to
  about 7. Both targets hold only for edits about 5 minutes apart or more.
