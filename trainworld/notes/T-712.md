# T-712 and T-721: the regional market's city pairs against published figures

2026-10-19. Measured only, nothing in the app or sim changed (Anita: measure and discuss; demand
changes go to her). Scripts and outputs in `../artifacts/trainworld/t712/`.

## For Anita

**How it was measured.** The regional layer alone, on a copy of the sim as it is now (T-717's
2 minute change cost, T-718's station-pair choice, `rel_soft` on), every market pack open, a
weekday with three crowding rounds. For each pair a good line was built as a test line, the way
T-717 did: real stations, real timings, a sensible frequency (the table says which). Japan ran on
its real network (T-737) plus a Nozomi; New York on its real network plus a Northeast Corridor
line; London to Paris both bare and with both cities' real networks. Every other city has a lone
intercity line and no local network, so its riders reach the station on foot or by car.

**What the game gets right.** The number of trips between big pairs is roughly right where the
published figure is comparable: Tokyo to Osaka has 137k rail and air trips a weekday against
153k a day published, Tokyo to Fukuoka 36k against 35k. Between the city centres, rail's share of
rail and air is also right: the busiest centre-to-centre zone pairs ride 76-98% by rail, as on the
real lines.

**What it gets wrong.**
1. **The plane takes too much.** Over whole corridors, rail's share of rail plus air is 34-64%
   where 78-85% is published (Madrid to Barcelona 50% against 78%, Milan to Rome 34% against 80%,
   Tokyo to Osaka 64% against 85%, London to Paris 36-50% against 70-80%, New York to Washington
   30% against 79%). Rail wins the centres and loses the suburbs, because getting to the station
   costs rail a lot (walks counted 1.5 a minute; rides 15 minutes plus the drive counted 1.33 plus
   a drop-off), while getting to the airport costs the plane only the plain drive minutes and a
   flat 60 minutes at the airports.
2. **The car also takes too much rail's way at 250-400 km.** Berlin to Hamburg 30% by rail against
   46%; Tokyo to Nagoya about 23k rail trips a weekday against 76k a day published. The same cause:
   the lone station's suburbs.
3. **Totals are mixed.** Japan right; Berlin to Hamburg 1.4 times the published; New York to
   Washington about right between the cores; London to Paris a third to a half of the real (the
   0.26 border factor); New York to Philadelphia 2.6 times high between the cores and far more over
   the whole corridor, which counts the short trips across the NJ-PA line.
4. **The car hardly flies against the plane at 500 km.** Madrid to Barcelona: 1% by car, against
   about a fifth at that distance in Japan's survey. A side effect of the cheap plane.

**T-721.** After T-718 the long pairs sit too low against the published shares (Tokyo to Osaka
44% of all trips on T-717's Tokyo-only network, 54% on the whole Japanese one, against about 64%
of all trips and 85% of rail and air). New York to Philadelphia's 0.4% looked alarming but is a
denominator effect: rail on that test line carries 1.5-1.9k trips a weekday and 4.3k on a fuller
Northeast Corridor line, against about 4k a day published. The shortfall is the plane's cost and
the station's reach, not T-718's station-pair rule; I would not undo T-718.

**Ideas, best first** (more in "Ideas" below):
1. Price the airport like a station: put the plane's terminal time back to 90 minutes (T-378's
   design value; it was lowered to 60 to sit between two studies) or give the airport drive the
   ride's weights. One constant, no pack rebuild. Measured at 90: rail of rail and air goes up 8-25
   points on every pair (Madrid 50 to 58%, Milan 34 to 44%, Tokyo-Osaka 54 to 70%, London-Paris 36
   to 61%, New York-Washington 30 to 55%) and the car gains too.
2. Leave the suburbs to the player, and check the access list. With London's and Paris's own
   networks, rail on London-Paris rises from 18% to 27% of all trips: feeders matter, which is the
   game working. One thing to check: on New York's real network the NEC line's longer trips to
   Philadelphia fall (6.7k bare, 4.3k with the network), likely because a zone's rides keep only
   its 6 cheapest lines, and subway lines push the intercity station out.
3. Leave damping to T-528. A softer damping (`damp_min` 180) helps rail against the plane (Madrid
   to 64%, Milan to 50%) but hurts it against the car in the Northeast; it moves every layer, so it
   belongs in T-528's fit, not here.

## Detail

### Why the corridor share is low when the centre's is right

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Milan - Rome | 580 | 408 | 90% | 34% |

| London - Paris | 425 | 372 | 82% | 36% |
| New York - Washington | 418 | 381 | 75% | 30% |
| Berlin - Hamburg | 322 | 295 | 76% | (no plane) |
| New York - Philadelphia | 179 | 214 | 11% | (no plane) |
| New York - Boston | 377 | 440 | 7% | 13% |

In the centres rail holds 75-92%, the published level. Away from the one station, a zone pays a
ride (15 minutes, the drive counted 1.33, a 10 minute drop-off) at each end, or is not served at all
(Berlin to Hamburg: 11.4k of the cores' 15.6k trips are served). The plane's access is the drive to
the airport in plain minutes plus 60 at the airports (`air_min`), so the plane loses nothing in the
suburbs. New York to Boston and to Philadelphia lose even in the centres: rail's fare ($1.50 + 10
cents a km, 3 minutes a dollar: 113 perceived minutes to Boston) against a car with no tolls.

### Ideas

Ranked by how much they would help over how simple they are. None built.

1. **The airport priced like a station** (help: large, every pair with a plane; simple: one
   constant in `intercity.rs`, no pack rebuild). Either `AIR_TERMINAL_MIN` back to 90 (T-378's
   design row: 99/94/80/47% of rail + air at 2/3/4/5 hours on ideal access) or the airport drive
   counted as a ride is (15 + 1.33 x the drive + the drop-off at each end), which is more
   consistent and moves a little more. Trade-offs: the car gains on far pairs (London to Paris
   becomes two thirds car, and the Channel has no price); far rail pairs gain a little (Tokyo to
   Fukuoka is 3% by rail now against 11% published; not measured with the change). Needs a
   DECISIONS line reversing T-378's 60.
2. **Check the access list on dense networks** (help: unknown, maybe large for big cities;
   simple: a look first). On New York's network the NEC line's longer trips fell by a third
   against the bare case. If the cap of 6 lines for rides (and 8 stations) pushes the intercity
   station out of a zone's options, always keeping each zone's best intercity station would fix
   it. This is a correctness check, not a new factor.
3. **Longer reach for rides on long trips** (help: medium, the suburbs of lone stations; simple:
   moderate). Rides reach 15 km; on a 300 km trip people drive 30-40 km to a station. A reach that
   grows with the trip's length would serve Berlin's and Hamburg's outer zones (a quarter of the
   cores' trips are unserved now). Trade-off: a lone station draws from further, so feeders matter
   a little less; it is a new rule.
4. **Damping in T-528** (help: medium; simple: a fit already planned). Measured here to cut both
   ways, so it should be fitted on corridor counts as T-528 plans, with this note's table as
   the targets.
5. **Open borders** (help: large for London-Paris, Brussels and the like; simple: a constant per
   group of countries, the kind of factor to think hard about). The 0.26 border factor puts London
   to Paris at a third to a half of its real traffic. A higher factor where travel is free (the EU and
   UK, say) would fix the busiest European pairs. Trade-off: a constant per country group, and
   the market's rows would need rebuilding.
6. **Coach** (help: small, 4-10% of the published trips; simple: no, a new mode). Leaving it out
   makes rail's share of all trips read a little high, not low. Not worth it now.
7. **Real flight passenger counts (T-738)** (help: small given 1; simple: no, per pair data). The
   plane's share is already close at the centres; the gap is access, which counts would not fix.

Not ideas: undoing T-718 (the station-pair rule is right in the centres, and the old levels came
from counting stations); a rail constant for long trips (would lift rail against the car and the
plane alike, where only the plane is clearly wrong).

### What was not checked

Paris to Lyon, Rome to Naples and the other Japanese pairs have no published figure here. The
game's other European packs were being built during the runs (the market's day moved 32.67M to
33.02M trips between runs); no pair in the table moved. Rail fares are the test's $1.50 + 10 cents
a km everywhere.
