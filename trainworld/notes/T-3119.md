# T-3119: where else a margin would help

Anita's idea (2026-11-10): the single-track margin stands for real trains being inconsistent and not slotting in perfectly. Are there other spots that want a bit of margin? No code changed. Everything below is a proposal for her.

## What the game holds today

## The bigger point

Every figure above is a minimum with no buffer. Real planners leave some. UIC 406 compresses a timetable to minimum headways and then caps occupancy: secondary sources give 75% in the peak hour for mixed and high-speed lines, 85% for dedicated suburban lines ([Aalborg paper](https://journals.aau.dk/index.php/td/article/download/5289/4646/16952); I could not open the UIC code itself). Toronto reaches 83% of its theoretical figure.

So one option is a single cap (say 85%) on how full a spot may be scheduled, rather than a margin per spot. Her idea of per-spot margins is more visible to the player, because each tooltip's number moves. I would keep per-spot margins and use 85% as the check on each: the default result should land at or under about 85% of what the best real system manages.

## Proposals

Real figures here are the best I found, but several are weak sources, and the junction and tram numbers are my judgment, not found anywhere.

1. **Terminal turn floor 200 s to 240 s.** Today a terminal pair can turn 36/h if the player sets a 2-minute turnaround, above Toronto's theoretical 30 and the Victoria line's 28.5 reported at Brixton. With a 240 s floor a terminal pair stops at 30/h and no turnaround setting beats it. The default 3 minutes is unchanged (still 15 and 30/h). One constant, `POOLED_TURN_MIN_S`.
2. **Junction and flat-crossing move 90 s to 120 s** (40/h to 30/h per conflict). A flat junction holds the route until the first train clears, then the next route is set and signalled; 90 s is just the gap on a metro with moving-block signals. 120 s also leaves room for a late train. `CONFLICT_S`. Tram's own 30 s: see 4.
3. **Platform margin 60 s to 75 s** (40/h to 34/h at default dwell). The game's 40/h is above the Victoria line's world record of 36. 34/h sits just under it. Low priority; the section rule binds first on most track anyway.
4. **Tram: section 40 s to 60 s, platform margin 30 s to 60 s, tram conflict 30 s to 45 s.** Tram is the one place the game is much looser than life. A tram section allows 53/h, a platform 80/h and a tram crossing 120/h. Real street running lines run a tram every 2-3 minutes, 20-30/h, and a stop on shared street track is held up by traffic and signals. New numbers: section 41/h at 60 km/h, platform 48/h, crossing 80/h. I did not find a published design figure for these, so the numbers are a judgment.
5. **Keep as is:** the double-track section (the game's 25/h at 160 km/h is already above the ordinary 3-minute mainline headway, 20/h, but below ETCS capacity; and 16.5/h at 320 km/h is under the 20/h of a 3-minute high-speed headway; both from memory, unsourced), two-way single track at 240 s (just raised), merges (already as strict as a junction), passing-track platforms and passing-train holds (never binding), the 200 s floor on non-terminal turns (15/h is already far under what a real through-station turn does).

## Where the game is much looser than life

- **Trams, everywhere** (item 4).
- **Terminals with a short turnaround**: 36/h from a 2-minute setting (item 1).
- **A spot filled to 100%**: nothing in the game stops a schedule from using every second of a resource. Real planning stops at 60-85%.
