# T-4542: Double up's price held the game seconds per run

Agent doubleup, 11-28; its first session stopped at the account's spend limit after the Asia run, and a second
session finished it (the NYC run, the read's worker times, the doc wording).

From T-4541 (notes/T-4541.md): on her 3,090-station save each run's Double up price on the selection bar took 0.2-4.8 s
of the clock worker, asked whenever a pick holds double track and again after every edit, and edits, previews and save
switches wait behind it in the worker's one queue. T-4541 cut that to one question in flight at a time; this makes the
question itself cheap.

## Where the time went (from the code)

`preview_double_up(edge)` (sim/src/track/wasm/crossings.rs) is one question per run. Two costs in it grew with the network:

- **Finding the run.** `double_run` asks `twin_at` of every piece of the run ("has this a pair beside it already?"),
  and `twin_at` (wasm/draw.rs) projected the point onto every edge of the network. A run of P pieces on a network of
  E edges was P x E projections: tens of thousands of edges and runs of 50-200 pieces is seconds, which matches the
  0.2-4.8 s spread (long runs slow, short ones not).
- **Pricing it.** `double_up_op` tried the pair with a trial edit per side tried (whole, or trimmed short of a curve
  too tight for the pair), then `preview_double_up` ran the chosen op through one more trial for its price: 2-3
  trials. Each trial sums the whole network's price twice (`total_and_built_cost`), applies the op with every check
  and derives it, then applies and derives the inverse.

Neither is new (Double up is from 10-28); a big drag box on a save this size is.

## Fix

- `twin_at` takes its candidates from the spatial grid (`grid.in_box`, the point's box padded by its reach; every edge
  only when the box spans more cells than the grid holds), in id order as before, so its answer is the same. Every
  live edge is in the grid (derive puts it there; the crossing finder relies on it). This also speeds the Double up
  click itself and a station placed on quad track (`station_beside`). Test: `twin_from_the_grid_is_the_scan_s`.
- `preview_double_up` makes no trial edit. `double_up_try` walks the edits in the order `double_up_op` tries them
  (whole run on the left, whole on the right, trimmed left, trimmed right); the edit takes the first its trial
  passes, the price the first. `Network::pair_price` then fits each piece of that pair and prices it as derive prices
  new track (the sampling of water, terrain and roads now one function, `ground_along`, for both), plus each flat
  crossing on the run's pieces once more. No whole-network sums, no checks, no rollback.
- The app is unchanged but for comments: the bar still asks one run at a time after the pick rests 300 ms
  (T-4541), so an edit waits behind at most one question.

## The lie (DECISIONS.md, Track, 2026-11-28)

Exact where the edit's checks pass on the side tried first and the pair crosses no track at grade (the two Double up
tests pin it at 1e-6, as before). Otherwise:

- A run whose pair the edit's checks refuse on the left (crossing other track, water) is laid on the right; the
  price is the left's. One refused on both sides is priced, and the button is on: the click then says
  "{done} of {total} runs doubled up. {why}" (or "This track cannot be doubled up." when none went in). Before, the
  hover named the refusal and greyed the button when every run was refused that way. Refusals found before the checks
  (already quad, single track, a crossover, a curve too tight at the picked piece) are still named and still grey it.
- Flat crossings: the pair is charged the run's at-grade crossings once more, and none of its own.

A first version priced the pair as the track it runs beside (each piece's own stored price, no fitting): on the NYC
game run below it was 2.3% high ($509M shown, $498M added), because a shoreline met at a slant reaches the pair, 8 m
over, some metres earlier or later and water is priced far above land (pieces 2-4% apart at the same length). Ruled
out: that shows at the hover's three figures, and her Asia network crosses many rivers.

## Where the left time goes (from the code)

Every remaining step of a question is the run's own size, not the network's: `double_run` (a grid look-up a piece),
`double_side` once or twice a side (`editable`, `express_verts`, `fit_in` a piece; `loose_near` scans the nodes at
the two run ends only), and `pair_price` (a fit and the water, terrain and road sampling a piece, the same work derive
does for any new piece; one pass over the crossings list). A run of hundreds of km in hundreds of pieces is the slow
case.
