# T-1005: zones that wrap another zone (design, 2026-10-23)

Anita (2026-10-23): "There's another weird region in Pinghu. We should probably join this with
Jiaxing or something, because they seem very integrated. Not sure how we'd make this general."
The same day she asked for Taiwan's Zhubei zone (zht) to be split (T-1002).

## Summary

**Why it happens.** When the planner (`world_plan.zone_groups`) grows a zone up to the floor, it
joins the *smallest* neighbour that keeps the two under the cap, with no look at shape. Jiaxing
prefecture went: Haiyan joined Xiuzhou District (the smaller neighbour), Jiashan joined Nanhu
District, then Pinghu joined Xiuzhou + Haiyan (1.11M, smaller than Nanhu + Jiashan's 1.38M). That
made Pinghu (pig) a horseshoe round Nanhu, and split Jiaxing's two city districts between two zones.

**The test.** Zone A wraps zone B when **at least half of B's area lies inside A's convex hull, and
A holds at least half of B's border with other zones** (coast and dead space don't count). Pinghu
around Jiaxing scores 0.79 and 0.68. Run over all 1,516 registered outlines it finds 124 pairs. A
compactness test alone (area over hull area) mostly finds islands and coasts (63 zones under 0.5,
from Okinawa to the Cyclades; only 6 of them wrap anything), so I use compactness only to choose
between splits.

**The rule I propose** (three parts):

1. **New plans (no rebuild cost).** `zone_groups` keeps "smallest neighbour first" but skips a
   neighbour if joining it would make a zone that wraps a third zone, taking the next one. Replayed
   over China, zone-zone wraps drop from about 45 to 2 (the rest wrap a metro, which is left alone).
   This applies to the US plan (T-1004) and to T-903's held European merge batch.
2. **Built countryside zones.** If A wraps B, both are countryside zones in one country, and both
   fit under the cap together (China and Taiwan 2.5M, elsewhere 1.5M), merge them. If they don't fit,
   re-split their units into two zones with the most compact pair of outlines (each 1M-2.5M in
   China). If no split is clearly better (fill gains under 0.05), leave them.
3. **Leave alone:** a zone wrapping a metro row (Prague inside Central Bohemia, Dublin, Xining in
   its ring of counties, and the county rings round about 20 Chinese metros); a metro wrapping a
   zone (Southend inside London's row, Beibei in Chongqing); and pairs across a national border. A
   ring of countryside round a city is a normal shape. The rule never grows a metro.

**What it gives.**
- **Pinghu and Jiaxing:** Nanhu + Xiuzhou + Haiyan (Jiaxing's city whole plus Haiyan, 1.82M) and
  Pinghu + Jiashan (1.45M). It keeps Jiaxing's two city districts together, which matches her "join
  with Jiaxing". A straight merge would be 3.29M, over the cap.

- **China, built zones:** 29 pairs re-split, 3 groups of three regrouped (Panzhihua/Xichang,
  Kashgar/Yarkant, Korla/Kuqa), 2 merges (Manzhouli + Yakeshi 2.38M, Lianyungang + Guanyun 2.47M), 8
  pairs left as they are because no split is better. Tibet's Lhasa/Nagqu pair has 74 units; that one

  Brugge with Kortrijk, Neuchâtel with Lausanne, Sorachi with Kamikawa (Hokkaido). Plus 2 re-splits:

## Details

### 1. The test

Per pair of touching zones (borders within 1 km), on outlines from `data/packs/<id>.boundary.geojson`,
each pair measured in a Lambert equal-area projection centred on A:

- `in hull`: share of B's area inside A's convex hull;
- `border`: B's border shared with A, over B's border shared with all registered zones. This is the
  "land" border, so a coastal B is not penalised. A B on the edge of covered land (dead space,
  another country) gets a higher share than it should, which is why both tests must pass.

Also measured but not used in the rule: `fill`, the share of A's hull-minus-A taken up by B. It tells a
horseshoe round B (Pinghu 0.55) from a chain or ring round several things (zht round Taipei 0.10).
It turned out not to be needed, because chains show up as a zone that wraps more than one neighbour.
Compactness (area / hull area) under 0.5 flags 63 zones, nearly all islands and coasts (Okinawa
0.02, Azores 0.05, Dodecanese 0.07, Magong 0.08, Kagoshima 0.13, Zhuhai 0.14, Livorno 0.16, Athens
0.20, Copenhagen 0.26, Bremen 0.28); only 6 of them wrap a zone.

Numbers: 124 pairs at 0.5/0.5 (79 in China and Taiwan); 70 at 0.6/0.6; 179 at 0.5/0.4.

### 2. Why the planner makes them, and the guard

`zone_groups` takes a zone under the floor and joins it to "its smallest neighbour that keeps the two
within the cap". Merge log for Jiaxing (`world_plan.json` `zones.CHN.merges`): "Haiyan County
(501,095) joins Xiuzhou District (smallest neighbour, 608,304)", "Jiashan County (670,843) joins
Nanhu District (smallest neighbour, 709,813)", "Pinghu City (777,722) joins Xiuzhou District
(smallest neighbour, 1,109,399)".

I replayed China's and Taiwan's grouping from the geoBoundaries units (`CHN_ADM3`, `TWN_ADM2`, GHS-POP
2025 people per unit; read-only), with four ways of choosing the neighbour
(`notes/t1005/sim.py`, `simeval.py`):

| Neighbour choice | Zones | Same as the plan | Over 2.5M | Wrap pairs | Of them zone-zone |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| smallest (as now; the replay) | 658 | 634 | 10 | 64 | about 45 |
| smallest, skipping one that makes a wrap ("guard") | 648 | 339 | 8 | 19 | 2 |
| longest shared border | 560 | 91 | 44 | 26 | 10 |
| most compact result | 558 | 87 | 28 | 27 | 14 |

The replay matches 634 of 658 plan rows (Taiwan all 5), so it is close enough to judge by. The
guard keeps zone sizes as they are and removes nearly every zone-zone wrap; the other two make
zones bigger and break the cap far more often. The guard's leftovers wrap metros, which it doesn't
look at (metro units are not in the pool). It changes about half of China's rows, because each
different choice changes later ones. So as a retrofit it would be a full China rebuild, and only
part 2's local fix is worth doing on built rows.

In code: in `zone_groups`, the `elif border:` branch sorts `fit` by (people, -border) and takes the
first neighbour whose union with the zone does not wrap any other current zone by the test above,
else the first. China's replay took 33 s with the guard.

### 5. Cost in detail

- **Rows to build:** 75 in China and Taiwan (about 72 packs after; China's rows ran 1.3-2 minutes
  each at 4 cores, so 2-4 hours with reads and gates); about 56 in Europe and Japan (about 30 packs).
  Each new row needs its caches cleared first (T-891) and a name look (`zone-names.json`).
- **Joins:** every solve region a changed row is in: 22 Chinese regions plus Taiwan (T-897's joins
  ran 0.12-0.4 minutes a pack, so most of a China re-join, several hours at 6 cores), and DE, PL, GB,
  AT, SK, BA, MK, BE, CH, RO and the Japan group. Then the market rows, `zone_outlines`, `zone_aliases`,
  realms, as any join.
- **Batching:** T-1000's fix reruns each country's join with no pack rebuild; T-903's European
  merges rebuild Italian, French, Greek and Portuguese rows. One batch for all three runs each join
  once. Planned for after China and the US.
- **Wave 4 option:** 26 of the 75 rows are wave 4's, gated and not joined (the join waits on T-998).
  Redoing them first costs about an hour of builds and saves a later rejoin of Xinjiang, Gansu, Inner
  Mongolia, Heilongjiang, Jilin, Liaoning and Tibet.
- **The planner guard** (part 1) is a change to `world_plan.zone_groups` only. A replan regroups only
  planned rows, so it changes no built row; it should be in before T-1004 plans the US and before
  T-903's batch is re-simulated (its groups were made with the smallest-neighbour rule).
