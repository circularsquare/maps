# T-314: the commute curve for a city with no flows (pipeline stage 3)

**Rule** (`tier0.borrowed_curve`, a city config's `"curve": "borrowed"`): of the fitted cities'
curves, the one that misplaces the fewest commuters beyond each city's own, averaged by country.
It reads `pipeline/fitted_curves.json` (each fitted city's own curve and its misplaced share under
every candidate), written by `notes/t314_loo.py table`. Today it picks **d^-1.8 exp(-d/80 km)**
(2.2 points over each city's own, by country), within about 0.1 point of d^-1.8 exp(-d/50),
Mumbai's d^-1.65 exp(-d/32.7) and d^-1.6 exp(-d/32): the surface is flat there.

## The test (`notes/t314_loo.py`)

59 packs with their own fitted curve: the nine US packs (one curve fitted on all nine, T-198),
Toronto, London, Paris, Berlin, Mumbai, Sao Paulo, the seven Japanese metros and the 38 Japanese
zones (New Haven left out: one county, every curve scores the same). Seoul and Shanghai fit only a
length to a published mean, so they are not in it. Each city is scored the same way: its shipped
pack's 2 km zones and commute margins, the game's gravity (US packs with their border factor), and
the share of commuters misplaced against its own observed table (municipality or district pairs;
county pairs for the US from LODES; Berlin's published Gemeinde pairs; Mumbai's and Sao Paulo's
distance bands, the table they were fitted to). "Excess" is a candidate's misplaced share minus
the city's own curve's, in points.

Candidates, each built from the other cities only: nearest by metro population and vehicles per
1,000 people (log scales, standardised; WHO's registered vehicles 2016, GHO RS_194, over the World
Bank's population); nearest by population alone; the median curve of the city's World Bank income
group (by country); the world median curve; the world best (the fitted curve with the least excess
over the others, by country); and one scaled to size (world median power, the length set so the
model's mean trip is the others' median share of the pack's own mean home-to-job distance).

**Leave one city out** (as specified): only Japan differs, since every other country has one
fitted curve (the US's nine share one). Nearest by population and vehicles then wins (+1.7 cities,
+2.0 by country, worst New York +14.0) against the world best's +2.1 and +3.0, because a Japanese
city borrows a Japanese one. Nearest by population alone: +5.2 and +9.3. Full lines in
`artifacts/trainworld/T-314-loo.json`.

**Why the world best.** In the pipeline's case it loses least on both averages and ties the
smallest worst case, needs no input at all, and does not hinge on which countries happen to be in
the table: nearest by population and vehicles sends every Japanese city to a small US pack's flat
curve once Japan is out (Gunma +24.8), and, with Japan's 45 packs in the table, would send most
small rich-country cities to a Japanese zone and most poor ones to Mumbai. The nearest rule does
better for Sao Paulo (Mumbai's curve, +0.6) and Toronto (+0.1), the world best better for London,
Paris, Mumbai and Japan. Vehicles per 1,000 is also a poor global input: WHO counts motorcycles
(Indonesia 485, near Brazil's 462) and its data are CC BY-NC-SA. The US is the outlier under every
rule (+4 to +14): no other fitted country has its flat d^-0.8 curve, so a car-sprawl city outside
the US is the case the table cannot answer yet.

## Road factor (`t314_loo.py road`, Mumbai)

The question applies only to a table of reported (travel) distances, so only Mumbai's B-28
(Sao Paulo's survey is straight-line); the leave-one-out test cannot settle it, since no city has
both a reported-distance table and flows. Instead, the pack's routed drive factors
(`data/work/mum.drive_near_f.npy` for near zone pairs, the supercell table beyond) weighted by the
model's commuters:

- Near zone pairs' unweighted median 1.62 (`routes.py`'s log line): 1.94 at 0-2.5 km, 1.55 at
  13-18 km. Weighted by commuters: 1.37 overall; 1.47 under 2.5 km, 1.32-1.30 from 5 to 30 km.
- At B-28's reported edges: 1.45 (1.5 km), 1.37 (5.5), 1.32 (10.5), 1.30 (20.5), 1.37 (30.5, 50.5).

Both parameters refitted with the 0-1 and 2-5 km bands merged (at a factor over 1.44 the 1.5 km
edge falls under the 1.04 km intrazonal distance and that band empties):

| Road factor | Curve | KL | Commuters moved against 1.3 | Model mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.3 | d^-1.5 exp(-d/23.3) | 0.0077 | | 9.9 km |
| measured at each edge | d^-1.45 exp(-d/18.5) | 0.0118 | 1.6% | 9.6 km |
| near-pair median 1.62 | d^-1.35 exp(-d/9.2) | 0.0023 | 8.6% | 8.2 km |

**Kept 1.3.** The commuters' own factor is 1.3-1.45 and gives nearly the same curve; 1.62 is the
median over all near pairs, inflated by sparse outskirts across creeks and hills, not the
commuters'. Its lower KL says only that the table's shape bends more easily into the curve family
at that factor. Using a pack's measured factor would also need a refit after `routes.py`, which
runs after the build. With the bands merged, Mumbai's own fit moves from the shipped d^-1.65
exp(-d/32.7) to d^-1.5 exp(-d/23.3) (T-546).

## Lagos

The rule picks d^-1.8 exp(-d/80 km) for Lagos (nearest by population and vehicles would have kept
Mumbai's d^-1.65 exp(-d/32.7), which is third under the rule, 0.1 point behind). The config now
says `"curve": "borrowed"`; `build_city.add_gravity` and `tier0.main` resolve it. The shipped pack
was not re-solved: the permission check stopped `merge_cells.py lag --undo` (a shared pack), so the
re-solve and its checks are T-545.
