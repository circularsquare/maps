# T-1854 Finer and hexagonal terrain cells

Anita, 2026-10-30: the hill cells are too big to be useful and valleys don't show; could we download
higher-resolution hills data locally wherever we build, what would that cost, and could the cells be
hexagons. Written from the code (pipeline/zone_terrain.py, sim/src/track/cost.rs, app/src/map/terrainTint.ts)
and T-1270.md. Sizes marked "guess" are not measured.

## 1. What there is now, and why valleys vanish

- Source: GEDTM30 at 240 m (pixels 1/450 degree). Cells: a world lattice of 1/240 degree, so 463 m
  north-south and about 350 m east-west at 40 N (230 m at 60 N). That is only 2 x 2 source pixels a cell.
- A cell's class is the mean slope over it (flat under 2 degrees, rolling 2-5, hilly 5-10, mountain 10+),
  then a 3 x 3 majority filter. The filter is the main culprit: it exists so the price doesn't flicker,
  and it erases anything narrower than about 1.4 km. A valley a kilometre wide is smoothed into the
  hills around it. The 240 m source blurs it a second time. The hillshade (Terrain Tiles, z12, 30-40 m)
  does show valleys; only the tint and the price don't.
- Shipped: one `data/terrain/<zone>.terrain` per zone (run-length classes), 58 MB for 1,770 zones, median
  13 KB, largest 1.9 MB. Fetched with the water mask. The clock worker installs it into the sim; the sim
  expands it to one byte a cell and samples the class every 100 m along each edge for the price. The tint
  draws the same grids, so what you see is what is charged.

## 2. Finer cells for everyone (recommended)

- Source: **Copernicus GLO-30** (30 m, AWS open data, no key, free for commercial use with attribution).
  It is a surface model, so towers and forests add relief; GEDTM30's own 30 m version is bare earth but
  I have not checked where it can be fetched (Zenodo gave 0.6-3 MB/s last time). SRTM is not an option
  (stops at 60 N, voids). Licence is fine either way: the classes are a separate file, not in the ODbL
  packs, so share-alike does not reach them.
- Cell size: 1/480 degree (about 230 m) is 4 times the cells; 1/960 (about 115 m) is 16 times. 115 m
  shows valleys down to roughly 400 m wide. I'd take 115 m and thin the majority filter (a plain 3 x 3 at
  115 m only smooths 350 m).
- File size (guess, runs get shorter on rough ground): 2x finer about 110-220 MB in all, 4x finer
  about 250-800 MB; the largest zone (cea) 8-30 MB. Fine on R2, heavy in `dist/`. Fix: compress (brotli)
  and split zones over about 5,000 km² into chunks.
- Build: the cost is the download. The tiles under the zones are hundreds of GB (guess); from home that
  is days, so run the build on EC2 in the same region as the bucket (docs/aws.md allows long data
  builds, tools/aws/ only), a few core-hours of slope maths, then pull only the small output. About a
  day of agent work to write plus one overnight build.
- **Recalibrate.** Slope measured at 30 m is steeper than at 240 m, so the 2 / 5 / 10 degree thresholds
  would reclassify the world (more hilly, more expensive). Compute slope at 30 m, then set the thresholds
  so class shares match today's (52 / 16 / 13 / 20) or re-check the probes (Pittsburgh, Seoul, Bergen).
  Prices in loaded saves will move a little, as they did for stage 2.
- **Memory.** The sim expands a grid to a byte per cell. At 115 m the largest zone is about 60 M cells
  (60 MB in WASM, against 5 MB today), and several load at once. Needs row-run lookups (binary search per
  row, no expansion) or chunked grids. This is the main engineering item and is where `memoryCheck.ts`
  would bite. The tint also needs a higher MIN_ZOOM (about 9) at 115 m.
- Valleys get cheaper to follow, which is good play: a line along a flat valley floor beats one over
  the ridge.

## 3. Fetch finer data where the player builds

- "On demand" is already how the per-zone file works (fetched when the view is within 20 km). The real
  question is where the finer classes come from. Two readings:
  - Our own class tiles on R2, cut once offline as in section 2 and versioned by URL. Same data for
    every player, saves stay deterministic. This is section 2, just stored in chunks. Cheap to host.
  - The player's browser computes classes from public elevation tiles (AWS Terrain Tiles, Terrarium PNG
    z12-13, 30-40 m, requester-free, already used for the hillshade). Not recommended for the price: that
    mosaic mixes sources (10 m in the US, 30 m elsewhere, edited without version numbers), so the same
    ground can class differently by place and by year; a save repriced after load would drift; the worker
    would decode dozens of PNGs (30-100 KB each, 1-2 s) before Construct; offline play fails. Fine for
    display only, which the hillshade already does.
- So: prices from our fixed tiles, hillshade from the public ones. A player sees and pays the same.

## 4. Hexagons

- Feasible, and it fixes real things: no grid-aligned look, equal neighbour distance, and cells that are
  the same size at every latitude (today's are half as wide at 60 N).
- H3 resolution 9 (edge 174 m, 0.105 km²) is about 2x finer by area than today, still too coarse for
  valleys. Resolution 10 (edge 66 m, 0.015 km², about 115 m across) matches the 115 m square option.
  About 3 billion cells over the zone boxes, built by averaging the 30 m slope into a 115 m raster and
  sampling it at each hex centre (about an hour or two on a few cores; not per-pixel h3 lookups).
- Costs: neither the sim (Rust) nor the app has an H3 library today. Sim needs `h3o` in WASM (point to cell
  is about a microsecond, fine at a few hundred samples a km) and the app `h3-js`. Row run-lengths no
  longer apply: store cells in H3 index order under a parent (digit order keeps neighbours together;
  expect slightly bigger files). Drawing: per-pixel lookups in JS are too slow, so draw polygons for the
  non-flat cells in view (about 16,000 at zoom 11, one GeoJSON fill layer, redrawn on moveend), tint
  from zoom 10-11 only.
- Cheaper alternative: our own global hex lattice laid out as offset rows (same row-run storage, same
  lookup cost as squares, draw on the existing canvas path with a hex test per pixel). Needs a latitude
  correction chosen once and written down; hex shapes then squash a little with latitude.
- Hexes do not show more valleys than squares of the same size; resolution does. Hex is a look-and-feel
  change worth about one more agent-day.

## 5. Recommendation

1. Do section 2 at about 115 m with a recalibration pass, built on EC2 overnight: 1-2 agent-days
   (pipeline script and chunking, row-run lookup in the sim, tint zoom, docs 6.1/6.4, a check), and
   an overnight build (a few core-hours of compute; the download is the wait). Opus for the sim
   memory change, Sonnet for the rest.
2. Hexes after, as a separate task: the own-lattice version is about 1 agent-day, H3 about 2. Doing both
   at once makes any price change impossible to tell from a shape change.
3. Not the browser-computed route for prices.

Needs Anita's call: (a) 115 m or the cheaper 230 m first; (b) whether prices may shift when we
recalibrate (all saves reprice a little); (c) surface model (GLO-30, fast) vs bare earth (slow download
from Zenodo); (d) hex: H3 or our own lattice; (e) whether a few hundred MB of terrain on R2 is fine.
