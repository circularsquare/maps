## The measure

Per drawn line (its pieces' polylines along its chain, runs in one direction merged): every direction change, by size
(45, 90, 135, 180 degrees). Each turn is placed on the line by station index (fractional between stations), and the
**real turn** there is the angle between the real chords from the start of the drawn run before it (at least one
station back) to the turn, and from the turn to the end of the run after it (at least one station on), using the
stations' true places. A **sharp turn the line does not make**: drawn 90 degrees or more where the real turn is under
60. A **sawtooth**: 3 or more consecutive turns alternating left and right (short: every leg between them at most 2
median hops). Crossings kept: T-2534's measure (strict, outside dense places). Kyushu and Kanto are the turns inside
lon/lat boxes (Kyushu 129.4-132.2 E, 30.9-33.93 N; Kanto 138.8-140.95 E, 34.9-36.6 N).

Graphs: the real New York save (855 complexes, 109 lines) and all Japan (`data/work/realnet/jpn_real.save`, 9,106
complexes, 709 lines), each laid out on its sheet exactly as `sheetLines.ts` calls `layoutDiagram` (checked equal to
the page's layout). Scripts in the session scratchpad `smoothlines/`: `prep.mjs` (the sheet and the layout's inputs,
cached), `m.mjs` (layouts and the measures; `both` alternates master's and the tree's layout in one process for the

## What was wrong

1. **A bug in the sheet put stations in the wrong place** (the main cause). `sheet.ts` places each station by its
   weights in its quadtree cell's triangle; for the upper-left triangle (corners a, c, d) the weights of c and d were
   swapped, so every station in that half of its cell was drawn at its mirror image across the cell's diagonal. The
   warp itself (`sheetWarp`, and so the water, borders and carry) was right. Stations sat a median 0.55 pitches off
   the warp of their real place but p90 36 and up to 164 (all Japan, where cells are up to 40 km); in Kumamoto the
   Kagoshima line, running straight north in reality (hops at 80 and 72 degrees), was on the sheet at 140 then 43.
   Of all Japan's station triples that turn under 30 degrees in reality, 29% turned over 60 on the sheet (New York 14%),
   before any octilinear stage; B kept them (it holds stations to the sheet) and the routing could only square them off.
   Those squared-off zigzags are the triangles: the mirror is across a 45 degree diagonal.
2. **The routing chose each bent piece's legs alone** (T-2529): a piece off a 45 degree direction is drawn as two legs,
   orthogonal then diagonal or the reverse, chosen by how near its stations they pass, so pieces in a row zigzag
   (45, 90, 45, 90). And the segments were split into pieces wherever a station lay half a median hop off a straight run
   or one bend, so a gently curving line became many short bent pieces.

## What changed

1. `sheet.ts`: the weights fixed (one line).
2. `layout.ts` routing: each bent piece's leg choice also costs 3 times its length for each 45 degree step a line turns
   at its ends, into the pieces it runs on to, past its real turn there plus half a step (`W_BEND`, `BEND_FREE`;
   `lineLinks` now keeps each link's real turn). So consecutive pieces line their legs up.
3. A segment is split only where a station lies a whole median hop off (from half): fewer pieces, fewer bends.

## Looked outward

- Nöllenburg and Wolff, "Drawing and labeling high-quality metro maps by mixed-integer programming" (IEEE TVCG 17(5),
  2011): a bend cost on every pair of consecutive edges of a line, growing with the bend (0 straight, then 45, 90, 135
  degrees), traded against geography. The leg cost here is that cost for the routing's one free choice per piece,
  counted against the real turn rather than straight. Their categories as implemented in Gurobi's OptiMod:
  https://gurobi-optimods.readthedocs.io/en/v2.1.0/mods/metromap.html
- Bast, Brosi and Storandt, "Metro Maps on Octilinear Grid Graphs" (Computer Graphics Forum 39(3), 2020;
  https://ad-publications.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/EuroVis%20octi-maps.pdf): lines routed as shortest paths on an
  octilinear grid with a cost per bend that rises as the angle gets sharper, so routes take few bends and long straight
  runs. The same idea, applied to pieces already placed.
- Stott, Rodgers, Martinez-Ovando and Walker (IEEE TVCG 17(1), 2011): "line straightness" as one criterion of the
  hill climber, the sum of each line's bends at its stations.
- Wang and Chi, "Focus+Context Metro Maps" (IEEE TVCG 17(12), 2011): straightness as a least-squares energy along lines;
  the B variant above (directions averaged along lines) is that idea, and did not help here because B holds stations to
  the sheet.
- What none of these has is the bug: they all draw a line from places that are already where they should be, which is
  why measuring the sheet's own station places first (`stage.mjs`) found it.
