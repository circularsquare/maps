# T-3804 Other worker work that holds edits up

Stage 1 (agent hourturn, 2026-11-20): the hour turn. Anita, 2026-11-20, wants edit speed worked on next.

## What the hour turn cost (her save t3600-asianita-1852.save, 221 lines, 2,647 stations, speed 2)

Stamps on the clock worker (BroadcastChannel probe, as T-3801's), 10-32 turns a run, the machine shared with
gapfill's 8-core join throughout. The `epoch` message (the page saying a new hour started) ran `settle` and
`sendTrips()`: both hours' slices, this one and the next, at once.

| part, per turn | ms |
|---|---|
| `settle` + `sendMoney` (an hour ended) | 5-20 + 15-100 |
| `TrackApi.trips`, 3 hours (this, next, the hour before for the bays) | 5-85 |
| `assignBays`, 2 hours | 14-290 |
| `boarding_hour`, 2 hours | 43-120 |
| unstamped (GC, load) | 0-130 |
| whole message | 70-460, and 3,100-3,400 when the account was not up to date |

The 3 s turns: `boarding_hour` starts with `Boarding::ensure`, which works out every out-of-date line at once. The
account is out of date at a turn after new riders (still being stepped through, 3 lines a step), after any edit
(even a station rename left 12-57 lines out of date; see "Found on the way"), and after a line's old pattern from
before an edit is forgotten (`prune_eras` changes the line's key). After new riders it was the whole account: 1,314
line-days redone in one turn.

The slice a turn sent for its own hour was already on the page: the turn before had sent it as its next hour.
Hashed at both sends over 58 turns (with a headway edit and a solve in the run): the trips, ends, next departures
and bays were identical every time; the boarding hour was identical unless lines had been redone in between
(`boarding_redone` > 0 in every case that differed).

## What changed

- **The turn sends only what is new** (`turnHour`, clock.worker.ts). The hour just begun is sent again only if its
  slice did not go out since the trips were last sent anew (`sentHours`: cleared by every full `sendTrips`, which
  every change to trips or riders ends in). Otherwise only the next hour's, after the turn's message.
- **The next hour's slice goes in two tasks** (`nextHourLater`): its trips and bays, then its boarding hour and the
  send. An edit or a state between the two begins it again; trips sent anew drop it (as T-1810's split did).
- **A turn never works out the boarding account.** If it is out of date, the turn starts its steps (3 lines a task,
  edits between them) and their last step sends both hours anew. An hour never sent before (the clock jumped) gets
  its trips at once, without loads, until then.
- **`assignBays` keys trips by number** (a map per profile, and per line and run), not by string: 14 ms against 78
  on the same hour, the output identical (18 of 18 hours compared, hashed).
- The parked path (`tripsNow`, a download holding the queue) turns the hour the same way.

- While a stop click's follow-up is held (T-2766) the turn does not set the steps going: trips sent then would be of
  the clicks' model ahead of their state (T-1407); that follow-up steps the account itself before its trips.

The lie (DECISIONS): when the account is out of date at a turn, the new hour shows the loads and platform crowds
worked out before, and its fares are paid on that hour's old shape, until the steps end. An hour's fares add up to
its own whatever the shape (`partFare` only rises to 1), so a total moves only if the fares themselves change within
the hour. An hour never sent before (the steps running past a second turn, or a clock jump) shows its trains without
loads until the steps end.

Checked on the final code with a train count edit on a mid line and the clock at speed 2 (scratch `stale.js`, two
runs of 140 s): the solve after it redid 1,314 and 1,950 line-days of the account, the longest boarding hour took
110 and 145 ms (`boardLog`), the longest step 154 and 100 ms, and one hour of 81 slices went without loads, for 0.14 s.

## Found on the way (for later stages)

- `today` (`Boarding::past_day`, stage 2) is now most of what an edit waits behind at a turn: the trips message's
  boarding hour moves `accountVersion`, and the panels ask `today` at once, 90-900 ms each under this load. The
  headless game asked it every ~250 ms with no panel opened by the probe.
- A boarding step (3 lines) took up to 315 ms; three big lines in one step (stage 3).
- An edit, even a station rename, leaves 12-57 lines' boarding accounts out of date (their key changed), worked out
  again at the next turn in master and stepped now. Not looked into: the rename changes no train's run, so the
  new accounts are probably the same as the old.
- A rename's follow-up (its light state) took 70-400 ms on this save.
- `sendMoney` at each hour's end is 15-100 ms (`moneyState` copies the whole ledger, hours, regions, line days).
- Loading the save can end on two demand results 14 ppm apart in rail trips (above; known from T-3686).
