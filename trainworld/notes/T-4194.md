# T-4194: the train-count settle never settles on big networks (agent settle, 2026-11-24)

Status: options measured, nothing landed; Anita picks. Prototype (all four options behind switches, plus a per-round
trace): `artifacts/trainworld/t4194/prototype.patch`. Natively the switches are env vars (`TW_CYCLE`, `TW_SWING_NEAR`,
`TW_DAMP`, `TW_CAP`; `TW_SETTLE_TRACE=<file>` writes every round); a WASM build takes the same names at compile
time (`option_env!`). The patch also lets `checks/lib/play.mjs` wait longer for a load (`TW_READY_MS`; 300 s is too
short for this save). Saves, traces, dumps: `artifacts/trainworld/t4194/` (the Community save stays out of git).

## Why it never settles

Not rounding and not a slow drift: exact cycles, plus one pair of lines that never repeats.

- **A line whose round trip jumps as its rate passes a threshold** (a hold at a shared merge coming and going) has no
  rate that fits its fleet exactly. It climbs toward the threshold, crosses, its round trip jumps 1-2%, it steps back
  down, and so on. T-1283 already has the rule for this (a shortfall that keeps turning without halving settles the
  line on its side with trains to spare, `SWING_TURNS`), but it only counts turns while the shortfall is under
  `SWING_NEAR` = 0.05 trains. On this save the shortfall at a turn is 0.05-0.19 trains, so it never fires (T-3233
  saw the same in London at +-0.10).
- **Each group of lines sharing track circles on its own, with its own period.** The settle stops early only when the
  whole network's state (rates, round trips, swing counters) equals one of the last 4 rounds (`CYCLE_MAX`, T-3233).
  The load's settle on jwxidghd (natively; ids are the save's line ids):

  | group (lines sharing track) | from round | period |
  |---|---|---|
  | Saikyo Line (Saikyo-sen, 9) with Yokosuka/Sobu Rapid, Narita Express and three locals (331, 333, 349, 350, 355) | 27 | 4 |
  | Toyoko Line F Liner (19) and a local (16) | 35 | 5 |
  | Yokohama Line (44) | 15 | 2 |
  | Commuter Semi-Express (251) | 27 | 3 |
  | Komachi (76), held | 5 | 1 |
  | Oshiage/Asakusa Line (7) and Keisei Main/Asakusa/Oshiage Rapid (198) | - | none in 80 rounds |

  Together that is a period of 60 or more, so the whole state never repeats and every settle runs all 80 rounds,
  about 180 ms each (the 1% probe ~80 ms and the service update ~110 ms, both over all 370 lines; classify and fill
  under 5 ms).
- **The headway-set edit** (edit_bench's first edit) has one circling group, period 5 from round 33: it runs 80 rounds
  only because the repeat check looks back 4.
- **Held lines** on full track with a circling line flip between two shares each round (the Asia save's line 16, a
  two-state flip); they end wherever round 80 leaves them.
- **Today a reload already moves figures on this save**: save after the edits, load again, and 23 lines' trains an
  hour move, up to 1.32% (Home Liner Morning Wing, 204), because round 80 lands at an arbitrary point of each cycle.
  "Same save, same answer" holds (the settle is deterministic: two game runs gave identical figures); "a load of a
  settled save changes nothing" (track-and-service.md) does not, on networks like this.

**Network shape that shows each shift worst**
- 1: none (exact).
- 2: a line with few trains on full track shared with a circling line. A circling line is kept up to 0.25 trains under
  its fleet (10% of a 2.5-train line's rate); a held neighbour is left on whichever side of its two-state flip the
  settle stopped (Asia's 3.5%).
- 3: a network whose settle needs more than 30 rounds to converge for real (a save from an older build, an edit that
  moves many lines at once) stops unconverged; circling lines stop wherever round 30 leaves them.
- 4: slower everywhere it acts; ruled out.

**Considered, not built**
- Per-group exact repeats (each sharing group stops at its own cycle, its round-80 state worked out from its phase):
  no figure moves, but the jwx load still waits on the Oshiage/Asakusa pair, which never repeats; and groups are not
  quite independent (a held line is stepped only while some other group keeps its level active), so exactness needs
  care.
- Warm start from the last settle: edits already start from it; a circling network has no settled state to start
  from. The load's 19 extra settles (one per bus zone) are T-4188's load fix, not this one; with both, a load pays
  one settle.

## t2018 comparison (trailcmp, 2026-11-24)

Question: did a7e292066 (T-4194) cause the 0.4.6 failure of checks/t2018-trail.mjs (4 of 109 standing frames at 7661 m, "no local ahead", "local":0)?

Ran the check unchanged (defaults: 1 local, 1 express, 3 h) in two trees, built WASM each: before = a7e292066^ (1c0455fb7), after = a7e292066. Two runs each, since the first pair differed.

| run | commit | frames | standing | behind standing local | halts | where |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | before | 928 | 69 | 66 | 3 | all s=7661.02, ahead null |
| 1 | after | 1131 | 142 | 133 | 9 | all s=7661.02, ahead null |
| 2 | before | 1073 | 101 | 93 | 8 | all s=7661.02, ahead null |
| 2 | after | 1112 | 97 | 90 | 7 | all s=7661.02, ahead null |

Result: it fails the same way before the commit. Every failing frame is at 7661.02 m with no local ahead, on both sides. The counts (3-9) vary run to run on either side (frame rate and lag differ per run), so T-4194 did not change it.

What "local":0 means: it is the line id of the Local line (first line in a new game has id 0), not a count of locals built. The setup does build the local (round trip 1885 s) and the express (907 s).

Why it looks like the check's own setup: the halt rule excuses a standing express only when the local ahead is standing, or when no local is ahead and a local is waiting at the start of the other run (`turning`). At 7661 m the express waits (about 340 m short of the 8000 m end) with no local ahead on that run: the local has already reached the terminus and its next trip is not at the run start the way the check's `turning` test expects. So this is the check's "no local ahead" branch misjudging a queue at the end of the line. Not investigated further in the game itself (read-only run).
