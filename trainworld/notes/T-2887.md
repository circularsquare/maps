## The design in short

A signed-in player clicks Share on a game. Their network goes up as **drawing data, not a picture**: the lines, stops and station names as numbers and text, which each viewer's game paints with the same code that paints the schematic now. Anyone can browse Community: Random, Best in a city, Top in a city. Signed-in players can like and report. Three reports hide a share until Anita looks.

## Why drawing data and not screenshots

This is the main decision, and it goes against "just use screenshots", so it is Question 1.

| | Picture (PNG or WebP) | Drawing data (recommended) |
|---|---|---|
| Offensive images | Anyone with dev tools can upload any picture: porn, gore, worse. Someone has to look at everything reported | Impossible. The server accepts numbers and short strings and rebuilds the file itself. The worst case is rude shapes made of lines, which a player can already build as track |
| Names in the image | Pixels: no filter can read them | Text: the existing filter runs on each one, and again on read |
| Illegal content duty | The real risk of a picture host. Cloudflare's free CSAM scan only covers what passes through its cache; `market.anita.garden` does not | Does not arise |
| Readability | NYC schematic at full size is 9893 x 8636 px, 4.6 MB. Shrunk to 3200 px (155 KB WebP) the names are too small to read | Sharp at any zoom, pans and zooms like the real schematic |
| Light and dark theme | Fixed when taken | Follows the viewer |
| Size, NYC, 109 lines | 4.6 MB full, 155 KB shrunk | about 60 KB |
| Work | Less: the picture export exists | A little more: a frozen file format, server validation |

A determined person could try to fake a tiny low-resolution image with thousands of short coloured lines. Caps on points and colours make it poor, and the report button covers it.

Measured in headless Chrome on the real saves. Line and stop data gzipped, then names (22 zoom levels of placed names, which 4 levels would cut to about a fifth):

| Save | Lines and stops | Names, all levels | Shared, with 4 name levels (estimate) | Schematic layout time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NYC, 1756 stations, 109 lines | 47 KB | 28 KB | about 60 KB | 19 s |
| All-Japan test save, 5992 stations, 491 lines | 126 KB | 1 MB | about 320 KB | 52 s |

(Three cores, loaded machine. The layout runs once on the sharer's machine, and not at all if they just looked at the schematic. Viewers do none of it.)

## What a player shares

- **Stage 1: the schematic** as drawing data, plus the figures below. No free text: no title, no description, no comments. The game's own name stays private. The card reads city, player name, points, lines, stations.
- **Stage 2: the to-scale view** as a second drawing: each line's route and the stations, in lng/lat. When sharing, the player picks which one is the cover, and viewers can flip between the two. It comes second because it is the one that needs new work: a new painter, and a real basemap behind it in the viewer (a second MapLibre map, opened only on the detail view). The schematic's painter already exists.
- **The save itself: not in stage 1.** Stage 3, one tick when sharing, off by default ("Let others open a copy"). It is the fun part (poke around someone's network, watch the trains), but it needs the most care: a stranger's file through the loader, private bits to strip (line notes, the game's name), and old shared saves stop opening when the format or a city changes, because keeping old saves loading is deliberately not a goal. A drawing never has that problem. That is why the drawing is the thing that lasts and the save is a bonus. See Question 3.

A share is a snapshot. Share again and it replaces the old one, keeping its likes. One share per game. Deleting the game deletes its share, as the privacy page promises for everything else about a deleted game.

## Who can share

Signed-in only, with a leaderboard-style display name (same field, same filter; sharing asks for one if the player has none, and does not put them on the boards). At least 60 minutes played on the account, from the playtime the server already keeps: a cheap brake on throwaway accounts. At least 8 stations and 2 lines, so Random isn't full of stubs. Accounts are already 13+.

Browsing needs no account. Liking and reporting do.

## Browse views

Twelve cards a page, each one the shared drawing painted small. One share per person per view.

- **Random.** Uniform over visible shares, Shuffle for another twelve. Includes sandbox games, tagged.
- **Best in a city** (her "sample of random cool ones that are like some of the best"). Twelve at random from a pool: the top quarter of that city's shares by points (at least 12 of them), plus any with 2 or more likes in the top tenth by likes. The pool is what gets gamed, and getting into it only earns a turn in the shuffle.
- **Top in a city.** Strict points order, top 50, the same shape as the city leaderboards, one per person. Never sandbox.
- Stage 2 adds **Most liked** (all time, past 30 days).

City picker: the leaderboards' existing search box, including the world.

**How "best" stays honest.** Points are client-asserted today and that can't be fixed cheaply, so the design limits what a fake buys:
- The share never sends a score. The server copies the game's figures already on file (the per-city points the leaderboards use), at share time. A game with no figures is unranked.
- A sandbox game (latched on the server) never ranks.
- The card shows the network next to the number, so 10 stations with a billion points is visible, and "Fake score" is a report reason.
- Likes need an account, so faking them means making accounts, and only accounts with 60 minutes played count.
- No fancy formula yet. The Wilson lower bound is the textbook fix for ranking by rating, but it needs a view count per share and there won't be enough votes for it to matter. A per-city ceiling on points waits for real data (stage 4).

## Likes

A heart. One per account per share, can be taken back, not on your own. The count is public, who liked it is not. No dislike.

## Where it lives

As she guessed: the saves panel and the leaderboards, no new tab.

- **Community button** in the head of the saves panel and in the head of the leaderboards modal. Both open one Community modal.
- **Share button** on the current game's card in the saves panel, and beside Save picture on the schematic.
- Stage 2: on a leaderboard row, a small icon when that player has shared a network for that board.

Opening a card fills the modal with the drawing to pan and zoom, plus like, report, and the player's name. Pausing the game behind it is the same call as T-2888's.

## Moderation and abuse

| Surface | What happens |
|---|---|
| Display names | Existing filter on set and on read |
| Station names (players can rename stations) | Each one through the filter on upload and on read. A name that fails is blanked, not refused, because the filter can catch real places. It reads Latin script only; CJK slurs get through to reports |
| Line names | Not sent in stage 1 |
| Rude drawings | Report (the Spore lesson below) |
| Bad uploads | Server parses, checks counts, colours and ranges, drops unknown fields, writes its own copy. Upload capped 512 KB gzipped, 4 MB unpacked |
| Spam | 3 shares a day and 20 in all per account; 60 minutes played; per-IP limits on every route; 5,000 shares and 2 GB in all (past that, 507 and one alert) |

**Reports.** Signed-in, once per account per share, reasons Offensive / Fake score / Something else. **Three reports from different accounts hide the share at once**, with no one looking. Anita gets one short mail on a share's first report, through the existing mail limiter.

**What Anita does.** A Community tab on the inbox page she already has (`localhost:8800/twfeedback/`): reported shares painted, with Keep, Remove, Ban account. Keep clears the reports. Remove deletes it. Ban hides everything of theirs and stops them sharing, reporting and liking.

**How little she can get away with:** nothing on a schedule, no approval queue. A mail when something is reported, about 30 seconds each, batched whenever she likes. If she ignores them all, reported things stay hidden. The cost of ignoring: three accounts together could hide a share they dislike until she looks.

I'd skip review before publishing: it makes every share wait on her, and it is the option that turns a hobby into a job.

## Data and storage

New file `trainworld_community.go`, behind `SHONEI_COMMUNITY=1`, reported in `/noritetsu/config` as `trainworldCommunity`. Same habits as the rest: temp and rename, an unreadable index refuses writes, per-IP buckets, `twRoutes`, account delete removes shares and likes.

    trainworld/community/index.json             every share's meta, likes and reports (written at most every 30 s, as playtime.json is)
    trainworld/community/drawings/<id>.<rev>.gz  the drawing, the server's own re-encoding
    trainworld/community/mod.json               banned accounts

Share meta: `id` (8 random characters, not the account or game id), `owner`, `game`, `rev`, `at`, `points`, `cities` (id to points), `day`, `lines`, `stations`, `sandbox`, `cover`, `bytes`, `likedBy`, `reports`, `hidden` (none, auto, anita). The display name is looked up live.

Routes: `POST /trainworld/community` (share or replace), `GET .../list?view=random|best|top&city=&seed=`, `GET .../<id>/<rev>` (drawing, cached forever since the URL changes with the rev), `DELETE .../<id>`, `PUT` and `DELETE .../<id>/like`, `POST .../<id>/report`, and admin routes under `/trainworld/inbox/community/`.

**Why not R2.** The box has 32 GB free of 38 (3.8 GB used; all of trainworld is 82 MB; read 2026-11-09), and serving from it is one code path with deletes that take effect at once. R2 would also be free at these sizes, but adds a second store and a Cloudflare credential on the server. Worth it only well past 1000 shares.

**Cost**, assuming 60 KB an average drawing (NYC at 109 lines is a big network, so probably high):

| Shared networks | Disk, stage 1 | With to-scale (+40 KB, estimate) | With saves too (+75 KB) | Worst case at the caps | Extra money |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | under 1 MB | 1 MB | 2 MB | 5 MB | none |
| 100 | 6 MB | 10 MB | 18 MB | 50 MB | none |
| 1000 | 60 MB | 100 MB | 175 MB | 500 MB to 1.5 GB | none |

Traffic: one page of twelve is about 0.7 MB, once, then cached. Even 1000 pages a day is about 21 GB a month, against the 1 TB a CPX11 in Ashburn includes (overage about €1/TB). Backups cost a fixed share of the server's price, however full. Memory: about 2.5 MB at the 5,000 cap.

Today's scale for reference: 447 accounts with games, 673 card pictures (median 16 KB). Those PNGs stay private and must not be reused here, since a client can upload anything as one.

## Stages

Each gets its own task id when she says go.

1. **Share, browse, like, report.** Schematic drawing; Random, Best and Top in a city; likes; reports with auto-hide; inbox tab; privacy and terms. Server about 700 lines of Go plus tests (T-2853's threads were 1178 and 691), game about 500 lines. Two tasks, one server and one game. Deploy order: server, then game. This is the smallest version that's still fun: the fun is seeing other people's networks, and likes and reports are small once a share exists.
2. **To-scale and either.** Second drawing, painter, basemap in the viewer, cover choice, Most liked, the leaderboard icon. About 400 lines, one task.
3. **Open a copy of the save.** The tick, stripping private fields, loader hardening for stranger's files, a clear message when a save no longer opens. About 300 lines plus a real look at the loader. One task.
4. **If it takes off.** A per-city ceiling on points from real data, a link to a share, a Picks row chosen by Anita, a title with the filter on it. Small pieces, only if wanted.

## How others do it

Little in this genre has an in-game gallery; most hand it to Steam or Discord, which we can't lean on.

- [Subway Builder](https://www.subwaybuilder.com/): community mods go through a Discord channel. I found no in-game sharing or leaderboards.
- [NIMBY Rails](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1134710/discussions/3/594009924939323192): Steam Workshop for mods, and players post networks on a separate share site.
- [Super Mario Maker](https://www.mariowiki.com/Super_Mario_Maker_Bookmark): stars with weekly and all-time rankings, and a [much-praised filter site](https://kotaku.com/finding-good-levels-in-super-mario-maker-is-way-easier-1749470775). Finding good ones took work. Its random mode keeps badly rated levels out of the harder tiers.
- [Dreams](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/ad-infinitum-handling-community-curation-on-a-cosmic-scale-in-dreams): likes and trending, plus people picking by hand from New, on purpose to surface hidden gems and not always the same creators. Hence the random pool, and the Picks idea in stage 4.
- [Spore](https://bit-tech.net/news/gaming/ea-clamps-down-on-sporn/1): even with no free drawing, people built rude creatures within days. EA used warnings, then suspensions, then removal, and [players chased the "banned" badge](https://www.gamesradar.com/busted-boobalicious-banned). So: report button from day one, and nothing that marks being removed as an achievement.
- [Opus Magnum](https://www.pcgamer.com/perfectly-solving-opus-magnums-puzzles-is-impossible-but-thats-ok): histograms over ranks, and [cheaters barely move a distribution](https://biggieblog.com/tracking-the-global-opus-magnum-records/) once players far outnumber them. Same reasoning as a pool over a single top slot.
- [Evan Miller on sorting by rating](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-sort-by-average-rating.html): the Wilson lower bound, for later.
- [Cloudflare's CSAM scan](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cache/reference/csam-scanning/) (only content through its cache), [R2 pricing](https://workers.cloudflare.com/product/r2/) (10 GB free, no egress fee), [Hetzner traffic](https://docs.hetzner.com/robot/general/traffic/).

## Questions for Anita

1. **Drawing data instead of screenshots?** It rules out offensive images altogether and is sharper and smaller, at the cost of a little more work. Recommend yes.
2. **Schematic first, to-scale in stage 2?** To-scale is the one needing new work. Recommend yes. The alternative is to-scale only at first, which means building the new painter before anything is fun.
3. **Should the save go too, so people can open a copy?** Recommend yes, but as stage 3 and off by default: shared saves will stop opening when the format or a city changes, and the drawing carries the feature without it.
4. **Hide on three reports with no review before publishing, and she deals with reports whenever she feels like it?** Recommend yes. The other way is approving each share first, which is safest and turns into a chore.
