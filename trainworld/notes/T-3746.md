## How it is now

There are two selections of several pieces:

- **The Select tool's selection** (key 4). Box, click, double click for a run, Shift+click. It holds blueprint, or
  built track and stations. It shows the selection bar at the top of the map: levels, Regular / High speed / Tram,
  single or double, Rebuild, Disable / Enable, Double up, one way, Speed limit, Remove, Clear. Shift+click there
  already adds the track in between (since T-1680, 2026-10-28).
- **Shift+click without the Select tool** (T-3634, yesterday). It works only with built track selected, adds or takes
  out one piece at a time, and opens a second track panel, "3 pieces of track": the levels, kinds, single or double,
  Rebuild and Remove, plus the summed cost, upkeep and the lines on the pieces. It has no speed limit, no one way, no
  Double up, no blueprint and no stations.

Both are already drawn in the same highlight band on the map. Under the hood they are two separate lists, and the
panel and the bar each have their own rebuild buttons calling the same rebuild code.

## 1. One selection

What the player sees and does:

- Without the Select tool, a plain click on track still opens that piece's track panel, as now.
- **Shift+click more track** adds it to the same selection the Select tool makes. From 2 pieces up, the track panel
  closes and the selection bar shows at the top, as if the Select tool had picked them. So Speed limit, one way,
  Double up and the rest are there. The highlight is the Select tool's.
- Shift+click follows the Select tool's rules: it adds the track in between (part 2); Shift+click on a selected piece
  takes it out; Shift+click on a station adds the station. Blueprint works too: with a blueprint piece clicked,
  Shift+click adds blueprint. A selection holds blueprint or built, never both, as in the Select tool.
- Taking pieces out down to one goes back to that piece's track panel.
- Rebuild is the bar's, which is what T-3634 gave players: one quote, one question, one charge for all, final,
  refused pieces drawn red.
- Delete removes the selection and Esc clears it, as with the Select tool. A plain click on anything else selects that
  thing and drops the selection.
- **Switching tools**: the selection stays when you switch between the Select tool and plain selecting, either way, so
  you can Shift+click a stretch and then press 4 to box more. Any building tool (track, station, delete, bus, ferry,
  line stops, trip explorer) clears it, since its own bar takes that spot.
- Dragging the selection and its white squares, the box and double click for a run stay with the Select tool.
- On a phone nothing changes: without the Select tool there is still no way to add a second piece (no Shift), and the
  Select tool's taps already add and take out.

What goes:

- The "3 pieces of track" panel. With it go its summed Cost to construct, summed Upkeep and the lines on the pieces;
  the bar has none of those. I'd add the summed upkeep to the hover on the bar's length, and let the lines list go.
- The two Shift+click hovers on the track panel title (below).

How: Shift+click without the Select tool feeds the Select tool's selection (`bpPicked`) by the tool's own click rule
(`under`, `pathTo`), and the `{ kind: "tracks" }` selection, `game/trackSet.ts` and `TracksInspector` are deleted.
What now hides things outside the Select tool gets loosened: `PickLimit` shows outside the tool for 2+ pieces, the
Delete key acts on a selection of 2+, the effect at the bottom of `blueprintPick.ts` stops clearing a 2+ selection
when the panel's selection is empty, and the tool-switch rule above replaces "leaving the Select tool clears it". The
`mousedown` guard against MapLibre's Shift+drag zoom stays, keyed to the new selection. T-2283 (a clicked blueprint
piece already shows the bar without the Select tool) is the precedent.

This reverses DECISIONS 2026-11-18 (T-3634), which kept the set apart for three reasons. The bar: that is now the
point. The keys: Delete and Esc get the same meaning in both places. The clearing rules: replaced by the switch rule
above. Its fourth reason, that the player asked to add one piece at a time, is question 2.

## 2. Fill between

Yes, it is well defined, and the Select tool already does it (T-1680). The rule:

- From the clicked piece's two ends, the shortest way **by length** along track to any end of a selected piece; every
  piece on that way is added with the clicked one.
- Only through track of the selection's kind (built, or blueprint), so built and blueprint never mix.
- Straight through junctions and stations. It follows pieces, not the moves a train can make, so it can turn sharply
  at a junction; that is fine for a selection.
- A piece touching the selection adds just itself.
- No way along track (a separate network, or only through the other kind): the piece alone.

Where it is ambiguous:

- **A loop**: with the selection and the new piece both on a loop, the shorter side wins. To get the long side,
  Shift+click a piece on it first.
- **Two equal ways**: an exact tie goes to whichever the search reaches first. Real lengths almost never tie exactly,
  and the player sees the result highlighted, so no tie rule is needed.
- **Two separate selected stretches**: the nearer one, along track, is joined.
- **A detour**: two parallel lines that meet only at their ends. Shift+clicking a piece on the other line adds the
  whole way round through the terminus, which is almost never meant. This is the one failure worth a rule.

Recommended rule, the same in both places:

- Keep T-1680's shortest way.
- **A detour adds the piece alone**: when the way along track is more than 3 times the straight distance between the
  clicked piece and the nearest selected piece (plus 2 km, so short wiggles never count), add only the clicked piece.
  This beats a km cap, which would stop the most useful case: click one end of a line, Shift+click the other end, and
  get the whole line. A U-shaped line (out and back nearby) trips the rule; Shift+click a piece in its middle first,
  then the far end fills from there.
- **Crossover diagonals are skipped by the fill.** Otherwise a fill can hop onto the parallel pair, and speed limits
  and rebuilds skip crossovers anyway. A crossover can still be clicked on its own.
- **No modifier key for "just this piece".** Fill is the default, as in the Select tool and as she asked. The free
  modifiers are poor: Ctrl+click is a right click on a Mac, Ctrl+drag rotates the map, and Alt+Shift or Ctrl+Shift
  switch the keyboard language on many Windows setups. The detour rule covers the usual "I wanted a separate piece"
  case, and a filled piece can be Shift+clicked out.

The search is a plain Dijkstra with a slow heap (a scan per step). It is fine at today's network sizes. If a Shift+click
on a big network ever feels slow, the fix is a proper heap, about 10 minutes.
