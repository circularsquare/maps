## 1. What exists

**How a complex draws** (docs/ui-map.md "Stations", "Station complexes"). Every station is its own
platform box from zoom 14: a white band along its track, 1.125 times a line wide (about 24 m on the
ground from zoom 15), flat-ended, with a dark rim (`stationInk`: map dark grey in light, #8f8b85 in
dark; the box white in both themes). Overlapping boxes are one shape with one outline (T-217). Boxes
of one complex that do not touch get a drawn bridge (T-1859); a built passage draws the same bridge.
Below zoom 14 each station is a dot and a complex's dots are joined by connectors. A complex is every
station whose platform comes within 50 m of another's, or whose centre is within 100 m, plus the
player's passages (`game/complexes.ts`); nothing about it is saved but the passages. A click on any
box selects the whole complex (complex view); a complex's stations sharing a name have one label at
their mean position. So a busy interchange today is the "amalgam": Shinjuku in the Tokyo save is ten
stations, five of them parallel JR boxes merging into one wide shape, with Odakyu, Keio, Marunouchi,
Toei Shinjuku and Oedo boxes sticking out of it at angles (left panel of sheet-shinjuku.png).
Akihabara in Anita's save is six boxes in a cross.

**What capacity a station has** (docs/track-and-service.md 6.6). None that limits riders. Trains are
the limit: demand holds riders to whoever the trains due in the next 30 minutes can take
(`WAIT_FOR_ROOM_S`); the rest go another way. Two station figures exist:
- `platformRoom` (game/boarding.ts): 2.5 m of usable width a side at 0.3 m² a rider, 1,250 a side on
  150 m, 1,667 on 200 m. **Only the drawing reads it**: a side's crowd grey fills to it and turns
  red past it, and the dot's pie is that share. Nothing in demand or the boarding account changes.
- `WAITING_CAP` (5,000 riders a platform side, the boarding account only): past it the account turns
  riders away. Demand's own queue has no such cap.

**The change penalty inside a complex** (docs/demand-kernel.md 4.4, demand-plain.md). A change costs
a fixed 2 perceived minutes (`Params::transfer_min`, in `change_min` with any transfer fare, on every
boarding after the first), plus the walk from the platform left to the platform taken (centre to
centre x 1.3 at 1.2 m/s, each minute weighted 1.39), plus the wait for the next train (half the
headway; 0.8 of it in a waiting room, `upgrades::WAITING_ROOM_WAIT`). A passage walks the straight line
at 2 m/s instead (`PASSAGE_MPS`). So: across one platform 2.0 perceived minutes plus the wait;
platforms 80 m apart 4.0; 200 m apart 7.0.

## 2. References

**Other games**
- NIMBY Rails. A station is "a set of one or more platforms whose footprints overlap"; players reach
  a far platform with a "platform footprint extension" building, visible only in the editor, that
  bridges footprints (devblog 2022-02, https://carloscarrasco.com/page/42/). Station capacity is a
  slider, 50 to 100,000 riders, and "Transfer pax overflow into the Station Hall" puts riders who do
  not fit on the platforms into a hall; walking links between stations run at 1 m/s up to 2.3 km
  (https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/Station). The nearest thing to this design: a drawn footprint that
  joins platforms, and a hall that takes the overflow.
- Transport Fever 2. Modular stations: platform and building modules of fixed size (40 m platform
  modules); passenger capacity is worked out from the length and width of the walkways in the
  modules, not set by the player (Steam workshop and discussions, e.g.
  https://steamcommunity.com/app/1066780/discussions/0/1746772488905106339). Building modules add
  capacity; the look is the module kit's.
- Cities: Skylines II. Stations are fixed assets with upgrades (extra platforms raise capacity); the
  2026 "City Stations" pack adds bigger station buildings as assets
  (https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/cities-skylines-ii/add-ons/cities-skylines-ii-city-stations).
  Nothing drawn by the player.
- Subway Builder. Stations are drawn as platforms of a length; shorter is cheaper; a patch replaced a
  fixed 10k transfer limit with a platform capacity the player sets (changelog,
  https://www.subwaybuilder.com/changelog; SteamDB patch notes). No station buildings.
- Mini Metro. The Interchange upgrade is exactly Anita's pair of effects: a station holds more
  waiting passengers without overcrowding, and transfers are faster (early alpha figures: 18 waiting,
  loading 4x; https://dinopoloclub.com/2014/04/21/mini-metro-alpha9/). Players call it the weakest
  upgrade because it adds no line capacity (https://hamy.xyz/blog/2024-03_mini-metro-best-upgrades),
  which is fine for something meant to be mostly cosmetic.

So nobody lets the player draw a free outline; NIMBY's invisible footprint is the closest. Drawing
one is new, which is a reason to keep its rules few.

**Real footprints** (OSM through Overpass, `osm_footprints.py` and `osm_fields.py` below; Berlin from
its sources):
- Grand Central Terminal: the head house is one building of 12,400 m² (121 x 104 m); the platform
  field behind it is 417 x 188 m, platforms covering 40% of it.
- Shinjuku: 18 platforms spread over 459 x 434 m (hull 128,000 m²), platforms 13% of it; the JR
  station building proper is 5,700 m². In the game's Tokyo save, a rectangle round the JR, Odakyu and
  Marunouchi platforms is 344 x 175 m, 60,300 m².
- Times Sq-42 St: 6 platforms over 265 x 242 m (hull 43,000 m²). The game's rectangle round its ten
  stations is 323 x 281 m, 90,700 m².
- Berlin Hauptbahnhof: an east-west glass hall about 321 m long crossing a north-south hall 160-180 m
  long and 40 m wide, so a cross in plan (berlin.de; Wikipedia "Berlin Hauptbahnhof").
- Tokyo Station: the Marunouchi building is about 335 m long (Wikipedia), a long thin rectangle along
  the tracks. Overpass timed out on its platforms.

What reads well at map zooms: a hub is 100-450 m, so 100-450 px at zoom 16 and 25-110 px at zoom 14.
Real hubs are rectangles along the tracks, or two crossing (Berlin, Akihabara's real station): few
corners, edges parallel to the tracks, one roof. Drawn the same way (one outline, a light floor, the
tracks visible through it) they read as one place at every zoom from 14 in, and the shops only start
to read from about 15.5, where a 13 x 9 m shop is 9 x 6 px.

## 3. The design

### The tool

- **Where**: the Build tab, beside Bus under the four tools (the full-width Bus button becomes two
  half-width buttons, Bus and Building), key **H** (free; B is the Build tab). Also an icon at the
  end of the complex view's upgrade row, after the passage, which starts the tool with a rectangle
  round the complex already drawn (below).
- **Drawing** works like the bus tool (map/busDraw.ts, T-1514): each click adds a corner; a ghost
  edge follows the pointer; the corners show as handles; a click on the first corner, a double click
  or Enter finishes; Backspace or a right click takes back the last corner; Escape drops the outline
  (a second Escape leaves the tool). The bar at the map's bottom left has Construct and Cancel.
- **The right-angle snap**, a light one (her "snap a little"):
  - the first edge turns to the nearest platform's axis (any station within 50 m of the corner) when
    within 10°, so a building lines up with its tracks;
  - every later edge turns to the first edge's direction or a right angle to it when within 10°;
  - a corner within 12 px of the first corner lands on it, and one within 8 px of the line along an
    axis through the first corner lands on that line, so the last edge closes square;
  - with three corners placed at a right angle, the fourth corner shows as a ghost; Enter takes it
    and closes a rectangle.
  - Ctrl held draws with no snap.
- **The rectangle offered**: the upgrade row's icon starts the tool with the smallest rectangle round
  the complex's boxes, turned to its longest platform, 15 m out from the boxes, its four corners as
  handles. Construct takes it as it is; most players will want exactly that.
- **Editing**: the building's section in complex view (below) has Edit outline, which reopens the tool
  on the outline: drag a corner to move it, right click a corner to delete it, drag the small handle
  at an edge's middle to make a new corner. The bar then says Save outline and Cancel.
- **Deleting**: Remove building in the section, or the delete tool on the building's floor; both ask
  first, as for anything built.
- **Which stations it takes in**: every station whose node (its platform's middle) is inside the
  outline, at any elevation, worked out again whenever stations or the outline change. A platform
  partly outside still belongs; one whose middle is outside does not, and is drawn over the building
  as its own box. Nothing is saved about members.
- **Rules**: 3 to 24 corners; edges may not cross; no two corners more than 1 km apart (the passage's
  reach); at least 1,000 m²; buildings may not overlap; it needs at least one built station inside
  ("built" because it is paid at once, below; a building round blueprint stations only would need a
  blueprint state of its own, which is not worth it for a mostly cosmetic thing). Over water and over
  other track is fine.
- **A building joins its stations into one complex**, as a passage would: everyone inside it changes
  on foot to any other station inside it. That is what a station building means, and it fixes the
  overlap Anita turned big stations down for: the player says which stations are one place by
  drawing round them.

### The cost

US$2,000 a square metre ($2M per 1,000 m²), paid at once, no upkeep, as the station upgrades. Moving
corners pays for any area added and refunds nothing for area taken away; removing refunds nothing.
The quote while drawing shows the price. Examples: the Shinjuku rectangle 60,300 m² is $121M, Times
Sq 90,700 m² $181M, the Akihabara cross 29,500 m² $59M; a ground station is $75M, a -2 one $150M.
Like passages and upgrades it is not in the Ctrl+Z history.

### What it does

1. **Joins its stations into one complex** (above).
2. **Room in the hall**: one waiting rider per 2 m² of the building's floor not under its own
   platforms (half the floor is shops, stairs and gates; the rest at a standing 1 m² each). Shinjuku's
   60,300 m² round 19,300 m² of platforms is room for 20,500; Times Sq 35,400; the Akihabara cross
   6,500. A platform side fills to its own room as today; riders past it stand in the hall, one pool
   for the whole building; a side turns red only when it is past its room and the hall is full. This
   is the "capacity in proportion with its area", and **like the platform room today it is for the
   drawing only**: demand has no station capacity, so nothing about who rides changes. Making it a real
   limit would add a station capacity rule to demand (riders turned away when the platform and hall
   are full), a new complicating factor, and is Anita's call (section 6).
3. **Changes a little faster: inside one building a change's fixed minutes and its walk count 0.8**
   (`BUILDING_CHANGE`, the same 0.8 as the waiting room's, so "20% shorter" again). Across one platform
   2.0 becomes 1.6 perceived minutes, 80 m apart 4.0 becomes 3.2, 200 m apart 7.0 becomes 5.6; the wait
   is unchanged (the waiting room covers that) and so is any transfer fare. A passage inside a building
   walks at 2 m/s as now, times 0.8. As a worldwide demand rule (docs/demand-worldwide.md "Network and
   stations", stage 4): a flat judgement figure the player buys, reading nothing local; why it may
   not carry over: some real hubs are slow to change in however big their roof (Shinjuku,
   Chatelet). Check: a few published interchange times inside big buildings against the rule.
   The lie (DECISIONS when built): the kernel sees one bit "in a building" per station, so a walk
   between two different buildings joined by a passage also counts 0.8.

### The drawing

- **One shape**: the building and the boxes of its stations are one white shape with one outline in
  `stationInk`, as overlapping boxes are now (T-217); the boxes' own rims inside it go, and so do the
  bridges and connectors between its members. A member's platform that sticks out past the outline
  stays drawn as its box, joined to the building. Non-member stations draw as now, over the building.
- **Inside**: the floor is a very light warm white (#f9f7f3); member platforms are pure white bands
  with no edges (as boxes are now, T-1121), so they read faintly as where riders wait; the track and
  line strokes run through the whole building as they run through a box today, members' and
  non-members' alike (a line passing under without stopping is drawn too, as the pink line through
  Akihabara shows). The floor and the platforms are the same in both themes, like boxes now; the dark
  mockup reads as a lit building on a dark city, which matched the boxes better than a grey floor did
  (a grey floor and a beige one were tried: beige vanished into the land colour).
- **Shops**: little rectangles on the floor left over (the floor less 4 m round every platform and
  3 m round every line), on a grid lined up with the first edge: cells 13 x 9 m with 2.5 m between,
  each one shop or two side by side, about 85% of cells taken, picked by a hash of the building and
  cell so they never shuffle. A clear cross-hall 28 m wide through the middle holds the name. Seven
  pale colours (peach, cream, sage, sky, rose, sand, yellow) with a rim 16% darker, the same in both
  themes. From zoom 17 a shop at least 9 px across shows a small glyph in its rim colour: a cup, a
  bag, a bowl or a book. Shops have no hover (they encode nothing). The car park places outside the
  outline (its obstacles gain the building); the waiting room annex draws on the floor beside its
  platform as now, the shops leaving room for it.
- **By zoom**: below 13 nothing changes (dots and connectors). From 13 to 14 the outline fades in
  under the dots, the floor at 60%. From 14 the full shape with no shops; shops fade in from 15 to
  15.5; glyphs from 17. A building with no station inside draws at half strength with no shops.
- **Crowds**: with platform crowds on, the hall's overflow stands on the free floor nearest the
  platforms spilling, drawn as a stipple of the crowd grey (2 px dots on a 4 px grid, the dark theme's
  lighter walkers grey `#b8b3ac` where the boxes use it) so it reads as people and not as more
  building; red once the hall is full. A member's dot pie, zoomed out, reads its fullest side against
  its own room plus an even share of the hall.
- **Label**: one name for the building, by default the part before any slash of its busiest member's
  name, renamed in its section like a station (NameEdit). Inside the building, on the cross-hall, when
  its short side on screen is at least the name's width plus 24 px; otherwise beside it where the
  complex's label goes today. Members' own labels go.
- **Clicks and hovers**: a click on a platform picks that station and selects the complex, as now. A
  click on the floor or a shop selects the complex too. The delete tool on the floor takes the
  building; the track and station tools ignore it (a station placed inside joins it). The floor's
  hover: the name, "Station building", and with crowds on "4,200 riders waiting in the hall".
- **Complex view**: a "Station building" section after Passages: Area, Room (with its hover), Edit
  outline, Remove building.

### The save

A new trailing block `BLD1` in the network encoding after the bus routes' block, only when there are
buildings, so a game without them encodes byte for byte as before and older readers stop before it:
the count, then per building its name, its corner count and the corners as millimetre deltas from
the previous (as the bus routes do). Kept in the sim as `Network::buildings`; members are worked out,
never saved.

### When a station inside moves or goes

Members are recomputed with the complexes on every change. A station dragged out leaves (it draws as
its own box again, its complex as the distance rule makes it); one dragged in joins; a station placed
inside joins. Removing a station never removes the building: an empty building keeps its outline,
draws at half strength without shops, says "No station inside" on hover, and costs nothing more; the
delete tool or Remove building takes it. Removing a station inside does not ask about the building.

## 5. Stages

1. **T-2217, the sim and the save** (own worktree: WASM interface and save format). `Network::buildings`,
   the `BLD1` block, edit ops to add, reshape, rename and remove a building with the price
   (`upgrades::BUILDING_PER_M2`, pays added area, refunds nothing) and the refusals (corners, crossing
   edges, 1 km, 1,000 m², overlap, no built station inside); members in `game/buildings.ts` (stations
   whose node is inside), joining complexes in `complexes.ts` and `ComplexBuilder`. Done when: cargo
   tests for a round trip, a save with no buildings encoding byte-identical, the price, and each
   refusal; `t146-check` with a building joining two complexes; smoke passes.

   **As built (T-2217, tree t2217).** Members are every station whose platform *touches* the outline
   (Anita, 2026-11-03), not only its middle: the platform's centre line inside or within 8 m of an
   edge (`building::TOUCH_M`, `BUILDING_TOUCH_M`: half a drawn box). The rule is in both places:
   `game/buildings.ts` over the drawn platform lines, for the complexes (passed to `buildComplexes`
   and `ComplexBuilder` as extra link pairs, `withBuildingLinks`; not passages, so no 2 m/s walk),
   and `track/building.rs` over the track model's own track, for `BuildingNoStation` and
   `building_members`; `checks/t2217-buildings.mjs` checks the two agree on the real save (21
   stations round Times Sq and Bryant Pk). A reshape pays for the floor the old outline did not
   cover (exact, by triangles), not the growth in area. Buildings count in the built value.
   WASM (corners are plane x, y pairs, flat): `add_building(corners, name)` (name "" takes the first
   constructed member's, before any slash) then `last_building()`, `building_charge()`;
   `reshape_building(id, corners)`, `rename_building(id, name)`, `remove_building(id)` (0 done, 1
   refused: `issue_kinds()` gives `BuildingCorners`, `BuildingEdgesCross`, `BuildingTooWide`,
   `BuildingTooSmall`, `BuildingOverlap`, `BuildingNoStation`, `NoSuchBuilding` or `Money:need:have`);
   `building_quote(id or 0xffffffff, corners)` = [m², US$M to pay, stations touching, constructed
   ones] and sets the issues to the refusal it would meet; `buildings_info()` (id, n, n x, y pairs per
   building), `building_name(id)`, `building_area(id)`, `building_members(id)`. The clock state
   carries `buildings` (`save.buildings` on the main thread). Not undoable. Stage 2 adds the worker
   messages, the money ledger entry (as `link_charge` is booked) and the words.
   **Merged after T-2193 (ferries), 2026-11-03**: `BLD1` follows `LFR1` in `encode`, `decode_read`

   `wasm/buildings.rs`'s `buildings_follow_the_ferries_in_the_save` checks a save with both. A port
   (a station with no track) reads as a member by its node's heading.
