## Method

Headless Chrome on my Vite (6154), a fresh profile per run. A save is imported into the slot,
Destinations on, then picked from the start menu twice (cold: no cached result; warm: the cached
result the first load wrote). Times are from the pick: "figures" when the zone in view has a city
view, "bubbles" when its bubbles are drawn, "final" the load's final round. Then, after the final,
bubbles switched None, Homes, Destinations, Total and back (time from the click to the frame after
`setBubbles`), then jumps into other cities (time to their bubbles) and switches right after each.
Scripts were scratch (`measure.mjs`, `loadtrace.mjs`, `ab.mjs`); JSON and logs in
`artifacts/trainworld/t1904/`. The Indonesia build held 10-12 cores throughout, so the real save
ran on 1 solving worker (`?demandWorkers=1`) and the big save on 2; read ratios, not absolutes.
"Before" on the real save is the code as it was; on the big save it is `?perfOff=daySums,prebuild`.

Saves: the real save (New York, 5 zones, 1 cluster) and Anita's "Tokyo Seoul Fukuoka Busan (from
anita.garden).save" (20 zones solved, 19 of them coarse, 3 clusters).

## Where the time went (before)

| Case | Real save (1 worker) | Tokyo Seoul Fukuoka Busan (2 workers) |
|---|---|---|
| Load, no cached result: figures / bubbles / final | 8.3 s / **61.3 s** / 104 s | 5.2 s / **12.0 s** / 60 s |
| Load, cached result: figures / bubbles / final | 3.4 s / **95.2 s** / 95 s | 2.9 s / **47.0 s** / 47 s |
| Switch to an end not yet built, after the final | 149-181 ms | 160-188 ms |
| Switch to an end built before | 6-19 ms | 6-16 ms |
| Jump into another city, to its bubbles | 0.7-1.1 s | 0.7-0.9 s |
| First switches right after a jump | 87-142 ms | 31-170 ms, Seoul's first **7.0 s** |

**The bubbles lagged the figures by a whole round, or to the final** (the biggest wait found). The
day shown is "average" by default. Each worker sends its bubbles' sums unasked with every round, but
only a weekday's (T-327, T-119); any other day was asked of the workers' live state, which is
already in the next round when the round is published, so the question waited for that next round
(T-1533's `liveAt`). On a load: the rough solve's round 0 (figures at 8 s) had no bubbles; the
question was answered after the rough solve had been superseded by the whole solve, so it came back
empty, retried, and the bubbles came only with the whole solve's round 1 (61 s; its round 0 at 44 s
was skipped the same way). The cached result keeps a weekday's bubbles only, so a warm load showed
none until the final (95 s). Trace: `loadtrace-real.json`.

**Switching ends** after the final is one bubble build in the zone's commuter worker: 120-165 ms of
worker time for New York and Tokyo, plus the frame. The legs per cell (`cellModes`) are already
kept per result, so no demand worker is asked; a build per end was kept too, but only until the
next result. Right after a jump a switch meets the zone's detailed solve (a coarse zone goes fine
after 2 s in view, T-1223's refine): its result replaces the figures and the ask for the new end's
legs starts over against the new result. The workers answered in 0.2 s, so the rest of Seoul's
7 s was most likely the bubbles' retry pause after an empty answer (1, 2, then 4 s). That is the
"sometimes".

**A city zoomed into after the load** shows its figures at once (every reached zone is solved,
coarse or fine) and its bubbles in 0.7-1.1 s (cells, legs, build); the detailed solve follows
without hiding them. Nothing waits for the world solve there.

**A city zoomed into during the load does wait for the whole solve.** The rough solve (T-1533)
covers the zone in view only while the whole solve waits for packs (on the big save until 21-27 s:
19 city packs open one at a time, then 51 market packs); a zone first in view after that has no
figures until the whole solve's round 0, which on the big save took 38 s of its 39 s (rounds 1-3:
under 1.5 s together, since 19 zones are coarse). Japan is one cluster through the Shinkansen
(Korea another), so T-402's "cluster in view first" does not help.

## What changed

All in the app (no sim change); final figures are untouched (the same sums, sent earlier).

- **The day shown's sums go with each round** (`workers/demand.worker.ts` `keepSums`, `view`
  message from `workers/demandClient.ts`): for the zone in view and the nearby zones, each worker
  also sends `sub_sums_day` and the everyday `cell_modes_day` of the day shown with every round, and
  keeps them for `subSums`; the client takes them as it does a weekday's. `?perfOff=daySums` for before.
- **The cached result keeps the day shown's bubbles** (`modes.day` in `demandCache.ts`), so a warm
  load of a game showing the average day draws them with the cached figures.
- **Every end's bubbles are built ahead and kept** (`map/commuterView.ts` `Built`, `buildRest`):
  while the result on show is final, the two ends not on show are built too, in the zone's own
  commuter worker, for the zone in view and the nearby zones; the builds survive a result whose legs
  did not change and change hands with the focus. A switch to an end built for the result on show
  shows it at once even while a newer result's legs are on their way (a refine), which then replace
  it. `?perfOff=prebuild` for before.
- **The status tag says which solve** (`ui/StatusTag.tsx`, `demandScope` in `game/demand.ts`): the
  zone in view alone (a rough solve, an edit's local solve, a refine) or a whole solve over several
  zones, counting the zones it has still to finish.

Same machine, same load from the other agents.

| Case | Real save before | Real save after | Big save before | Big save after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Load cold: bubbles (figures) | 61.3 s (8.3 s) | **8.6 s** (8.4 s); 7.5 s (7.2 s) on 2 workers | 12.0 s (5.2 s) | **8.0 s** (7.5 s); 5.5-6.2 s (5.3-5.9 s) |
| Load warm: bubbles (figures) | 95.2 s (3.4 s) | **3.2 s** (1.3 s) | 47.0 s (2.9 s) | **5.2 s** (2.6 s) |
| Switch after the final, every end | 6-181 ms | **9-25 ms** | 6-188 ms | **7-32 ms** |
| Switches right after a jump | 87-142 ms | 15-27 ms, two of 118 ms | 31-170 ms, Seoul's first 7.0 s | 8-73 ms, two of 118 ms; Seoul's first **6.1-6.8 s** still |

The two 118 ms switches are ends not yet built when picked (the jump's refine had just replaced
the result). **Seoul's first switch after a jump is not fixed.** It happens only while Seoul's
detailed solve runs (it is coarse; the solve lands about 6 s after the switch), on every run
before and after. The main thread is idle meanwhile (a CPU profile: 6.9 s idle of 8.1), no demand
worker is asked anything, and the bubbles of the picked end come with the detailed solve's result;
in one run the stand-in drew them within 5 ms but my sampler's frame wait still took 6.7 s, so a
headless frame stall is not ruled out. Filed as T-2046. Bus and Fukuoka, also coarse, were fine.

**Cost of the day's sums.** Working out a day's bubble sums for Tokyo takes a worker about 0.3 s a
round (its weekday's, sent already, the same), so a load of the big save showing the average day
spends about 0.3 s more a round per worker; four A/B loads alternating (`ab.mjs`) had the whole
solve's round 0 2-3 s later with them (39.6 and 38.3 s against 36.1 and 36.6 s; the machine's load
moves this much too). Only the zone in view and the nearby zones pay it, and none while the bubbles
are off. Figures are the same: smoke's real-save rail trips stay 11,297,375.

## Proposal: a city's demand during a big load without waiting for the whole solve

Not built: it changes the demand worker's round loop, which is more than this task's safe part.

**When it matters.** Only during a load (or a zone newly opened) of a big game, between the whole
solve starting and its round 0: on Tokyo Seoul Fukuoka Busan from about 21 s to 59 s of a cold
load with 2 workers, on all-China several minutes (T-1533: round 0 at 375 s). A city zoomed into
then has no figures or bubbles until round 0 lands for every zone. Before the whole solve starts
the rough solve already covers the zone in view, and after round 0 every zone has figures, so a
zoom is quick (under 1.1 s to bubbles, measured above).

**What would change.** Inside the whole solve's round 0, each worker takes the zone in view's own
populations first in its first period, estimates its other periods from them (T-1538's
`estimate`, a few ms) and sends that zone's figures and bubble sums at once as an estimate, as a
rough solve does; then it goes on with round 0 as now, solving those periods exactly in their turn.
The zone in view is read from the `view` message this task added, so a zoom mid-round moves the
next zone up among the populations not yet solved. Round 0 is free flow (no loads yet), each
population's periods are solved over again whole after an estimate (as after an edit's preview,
T-1538), and the figures are added in the registry's order, so round 0, the later rounds and the
final stay the same to the bit; a check would hash every final figure against a `?perfOff=` switch
of it, as t409 does.

**What a player would see.** During a big load, a city zoomed into shows its figures and bubbles
(marked "Trips here being recomputed…") a few seconds after its pack is open: its own first period,
1.7-2.7 s for Tokyo alone here, against up to 38 s now on this save and minutes on all-China. The
regional market's longer trips stay out of those first figures, as in a rough solve, until round 0
lands. Nothing else changes.

**Cost.** About a day of agent work in `demand.worker.ts` (the round loop and a one-zone result)
and `demandClient.ts` (showing one zone's estimate inside a whole solve's, as T-402's group ahead
is shown), plus the to-the-bit check on the real save and a big save. No extra memory; the CPU is
the same work in another order plus one zone's estimate. Risk: the round barrier's bookkeeping
(`go`, `wait` questions by round) has to treat that zone's early figures as no round at all.
