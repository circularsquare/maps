## What changed

- **Dark theme.** The pills were already white with the ink ring (`--net-ink`, #2b2b2b, in both themes), but on the
  dark background the ring merged into it, so a pill read as a white blob, and a faded one (another line selected)
  as a dark smudge. Now on a dark background (lightness of `--bg` under 0.5) a thin edge of the map's ink (the light
  `--map-ink`, 0.16 of a stroke) runs round the ring: white centre, black ring, light rim.
- **Local stops** are drawn small: fill half a stroke each side, outline 0.24 of a stroke, about 1.2 pitches across
  against 2. A local stop is a station one line stops at, or one a line passes without stopping while every line
  stopping is of one colour (28 St on Broadway: N, R and W stop, the Q passes). A line's end never is one, so
  termini keep the full pill. New York: 367 of 855 stations; Tokyo 1,096 of 1,447; all Japan 7,867 of 9,106. Names
  still sit where they would round a full pill: placed nearer, a name came within its own line's clearance and
  was dropped (New York's 7 px level fell from 724 names to 695); placed as before, 724 again, and other names
  keep off a small pill at its drawn size.
- **High-speed lines** (a line with high-speed trains, `fast`, T-817) get a stripe of the background colour along
  their middle, 0.34 of the stroke, so they read as two thin strokes of their colour. None of the saves on disk has a
  high-speed line (their fleets have no high-speed trains), so the screenshots mark some by hand (the shots script
  sets `fast` on the graph the page posts its worker).
- **Pills** are kept to a pitch of 0.625 device px, four times further out than 2.5.
- **Faint names** at eleven more levels below the first (pitch 0.62 to 3.5 px), for the big stations only: of the
  stations with 3 lines or more stopping, most lines first, each one with no station kept before it within 120 px at
  that level. They keep off each other only (over lines and pills, with the halo), drawn at 0.6 opacity. The levels
  proper are placed as before, as if these were not there.

## High-speed lines: what maps do

- Japanese atlases kept the old striped rail symbol (fuemaki, black and white alternating) for the shinkansen only,
  once ordinary lines went solid as the city networks got dense (Toyo Keizai,
  [https://toyokeizai.net/articles/-/422988](https://toyokeizai.net/articles/-/422988)).
- Ekispert's route map draws shinkansen as "orange and white striped lines"
  ([https://roote.ekispert.net/en/info/how-to-use_route-map](https://roote.ekispert.net/en/info/how-to-use_route-map)).
- JNR's 1964 operations map drew the new Tokaido Shinkansen as a thicker line
  ([https://serai.jp/?p=1058163](https://serai.jp/?p=1058163)); JR West's system map gives it its own colour
  ([https://transitmap.net/jr-west-isometric](https://transitmap.net/jr-west-isometric)).
- China's HSR maps: nothing authoritative found; the commercial maps tell services apart by colour.

Chosen: a stripe inside the stroke, the white of the striped symbols, made one continuous stripe. A thicker stroke
does not fit the diagram's fixed pitch (bundles would no longer sit a gap apart), the line's colour is the player's,
and alternating dashes read as a planned line or a line under construction on many maps.
