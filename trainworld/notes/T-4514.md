## Trains on top of each other (her question, 11-27)

- In the timetable, trains of two lines on one stretch can leave seconds apart or together: the spacing (`spacing::place`)
  only scores phases by how far trains fall short of half the smaller headway apart and picks the best of 24; lines at
  different rates drift, so some always meet. The track's minimum gaps (`SECTION_BASE_S` 70 s + v/0.6,
  `PLATFORM_MARGIN_S` 60 s) set how many trains an hour fit and an averaged delay (Kingman), not per-train separation.
  `MIN_GAP_SHARE` keeps one line's own trains apart where its pattern changes, not two lines'.
- Belgrade's morning (60 logged stretches): 247 of 1,764 gaps under 1 minute, 91 under 30 s. Trams 7L (every 5.8 min),
  12 (8.2) and 9L (13.0): 0 s at closest, 19 of 89 gaps under a minute; trams 5 (10.8) and 10 (15.3): 3 s; regional
  Novi Sad (46.7) beside Šid and Subotica: 10 s.
- The factor reads the timetable (as the board and chart show it). The map holds a train behind another on screen
  (the display guard; flat crossings from zoom 14, at most 240 s, DECISIONS 2026-11-03), drawn only.
- Pushing a train that comes within 60 / 90 / 120 s back to that gap: Belgrade's mean morning factor 1.39 -> 1.37 /
  1.34 / 1.31 (estimate from the logged phases). The long gaps a bunched pair leaves are most of the wait.

## What changed

- **The rule** (docs/demand-kernel.md "Lines on one track and stops are one service"): where two or more lines join
  as one service for a rider (`Search::merge_even`), the wait is the mean wait of riders turning up at random against
  their combined departures, the sum of the squared gaps over twice their sum. Demand takes it as a factor F on the
  evenly spaced wait, n x (sum of squared gaps) / (sum of gaps)², 1 exactly where every gap is the same; the merged
  service runs in the pool as if its trains an hour were divided by F (its wait spread F times as long), and riders
  split by the real trains an hour (`Search::pmf`). A class with only one line joining keeps that line's own wait.
- **Where F comes from** (`sim/src/track/span/gaps.rs`, new; the clock worker): per period, the runs of running train
  and tram lines that leave one station node for one at the next stop with rides within 30 s of the shortest
  (`SAME_RIDE_S`, which demand's `EVEN_RIDE_MIN` now is), of two or more lines: demand's `even_classes` by node. Each
  member is laid out from its weekday departure nearest the period's middle and that departure's headway; over one
  headway, round, when they all run at one (exact), else over the period's length with the gaps between departures
  only. Sent to hundredths (`TrackApi::shared_gaps`, beside `line_span`), in `span`'s layout, only for stops past 1.
  Hops no two lines run are skipped before any departure is laid out: 0.3 ms native for the whole NYC save.
- **Demand** (`Network::gap_f` -> `Graph::rn_gap`, `set_gaps` on all three populations): carried as `span` is
  (`Service::build` with busmerge's fold, `NetInputs` length check, `same_periods` per period, `period_net`, the
  skim key). App: the snapshot line's `gaps`, `packService`, the cluster slices and their `sig` and per-period
  `psig` (`shape` leaves it out, as it does every service value), the result cache's key, `withinNoise`.
- **The trigger** (her 5 s): `TrackApi::dirty_demand_lines` (`wasm/snapshot.rs`) gives, for an edit with no demand
  lines of its own, its lines whose shared stretch's wait moved more than 5 s (`MOVE_S`) from what `shared_gaps`
  last sent; services worked out after an edit's answer are checked the same way (`gaps_moved` in
  `clockFollowUp.ts`). So a row Offset or a re-spacing calculates trips only past 5 s, measured from what demand has,
  so small steps add up. Edits that already change demand are unchanged.

## Reasoning (player's action to what they see)

A player nudges a row of one of two interlined lines. Before: the board, chart and trains move; trips never do (the
wait was counted as if even). Now: the nudge's edit result names the line when the stretch's wait moved past 5 s; the
clock worker posts a network whose `gaps` changed; only the clusters and periods whose per-period key moved are
solved again. What could go wrong and how it is covered:
- Even lines changing figures: F is 1 to the bit for equal gaps (round method), sent as nothing; demand's path with
  no factor is the old one to the bit. Tested (`the_wait_is_the_real_gaps`, `a_shared_stretch_waits_by_its_real_gaps`).
- A nudge that never reaches demand: the 5 s check measures from what was sent, not the last edit, so ten 1 s steps
  calculate trips once they add up (`dirty_demand_lines_take_a_nudged_shared_stretch`).
- Stale skims: `rn_gap` is in `SkimKey` (the kernel test would read the even graph's skims otherwise).
- Kept results on a changed factor: `psig`, `sig`, `withinNoise` and the cache key carry it. A factor that moves
  under 1% is the same view to `viewDiff` (its `VIEW_TOLERANCE`, as for run times), so a 5 s move on an hourly
  stretch (0.55%) posts a network that keeps its results until the moves add up.
- Track and demand grouping disagreeing: both group by platform and next platform with the same 30 s; demand also
  splits platforms of a merged station by position, which is the station node here. The service takes the largest
  factor of its joined lines, so a mismatch errs towards the longer wait.

## Lies (DECISIONS 2026-11-27, T-4514)

trains held on the map; the service's largest factor whichever of its lines join.
