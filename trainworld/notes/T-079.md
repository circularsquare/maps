## Model: PIs underneath, clicks beside them

- The geometry is still the PI fit (`geom::fit`): PIs with a set radius each, straights between,
  arcs at the PIs. So exact lengths, one speed per arc, parallel tracks as arcs, splits, crossings,
  costs, saves and the renderer are all unchanged, and old saves look exactly as they did.
- `EdgeData::thru` (and `Network::edge_thru`) keeps the points the player clicked, with their
  levels. Geometry never reads them: they are what the reshaping squares show and what the PIs are
  refitted through when a square is dragged.
- `geom::through(points, dir0, dir1)` turns end nodes plus clicks into PIs:
  - heading at each click: the tangent of the circle through it and its two neighbours
    (`|u|² v - |v|² u`, which is the chord direction when they are collinear); a fixed end (a
    node with track already there) takes the node's heading; a free end mirrors its neighbour's
    heading across the chord (one arc on that stretch);
  - each pair of points: a biarc with equal tangent lengths (two arcs tangent to each other and
    to both headings); arcs turning under 1e-7 rad are left out, so lined-up points give a
    straight; no positive solution (a fixed heading pointing away from the next point) is
    refused as "turns back on itself";
  - each arc becomes one PI with its radius set, or several (same radius) when it turns more than a
    right angle, so `fit` never sees a turn near 180°; a click with no arc on either side becomes
    a PI of its own so its level still counts;
  - levels: the first arc of a biarc takes the level of the point it leaves, the second the level
    of the point it reaches, so a ramp is centred between two clicks;
  - quantising: PIs are put on the 1 mm grid, then each radius is set from the quantised legs so
    the arc's tangent is the biarc's less 2 mm. Neighbouring arcs can then never overlap; the gap
    is a straight of a few millimetres, and the track passes within about a centimetre of every
    click (tested). A near-straight arc's radius is mostly rounding then, and does not matter
    (far above the 1,796 m that allows top speed).
- The minimum radius is not special-cased: radii are set, so `fit` reports `RadiusTooSmall` as for
  any edge and the edit is refused with the usual plain sentence.
- `route_op` (drawing) uses it with the ends' headings (`leave_dir`, replacing T-023's lead-in
  PI); `set_edge_thru` / `preview_edge_thru` reshape an edge with the same end rule (an end where
  other track meets keeps its current heading, a free end is free). `set_edge_level` puts a stretch
  on one level without refitting. `edge_shape` gives the squares and the curves. The T-064 PI calls
  (`set_edge_pis`, `preview_edge`, `edge_pis`) stay for tests and tools; setting PIs clears the
  clicks.
- Splitting an edge (a station on blueprint track, a branch) hands each half the clicks on its
  side; one within 5 m of the split is the new node.

## Saves

- Track save format TWT3: an edge with clicks sets `tracks | 8` and lists them after its PIs (mm
  deltas and level). TWT2 still loads (no clicks), so every existing save and autosave loads as it
  was; `T-007-real-nyc.save` loads in the app with 1,647 edges, 4,500.6 route-km, all 109 lines
  running and none broken, drawn as before.
- Track from before T-079 has no clicks: its squares are the middles of its curves (and any PI
  with no curve). The first drag refits the whole stretch through those points, so its shape
  changes a little then; from then on it has clicks.

## UI

- Drawing: the squares are the clicks; curve labels sit at the middle of each curve (one label per
  curve, gentle curves over 5,000 m unlabelled); the tag gives length, cost multiplier and price
  (T-085).
- Reshaping: drag a square; the inspector lists the curves (radius, speed) and the stretch's level.
  The T-064 radius stepper is gone: with the track through the clicks, a radius follows from where
  the points are (move them apart for a gentler curve). DECISIONS 2026-10-09.
