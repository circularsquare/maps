# T-288 stage a: London from tier 0

2026-10-14. London (`lon`) built by `pipeline/tier0.py` from global sources only, registered and
playable (`market: -1`, `utcOffset` 0). Stage b, England's own inputs and tier 0 measured against
them, is T-360.

Scripts: `notes/t288_figures.py` (the figures below), `checks/t288-browser.mjs` (the browser

## Footprint

Whole ITL3 units (ONS, January 2025, generalised 20 m, clipped to the coast; 182 UK units), the
unit T-316 and research/world-sampler.md name for the UK. tier0.py reads a GeoJSON, so
`england.itl3_boundary` writes one from the units listed in the config (`boundary_file` may now be
a callable). Rule: Greater London's 21 units plus every unit touching it (11), plus West Sussex
(North East) for Gatwick and Medway (without it Gravesham and Swale left a hole and Sheppey came out
as an island). 35 units, 75 districts, 14.45M people, about 150 x 140 km. Inland except the
Thames estuary, so no sea outline. Luton and Southend airports are outside; Stansted (Uttlesford,
West Essex) is in.

## Decisions

- **Curve: Toronto's, borrowed.** tier 0's own fallback (power 1, length fitted to one median)
  was far too flat on Toronto (T-287: 17.5% of commutes misplaced against 11.3% with Toronto's
  curve; T-314). Toronto is the only fitted city outside the US and, like London, strongly centred.
  The model's straight-line commute with it: mean 10.7 km, median 6.3 km between 2 km zones. For
  T-360: fit London's own on the 2011 MSOA flows and compare.
- **Universities scaled to the national rate.** Before scaling the pack had 2.42M students: the 19
  institutions with Wikidata counts are mostly dense central buildings (Birkbeck 19k on 0.2 ha), so
  the median density for sites without a count was 1,925 a hectare and suburban campuses got
  50-150k students each (Imperial's Silwood Park 148k). Now population x the national tertiary
  rate (538k), the counts and areas only spreading it. In tier 0 for every city; Toronto's scratch
  build would go from +20% to about -20% against Ontario's files (not rebuilt).
- **Heathrow's O&D share 0.7** (judgement, as Pearson's 0.75); the other airports 1.
- **GHSL tiles cut short**: GHS-BUILT-V NRES R3_C18 is 9,000 pixels wide and R4_C18 8,000, starting
  east of their nominal corners; `tier0._read_tile_window` now places a tile by its GeoTIFF tie
  point. Full tiles read exactly as before, so Toronto's scratch build is unchanged.

| Figure | Pack | Published | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Greater London population | 8,800,247 | 8,799,728 (Census 2021) | scaled, exact |
| Before scaling, central boroughs | | | GHSL 12-19% high: Westminster -19%, Kensington and Chelsea -17%, Camden -17%, Hammersmith and Fulham -12% |
| Greater London jobs | 4.36M | about 6M (ONS workforce jobs, roughly) | -25 to -30% |
| Westminster and City jobs (TLI35) | 483k | about 1.3-1.4M (BRES, roughly) | about a third |
| Jobs within 1 / 2 / 5 km of Bank | 248k / 490k / 1.18M | the City alone is about 600k | far short |
| No-rail split of local trip legs (weekday) | walking 11.5%, driving 88.5%, 30.7M rides | | the game has no buses or existing rail, so every non-walk trip drives |
| Central test, Notting Hill Gate to Liverpool Street (12 stations, 10 trains an hour at the peak) | 262k riders a day | the whole Central line about 0.8-0.9M journeys a day | same order; train share 0.9% of London's legs |

The published job figures are from memory of ONS and BRES releases, not downloaded; T-360 sets
them properly from BRES.

What they say:

- **Population is fine** once scaled. GHSL alone puts too many people in the old dense centre
  (probably offices and shops read as homes).
- **Jobs are too few in the centre and in Greater London as a whole.** Two causes: the job total
  is the national rate x residents, which cannot give a commuter city's core its surplus of jobs
  over residents (London has more jobs than working residents; the ring fewer), and the
  height-weighted spread still draws too little to the City and West End. So commutes into the
  centre, and riders on central lines, are short. This is T-360's first fix.
- **Walking at 11.5%** is low for London (TfL's Travel in London puts walking as a main mode
  near a quarter to a third of trips), and the driving share is not comparable with London's real
  car share of commutes (about a quarter to a third in inner London) because the game's only
  alternatives are walking and the player's rail. Dispersed tier 0 jobs lengthen trips and cut
  walking; the borrowed curve and the US walking curve may also. Toronto's real pack walks 10.8%
  of commute legs, so 11.5% is in the game's normal range, not a London number.
- **The test line** carries the right order of riders for a central tube line with a short
  centre.
