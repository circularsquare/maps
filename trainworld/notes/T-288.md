## Footprint

Whole ITL3 units (ONS, January 2025, generalised 20 m, clipped to the coast; 182 UK units), the
unit T-316 and research/world-sampler.md name for the UK. tier0.py reads a GeoJSON, so
`england.itl3_boundary` writes one from the units listed in the config (`boundary_file` may now be
a callable). Rule: Greater London's 21 units plus every unit touching it (11), plus West Sussex
(North East) for Gatwick and Medway (without it Gravesham and Swale left a hole and Sheppey came out
as an island). 35 units, 75 districts, 14.45M people, about 150 x 140 km. Inland except the
Thames estuary, so no sea outline. Luton and Southend airports are outside; Stansted (Uttlesford,
West Essex) is in.

## Decisions

- **Curve: Toronto's, borrowed.** tier 0's own fallback (power 1, length fitted to one median)
  was far too flat on Toronto (T-287: 17.5% of commutes misplaced against 11.3% with Toronto's
  curve; T-314). Toronto is the only fitted city outside the US and, like London, strongly centred.
  The model's straight-line commute with it: mean 10.7 km, median 6.3 km between 2 km zones. For
  T-360: fit London's own on the 2011 MSOA flows and compare.
- **Universities scaled to the national rate.** Before scaling the pack had 2.42M students: the 19
  institutions with Wikidata counts are mostly dense central buildings (Birkbeck 19k on 0.2 ha), so
  the median density for sites without a count was 1,925 a hectare and suburban campuses got
  50-150k students each (Imperial's Silwood Park 148k). Now population x the national tertiary
  rate (538k), the counts and areas only spreading it. In tier 0 for every city; Toronto's scratch
  build would go from +20% to about -20% against Ontario's files (not rebuilt).
- **Heathrow's O&D share 0.7** (judgement, as Pearson's 0.75); the other airports 1.
- **GHSL tiles cut short**: GHS-BUILT-V NRES R3_C18 is 9,000 pixels wide and R4_C18 8,000, starting
  east of their nominal corners; `tier0._read_tile_window` now places a tile by its GeoTIFF tie
  point. Full tiles read exactly as before, so Toronto's scratch build is unchanged.

The published job figures are from memory of ONS and BRES releases, not downloaded; T-360 sets
them properly from BRES.

What they say:

- **Population is fine** once scaled. GHSL alone puts too many people in the old dense centre
  (probably offices and shops read as homes).
- **Jobs are too few in the centre and in Greater London as a whole.** Two causes: the job total
  is the national rate x residents, which cannot give a commuter city's core its surplus of jobs
  over residents (London has more jobs than working residents; the ring fewer), and the
  height-weighted spread still draws too little to the City and West End. So commutes into the
  centre, and riders on central lines, are short. This is T-360's first fix.
- **Walking at 11.5%** is low for London (TfL's Travel in London puts walking as a main mode
  near a quarter to a third of trips), and the driving share is not comparable with London's real
  car share of commutes (about a quarter to a third in inner London) because the game's only
  alternatives are walking and the player's rail. Dispersed tier 0 jobs lengthen trips and cut
  walking; the borrowed curve and the US walking curve may also. Toronto's real pack walks 10.8%
  of commute legs, so 11.5% is in the game's normal range, not a London number.
- **The test line** carries the right order of riders for a central tube line with a short
  centre.

Proposals for the next city:
1. **Read a region's OSM once**: one script reads the extract's points and multipolygons a single
   time into a cached file with every tag tier 0, `places.py` and the enrich scripts use (as
   `osm_water` does for water). The city build then takes minutes, and Overpass is not needed.
2. **Brief: wait in one piece.** One background wait until the build ends, no progress monitor;
   do docs and the browser check script in that time, but avoid waking repeatedly.
3. **Checklist step 1: draw the outline first** (a 10-second picture of the units) and look for
   holes and islands before building.
4. **Parameterise `t288-browser.mjs`** (city id, centre, a water crossing, a station list) so the
   next city passes arguments rather than copying the file.
5. **Brief: give the published reference figures** (population, jobs in the core, mode shares,
   one real line's riders) with sources, so the sanity table is not from memory.
6. **Reading**: the brief's list was right but could name sections: world-sampler.md's London
   column, world.md's "Local time", T-041's London paragraph. adding-a-city.md and tier0.py were
   the parts that mattered.

# T-360 stage b: England's inputs, and tier 0 measured against them

2026-10-15. `lon` rebuilt with BRES jobs, Census 2021 LSOA population, GIAS schools and a curve
fitted on the 2011 flows; universities still tier 0 (HESA is T-382). tier0.py gained hooks a
country module fills: `population`, `jobs`, `schools` (config keys, tier0.py docstring); England's
are in `england.py`. Scripts: `notes/t360_jobs.py`, `t360_curve.py`, `t360_pop_schools.py`; output
in `../artifacts/trainworld/T-360-*.json` and `.log`.

**A bug found on the way**: `tier0.zones_of` folded zones south of the origin to far-off keys, so
every gravity it fed (tier 0's fallback fit, `t287_compare.py`, `t288_figures.py`) had no
commuting across the origin's east-west line. Fixed. The game's own gravity (Rust) was never
affected. Rerun on Toronto's real inputs: its own curve 4.9% misplaced between municipalities,
the US curve 15.4%, T-287's fallback (d^-1 exp(-d/95 km)) 19.6%; T-287's ranking holds, its
numbers (11.3%, 15.7%, 17.5%) do not. T-288 stage a's "mean 10.7 km" came from the same code.

## Population and schools

Census 2021 usual residents by LSOA (TS001), spread inside each LSOA by GHSL: tier 0's
district-scaled GHSL misplaces 0.2% between districts, 3.3% between 2 km zones, 13.4% between
LSOAs (GHSL alone: 2.5%, 4.1%, 13.5%). The densest fifth of LSOAs gets 26% too few people, the
middle three 1-12% too many (sorted by the census's own density, which exaggerates the gap).
Small at the game's scale; used since it is free once the LSOA polygons are in.

GIAS (open establishments, 2026-10-14): 5,485 with pupils, 2.39M pupils; tier 0 2.21M at 5,124
OSM sites (-8%; GIAS includes sixth forms and nurseries). Misplaced 14.2% between districts,
21.0% between 2 km zones. No tier 0 rule change: OSM school coverage, not the rule, is the gap.

Proposals:
1. **Measure scripts share one cached cell-to-unit table** (cells to LSOA, district, 2 km zone),

2. **Start every independent long job at once, then one wait** (the brief's rule, applied across

3. **Every country's stage b reruns Toronto's curve check** with the fixed `zones_of`; nothing
   else to change in the list.
4. **The checklist's sanity table names a reference line with its source** (Central: 301M a year,
   2019), as proposal 5 of stage a asked.
