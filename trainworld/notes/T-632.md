# T-632: walking feels too strong to play against (2026-10-17)

Anita: "walking is still a bit overpowered. gameplay-wise it doesn't feel like there's much way to
beat it for small distances, and it means a good 25% of demand is just completely unreachable."

## Against the data

The model walks at or under the surveys, not over: New York's commutes walk 5.35% (T-587; ACS for
the metro about 6%), its everyday legs 22-24% against NHTS's 26% (errands) and 35% (social) (T-509
section 14). Rail does take walkers (T-558: its share of the pair's walking), but on a trip under
about 1.5 km the walk to and from stations and the wait cost more than walking the whole way, in
the model as in life.

## Options (Anita picks)

1. **Show it differently, model unchanged**: walking trips short enough that no train could win them
   (under about 1.5 km, say) are left out of the demand rail competes for in bubbles, bars and the
   headline share, or drawn as their own quiet part. Honest, and the bar the player plays against
   becomes winnable. Proposed.
2. **Give rail the real short-trip help it has**: several entrances on a long station (a shorter
   walk to the platform), so rail wins some 1-2 km trips in dense places. Realistic but small.
3. **Walk less than the surveys** for game feel: against the realism rule.
4. **Leave it to the deep calibration** (T-621), which fits walking to the surveys per country:
   in New York that would add walking, not take it away.
