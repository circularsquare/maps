# T-3129: snap to road for normal track, design

Design only, 2026-11-11, agent roadsnap. Anita's TODO (2026-11-10): "snap to road for normal track".

## What the player sees and does

- The track bar's snap buttons (keep placing, real network, beside track) get one more for rail: **Snap to roads**,
  off by default and remembered. It turns off Snap beside track and Snap to real world network, as those two turn
  each other off today. Tram keeps its own Snap to streets.
- While it is on, the roads rail may follow are tinted over the map (the tram's green hint, its own colour). A first
  click within 100 m of one lands on it. The second click places a route along the roads to the pointer, at the
  level, track count, type and target speed picked, in one undo step. Holding Ctrl draws by hand, as Ctrl already
  means "no snap" for rail.
- The ghost follows the road while the pointer moves; the tag is the usual one (length, price, tightest curve,
  lowest speed limit). A pointer far from any road draws an ordinary segment.

Real lines this is for (from memory, not checked): Chicago's Blue and Red lines in expressway medians, Washington's
Orange Line on I-66, LA's C Line on I-105, Cologne-Frankfurt high speed beside the A3, Bangkok's BTS over
Sukhumvit Road, New York's IRT under Broadway (cut and cover).

## The shape of the problem

Tram street snap, the real-network snap and this are one tool with three settings: a line from somewhere (streets,
roads, a real railway), fitted as corners by the kind's curve rules, placed as one route with corners. So the build
should turn `StreetSnap` into a road snap with a profile per use (which roads, turn cost, tolerances, tight or wide
curves), in the same way `TRACK_KINDS` holds each kind of track. Trams become one profile and rail a second.

## Design choices, each with a recommendation

1. **Where the track sits.** The road's centre line at every level: over it at +1, under it at -1, on it at ground.
   On a divided road (two carriageways) the search follows one carriageway. Moving it to the median is stage 4.
   *Recommend:* centre line now. Beside the road (a set offset, as QGIS and the Cities: Skylines parallel tools do) is
   a question for Anita.
2. **Which roads.** Motorway, trunk, primary, secondary, tertiary. No minor streets, slip roads (links) or service
   ways: rail runs straight through an interchange, and a train on minor streets is a tram. *Recommend:* these.
3. **Curves too tight for the target speed.** Rail cannot turn a street corner the way a tram does. Each corner
   gets the widest curve, up to the target speed's radius, that stays within 20 m of the road (else 40 m, the
   tram's tolerance cascade). A curve that comes out slower than the target speed is slow, never refused, and the
   tag's lowest limit shows it. This is the real snap's fit (`Tracer` without `tight()`), with the kind's own
   minimum (25 m rail, 50 m high speed, 8 m more for quad's inner pair). Roughly:

   | Road turn | Curve within 20 m | Within 40 m |
   |---|---|---|
   | 90 degrees (a city corner) | 68 m, 31 km/h | 137 m, 44 km/h |
   | 45 degrees | 263 m, 61 km/h | 525 m, 87 km/h |
   | 20 degrees | 1,316 m, 137 km/h | 2,630 m, 194 km/h |

   A motorway's own curves (about 700 m and up) suit about 100 km/h rail, and a city grid suits 20-45. The tram
   lesson (T-2997): the refusals came from the ends, not the middle. So the lead-in from existing track gets room
   for its curve onto the road, and corners near the end are dropped where they must be (`tram_ends`, which already
   takes a radius). Carrying on from the last road route never U-turns (`startDir`). *Recommend:* follow the road
   and slow the curve. The other way, keeping the speed and leaving the road, is what drawing by hand (Ctrl) is for.
4. **Which way the search goes.** The tram search adds 200 m for each turn. For rail a turn should cost **the time
   lost slowing for it, as metres at the target speed**. A right-angle corner (44 km/h) then costs about 20 m at a
   60 km/h target, 250 m at 100, and 1.1 km at 160. Slow lines will turn at a grid corner; fast ones stay on the
   big smooth road. *Recommend:* this. It is one function in `RoadGraph.turnPath` in place of the fixed `TURN_M`.
5. **Price.** No new price rule. One fix is needed: today a road counts as crossed wherever track meets its line, at
   any angle (`roads.rs` `cross`). Ground track along a road would pay US$10M (US$30M on a motorway) each time the
   fit wanders across the road's centre line. *Recommend:* a road meeting track at under 20 degrees is not a
   crossing, for all track. That fixes hand-drawn track along roads too, and changes old saves' prices a little.
   With that fix, ground track in a motorway's median pays no crossings, because the cross roads go over on bridges
   (bridges count none). It comes to about US$37.5M a km against US$145-165M through city blocks. That is honest:
   it is why Chicago built in its medians. Whether following a road should also make a viaduct or cut and cover
   cheaper is a question for Anita.
6. **Track counts, levels, ramps.** Single, double and quad work as the real snap's route already does
   (`realReq` sends single, quad, high speed, ramp and joins; T-3137 joins at the ends). Every corner is at the level
   picked. Water under a ground route is bridged as any long route is (`bridge_long`). Unlike street tram, rail never
   runs on a road bridge at grade. A road through a tunnel is a hill for ground track, priced by terrain, and the
   player picks -2. The ramp switch's ramp over track and the automatic flyover at a new junction (T-3092) should work
   on a many-corner route, but neither was checked on the real snap's routes, so stage 3 checks both.
7. **Ghost and refusals.** The tram's: the road line stands in until the fit is ready, and the last fitted ghost
   stays until the next one is ready (T-2996). Real snap still blanks its ghost on each move; it is the same code,
   so the same fix goes in. Refusals reuse the bus and tram text: no road path, too long, and the fit failing even
   at 40 m.

## Reused from tram street snapping

`BusRoads`/`RoadGraph.turnPath` (directed search with turn cost, the back-turn and median rules, `startDir`),
`StreetSnap`'s spot, rest, fit cascade, ghost-keeping and click-waits-for-route logic, the router worker's `trace`
message and `Tracer`, `tram_ends`, the green hint layer (`setRoadHint`), the Ctrl handling in `streetSwitch`, and from
the real snap, the rail route request. New: the rail profile, a time-based turn cost, motorways in the street search,
a `trace` variant with the target speed's radius and no `tight()`, and the crossing angle rule. Not needed for rail:
`StreetTram`, `street_pieces`, or any new track kind or save field.

## Questions for Anita

1. **Should following a road make track cheaper?** A viaduct over a road or cut and cover under one costs the same
   as anywhere else today. Real metros follow streets because the land is public. My pick: no discount for now, so
   the tool only helps with drawing. Ground track in a motorway median is already cheap once the crossing fix lands.
2. **At ground, on the road or beside it?** My pick: on its centre line now (a divided road's median later), the
   same rule at every level. Beside the road (about 15 m off, on the side of the first click) looks more like real
   ground rail along an arterial road and is stage 4.

## Elsewhere

- **NIMBY Rails**: no road snap. It has parallel track parented to another track, offset 0-1,000 m, flipped with F;
  curves lower the speed limit and the ghost shows radius and length. https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/Track ,
  https://wiki.nimbyrails.com/How_to_build_track
- **Transport Fever 2/3**: trams are tied to roads (players complain); rail does not follow roads, and rail in a
  motorway median is a long-standing wish. Transport Fever 3 adds snapping guide lines for parallel roads and
  tracks. https://steamcommunity.com/app/1066780/discussions/0/2519149567495323668 ,
  https://www.transportfever3.com/news/dev-blog-episode-3-infrastructure/
- **Cities: Skylines**: elevated metro over a road means building along it by hand. Network Multitool's "Create
  parallel" mode lays a network beside an existing one with an offset, a height and a side.
  https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/1400711138
- **Subway Builder**: cost is base x elevation x track count (at grade 0.3, elevated 0.8, cut and cover 1.0, tunnel
  2.0). I found no street rule or road snap, but only through search summaries: the wiki page answered 502.
  https://wiki.subwaybuilder.com/index.php/Construction
- **Map editors**: JOSM's Follow line (F) extends a new way along an existing one's nodes; QGIS tracing follows
  existing lines with an offset setting (positive right, negative left).
  https://josm.openstreetmap.de/wiki/Help/Action/FollowLine ,
  https://docs.qgis.org/3.4/en/docs/user_manual/working_with_vector/editing_geometry_attributes.html
- **Rail design software**: Civil 3D fits arcs and straights to a line ("best fit") and warns on curves below the
  design speed's criteria rather than refusing them, the same choice as 3 above.
  https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2023/ENU/Civil3D-UserGuide/files/GUID-CC6B6C3F-57CC-41DE-9498-F3D969AD37A0.htm
