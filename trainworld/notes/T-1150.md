Investigation 2026-10-24 (sections 1-3), then Anita's call the same day: both homes fixes built in
code, the jobs blend held, no rebuild until she says. The code is in (section 4); the China rebuild
is planned to the command (section 5, task T-1184). No pack, cache or registry was touched. The

## For Anita

**What is wrong.** Shanghai's homes are right by district (the pack scales to the 2020 census for
each of the 16 districts) and right at the 2 km scale on the whole, but inside each district they
come in sharp-edged blocks: one patch of a few km² at 150-275k people a km², the next at a tenth of
that. 68 cells are over 100k a km²; the real densest township (Laoximen) is 63k. Measured against
the 2020 census by township (225 townships), **13% of Shanghai's homes sit in the wrong township**;
79 of 215 townships are more than 30% off (Lujiazui +91%, Huamu +63%, Caohejing's development zone
86k against 4.9k; Youyi Road -90%, Puxing Road -63%).

**Why.** GHSL's population grid is built by spreading each of *its own* census units (for China,
older township counts) over the residential building volume inside it. So inside each GHSL unit
people per cubic metre is one flat number, and next door it can be ten or thirty times different
(`sha-ghsl-units.png`, right panel: the patchwork). Our district scaling cannot fix steps that lie
inside a district. Beijing has the same patchwork (284k a km² at worst, 48 cells over 100k).
Moscow's units are its districts and step gently; Mumbai's are large and flat; London and Paris
are fine.

A second, separate cause: GHSL's split between homes and other buildings misses Chinese and
Russian business districts. It calls only 4-28% of the building volume non-residential at
Xujiahui, People's Square, Jing'an Temple and Lujiazui, 3% at Beijing's Financial Street, 26% at
Moscow City. So office towers count as homes (part of Lujiazui's +91%) and the jobs proxy's
building part does not see them: Lujiazui gets 83k jobs within 1 km in the pack, which looks far
short for 285 office towers, and Moscow's jobs come out as even as T-791 says.

**Proposed fix, homes (general).** Keep GHSL, but hold each 100 m pixel's people per residential
cubic metre within a factor of 2 of its 2 km neighbourhood average, then re-spread. Where GHSL's
units are sound nothing moves (London, Paris, Moscow, Mumbai shift 0.1-0.4 points); in Shanghai the
worst blocks soften: wrong-township share 13.0% to 12.2%, densest cell 275k to 133k a km², cells
over 100k from 68 to 20. Pictures: `sha-pop-cells.png` (now) against `sha-fix-k2-pop-cells.png`.

**Proposed fix, homes (China).** Spread the 2020 census by township instead of by district, as
T-897 already suggested (the township counts sit in helper1m's China table, from the ASPECT grid).
That puts every township right by construction and, with the fix above inside each, leaves 8 cells
over 100k a km² (`sha-fix-township-k2-pop-cells.png`). It is the real fix for Shanghai's "weird"
homes; the general fix alone only softens them.

**Jobs.** Shanghai's jobs are already right by district (economic census). Inside districts nothing
I tried helps Lujiazui, because GHSL's volume there is low too. For packs whose jobs are the proxy
alone (other Chinese metros, Moscow), half the proxy's building part could come from "built
intensity" (all building volume, weighted by how built-up the surrounding 1 km is): on Shanghai's
16 districts the proxy's misplaced share falls from 14.7% to 10.7%, Paris (INSEE) from 17.1% to
14.7% at 2 km, London (BRES) is unchanged (26.3% to 26.7%), and Moscow's centre gains a little
(5.8% to 7.4% of jobs within 3 km of the Kremlin). This is a demand-rule change, so it is yours to
call; I would do the homes fixes first.

**Cost.** Code: a day's agent work (tier0.py and china.py, about 1-2M tokens). Data: GHSL total
volume tiles for every tier 0 tile, about 69 tiles, roughly 3 GB. Rebuild: every pack built on a
changed cache; done for China only, Shanghai plus the 80 Chinese metro rows and their joint solve,
about 5 machine hours at 10 cores (T-897's waves ran 1-4 h each); the rest of the world can pick
it up at its next rebuild.

## 2. The causes

1. **GHSL's input units.** GHS-POP R2023A spreads each input census unit's count over the
   residential built volume (GHS-BUILT-V total less NRES) inside it, so GHS-POP over residential
   volume is flat inside each unit (`*-ghsl-units.png`, right panel; no person sits on a pixel
   without residential volume, in all five cities checked). In Shanghai neighbouring units differ by
   up to 30x (people per 1,000 m³, 5th-95th percentile 0.5-10); Beijing the same; Moscow's units
   (its districts) step gently from 2 in the centre to 10 at the edge; Mumbai's ratio is one value
   over most of the city (11.4-11.8). Raw GHSL is also 24% high in Shanghai and 30-56% high in the
   central districts, which the district scaling removes; it cannot remove steps inside a district.
2. **The residential/non-residential split.** GHSL's NRES share within 1 km: Lujiazui 28%,
   People's Square 12%, Jing'an Temple 12%, Xujiahui 4%, Zhangjiang 36%, Baosteel 51%; Beijing
   Guomao 29%, Financial Street 3%; Moscow City 26%, the Kremlin 20%; Mumbai BKC 38%, Nariman
   Point 51%. Office towers become homes and drop out of the jobs proxy's building part.
3. **Not the cause:** the caps (`PLACE_JOBS_CAP`, `CELL_JOBS_K`) do not bind in Shanghai (its jobs
   use the table path) nor in Moscow (no jobs moved, per its header); the height cap (40 m) and
   power barely matter (raising the cap to 150 m moves Lujiazui from 83k to 86k); merging touches
   only sparse cells.

## 4. The code (2026-10-24)

- **`pipeline/tier0.py`** (fix 1, every tier 0 pack): `PRODUCTS["tot_v"]` is GHS-BUILT-V total.
  `signals()` reads non-residential volume, then total volume (residential = total less NRES, as
  float32), then GHS-POP; `bounded_population()` holds each pixel's people per residential m³
  within `POP_BOUND` (2) of its `POP_BOUND_SIGMA_M` (2 km) Gaussian mean, in bands of 1,024 rows
  with the filter's 80-pixel reach each side so a desert zone's window costs one array more, not
  five; pixels with no residential volume keep GHS-POP. `grid_to_cells` takes a list of grids and
  does the H3 pass once for both, so the cache holds `pop` (bounded, scaled to GHS-POP's total over
  the pack's cells) and `pop_ghs` (as published). `pop_ghs` in a cache marks it as bounded;
  `ghsl_pop_name()` names the source accordingly. A missing total-volume tile (open sea) leaves
  GHS-POP as it is. Signals for Shanghai took 15 s, Beijing 11 s, London 28 s.
- **`pipeline/china.py`** (fix 2): `python pipeline/china.py townships <helper1m adm4 folder>`
  wrote `data/raw/tier0/china_townships/townships.fgb` (43,394 townships with a 2020 count,
  1,409,778,746 people; 261 townships without one left out, their cells keep GHSL).
  `townships(boundary)` gives each township meeting an outline and its share inside: its GHSL
  people inside over its GHSL people (`osm_units._people_many`), or its area share where GHSL has
  none, since coastal townships' 2018 outlines reach out to sea. `township_population` spreads
  township count x share by GHSL's residential volume (`res_v`, which tier 0's cache now keeps per
  cell; a township with none, by the bounded GHSL; the orchestrator's call, 2026-10-24, replacing
  the bounded GHSL first built here); cells in no township keep the bounded GHSL at the pack's
  census-to-GHSL ratio. A signal cache without `res_v` stops the hook with a message. Shanghai's hook then holds it to the 16 districts' bulletin totals;
  `plan_hooks` gives every mainland metro row (module china) the township hook. The 660 zones
  (module none) stay on GHSL, bounded once rebuilt (T-1185).
