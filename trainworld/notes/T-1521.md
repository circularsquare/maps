starting curve and the plane from published fares, in PPP dollars, and read T-1431's shares with

## As built

- **Game dollars are US dollars at market rates.** A PPP dollar would show: Beijing - Shanghai at
  $158 on a ticket that is about $83, Mumbai's suburban train at $0.82 on one that is $0.11.
- **The income rule reads GDP per head at market rates** (World Bank NY.GDP.PCAP.CD, 2025;
  `countryIncome.json` `usd`; Taiwan $34,400 from the IMF; 25 territories with none take their PPP
  figure times their subregion's median ratio). A fare dollar costs 4.4 x (GDP per head /
  $90,000)^-0.7 minutes: the US 4.4, the UK 6.0, Japan 8.4, China 16.3, India 51, Nigeria 89. Each
  country's fare scale is its income over India's to the same 0.7: the US 11.6, the UK 8.5, Japan
  6.1, Korea 6.2, China 3.1, Nigeria 0.6. The player's scale goes to 19.9 (was 9.9).
  - Why 0.7: price levels rise with income (India's PPP factor is a quarter of its market rate), and
    at market rates 0.7 gives each country the same minutes per unit of its own money as T-1277's
    PPP rule to the 0.5 did (India 51 minutes a dollar, 53 before; China 16.3 against 16.3). So
    every local fare weighs as before; only the printed dollars change. It is also HEATCO's
    elasticity and T-1277's first proposal.
  - Income groups inside a country keep 0.5 (`GROUP_E`): prices are the same within a country.
- **Rail's starting curve**: New York $1.50 + $0.10 a km to 20 km, then $0.25 a km. Nodes in India
  terms $0.1293, $0.3017, $2.0259 at 0, 20, 100 km (`START_NODES`).
- **Longer trips weigh every mode's money at 0.4** of the country's (and income group's) minutes a
  dollar (`LONG_TRIP_MONEY` in `income.ts`, folded into the groups' factors, so the car, the plane
  and rail alike). US DOT's 2016 value-of-time guidance: an hour of local travel $14.10 (all
  purposes), intercity $20.40 by road and $47.20 by air or high-speed rail; 0.4 sits between 0.69 and
  0.30. Commutes and outings between zones, and every local layer, keep their weights.
- **The longer trip's car costs its countries' fuel and tolls** (`countryIncome.json` `car`, the two
  places' mean, sent on each market row as `cu`): gasoline at the pump x 8.5 litres a 100 km, plus
  the motorway toll a km where tolls go by distance. No upkeep: riders weigh what the trip costs
  them. Was $0.15 a km everywhere (AAA fuel and upkeep).
- **The plane is one world fare, $100 + 5 cents a km** (T-712's figure again).
- **Old saves** load with their home country's fares weighing exactly as before: the curve times
  `oldCurveFactor(home)` (0.242 for a New York save), a scale the player set keeping its factor over
  the income's; the save is then marked `basis: "usd"`. Other countries in an old save move by the
  scales' rounding (about 1%).

What reads off, and why (nothing tuned):
- **The car swung from too strong to too weak outside the US.** Real fuel and tolls are 1.5-3x the
  old $0.15 a km in Japan and Europe, and at market rates those countries weigh a dollar at 6-8
  minutes (5.4-5.6 at PPP), so the car's money is two to four times heavier there. In the US the
  car got cheaper ($0.104 against $0.15, no upkeep) and stays too strong (88% against 64% on New
  York - Washington). The harness reads only zones near a station, which T-1431 and T-712 found puts
  the car too low on 250-650 km pairs; in the game every zone counts.
- **The plane is too dear in China and Japan, too cheap in the US.** At one world fare, Beijing -
  Shanghai's flight is $154 against a real $114 average, while rail is about right ($74 against $83):
  the plane gets 0% where it has about 14%. Tokyo - Fukuoka goes the other way (51% rail against
  11%): its flight is $144 against the real ¥15-27k, and the 0.4 makes the 3 h 40 min saving count.
  A base sweep would pick $60 (error 1.17), but that is a fit, not a published fare.
- **0.4** is what brings Paris - Marseille (72%), New York - Washington's rail of all (12% against
  20%) and London - Edinburgh's plane in. The sweep would pick 0.3 (error 1.71 against 2.13); not
  built.

Proposals, for Anita: (1) the plane scaled by the departing country's price level (published
fares at market rates run US 1.5x, Japan and China 0.7x the formula), one more column per country;
(2) leave the car as built until a corridor run with every zone (T-712's harness) shows where it
sits, since this harness is known to read the car low. Round 3 below runs both.

**2. The plane at one world fare against one scaled by price level.** Scaling by price level
improves the error (1.42 against 1.80), but not where it was meant to:
- It helps Europe (Paris - Lyon 86%, Marseille 42%, London - Edinburgh 35%, all toward published) and
  the US's rail of rail + air (77% and 63% against 79% and 53%, though there the plane's share of
  all goes to 0-1% and the car takes everything).
- It hurts China and Japan: the scaled plane is cheaper there ($99 for Beijing - Shanghai against

- So the world fare's fault is less the price than the access: corridor rail is low wherever it runs

**3. Car upkeep.** Adding $0.073 a km takes the car down everywhere: Tokyo - Osaka 19% to 7%,

Japan and Europe farther; leaving it out (as built) fits better overall.

**What it says about round 2.** Over every zone the built model is closer to the published figures

in every variant).
