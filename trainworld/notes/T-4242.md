# T-4242: weekend riders follow the weekend's trains (design, agent weekenddesign, 2026-11-25)

**Summary for Anita**
- Why it happens: weekend riders are the weekday's riders, scaled up or down by purpose. They are worked out on the weekday's trains only, so a line with no weekday trains at midday has no midday riders to scale, and its weekend trains run empty.
- The design: wherever a city's weekend trains differ from its weekdays in a period, trips are calculated again for the weekend on the weekend's own trains, the same way as weekdays. Saturday and Sunday share that calculation, each scaled by its own trips. Where weekend trains match the weekdays, nothing changes and nothing extra runs.
- Cost on your setting (3 workers): with weekend percents on every line, a load calculates trips about 45-55% longer (New York 78-81 s to about 115-120 s, Tokyo 101-105 s to about 145-150 s), and an edit up to twice as long. One line's weekend change alone costs little.
- On one worker (phones, your Asia save) it is up to twice as long (Asia's whole calculation 220-243 s to about 450-490 s). Memory: about 7% more per worker when every weekend differs.
- What you'd see: the short-turn gets weekend riders. Cutting weekend trains now loses weekend riders and fares (today it only crowds them). A weekend edit shows "calculating trips". The average day and the goal share move; points don't.
- The lie: Saturday and Sunday share one calculation's crowding. The first weekend change in a city also moves its other lines' weekend riders, as they switch from weekday crowds to weekend ones (the build measures how much).
- Not a new demand factor (the trips people want are the same), but it reverses two of your calls: "no second solve" for weekends (10-26) and "fewer weekend trains crowd riders rather than lose them" (11-12, 11-17).
- Yours: go ahead or not, and whether the cost is fine on saves that change weekends. Building it is about 10-12 hours in 9 stages.

## The design: weekend periods

The demand model solves one service, the weekday's, and gets every kind of day by scaling trips. Since T-3521 there are two services. The general fix: solve each service that differs, and let each kind of day read the solve of the trains it runs. Weekdays and superholiday days read the weekday periods; Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays the weekend periods where a city has them, else the weekday periods as now.

- **Where**: per cluster and period, a weekend period exists when any line of the cluster runs other weekend trains an hour or span there (past `DEMAND_TPH_EPS`, as `demand_moved`). No weekend period: the weekend reads the weekday period, exactly as today, at no cost. A save with no line's weekends changed (`rest_solved` false) runs nothing new and keeps every figure to the bit.
- **What it reads**: each line's weekend trains an hour as demand reads them, through the weekend share-out (`fleet_on`, `place_tph_in`; going around it would repeat T-4227), the weekend span from a weekend day's departures, and `on_train` counting either service. Stop times stay the weekday's (weekend trains run their slot's timetable, T-3521). The boarding account's arrival masks are made per kind of day's trains.
- **How**: a weekend period is solved exactly as a weekday one: free flow, up to three crowding rounds, the room fit, on the weekend's trains. Its crowding, left-behind waits and room fit read the weekend's riders: each population's loads times the weekend's share of them in that period (each purpose's mean of the Saturday and Sunday factors). Not through `period_share`: that also sets which commute pairs are dealt (kernel.rs 157, mode.rs 146 and 633), so a weekend period with the weekday's trains would deal other pairs and differ.
- **Days**: Saturday is the weekend period's purposes times Saturday's factor over the weekend mean, Sunday likewise, a public holiday as Sunday. The average day blends five weekdays and the two, as now. The boarding account, line riders today, money and fares follow (they read per day already).
- **Queries**: bubbles, stations, selections, transfers and trip lengths on a weekend or the average day read the weekend periods the same way (one helper that gives a day's periods and factors, used by the ~30 `_day` functions).
- **Edits**: a weekend-only edit now starts a solve of the weekend periods it moves (weekday periods kept, T-124). A weekday train edit moves the weekends at a percent too, so their periods re-solve with the weekday ones.
- **Workers**: weekend periods are dealt after the weekday ones in the same order (slot k of 10 to worker k mod n), each worker solving its weekday periods first. One final for both.

## The lies

1. Saturday and Sunday share one weekend calculation, crowded at their mean riders (errands 1.39 and 1.05, social 1.54 and 1.67): Saturday's crowding a little light, Sunday's a little heavy.
2. Until a city's weekend trains differ in a period, its weekend keeps the weekday's crowded choices scaled (today's rule, kept so the default costs nothing). The first difference re-solves that period at weekend crowds, so lines the player didn't touch move on weekends too, mostly those crowded on weekday peaks (they gain weekend riders, as weekend peaks are lighter). The build measures it: one line 100% -> 95% on the real New York save, the lines that move most.
3. Weekend run times stay the weekday's (T-3521's, unchanged).
4. Superholiday days stay the weekday solve's, scaled (unchanged).

The alternative to lie 2, weekend periods crowded at weekday volumes, keeps the first change smooth but has the room fit turn away riders on cut weekend trains who would fit at weekend volumes (a line at 100% of rated on weekdays is at 143% at 70% weekend trains), while the drawn weekend trains run half empty. I'd not take it.

## Complicating factor?

No. Nothing new goes into the trips people want (gravity, purposes, day rates and timing are unchanged); riders choose among the trains that run that day, as weekdays already do. It does reverse her recorded calls: DECISIONS 2026-10-26 (T-266, "no second solve"), 2026-11-17 (T-3521's lie "riders don't react to weekend service"), and docs/demand-timing.md's "(Anita, 2026-11-12, assumed) fewer weekend trains crowd them rather than lose them". It also changes the weekend's money: cutting weekend trains stops being nearly free fares. So it is hers to say yes to.
