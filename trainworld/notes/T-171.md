# T-171: PATH's riders and the border factor (diagnosis)

Diagnosis only, 2026-10-13. No pack, pipeline, sim or doc change. Scripts in notes/:
`t171_gravity.py` (the gravity variants against LODES; `--variants k,pair,kd,metro,water,waterfit`),
`t171_net.rs.txt` (the scratch real-network bin, a copy of realnet with the everyday layer,
route-node boardings and all transfers; kept as text, it is not in the crate; it was built in a
scratch copy of sim/, where `T171_XPEN` also added minutes to drives between states in
`kernel::mode_choice`), `t171_path_day.py` (PATH by station, crossings, trip explorer answers),
`t171_operators.py` (trips using each operator), `t171_waterfront.py` (rail share by group
against ACS tracts). The latter three read the bin's JSON (paths at their top point at the scratch
folder of this session; pass a result file).

## Answer in short

**PATH is not a quarter of its real ridership; most of the 48k was how it was counted.**
`realnet.py compare` counted only first boardings at stations labelled PATH, so every trip
starting at PATH's Manhattan stations (which share a demand station with a subway complex) went
to the subway, and every rider reaching PATH from NJ Transit, the light rail or the subway went
uncounted. Counted the way PATH counts its riders, the same commute day has **125k** PATH trips
(48.6k before). Against an all-purpose 212k with perhaps 65-80% commuting, that is 0.75-0.9x of
PATH's commuters, about a third lower than the subway's standing, not three quarters. With the
everyday purposes the model has 372k (1.76x), the everyday layer's general level on every
operator.

**What is genuinely short is the Hudson waterfront** (Hoboken, downtown Jersey City): rail
share 26.9% against ACS's 40.9%. Mostly because the model's drive across the river is cheap (no
tolls or congestion charge, $5 parking at Wall St, no tunnel queues; a 30-minute toll stand-in
lifts PATH to 185k and the waterfront to 37%), and partly the border factor, which sends the
waterfront to Manhattan at 0.67x LODES (about 20k PATH trips with Essex).

**Keep the border factor (one per pack, as shipped).** Tested against LODES in five packs it
is clearly better than every alternative but per-state-pair factors, which tie it. A factor only
between metros cannot reach LODES in New York (half the cross-state commuting is inside one
urban area and is cut just as hard). A water or toll term cannot replace it: land borders with
no water or tolls show the same cut, and a crossing penalty strong enough to match the totals
wrecks in-state flows across the same rivers. Short crossings are not under-predicted (under
3 km they are at or over LODES). The one judgement call is a fade near the line,
k(d) = k + (1 - k) exp(-d / 3 km): score-neutral, fixes the waterfront to Manhattan (0.92x), but
overshoots the shortest crossings elsewhere; not recommended on PATH's account (section 2.4 has
the change if wanted).

## 1. PATH

### 1.1 The 48k was a counting artefact

`realnet.py compare` counts "trips starting at each operator's stations": first boardings at
stations whose demand station is labelled with that operator. For PATH that misses most of its
riders:

- PATH's four Manhattan stations share a demand station with a subway complex (14 St, 23 St and
  33 St within 100 m, WTC with WTC Cortlandt), and the comparison labels a shared station
  subway. Every trip home from Manhattan, half of PATH's ridership, was counted as subway.
- A rider who reaches PATH by another train (NJ Transit at Hoboken or Newark, the light rail at
  Exchange Place and Newport, the subway in Manhattan) pays at PATH's fare gates and is in the
  212k, but the comparison counts that trip at its first station only.
- The other way, NJ Transit's Newark Penn entries were counted as PATH (the 150 m merge put
  them under PATH's key).

Counted the way PATH counts (boardings on PATH lines less changes between two PATH trains),
with the scratch bin (`notes/t171_net.rs.txt`: DemandApi and EverydayApi on the real network,
3 crowding rounds shared between the two, current packs and code, no fares as in realnet):

|---|---|
| realnet's measure, commutes | 48,600 |
| the same day, counted as PATH counts, commutes | **124,500** |
| of which reached PATH by another train | 72,200 |
| everyday purposes (shopping 104.6k, social 94.6k boardings, school, university, medical, airport) | 247,800 |
| all purposes | 372,300 |

What share of real riders commute is not published for PATH (the last PATH passenger survey
found was 1974, "predominately work-related"). Other rail systems' surveys put work trips at
about two thirds of transit trips (CTA 2007: 67%) and higher on peak-heavy commuter lines
(NJ Transit's trans-Hudson ferry riders: 91% work in the morning peak). Taking 65-80% for PATH,
its commute riders are about 140-170k, so the model's 125k is **0.75-0.9x**. The subway's
commute share is lower (about 55-60%), which puts the model's 2.95M at about 1.15-1.25x of the
subway's commute riders. So PATH is short by roughly a third relative to the subway, not by
three quarters. Commuter rail is the opposite and much bigger: LIRR and Metro-North carry
1.4-1.8x their all-purpose counts in commutes alone (worth its own task; it also matters for
T-268's commuter rail between cities).

All purposes, every operator is over (1.65-3.1x): the everyday layer sends about 1.4x as many
rail trips as the commute (4.9M everyday rail trips against 3.4M commute) on every operator, so
PATH's all-purpose 1.76x is the everyday layer's general level, not a PATH matter.

PATH's service and access in the network (`nyc_real.json`, from PATH's GTFS) look right, so
they are not the cause:

- All four weekday lines are there, with peak / midday trains an hour each way: Newark-WTC
  11.9 / 3.5, Journal Square-33 St 12.8 / 5.3, Hoboken-33 St 7.1 / 4.7, Hoboken-WTC 7.0 / 4.2,
  plus the night Journal Square-33 St via Hoboken. Run times Journal Square-33 St 23 min,
  Newark-WTC 25 min. 5 game cars (7 x 51 ft). (Midday frequencies count only 10-16, so they look
  thin; real midday service is every 10-12 minutes a line, about the same.)
- Changes: 33 St, 23 St, 14 St and WTC share a demand station with the subway complex (within
  100 m), so changing there is free of walking. Hoboken PATH and NJ Transit are 150 m apart
  (separate stations, a walking change within 300 m); Newark Penn is one station.
- Walks from the waterfront: Grove St 353 m, Hoboken 385 m, Newport 271 m, Exchange Place 78 m
  (subzone to station, as the explorer gives them); Journal Square 670 m.
- PATH's busiest morning segments run at 53-59% of crush (all purposes), so crowding does not
  push riders off it.

Real journeys: almost nobody drives from Hoboken or downtown Jersey City to lower Manhattan.
The model's drive misses three costs of this crossing:

- **Tolls.** The Holland and Lincoln tunnels charge about $15-17 eastbound, and the Manhattan
  congestion charge adds $9 (less a $3 tunnel credit) since January 2025. At the model's 3
  perceived minutes a dollar, about 30 minutes a leg averaged over both directions.
- **Parking downtown.** Parking is 3 minutes plus the destination zone's jobs per km2 / 2,500,
  capped at 40. The 2 km zone holding WTC and Wall St averages about 33k jobs a km2 (it takes
  in harbour and Battery Park City), so they get 16.3 minutes (about $5 a leg) against Midtown's
  43; real daily parking there is $30-50.
- **Tunnel queues.** The drive is crow-fly x 1.3 at density speeds: Hoboken to WTC in 13.6
  minutes; a peak Holland Tunnel crossing is often 30+.

Sensitivity (scratch only: 30 perceived minutes added to every drive between two states, a
stand-in for tolls, `T171_XPEN=30`): PATH commuters 125k to **185k** (0.87x its all-purpose
count, about at its commute count), Hudson rail share 18.3% to 26.5%, NJ Transit rail 268k to
335k, the subway unchanged (2.95M to 3.07M). It also pushes Bergen, Passaic and Union further
over ACS (they lack the bus). So drive pricing is the lever with the right size; the same
stand-in on all state lines would be wrong elsewhere (Rockland to Bergen has no toll).

## 2. The border factor

### 2.1 What it stands for, and the evidence

k multiplies the distance decay between zones in different US states. It stands for everything
that keeps people working in their own state: separate labour markets, licensing and residency
rules, state income tax, tolls, habit. The model need not know which. The evidence (T-159,
T-162, T-198, and this task's fits): without it the crow-fly gravity sends 2-4x LODES across
every state line, land and water alike, while water inside one state (East River, Newark Bay,
Long Island Sound) is about right. Fitted per pack at the shipped curve: New York 0.227,
Philadelphia 0.207, Boston 0.285, Providence 0.319, Washington 0.454.

### 2.2 The alternatives

Each fitted to LODES OD 2023 in numpy on copies of the packs' zones (`notes/t171_gravity.py`;
nothing written to data/packs). Scores are t162's: share of trips misplaced between county pairs,
and by 2 km length band. "x" is the model's share of the home group's commuters over LODES's.

- **k**: one factor per pack, fitted (as shipped, 0.23; Washington 0.45).
- **pair**: one factor per state pair, each fitted to its pair's LODES flow.
- **kd3, kd8**: k(d) = k + (1 - k) exp(-d / 3 or 8 km): no cut at the line, the full cut further out.
- **kbeyond6**: factor only beyond 6 km.
- **metro**: no factor inside one urban centre, defined as GHSL defines one but from the pack's
  own cells: 1 km squares with 1,500+ residents a km2, joined edge to edge, 50k+ people. New
  York's is one 13.6M centre spanning the Hudson; Philadelphia's 2.25M spans the Delaware.
- **water**: no state factor; exp(-g x km of water on the straight line) from the pack's water mask.
  **waterfit**: the same with g as strong as needed to match the between-state share.
  **crossing**: no state factor; a flat factor on any pair whose line crosses 300+ m of water
  (a toll or bridge stand-in, T-268's question). **water+k**: water term and a state factor.

### 2.3 What each alternative does

- **Per state pair**: matches LODES as well as one k, better in Washington (county pairs 9.1 to
  7.9; DC to its suburbs 0.77 to 1.1-1.3x) and no better elsewhere. The pairs that are real
  states come out 0.17-0.37 (MD-PA 0.09 and the WV pairs are thin flows); the pairs with DC,
  a city counted as a state, 0.61-0.66. So the variation that matters is "is one side a
  city-state", which the per-pack 0.45 already handles. It does nothing for the Hudson
  waterfront (0.66).
- **By distance (kd3)**: about score-neutral everywhere (New York 11.2 against 11.4, others
  within 0.2) and the only variant that brings the waterfront to Manhattan near LODES (0.92),
  and Center City to Camden city (0.86). Its costs: Camden city to Center City 5.8 to 8.6x, the
  shortest crossings 1.9-2.7x LODES in every pack, Manhattan to the waterfront 0.82 to 1.64x.
  Small volumes either way (crossings under 3 km are 0.01-0.2% of all commutes).
- **Only between metros**: wrong. About half of New York's and Washington's cross-state LODES
  commuting is inside one urban centre (NYC 3.9% of all commutes inside, 4.6% between) and it is
  cut just as hard; with the factor off inside, New York cannot reach LODES even at k 0.001,
  Fairfield, Rockland and the outer NJ counties collapse (0.00-0.22x), and Philadelphia's county
  score worsens 7.2 to 8.7. A worldwide definition exists (GHSL urban centres), but the data
  say the state effect lives inside metros.
- **Water / travel time instead of the factor**: no. A water term alone cannot reach LODES's
  between-state share in any pack even with water crossings all but closed (Philadelphia 13.0%
  against 10.1%, Washington 27.1% against 24.9%, Providence 14.3% against 9.5%), because the
  same cut shows on land borders with no water and no tolls (Rockland-Bergen, Delaware-
  Pennsylvania, MA-RI, MA-NH, DC-Maryland). Strong enough to matter, it wrecks in-state
  flows: Kings to Manhattan 0.50x, Camden County to Philadelphia 0.34x, Essex to Manhattan
  0.52x. A mild water term alongside the factor (water+k) helps Philadelphia and Boston slightly and
  hurts New York and Providence (New York 9.5 + 5.1 against 8.7 + 2.7). T-159 found the same for a bridge-detour road
  distance (closed a tenth of the Delaware gap). Tolls are real, but they belong in the drive's
  cost in mode choice (section 1.4), where they move mode share, not where people work.
- **No factor**: 2-4x LODES across every line; on the real network it overfills NJ Transit and
  empties the boroughs' rail (section 1.5).

### 2.4 Recommendation

**Keep one border factor per pack as shipped.** On the evidence it is clearly better than every
alternative except per-pair factors, which tie it (and beat it only in Washington, already
handled by its own 0.45). Both rejected ideas fail clearly, not narrowly: "only between
metros" cannot reach LODES in New York at all, and a water or toll term cannot replace it because
land borders without water or tolls show the same cut. The factor is not behind PATH's low
count (section 1).

**The one judgement call: a fade near the line (kd3).** k(d) = k + (1 - k) exp(-d / 3 km)
fixes the Hudson waterfront to Manhattan (0.67 to 0.92x) and Center City to Camden city, at no
cost to the overall scores, but overshoots the shortest crossings elsewhere and Camden city to
Center City. Worth about 10-15k PATH riders. It is plausible ("labour markets overlap within a
few km of any line") and worldwide-definable (distance only). I would not ship it on PATH's
account: the drive pricing is the bigger lever (section 1.4) and the fade's gain is one place.
If Anita wants it, the change is:

- `sim/src/demand/kernel.rs`: `Gravity` gets `border_fade_km` (0 = none). Where `Gravity::k`
  and the Furness loops multiply by `border` between regions, use
  `border + (1 - border) * exp(-d / border_fade_km)` with d the zone-centre distance (the
  intrazonal 1.04 km for a split square). Cheapest as a second decay table by grid offset
  (`ftab_x`, as `ftab` but times the faded factor), so a cross-region pair is one lookup, not an
  exp; the mode choice's per-destination loop (`fz` in `mode_choice`), `walk_trips_by_zone`,
  `Gravity::t` and `selection_flows` read it the same way.
- `sim/src/demand/pack.rs` and `pack_gravity`: `gravity.border_fade_km` in the header
  (absent = 0, old packs unchanged) and `--border-fade-km`; `pipeline/build_city.py`
  `BORDER_FADE_KM_US = 3.0`, passed with the factor; refit the factor (kd3's fitted k: New York
  0.214, Philadelphia 0.196, so about 0.21 for the US; Washington 0.44).
- `pipeline/regions.py <city>` re-solves each pack in place; `read_pack.py` and
  `county_flows.py` apply the fade in their independent checks; `t162_tables.py` likewise.
- docs/demand.md 4.4 item 1 and docs/demand-worldwide.md's border entry.

**For the joint commute gravity across pack lines (T-268's option 2).** Whatever crosses a pack
line should use the same factor: the fits say MA-RI about 0.32, NJ-PA 0.17-0.21, MD-DC 0.45-0.61
(DC is the city-state case). A joint gravity covering several packs should carry the factor per
region pair, or at least the pack's own value for its own pairs, rather than one global 0.23:
the per-pair fits differ by up to 3x between city-state pairs and real state pairs. T-268's
idea that the factor mostly stands in for tolled crossings of the Hudson and Delaware does not
hold up (above): the same cut is on untolled land borders, and a crossing penalty strong
enough to match the totals destroys in-state flows across the same rivers.

### 2.5 Other findings to queue (not done here)

- **realnet's operator count** (`pipeline/realnet.py compare`) should count trips using each
  operator as boardings on its lines less changes between two of its lines, not first boardings
  at stations labelled with it. Needs the realnet bin to write route-node boardings
  (`DemandApi::board`) and transfers (`DemandApi::transfers` with every route node selected), as
  `notes/t171_net.rs.txt` does; `notes/t171_operators.py` is the counting. The same flaw hides
  commuter rail's returns from Penn Station and Grand Central.
- **The drive across rivers and into CBDs is cheap** (section 1.4): parking from 2 km zone
  density undercounts dense centres whose zone takes in water; no tolls or congestion charge;
  no tunnel queues. A worldwide-safe start: parking from land-only job density (jobs per km2 of
  land, the pack has the water mask) or a finer radius. Tolls are city-specific data (OSM tags
  `toll=yes` on bridges and tunnels are global, though).
- **Commuter rail is 1.4-1.8x over** on commutes alone (LIRR 483k, Metro-North 336k trips using
  it against 268k and 236k all purposes), on top of which the everyday layer adds more.
- **The everyday layer's rail** is about 1.4x the commute's on every operator (all-purpose
  1.65x the subway's count): either everyday rail shares or volumes are high.
- **No bus**: North Hudson (ACS 26% bus, 5% rail) and Bergen, Passaic, Union go to rail in the
  model at 1.7-6x ACS rail.
