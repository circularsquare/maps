# T-3887: a mode frontier chart

Anita, 11-20: "to more formalize what we're talking about with modes of transport we could make a graph of some
sort. so that we could draw like a frontier. the y axis is just profit per demand i guess? ... buses should be good
for short hops i guess? ... if we have some sort of graph like this it would be very helpful to keep ourselves
organized but also to communicate to players what they should do. cuz currently its kinda opaque."

Later the same evening: "maybe for the frontier chart we could do x axis trip length z axis stop spacing? and y axis
is share of people that would take each mode at that point, so whatever color is on top is what the dominant mode is
there?" That is the second chart below.

Stage 1 (this note): the method, a rerunnable script, two first charts from today's rules, and a read of them.
What transport research does: research/mode-frontier.md.

## The two charts

- `artifacts/trainworld/t3887-frontier.png` (CSV beside it): money. One straight line of each mode through three
  parts of Chicago, 2 to 40 km long; net profit a day after building costs, per person or job within 800 m of the
  line, against the line's length. The winner at each length is circled and named.
- `artifacts/trainworld/t3887-shares.png` (CSV beside it): who rides. Trip length across, stop spacing up; each cell
  is coloured by the mode with the biggest share of the trips when it alone runs along the line, with that share
  written in. Two slices beside it give every mode's share against trip length at 400 m and 1,500 m spacing.

## Method

**Harness.** The real game, through `checks/play.mjs` (headless, sandbox, one core): each point is a fresh Chicago game
(`play.mjs reload cic`), so lines never share riders; the line is built by edit ops (`tools/frontier.js`), its
headways set, its trips calculated to a final, and its day read the way the game shows it: weekday fares from the
Money tab's income, running cost and upkeep by the Lines tab's own functions (`lineRunningADay`, `lineStats.upkeep`),
building cost and fleet as the drop in cash. Checked: the fares read this way equal the line panel's day fares (US$38.00M
against 37.999M on a test line); the same point run twice gives the same figures to the dollar; the edit-API bus runs
at 17-18 km/h with turnarounds, as the docs' 19 km/h at 400 m stops; and its riders match T-1466's calibration runs
(notes/archive/t1000-1499/T-1466.md): through the Loop 5,500 a route-km a day at 8 minutes against Manhattan's
2,500-5,500, on Western Avenue 200 a route-km against T-1466's crosstown bus with no rail to feed at 34 and its
Queens feeders at 1,300-2,000. Buses were tuned as feeders; alone on a line they are weak by design.

**City and corridors.** Chicago (`cic`): flat, US prices, a grid of long straight arterials, so a straight line is a
fair stand-in for a real street. Three straight corridors centred on a place, each 2, 5, 10, 20 and 40 km long:
- dense: through the Loop, tilted NNW along the lakefront and SSE to the South Side (17,000 people and jobs a km² over
  40 km, 105,000 over the middle 2 km);
- medium: Western Avenue, centred at Division (7,000-10,000);
- thin: north-south through Wheaton in DuPage County (2,400-4,200).
Density: people plus jobs from the city pack within 800 m of the line, over that band's area.

**Modes**, each at the game's new-line settings where it has one (a tram's 2 cars, the bus's own stops), the rest
mine:

| Mode | Stops | Level | Cars |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bus | its own placing (400 m street grid, a demand stop about every 2 km) | road | 1 bus |
| Bus, stops 1 km | the bus panel's spacing at 1 km | road | 1 bus |
| Street tram | 500 m | street | 2 |
| Tram | 700 m | ground, off street | 2 |
| Metro | 1 km | -2, bored | 6 |
| Rail | 3 km, one at the centre | +1 viaduct (no road crossings to price) | 8 |
| High speed | 20 km, so three stops (40 km lines only) | +1 viaduct, high-speed trains | 8 |

A bus route is the corridor's points every 100 m marked as on the road, so it counts at its drawn length (a real
arterial of the grid would be the same length). A stop the game refuses over water (the dense line runs along the
Chicago River's branches) is left out, ends excepted; the CSV's issues column says how many. The street tram's
2 km line through the Loop could not be built at all: both its ends land on the river.

**Service.** New rail lines start with no trains, so "the defaults" fix only the bus. Every mode runs the same
headways and the best is kept: S every 15/30/60 minutes (high/medium/low demand), A 8/15/30 (the bus's default),
B 4/8/15, C 2/4/8, and D 1/2/4 where C was best and still 90% full. S runs only where A was the best of A-C, D only
where C was. Capacity is how a busy line loses riders, so a second service level mattered more than a second stop
spacing (the bus has two spacings; the share chart varies spacing for every mode).

**Money.** Profit a day = weekday fares minus running cost minus upkeep, all in game dollars (the x100 economy).
Building cost (track, stations, roads, and the fleet the service needs) is spread over 60 game days, 6,000 real days
or about 16 years at x100, which is also the loan's term; a 7% rate over 30 years would be about 45 game days, a
straight 30 years 110. The CSV also has payback days, which needs no such choice. The chart's y is that net profit per
person or job within 800 m: the same denominator for every mode at a point, so the winner is the mode with the most
money on that corridor (what "profit per demand" asks), not the one with the best margin on its own few riders. Profit
per rider and per peak-hour load are in the CSV.

**Shares.** For the second chart, one 28 km line of each mode along Western Avenue at stop spacings of 400 m (the bus's
own placing), 800 m, 1,500 m and 3,000 m, every 8 minutes at the peak; then the trip explorer's own answer
(`askTrip`, the morning peak, as a commute) for trips of 1-18 km with both ends on the line, from 25 starts spread
along it, averaged (five starts left the wide spacings hitting or missing a stop). Rail is left out: it runs the
metro's trains, so at one spacing it takes exactly the same trips (checked: identical shares); the chart says "Metro
or rail". "Share" is each mode alone against the car and walking, not all modes offered side by side: the game picks
between routes by their perceived minutes, so the mode with the biggest share alone is also the one that would win
side by side (route choice is near winner takes all past a few minutes), and alone is what the game measures cleanly.

**Design calls I made** (sensible defaults, say if any is wrong): Chicago and these three corridors; the stop
spacings and levels above; 60 game days for building; one corridor's share of the city as the people and jobs within
800 m; rail and high speed on viaducts (no crossings, cheaper than ground through a dense city); the share chart on
the medium corridor only, commutes, morning peak.

**How research shaped it** (research/mode-frontier.md). Studies put corridor demand as passengers an hour each way
at the busiest point on x and total cost per passenger on y (building annualised, plus running, plus the riders'
own time). The game's profit has no riders' time in it and its demand answers the service, so the money chart
keeps her axes (length, density) and an operator's y (profit), and the CSV carries the busiest stretch's riders an
hour (`peak_per_hour`) so the research's x can be drawn too. The 60 game days for building sit between the
research's 7% over 30 years (about 45 game days) and a straight 30 years (110).

## What the charts say (today's rules)

Net profit a day, game US$M, each mode at its best service:

| Corridor | 2 km | 5 km | 10 km | 20 km | 40 km |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dense, winner | tram 23 | street tram 74 (a near tie) | rail 116 | rail 171 | rail 190 |
| Dense, best bus | 5 | 18 | 21 | 20 | 16 |
| Medium, winner | street tram 0.6 | street tram 2.7 | street tram 6.5 | street tram 13 | street tram 26 |
| Medium, best bus | -0.1 | -0.3 | -0.4 | -0.8 | -0.4 |
| Thin | nothing pays | | | | |

- **Dense** (the Loop): every rail mode pays. Trams win short lines, rail (3 km stops on a viaduct) long ones. At
  5 km four modes are within 15% (street tram 74, tram 72, rail 71, metro 65), and this corridor runs beside the
  Chicago River's branches, which cost trams some stops and add water to every surface mode's price; call that point
  a tie. The
  metro carries the most riders (up to 707,000 a day) but its tunnels cost about three times as much, so it comes
  second or third. High speed at 40 km (three stops, one in the Loop) makes US$26M a day, a fifth of rail's: inside one
  city its speed has no room to count. Buses pay too, but stop at about US$18-21M a day whatever the length: even a bus every minute is
  full (80 at crush), so they carry 64,000-115,000 riders a day where a street tram carries 250,000-450,000.
- **Medium** (Western Avenue): only the street tram pays, at every length. The tram carries as many riders but its
  track costs five times as much and it about breaks even. Metro and rail lose US$10-150M a day: their busiest
  stretch carries 300-800 riders an hour, far below the 5,000-20,000 at which studies find rail overtakes buses.
  Buses carry almost nobody: 400-11,000 riders a day, where a street tram on the same street carries 5,000-120,000.
- **Thin** (DuPage): nothing pays at any length or service. The bus loses least only because it carries almost
  nobody and costs little.
- **Who rides** (the share chart): the tram and the street tram take the most trips nearly everywhere, within a
  point or two of each other (up to 46% of all trips at 5 km with stops 400-800 m apart). Trains lead at 3 km
  spacing and on 1-2 km trips at 1.5 km, and draw level with trams at 1.5 km spacing on long trips; at 400 m they
  trail trams by 3-8 points, most likely because a tram stands 15 s at a stop and a train 30 s. The bus never leads:
  4-30% at its own stops, 8-17 points under the trams. At 1 km walking takes half the trips whatever runs.
- **A stop at the centre is worth a lot.** Rail with its stops falling either side of the Loop made US$29M a day at
  10 km; with one stop in the Loop, US$116M. The sweep now always puts a stop at the centre for spacings of 2 km and
  more. A player's line through a CBD will feel the same.

## Why buses lose, and what T-3856 would likely do

Same street, same headways, medium corridor, 10 km: bus 2,000 riders a day, street tram 33,000. Per trip with both
ends on the line the gap is much smaller: 21% against 38% of all trips (averaged over 1-18 km). So most of the gap
is not the trip itself:

1. **Reach (the biggest, measured).** Only homes within 300 m of a bus route can use it (docs/demand-worldwide.md
   "Bus corridors"); a tram stop is a station, walked to from much further. The same trips with both ends moved off
   the line, average share over 1-18 km:

   | Both ends off the line | 0 m | 200 m | 500 m | 1 km |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Bus (its own stops) | 21% | 13% | 0% | 0% |
   | Street tram (stops 400 m apart) | 38% | 32% | 19% | 8% |

2. **Speed.** 17-18 km/h on the bus's own stops (21-23 at 1 km), the 30 km/h bus-lane floor binding everywhere,
   against 25-28 for the street tram and 30-34 for the tram, turnarounds included.
3. **Boarding.** 4 perceived minutes on every bus boarding; trams pay none.
4. **Room** (dense only). 80 at crush against a two-car tram's 360.
5. **Not the fare.** Measured: the bus fare at 0.8x moves riders up 0-3% and net down US$0.02-2.4M a day.

T-3856's numbers, estimated (no game code was changed here; rerun the sweep once they land):
- **No boarding cost**: takes 4 minutes off a 40-50 minute perceived bus trip; at the game's curve (the share goes
  roughly with the cost ratio cubed) bus riders rise by roughly a third. Not near a 15x gap.
- **85% of car speed**: in a city the 30 km/h lane floor already binds, so little change; open-country buses slow
  about 6%.
- **Near-free building**: the road is US$1M a km, about a tenth of a bus line's daily cost when spread over 60 days
  (US$0.08M of US$0.95M a day on the medium 5 km line). It trims losses a little and brings no riders.
- **0.8x fare**: measured above, slightly worse.
- So plain buses would still lose to street trams on every corridor tried, and stay capped by room in dense ones. A
  niche for them would need one of: a wider reach than 300 m; dearer or slower street trams (T-3378 says players
  find trams too strong, and these charts agree: the street tram is the medium corridor's only winner); or the
  feeder role, which this chart does not test (one line alone has no trains to feed). Short hops are not a niche
  either: at 1-2 km walking takes 40-50% and the bus 11-24%.

## How to rerun

    node trainworld/checks/dev-nowatch.mjs 6196                         (a Vite of your own)
    node trainworld/checks/play.mjs start cic --port 6196 --mode sandbox
    python trainworld/tools/frontier.py sweep --port 6196 --out <csv>    (run it twice: the second run adds the S and D levels; about 2 hours in all on one core)
    python trainworld/tools/frontier.py chart <csv> --png <png>
    python trainworld/tools/frontier.py shares --port 6196 --out <csv>   (the share chart's runs, about 20 minutes)
    python trainworld/tools/frontier.py sharechart <csv> --png <png>
    python trainworld/tools/frontier.py table <csv>                     (each point's modes, best first)
    node trainworld/checks/play.mjs stop --port 6196

`--fare-bus 0.8` sets the bus fare multiple for a sweep; `--only dense:5:bus,...` runs a few points. Two games on two
Vites halve the time (split with `--only`). Corridors, lengths, modes and service levels are tables at the top of
tools/frontier.py, so a BRT row is one more `MODES` entry. The shipped CSV's rail rows at 10-40 km came from a rerun
after the stop-at-the-centre rule was added; a fresh sweep has it throughout.

## Open

- The dense 2 km street tram could not be built (both ends on the river); the money chart has no point there.
- The share chart is one corridor (medium), one purpose (commutes), one period (the morning peak). Dense and thin
  ones are a flag away (`SHARE_CORRIDOR`).
- High speed is only run at 40 km (three stops); inside one city that is not its market. A between-cities sweep would
  be its own chart.
- Next stages: BRT as one more `MODES` row once T-3378 has it; the whole sweep again after T-3856; a feeder test
  (a bus to a rail line) if buses' niche is meant to be feeding.
