## The charts (rerun 11-20, on the frozen set: T-3856 reach 600, T-3888 option 4, T-3892, T-3895, T-2216, T-3903)

- `artifacts/trainworld/T-3887/share.png`: who would take each mode. One panel per stop spacing (400 m, 800 m,
  1.5, 3, 6, 12 km), trip length across (2, 5, 15, 40 km), the share of commutes of that length along the line
  taken by the mode when it alone runs there (the rest drive or walk). The line on top is the mode most people
  would take; it is named over each point.
- `artifacts/trainworld/T-3887/profit.png`: what one trip of that length makes, same panels.
- `result.json` beside them holds every run (riders, passenger-km, fares, running cost, upkeep, building cost,

## What they say

- **Who rides.** The tram takes the most trips wherever stops are 1.5 km apart or closer, at every trip length:
  27-55% of 2-15 km trips, against 21-50% for the metro. It is faster than the metro there (25.7 against 20.3 km/h
  at 400 m stops, 49.5 against 47.6 at 1.5 km) and its stops at grade have no stairs. Its lead over the metro is
  9-12 points at 400 m stops, 7-9 at 800 m, 5-6 at 1.5 km and 1-3 at 3 km. The metro or rail leads only on long trips
  with wide stops (40 km trips at 3 and 6 km stops), by 1-3 points; at 12 km stops every rail mode is level, and
  no mode gets past 11%, since so few people live near a stop. High speed trails the metro everywhere by up to 5
  points: its fare is 1.25x, and inside one city its speed only shows at 12 km stops (150 against 124 km/h). The bus
  never leads: 23-32% of 2-15 km trips with stops up to 1.5 km apart, ahead of the metro on 2 km trips at 800 m and
  1.5 km stops, 14-26 points under the tram on 5-15 km trips. It does best with stops 800 m apart (23 km/h), not at
  its own placing (19 km/h).
- **Money.** Only the bus and the tram pay. The tram makes US$74-93M a day at stops 400 m-1.5 km apart, US$25M at
  3 km, and loses from 6 km; the bus US$11-14M up to 1.5 km. Metro, rail and high speed lose money at every spacing
  (US$62-373M a day): at this density their busiest stretch carries 1,400-1,850 riders an hour, far below what their
  tunnels and viaducts cost. Per trip, bus and tram make about the same, US$180-235 on a 2-5 km trip, wherever stops
  are 1.5 km apart or closer (the bus a little ahead on long trips, both losing on 40 km ones); at 3-6 km stops the
  tram makes more. Rail beats the metro per trip only because a viaduct costs less than a tunnel.
- **So, for a player:** in an ordinary dense city, trams with stops 400 m-1.5 km apart; buses where a tram does not
  pay, for each trip makes as much but they carry a sixth of the riders; metro, rail and high speed need more people
  than 15,000 residents and jobs a km² along the whole line (the first run's Chicago Loop, 105,000 a km², is where
  rail won).

## Why the tram beats the metro (reruns, 11-20)

Asked: what makes the tram take more trips than the metro at stops up to 1.5 km apart. Reruns of both at a train
every 4 minutes, share of trips of 5 and 15 km:

| Line | 400 m stops: 5 km | 15 km | km/h | 1.5 km stops: 5 km | 15 km | km/h |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tram, at grade (today) | 49.5% | 48.6% | 25.7 | 44.5% | 54.6% | 49.5 |
| Tram, stations at -1 (stairs) | 43.1% | 44.7% | 25.7 | 38.6% | 50.3% | 49.5 |
| Metro, bored (today) | 38.7% | 36.5% | 20.3 | 38.2% | 49.5% | 47.6 |
| Metro, at grade (no stairs) | 44.6% | 39.9% | 20.3 | 44.1% | 53.7% | 47.6 |
| Metro, at grade, 15 s dwell | 49.4% | 48.6% | 25.7 | 45.3% | 56.5% | 54.6 |

Two things make the whole gap, and nothing else: the stairs (T-3888: a stop above or below ground costs 4-6 points
of 5 km trips, 3-4 of 15 km ones, for either mode) and the dwell (a tram stands 15 s at a stop, a new train line
30 s: at 400 m stops that is 149 x 15 s = 37 minutes over the line, all of the 25.7 against 20.3 km/h). A metro at
grade with a tram's dwell takes the tram's trips to the tenth at 400 m and more than it at 1.5 km, where its top
speed counts. Capacity and fares play no part: both lines run 15 trains an hour, neither is full (the busiest
stretch is 1,700-2,200 an hour), and both pay the plain fare. On money the metro loses on building: bored, its 400 m
line costs US$25.8B against the tram's US$2.8B; even at grade US$12.2B.

## At 30,000 residents a km² (reruns, 11-20)

Capacity is where the metro should win, and at 10,000 a km² nothing fills. The same tram and bored metro, today's
rules, in the same city at three times the density (30,000 residents and 14,814 jobs a km²). Full: the busiest
stretch's riders an hour one way at the peak against the line's room at crush (a car holds 240, a tram section
three quarters of that; the rated room is two thirds of crush). Money a day, building over 60 game days.

| Line, stops | Service | Share of 5 / 15 km trips | Busiest stretch an hour | Of crush (rated) | Net a day |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tram, 400 m | every 8 min | 15.2% / 3.6% | 2,514 | 93% (140%) | US$271M |
| Tram, 400 m | every 4 min | 51.8% / 33.0% | 3,935 | 73% (109%) | US$474M |
| Tram, 400 m | every 2 min (best money) | 51.8% / 33.0% | 3,942 | 73% (109%) | US$504M |
| Metro, 400 m | every 4 min | 67.5% / 65.4% | 8,092 | 37% (56%) | US$165M |
| Metro, 400 m | every 2 min (best money) | 67.5% / 65.5% | 8,107 | 38% (56%) | US$166M |
| Tram, 1.5 km | every 4 min | 49.7% / 39.7% | 4,072 | 75% (113%) | US$424M |
| Tram, 1.5 km | every 2 min (best money) | 49.8% / 39.9% | 4,096 | 76% (114%) | US$449M |
| Metro, 1.5 km | every 4 min (best money) | 66.4% / 76.2% | 8,593 | 40% (60%) | US$371M |

The track runs at most 15 trains an hour, so every 2 minutes adds only evening and night trains. Here the metro takes
the most trips at both spacings, by 16-37 points: the tram's two sections are past their rated room at the peak,
and crowding drives riders off it, most on long trips (at every 8 minutes, near crush, it keeps only 4% of 15 km
trips). Both now pay, but the tram still makes more money: the metro's tunnels and stations cost US$430M a day at
400 m stops and US$208M at 1.5 km, more than its extra fares bring in. At 1.5 km stops the two are closest
(US$371M against US$449M); a denser city might tip that, not run here.

## Method

**What runs.** `sim/src/bin/frontier.rs`, a native Rust binary on the game's own code: the track model (`TrackApi`)
lays the line, stations and service and gives its run times, building cost, fleet, running cost and upkeep; the
demand kernel (`DemandApi` commutes and `EverydayApi` errands and social trips) calculates its trips over a weekday's
five periods with three crowding rounds and the trains' room, as the demand client does; the trip explorer
(`DemandApi::trip`, what the game's trip panel asks) gives each mode's share. The fares are the game's for a new
game in the US, worked out by its own TypeScript (`tools/frontier_fares.ts`, bundled with rolldown and run in
node): US$2.50 plus US$0.20 a km, a bus's rides 0.8x above the base, high speed's 1.25x. What is copied rather
than called (Rust ports of a few lines of app glue, named in the binary's header): how the clock packs a line for
demand (`buildSnapshot`, `packService`, `setUpPops`: times, trains an hour, cars, stops at grade, bus catchments, fare
multiples), a new line's dwell and turnaround, and income for one line (`incomeFor`: the base on each ride, the
rate times the line's multiple on each passenger-km, the curve's extra). Checked by hand on the bus line: the track
model's running cost equals the game's rule (trains an hour, both ways, km, US$6 a bus-km, x100, by the period's
hours) to the dollar, and the bus runs 18.9 km/h at its own stops, as the first run's 17-18 in Chicago. The track
model and the demand side share one frame about the same origin, so a metre is a metre in both.

**The made-up city.** 120 by 60 km, so the line has 30 km of city past each end and to each side (a nearer edge
crowds trips onto the line: 80 by 30 km gave it 14-23% more riders than 80 by 60, and 80 by 60 4-6% more than 120 by
60; shares hardly move). Cells of 324 m (the size of the game's cells), every cell alike: 10,000
residents a km² (New York City's average) and Chicago's pack's ratios for the rest: 4,938 jobs a km², 0.417
commuters per resident, errand and leisure attractors, US everyday rates. Street speeds for the bus from the same
density, as the game makes them from a pack's zones. No water, no schools, hospitals or parks (purposes with no
sites drop out), no regional market. Its origin is Chicago's, only for the US prices and fares.

**The line.** One straight 60 km line through the middle, stops evenly spaced. Modes:

| Mode | Track | Cars | Platform |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bus | street, 400 m is its own placing (demand stops about every 2 km, catching all along), wider its spacing | 1 | |
| Tram | tram track at grade, the tram's dwell and turnaround | 2 | 40 m |
| Metro | bored, 2 levels down | 6 | 120 m |
| Rail | viaduct | 8 | 160 m |
| High speed | high-speed track on viaduct, high-speed trains | 8 | 200 m |

Each runs at three services: every 8/15/30 minutes (high, evening, night demand; the bus's new-line default), 4/8/15
and 2/4/8. The track tops out at 15 trains an hour, so the last adds only evening and night trains.

**Share.** Each mode alone against the car and walking, every mode at the same service (every 4 minutes at the
peaks) so frequency does not decide. A trip of each length runs along the line from 12 starts spread over one stop
spacing, both ends on the line, one row of cells off it and two rows off (324 and 648 m), weighted by the people
each row stands for: the share of commuters living within 810 m of the line. Morning peak commutes only (the trip
explorer's question); everyday trips count in the money. Metro and rail run the same trains on their own track, so
they carry the same trips (checked: shares equal to the digit, riders within 0.1%); the share chart draws them as
one line.

**Profit per trip.** Each mode at the service that makes its line the most money. A trip of length d makes its fare
(base plus the rate times d times the line's multiple, at the x100 economy) minus d times the line's cost per
passenger-km (running, upkeep, and building and the fleet spread over 60 game days, as the first run). Costs here
are per train-km and per km of track, not per rider, so sharing them out by passenger-km is the plain way.

## How to rerun

    venv\Scripts\python.exe trainworld/tools/frontier.py     (from the maps root or a tree's) build the binary (agent profile, 3 cores), run all 90 lines, draw both charts

About 4 minutes, nearly all of it the build; the runs take about a minute. Needs node (the fares) and the app's
node_modules (rolldown). `run --only tram:800,bus:400 --services B`
runs a few; `charts [result.json]` redraws. The city, modes, spacings, trip lengths and services are tables at the
top of tools/frontier.py, so BRT is one more `MODES` row once T-3378 has it.

## Found on the way

- Read from the code, not tested: the everyday layer seems never told which lines are trams. The demand worker calls
  `set_tram_lines` on the commutes only and `EverydayApi` has no such setter, so errand and social trips would ride
  trams as ordinary trains (a train's cars, no tram-only stop slots). The model here does as the game does.
  T-3909 closed it in the game and in frontier.rs; the charts here were not rerun since.
- The track tops out at 15 trains an hour on these lines, so headways under 4 minutes add nothing at the peaks.
- The share chart's colours pass the dataviz validator with one CVD pair in the 6-8 warn band (metro and high
  speed); each mode also has its own marker shape and the top mode is named over its point.
