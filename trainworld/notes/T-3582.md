# T-3582 WebGL2 error after the map glitches

2026-11-17, agent glcontext.

## The report

A player, via Anita's pad: the map "starts glitching and zooming out and in on its own sorta", then the game shows
its "no WebGL2" screen; reloading does not fix it, restarting the browser does; WebGL2 is on in the browser's config.

Not in the feedback inbox: the one-line list of every thread (one GET) has nothing on WebGL, graphics or glitching,
and the full text of the 71 threads from the last 96 hours has none either (the server answered 429 after that, so
older threads were read by their first line only). The inbox's context has no GPU, renderer or console lines, so even
a match would not have told us the GPU. Browser unknown ("checked my browser's config" could be Chrome's flags or
Firefox's about:config).

## What happened (likely, not proven)

The map's WebGL context was lost mid-game and did not come back; on reload the browser refused the page a new one.
Chrome is known to do this (unverified here; the player's browser is unknown): after a GPU process crash or reset it
can block the guilty page's domain from 3D APIs, and after several resets in a short time every page, until the
browser restarts. Creation then fails with a `webglcontextcreationerror` while `WebGL2RenderingContext` still
exists. The game's T-3307 probe saw "no context" and showed the no-WebGL2 advice, which is wrong for this player.

## Stage 2: the cause (not established)

Measured on the real save in headless Chrome (1600x1000 window, canvas 1283x923 at DPR 1, ANGLE), with every
buffer, texture and renderbuffer allocation counted live per context by an init script
(scratch script, numbers in artifacts/trainworld/t3582/gpumem.json; its rows after a restore count the dead
context's objects too, since the meter keeps them, so read them as "everything was uploaded again", not as a leak):

| moment | MapLibre | overlay | demand overlay | total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| real save loaded | 6 MB buffers, 14 MB textures, 10 MB canvas | 38 buffers, 15 textures, 57 renderbuffers, 43 canvas | - | 182 MB |
| after a camera tour (zoom 8-16) | 39 + 50 + 10 | 102 + 15 + 57 + 43 | - | 314 MB |
| demand views on | 33 + 50 + 10 | same | 3 + 5 | 315 MB |
| the same tour three more times (a separate run) | 39 + 50 + 10 | 102 + 15 + 57 + 43 | - | 314 MB each time |

It grows over the first tour (MapLibre's tiles, the overlay's buffers for the zooms seen) and is then flat: no
growth from repeating the same views.

- The overlay's screen-sized targets are the biggest fixed cost: the canvas with 4x MSAA and stencil (about 36
  bytes a device pixel) plus the stroke cache's two 4x MSAA layers, shared depth-stencil and two resolve textures
  (about 56 bytes a pixel), so about 92 bytes per device pixel. At 1.2 Mpx that is about 100 MB; on a 4K screen
  (8.3 Mpx) about 760 MB for the overlay alone, before tiles. A candidate for an integrated GPU on a big screen,
  not proven: we do not know the player's screen or GPU.
- Contexts: 3 live (map, overlay, demand overlay) plus the start probe, well under the cap browsers put on
  contexts a page (Chrome's is said to be 16; not checked).
- A real GPU process crash (`chrome://gpucrash` from a second tab, four times) did nothing in headless Chrome: no
  context of the game's was lost and a fresh probe kept working, so neither the real creation-error text nor a
  real block could be seen here.
- Lost and restored, three times on the map's canvas and once on the overlay's: everything draws again (map,
  track, trains, demand bubbles), no page errors. The camera does not move: zoom and centre are the same after,
  the three `move` events are MapLibre's own `setStyle` on restore, at the same view. MapLibre reloads the whole
  style on restore, so tiles come back coarse first; a GPU that keeps resetting would show the map blinking and
  re-sharpening, which may be the "zooming out and in" the player saw. Our code does not move the camera.
- Not tried: a long playing session (building, the clock running for hours) for a slow GPU leak, and a real GPU
  (headless numbers are the game's allocations, not the driver's own use).

Nothing changed for stage 2: no fix is clearly safe without knowing the player's GPU and screen.
