# T-4343: Big loads that show over 50 minutes left

Anita, 11-25: "some people are complaining about >60 minute load times. or at least, thats what the estimate says";
then "for some people like the twfeedback from u8f7b4eb12bac4dbc theyre saying theyve been waiting for 3 hours for the
initial game load and the time estimate says 50 min left". Also rotas04 on Discord: 72 min left on the Calculating

## For Anita

- That player's load was real and long, not a wrong number. Their game (0.4.6, Windows laptop with Intel Iris Xe
  graphics, 16 GB, Chrome, signed in, 121 lines) was calculating trips on **one** solving worker: 20 minutes in, 3
  of the 5 periods of the first of 4 rounds were done, and the fourth period had run 8 minutes 17 s with no word
  from the worker. The card said 13% and "About 50 min left".
- What turned about an hour into 3 hours, most likely: the loading card's watchdog. A demand worker says nothing
  between a period's start and its end. After 10 minutes of quiet with the card in view, the watchdog restarts
  every worker (all the work so far is lost, the solve starts over), and after the next 10 minutes of quiet it shows
  the failure screen ("couldn't load ... in 10 minutes"). Their fourth period was 103 s from that line. Report
  1795092485-688b2e15 (11-19, 0.4.2, 32 GB desktop) is that failure screen, 10 minutes into a load's first round.
- Fixed in the tree: while a worker is inside a period, the watchdog waits 4 times the longest period timed on the
  page (30 minutes for a solve's first period). A real stall outside a period is still caught at 10 minutes.
- The estimate runs high early in a load, by 2.5-3x on one worker (measured below), not low. So "50 min left" was
  probably nearer 20-30 minutes of real work. Not fixed here (follow-up below).
- Why one worker: with "Use more cores" off, the solving workers together may take 17.5% of the game's 8 GB
  budget (1.4 GB); a big save whose worker needs over 0.7 GB loads on one. A 4-5 thread CPU gets one either way.

## The estimate, from the code

The card's time left (workers/demandClient.ts `jobProgress`) is the workers' own units (`unitsBound` ->
game/editLeft.ts `editLeftOf`) once a period has ended, from the workers actually solving (`computeN`). On a load
there are no passes yet, so every unit is "blind": timed by its size (populations x 0.9^round, demand.worker.ts
`sizeB`) at the ms per population the ended units ran. Two things make it run high early:

1. The periods ended so far set the rate for every unit left: a heavy first period, or a machine busy at the start,
   times them all.
2. Later rounds are sized at 0.9 of the round before (the North America save's flat decay), but on New York they ran
   at about 0.4 of round 0 (T-1479: 0.26 all-China, 0.6 New York, 0.8 Japan).

T-4264's rule holds for the bar (past half only on this `bounded` time), and the time is the workers' pace, but the
0.9 decay is a guess the workers can't back until a later round's unit has ended.

How it can say over 60 minutes honestly enough: one worker, 5 periods x 4 rounds, a period of 4-8 minutes on a
laptop: an hour or more is the real size of that load.

## "Use more cores" (rotas04)

- Same in 0.4.6.1 and master: on = half the threads minus one, 1 to 5 (`poolBySetup`): 4-5 threads give 1, 6-7 give
  2, 8-9 give 3, 12+ give 5. Off: 12+ threads 3, 6-11 threads 2, fewer 1. So below 8 threads it adds nothing.
- With it on, the 17.5% share cap is off, but the budget check (`capAhead`, 8 GB less the rest of the page and the
  aux worker) still cuts a very big save's pool.
- It applies from the next load (the pool is built when the page starts; a game switch reloads the page). Turning it
  on during a 72-minute load changes nothing until that load is reloaded. The tooltip says so.
- The card's time left is from the solving workers that are running (their units), and `solveWaves` follows a cut.

## Run (one, NYC real save, `?demandWorkers=1`, this tree)

- During v2's first period: watchdog allowance 1,800 s (30 min); after it ended (42 s): 168 s, so the 10-minute floor
  holds on a fast machine.
- First load: 65 s into the solve "About 2 min left", done 78 s later. Reload (machine busier): 55 s in "About 8 min
  left" at 10%, done 185 s later (241 s solve). The estimate early in a one-worker load runs 1.5-3x high.
