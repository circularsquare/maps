# T-710: riders' route choice, sharper where it matters

2026-10-19. Proposed, measured, approved by Anita with crowding from 70% of crush, and built.

## For Anita

**What riders do now (built).** A trip's train riders are shared over the pairs of stations near
its two ends by how much slower each option is compared with the trip's length, up to a 60
perceived minute trip; past that a minute matters as on a 60 minute trip. At a station they take
the first train that helps. A train counts as crowded only from 70% of its crush load. The faster
of two options gets:

| Difference | Before, any trip | Under 3 km (about 30 min) | 3-7 km (40 min) | 12 km and over (60 min or more) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 minute | 55% | 66% | 62% | 58% |
| 2 minutes | 60% | 79% | 73% | 66% |
| 5 minutes | 73% | 97% | 92% | 84% |
| 10 minutes | 88% | 99.9% | 99.3% | 97% |

Crowding: below 70% of crush nothing; at 85% a minute aboard counts 1.75, at crush 3, at 110%
4.1, at 120% 5.4. Before, it started where the seats run out (27.5% of crush): 1.65 at 60% of
crush, 3 at crush, 3.9 at 120%.

- Tests A and B do not move (one straight train per trip, no crowding): New York 86.3% and 79.3%,
  London 85.5% and 78.9%.
- Trips with a clearly better option now take it: Brixton to Oxford Circus on the Victoria line 61%
  → 98%, Stratford to Bank by the Central line direct 76% (before the biggest group, 24%, took the
  Elizabeth line and changed), Toronto Bloor to Union 23% → 73%, Eglinton to Queen 53% → 95%.
  New York stays more spread, as expected: its biggest path per trip went from 4-41% to 15-97%.
- Richmond to Waterloo is the exception: at 80% the direct train took 95%; at 70% the direct
  train is crowded enough that most riders still change at Clapham Junction (direct 24%).
- Speed: about the same. Each pass is about 13% dearer (four bands), but crowding from 70% lets
  quiet periods skip rounds (New York solves 9 crowding rounds of 15 instead of 12): New York's
  weekday 29.4 and 30.2 s against 31.7 and 37.2 s before, on a machine shared with other work.
- Stability: the last peak round moves New York's loads 12.1% (13.1% before), London's 4.6% (5.8%).

**Your two questions** (a trip like your screenshot on the real New York network, which has the
same pattern: a direct line, express-and-change routes, and stations one stop past the
destination; `E 98 St (Lexington) to Bleecker St` and `W 98 St (Broadway) to Houston St`).

- **(b) Riding one stop further for a longer walk: T-710 cuts it, does not end it.** W 98 St to
  Houston St: leaving at Houston St (1.5 minute walk) 50% → 80%; at Spring St, one stop on (6.0
  minute walk) 21% → 11%; Canal St, two on, 4.2% → 0.8%. E 98 St to Bleecker St: Bleecker 37% →
  59%, Spring St one stop on 6.8% → 2.4%. What remains is the rule doing what it says: the later
  stop costs only a few perceived minutes more on a 30-40 minute trip (Spring St 4-5 minutes, so
  it keeps about a sixth of Houston St's riders). It is also helped by a destination's walks being
  averaged over the cells around it, so a further station is a bit nearer for part of them.
- **(a) Changing twice instead of riding straight through: T-710 cuts the clearly slower double
  changes (W 98 St: a three-train route with 2.5% before has none now), but not changes that are
  as fast or faster than the straight ride.** A change costs only its wait and walk, with no
  penalty of its own (your decision in T-557). So where an express plus a change is a minute or
  two quicker than the local, riders change: W 98 St to Houston St, before and now, nobody rides
  the 1 local straight through; they take the 2 or 3 express and change to the 1 at 14 St (2.7
  minutes quicker). With a 5 minute penalty on each change, as a test, 49% ride the 1 straight
  through and the double change disappears. If you want riders to prefer the straight trip, that
  is the change to make; it is not part of T-710.

## Where the spread came from

Measured on the T-709 trips and a few with clearly unequal routes, after a peak-period solve with
its three crowding rounds (`notes/t710_trips.rs`).

1. **The station-pair choice** (one scale, 0.2 a perceived minute, for every trip) was the main
   source: with 34-60 pairs and many within a few minutes, a trip's busiest pair took 13-46% in New
   York. It also priced rail at the logsum, ln(n)/0.2 below the options when n are near equal (36
   equal ways counted 18 minutes cheaper than any one of them). A fixed sharp scale was not
   possible: the pair loop keeps exp(-scale x station-to-station minutes) in f32, and New York's
   costs run to 715 perceived minutes (median 115, 36% over 140).
2. **The split between lines at a station** by frequency is right (the first train that helps);
   on the real networks one service is several lines (LIRR branches, South Western patterns), so
   the trip explorer lists one physical trip as several routes. The tables below group routes by
   the stations ridden; the explorer does not (Anita).
3. **The crowding penalty** started at the seats, so standing-room trains already lost riders
   (Richmond to Waterloo direct, Stratford to Bank by Central).
4. **Averaging**: a subzone's walk to a station is a logsum over its cells (sets the option's cost);
   the crowding rounds average their loads. Neither spreads the explorer's riders.

## The rule in model terms

- `Params::band(b)`: scale `rel_gamma` / min(`rel_t0` + `rel_tkm` x km, `rel_tmax`) = 20 / min(25
  + 3 x km, 60), km the band's centre (`REL_EDGES` 0, 3, 7, 12 km; the last band at 18 km, capped):
  0.68, 0.50, 0.37, 0.33 a perceived minute. A commute's band is its zone centres' distance, an
  everyday trip's its pair's mean distance (`band_of_d2`). Each band's exp table is offset by 60 /
  scale and the offset added back after the ln, so a band keeps costs from 0 to 129 / scale minutes
  in f32 (190 in the sharpest band, 387 from 12 km). `rel_gamma` 0 gives the old model exactly
  (scale `station_theta`, offset 0); the regional layer keeps `station_theta` 0.2.
- `mode_choice` builds each origin's zone tables once, notes each destination zone's band, then
  builds the origin's rows only for the bands its pairs use (2 rows of S per band), and each lane
  reads its band's row, egress weights, scale and offset; the split back onto access stations runs
  per band. `station_exp` returns one S x S table per band. The everyday pass and the trip explorer
  take each pair's band.
- `crowd_factors`: x = max(load - start, 0) / (crush - start), start = max(seats, `crowd_start` x
  crush), `crowd_start` 0.7; alpha 2 and the shape unchanged.
- Tests: `station_choice_scale_follows_the_trip_length`, `crowding_starts_near_full`,
  `a_long_trip_keeps_its_rail_cost_in_a_sharp_band` (kernel.rs). Two changed:
  `pair_choice_reproduces_mode_choice` crowds its check round with 8x the loads (30x emptied the
  network under the steeper penalty), `a_line_with_too_few_trains_loses_riders_to_the_wait` cuts
  line b to 6 trains of 24 cars (cut to 4 of 8, crowding alone empties line b in every crowded
  round and the free-flow pass's quarter of the average is all that is left: the wait takes 7.7% at
  6 x 24). `cargo test --release --features demand`: 231 passed.

## Method and files

- `notes/t710_copy.py` (the proposal round's copy, parameters added off by default) and
  `notes/t710b_copy.py` (the built crate plus the trip test and calib's readouts of round movement
  and the busiest segments). `notes/t710_trips.rs` is the trip test; `t710_run.py` (calib runs,
  scored by `pipeline/calib.py`'s `score`), `t710_trips_run.py`, `t710_table.py`,
  `t710_trips_table.py`, `t710_q.py` the readouts. Run them from the copy's folder.
- Outputs: `../artifacts/trainworld/t710/`: `run_<city>_<tag>.json/.log`, `score_*`,
  `trips_<city>_<tag>.json`. Built-code tags: s2old (before), s2new (now), s2tA/s2tB (timing),
  s2x5 (now with 5 minutes a change). Proposal-round tags: base, r20/b4r20 (rel), c8, r20c8/b4r20c8,
  t5, t10, r10.
- The trips are commutes at the morning peak (`DemandApi::trip` after the period's solve and three
  crowding rounds shared with the everyday layer).
