# T-2603 timetable chart (Marey chart) — notes

Agent linepanel, 2026-11-06. The rules are in docs/ui-inspectors.md "The timetable chart".

## References looked at

- Charles Ibry's and E. J. Marey's 1840s-1885 time-distance charts: distance down, time across, each
  train a line, stations spaced by real distance so slope is speed. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Ibry,
  https://chance.amstat.org/?p=6922
- Mike Bostock's D3 "Marey's trains" (Caltrain): stations to scale, both directions in one colour per
  service type, the hovered train picked out. https://observablehq.com/notebook-kit/ex/d3/mareys-trains,
  https://gist.github.com/mbostock/5544008
- The MBTA visualisation (Barry and Card): a Marey chart of a day's actual runs beside the map.
  https://github.com/mbtaviz/mbtaviz.github.io/wiki/The-Trains-Visualization
- Japanese railway "dia-grams" (OuDia and the like, from memory): the same layout, time across,
  terminal standing drawn flat.

Taken: time across and stops down by distance (so speed is slope), north up as the stop list, one
colour for both directions, the past veiled, hover on a trip. Not taken: a per-train-type colour (a
line here has one kind of train), scrolling back through the day (the window follows now).

## Why a new WASM function

The page holds only this hour's and the next hour's trips (renderer `tripsBy`), and an infrequent or
long line needs up to 8 hours; `trips(epoch)` per hour does every line's work for each hour.
`TrackApi.line_trips(line, t0, t1)` is `Service::trips` for one line (`line_window`, shared with
`trips`), checked against `trips` in `blueprint_construct_run`.
