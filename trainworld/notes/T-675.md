# T-675: zoom runs away after a lag (Anita's playtest, 2026-10-18)

"sometimes when zooming in or out and theres lag of some sort, we stop zooming at the normal rate
and instead zooming in/out happens instantly and we go like 4x as far. after a split second it
resolves but its annoying and we're now zoomed not how we want."

Undiagnosed. A debugging task: find the cause, fix it, put a check in `checks/`.

Ideas to test, in order:
1. MapLibre's scroll zoom accumulates wheel deltas and eases toward a target over a few frames. If a
   frame takes long (the main thread blocked: a long task from a demand publish, 70-110 ms,
   T-354; a worker result applied; a world rebuild after a far move, T-356), the pending delta of
   several wheel events lands in one frame: that is a 4x jump. Look for what the map's handlers do on
   a wheel event (`mapView.ts`, `overlay.ts`, any `scrollZoom` options, `wheelZoomRate` and
   `zoomRate`), and whether our code calls `map.jumpTo`/`easeTo` during zoom that fights the ease.
2. Wheel events that arrive with `deltaMode` lines or a trackpad's high rate, or the page's own
   `wheel` listener (the dock, tooltips) calling `preventDefault` late.
3. The draw anchor (T-307) moving during a zoom and resetting the camera.

Method: `?debug=1` has `window.tw.lagStart/lagStop` and `checks/t348-lag.mjs` finds long tasks. Script a
steady wheel (dispatch real wheel events from Playwright at 60 Hz) while a long task is injected
(a busy loop on the main thread for 150 ms), and compare the zoom travelled with the same wheel
and no lag. If it reproduces, the fix is probably to cap the zoom taken by one frame (clamp the
delta applied per frame, or use the events' timestamps instead of the frame count), in our code
over MapLibre's handler. If it does not, report what was tried and add an always-on, cheap
detector in `?debug=1` (zoom change per frame over a threshold logs the frame times) so a
playtest can catch it.

Files: `map/mapView.ts`, `map/overlay.ts`; no UI.

## Found and fixed (2026-10-19)

Idea 1's mechanism was close but not the cause: a stall does make several ticks' delta land in one
frame, but MapLibre's scroll zoom compresses that (a sigmoid, at most one zoom level a frame), so a
stalled mouse-wheel scroll goes slightly *less* far. The cause is MapLibre 6.13's guess of the
device. `ScrollZoomHandler.wheel` takes a scroll's first event, then calls it a trackpad if the
second comes within `200 / |deltaY|` ms of it (2 ms for Chrome's deltaY 100 per notch), measured with
`performance.now()` when each is *handled*. A main-thread stall as the scroll starts queues the
first two notches and they are handled back to back (0.4 ms apart, while their own timestamps are
24 ms apart), so the wheel is a trackpad for the whole scroll: the trackpad rate is 1/100 against
the wheel's 1/450 per delta unit, and the trackpad path jumps straight to the target with no 200 ms
ease. That is Anita's "instantly, about 4x as far" (3.6x per notch; 2.8x over a 12-notch scroll),
and it clears at the next scroll after a 400 ms pause, when the guess starts over. A stall over
400 ms in mid-scroll also restarts the guess (seen, little effect on travel).

Measured (`artifacts/trainworld/t675/repro-before.json`): 12 notches of deltaY 100 at 60 Hz from
zoom 11 travel 1.80 zoom calm; with a 150 ms busy loop started with the first notch, 5.08 (both
runs), largest step 0.97 zoom in one frame against 0.22. A 150 ms stall mid-scroll: 1.73. Through
Playwright's awaited `mouse.wheel` it never reproduces (each tick waits for the last to be handled).
Ideas 2 and 3 not needed: our own `wheel` listeners (tip.ts, trackHover.ts) are passive, no code of
ours moves the camera during a zoom.

Fix (`map/mapView.ts`, `wheelGapsByEventTime`): the handler instance's `wheel` is wrapped to set
`_lastWheelEventTime` back by the gap between the events' own `timeStamp`s just before each event,
so its guess sees when the notches happened. With it: 1.71-1.75 under the same stall, every event
classed as a wheel, the 500 ms mid-scroll stall no longer restarts the guess. It leans on a private
field: if a MapLibre upgrade renames it, the wrapper does nothing and checks/t675-wheel-lag.mjs
fails again. A stalled scroll still goes a little short (MapLibre's compression of a burst; the
replay holds 9 notches into one frame and travels 1.27 against 1.80), left as it is.

checks/t675-wheel-lag.mjs: a replay in the page (12 wheel events made 16 ms apart, the first 9 held
and handled together, the same every run) and real CDP input behind a busy loop (varies with
Chrome's queueing). `RAW=1` deletes the wrapper: FAIL (replay x1.47, step 1.00; real x2.82, step
0.97); with the fix PASS (replay x0.71, real x0.95, steps 0.23-0.26).

## Our stalls during a zoom (T-689)

Real save, solved, eight wheel scrolls in and out between zoom 7.5 and 13.4 over 40 s, traced
(`artifacts/trainworld/t675/trace-zoom.json`, `checks/t348-analyze.mjs ... 20`). No task over 40 ms
apart from the trace's own start; none from a demand publish (T-354) or a world rebuild (T-356) in
that window, though either can land in a zoom on her machine. Over 20 ms: a zoom's first frame
36 ms (station label placement 12 ms, `capacityMarkers.ts` place 6 ms, layout), the hover pick on
the mousemove just before a scroll 30 ms (`pick.ts` topSeg, `trackHover.ts`), and frames of
24-28 ms. With notches 20-30 ms apart a 30 ms task at the start of a scroll is enough to queue two,
so before the fix even these could set it off. Filed as T-689.
