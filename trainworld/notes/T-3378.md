## What the frontier runs say (the deciding evidence)

The T-3887 made-up city (even density, one straight 60 km line, the game's own track, demand and fare code), run
from a scratch copy of the sim with knobs for the BRT floor, the stop time, a fixed off-road speed, a waiting room at
every stop and stand-in bus stations (street tram stops 30 m off the road that no line serves, so the bus stops at
them as at any station; their track's price and upkeep taken back out, US$2M and US$50 a day each put in). Scripts, results
and the scratch sim's patch: `artifacts/trainworld/T-3378/` (`run.py`, `rows.py`, `out/*.json`, `sim-patch.diff`;

**Rerun of the bus on today's rules** (the T-3887 Open item: those bus rows ran at the old 30 km/h floor). Plain bus,
10,000 residents a km²: 16.8 km/h on automatic stops (was about 19), 20.1 at 800 m stops (was 23), 22.1 at 1.5 km.
BRT today (35 km/h floor, 28 s stops): 20.8, 26.1, 29.6.

What decides it is **how riders reach the bus and how many buses fit**, not lane speed or stop time:

- **Reach.** A bus stop that is no station is reached only from within 600 m of the route, on foot (`BUS_REACH_M`,
  two such stops a cell, `BUS_SLOTS`); a station is reached like any station (the 6 nearest within the walk's 3 km,
  and by car, per docs/demand-kernel.md; how much each part gives was not split out). The by-distance shares show it: of 5 km trips by people living 648 m off the line, a plain bus takes
  0%, BRT at stations 19%, the tram 31%. Stations multiply a bus line's riders by 3.3 at every 4 minutes (82k to 269k)
  and 4.2 at every 2 (88k to 369k). This is today's rule, not a new one: a bus passing within 200 m of any station
  already stops there and gets its reach (`bus::reach`), so a player can do it now with tram stops beside a bus route. The
  0.8 wait alone is worth about 1% of riders, the 18 s stop 2-3%, 40 against 35 km/h 3%. Without stations no on-street BRT beats a
  plain bus on money at this density (US$10.5-12.9M against 13.0M); lanes alone pay only in a dense city (30,000 a
  km², every 2 min: US$76.0M against 68.6M).
- **Room.** A bus holds 80 at crush, a 2-section tram 360. At every 4 minutes the station variants are crowded (their
  shares drop under the plain bus's while riders are 3.5x); every 2 minutes clears it at 10,000 a km². At 30,000 a
  km² a bus every minute (60 an hour, the least headway the game allows, `HEADWAY_MIN_S`) carries 1.49M riders against
  the street tram's 1.59M and nets US$397M against 486M; every 30 s (two lines on one lane, 120 an hour) 2.13M and
  US$549M, past every tram. Slowing the buses past 60 is not enough: the worst case of that rule (buses at plain speed,
  at stations, 120 an hour) still carries 1.98M riders and nets US$468M. So a lane limit of **60 buses an hour each
  way, with each line's buses cut to fit past it** (as track cuts trains) keeps BRT under the tram where people are
  dense. A line on headways never reaches it alone (a bus a minute is the least headway); a line set by bus count on
  a short route can, and is then held to 60 as a train line is to its track.
- **Ladder with the recommended answers.** Plain bus (cheap, narrow reach, its niche), then BRT with stations (about
  the street tram's riders and 93% of its money at 10,000 a km², well under it at 30,000 for lack of room), then trams
  and metro where density needs room. That is her "plain buses still have a niche, brt is more competitive with
  trams".
- **400 m point (coordinator's question).** The bus at 400 m has 31 stops because 400 m is the automatic rule
  (`spacing_from` stores 400 as 0): demand sees about one stop every 2 km (`DEMAND_STOP_M`) with each stop's stretch of
  route as its catchment, while the run pays 28 s every 400 m. A spacing of 401 m gives 151 real stops at the same
  16.8 km/h: riders +18% at every 4 minutes (65,962 to 77,537), 2 km trips +1.6 points, 5 km -2.2. Stops placed by
  hand 400 m apart use the same rule as a spacing (`StopRule::Hand`, `spaced_profile`), so they should match the 401
  m run; not run by hand. The automatic 400 m point is a little worse than real 400 m stops, not much.

## Stages (each ships alone; hours of agent time)

### Stage 1: shared lanes in town (about 3 h)

What a player sees: the BRT box on a bus line is unchanged, but where another BRT line already has lanes on the same
road, switching on pays nothing for that stretch. The hover says how much is new and how much shared. Upkeep is paid
once per road stretch. Plain lines on the road stay in traffic (question 4).

Rule: the lanes are the union of the in-town road of every BRT line (today's `brt_in_town`, 100 m pace steps). A step
of line B shares line A's lane when its middle lies within 20 m of A's in-town route and the two headings are within
30 degrees, either way (two lines on one road never have the same points: each leg is thinned on its own,
`roadGraph.ts` `thin`, and a divided road has two carriageways). Built value counts each shared step once (lines in
id order, a step counted only when no earlier line's step matches it); a switch on pays the rise in built value, as
today (`set_bus_brt` through `run`), so a second line pays only its new road. Planned lines: the blueprint's BRT row
(kind 16) is the union over every BRT line minus the union over constructed ones. Lanes no BRT line uses any more are
gone: no refund, paid again if a line comes back (today's "off gives nothing back").

Prices stay: US$5M a road-km in town and street tram track's upkeep. With stations (stage 3) that puts BRT at 93% of
the street tram's money at 10,000 a km² (US$94M against 101M a day), which is where she wants it.

Sim: `Network::bus_cost` and `brt_town_m` (net/store.rs) and `bus_upkeep` (wasm.rs) read a lane union worked out once
per settle (a grid of the BRT lines' in-town steps), `bus_quote` (wasm/bus.rs) prices against it, blueprint row in
net/costs.rs. No save change: `LBR1` and level 34 stay; a level-34 game loads with its lanes shared, its upkeep lower
where lines overlap, cash unchanged. App: `BrtRow` in ui/inspector/BusInspector.tsx, its hover's new and shared road.

### Stage 2: shared lane room (about 3 h)

What a player sees: on a BRT line's panel, under the box, the busiest lane on its route as buses per hour against 60.
Past 60, the lines on that lane run fewer buses than asked, each cut in the same share, with the held-out note and
red headway a train line gets when its track is full.

Rule: per period slot, a lane step's load is the sum of the buses an hour each way of every BRT line sharing it (stage
1's match; a one-way loop counts its one way). Past `LANE_BUSES_H` (60) each line on the step is cut by 60 / load, a
line by its fullest step, as track's count schedules are cut to fit (`FULL_RHO`, capacity.rs). Loads come from the
lines' headways or counts as asked, before the cut, so there is no loop. No slowdown: slowing past 60 still let 120
buses an hour through (the dense runs above).

Sim: a pass over the BRT lines' lanes before the bus lines' rates are set, feeding the same held-out figures the
track's cut gives a train line (`line_demand_tph`, the held messages); an edit to one BRT line's buses then moves the
others on its lanes (the settle's existing "a rate on another line moved" wake). No save change.

### Stage 3: BRT stations (about 4-5 h)

What a player sees: with the Station tool, a click off track within 50 m of a road a bus may use places a **bus
station**: drawn like a tram stop (a short platform beside the road, the station symbol and name), listed in the
Stations tab, with its panel and the Park and ride and Passage upgrades. A BRT line within 200 m of it stops there
(today's station reach), stops 18 s instead of 28 and stands no dwell there; a wait there counts 0.8, as in a waiting
room. Which lines stop there and how riders reach it: question 1.

Rule (recommended answer to question 1): a bus station is a station for riders, as a tram stop is; only BRT lines stop
at it, plain lines pass it. US$2M and US$50 a day real (US$5k a game day), a tram stop's. A station only bus lines
serve takes the tram stop's slot rule (`TRAM_SLOTS`, at most 3 of a cell's 6), so a row of them never hides the metro
(not in any run: the frontier line has no other stations).

Sim: a trackless station like a port without a berth (`StationData`, a new flag `bus`), placed by a new
`add_bus_station(x, y, name)`; `bus::reach` keeps bus stations for BRT lines only; `Pace` gets the stop time per stop
(18 s at a bus station on a BRT line); demand gets the 0.8 through `upgrades::WAITING_ROOM` or its own bit read by
`wait_factor` (kernel/subzones.rs), and the tram-only slot rule through the snapshot. Save: a trailing block listing
the bus stations (say `LBT1`), the next save level. App: the Station tool's placing off track (ui/ToolBar.tsx, the
station placement code), a bus station look in the network layer, Construct rows.

### Stage 4: off-road busways (about 5-6 h)

What a player sees: a fourth track type, **Busway**, after Tram in the track tool, drawn and built as off-road tram
track is (curves, levels, bridges, blueprint and Construct); no snap to streets. In the bus tool, busway counts as
road: a route follows it and its road hint tints it. Any bus on a constructed busway runs 60 km/h (question 3);
trains and trams can't use it; it holds no stations (bus stations go beside it).

Rule: `TrackKind::Busway = 4` (edge flags bits 8-10 hold it), its own family 2 (joins nothing else, crosses rail and
tram flat), a `TRACK_SPECS` row at two thirds of off-road tram track's prices (ground 0.333 of `BASE_COST_PER_KM`, so
US$25M a km; tram's other levels times 2/3), tram's terrain and full water, no road crossings, upkeep 1.0 (US$350 a
day real, US$35k a game day, off-road tram track's), cap 60, smallest radius 12 m. A route's 100 m pace step lies on
busway when its middle is within 8 m of a constructed busway piece (checked by the sim, not trusted from bits the
tool set, so deleting a busway takes the speed away); there it runs at the busway's speed with 7 s more for each stop
on it (the run has no acceleration: 8 s covers 35 km/h, about 15 s 60), pays no lane price (the track paid), and
counts towards stage 2's room. Out of town nothing else changes.

Sim: params.rs `TRACK_SPECS`, net checks for the new family (`StationFamily`, `TramTrack`-like refusal), `Pace` (the
busway match), `bus::Poly` road factor 1 on it. Save: the new kind needs the next save level (an older build drops kind
4). App: the track type buttons (ui/ToolBar.tsx, game/buildMode.ts), a busway look (a grey road strip with white
dashes, the tram wires' place), `busRoads.ts`/`roadGraph.ts` getting the network's busway pieces as roads.

## Open questions (hers), with my answers

1. **What a BRT station is.** (a) A station people walk and drive to like a tram stop, for BRT lines only: BRT with
   stations about matches the street tram at 10,000 a km² (412k riders, US$94M a day against 390k, US$101M), plain
   buses unchanged. (b) The same, but every bus stops there: a plain bus with stations gets 369k and US$77M, past the
   off-road tram's money. Plain buses can already get this today by passing train or tram stations (any station
   within 200 m is a stop), so (a) leaves the plain bus as it is now, not weaker. (c) A bus stop with the 0.8 wait and the 18 s stop, reached as bus stops are: about 5%
   more riders, and BRT stays under a plain bus on money. **Recommend (a).**
2. **Lane room: 60 buses an hour each way, the lines on it cut to fit past that.** Keeps BRT under trams in dense
   places (slowing them instead would not); never limits one line on headways. **Recommend yes.**
3. **Off-road busway: US$25M a km (two thirds of off-road tram track, as in town 5 against 7.5), upkeep as off-road
   tram track, 60 km/h, any bus.** At 37.5 it would make what the off-road tram makes. **Recommend 25.**
4. **Plain lines on BRT lanes in town stay in traffic** (the box is the upgrade); off road there is no traffic, so any
   bus runs the busway's speed. **Recommend yes.**
5. **Keep 35 km/h** (40 tested: +3% riders, +5% money) **and 18 s at a bus station; a bus station US$2M** (a tram
   stop's). **Recommend yes.**
