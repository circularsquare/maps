# T-3435: long-distance ferries, a study (agent ferries, 2026-11-16)

Anita, 2026-11-16: "long distance ferries kinda suck, probably need to make the "ferry fan" mechanism a bit more
prominent especially for super long ferries. do a general recalibraiton of like how our ferries currently are relative
to irl ferries... are they like NYC ferries? consider splitting ferries into 2 types, one for longer, not sure what would
make sense / be realistic / get realistic usage". Players say the same (board "Better long-distance ferries"; T-3431).
A study; nothing changed.

## Our ferries now

- One boat kind: 320 riders (480 crush), top speed 32 km/h in open water, 10 km/h in narrow channels, slow to
  speed up. Port US$10M, boat US$8M, US$40 a boat-km. A new line runs every 20 min at high demand, 30 at medium, none
  at low (T-3400 would add every 60).
- Demand treats a ferry as a train line: half the headway as the wait, no boarding cost, the normal fare curve times
  the Ferry multiple (1.00). Inter-city trips wait at most 30 min.
- Ferry fans: a tenth of visits and attractions trips count each minute aboard as 0.1. Only for trips inside one zone.
  Inter-city trips (the "longer" ones a long ferry carries) have no fans at all.

So yes, our boats are NYC Ferry boats: NYC Ferry runs 150-350 seat boats and averages 19 km/h door to door.

## What long routes get today

A new game, each line built on its own with the default service, weekday riders (both ways together):

| Route | Ours: km, minutes | Ours: riders weekday / Sunday | Real |
|---|---|---|---|
| Staten Island (city, for scale) | 8, 19 | 14,700 / 9,700 (no subway in a new game) | about 45,000 a weekday, 25 min, 4,500-seat boats at 16 knots [1] |
| Helsinki - Tallinn | 81, 159 | 16,500 / 24,300 | 7.5-9M a year, about 20,000-25,000 a day; 2 h at 27 knots, 2,800 seats [2] |
| Hong Kong - Macau | 66, 129 | 1,800 / 1,400 | 7.0M a year at the Sheung Wan terminal, about 19,000 a day; 55-60 min [3] |
| Vancouver - Nanaimo | 67, 130 | 424 / 106 (commutes only) | Hullo fast ferry about 400,000 its first year, 1,100 a day, 38 knots [4]; BC Ferries 22.7M a year in all [5] |
| Seattle - Victoria | 131, 251 | 0 / 0 | Clipper about 200,000 a year (8M since 1986), 2 h 45 [6]; Coho from Port Angeles about 400,000 [7] |
| Dover - Calais | 41, 83 | 321 / 490 | Dover 8.9M passengers in 2023, nearly all in cars and coaches; only P&O takes foot passengers [8] |
| Kobe - Takamatsu | 116, 223 | 26 / 6 | no current figure; one 620-seat ship pair, 4-5 round trips a day, about 4 h 15, 1,990 yen [9] |

Helsinki - Tallinn already comes out close to real. The others are low for four different reasons:

1. **Too slow.** Real long ferries run 45-50 km/h (ro-pax, 24-27 knots) or 65-78 km/h (fast craft, 35-42 knots).
   Ours do 32. Measured by standing in a train line in a bored tunnel with a speed limit, same headways
   (the demand rules are the same for a ferry): Hong Kong - Macau carries 4,300 at 40 km/h, 6,700 at 50,
   9,900 at 65, 12,400 at 80. Helsinki - Tallinn hardly moves (17,100 to 17,200): it already wins against a car
   that has to take the car ferry.
2. **Island trips are never solved.** Inter-city trips between places on two landmasses with no car and no plane
   between them (under 250 km) are counted but never offered the player's network (`solvable` needs `has_alt`).
   Vancouver and Vancouver Island have 51,000 trips a day each way and none can ride; the same for Aland,
   most Greek islands. This is why T-3431's Seattle - Victoria gets 0. The fix is the open task T-2216 (rail as the
   only ground mode between landmasses), already your call from 2026-11-03.
3. **Fans do nothing for long ferries.** They only exist inside a zone. Even there they are small: Staten Island
   with fans 14,717 a weekday, without 14,670 (+0.3%).
4. **A ferry alone reaches only its ports' neighbourhoods.** Kobe - Takamatsu gets 113 even at 80 km/h: most of
   Osaka's trips start far from Kobe port. With rail feeding the ports this would grow; that's realistic.

## How real ferries differ

| | City ferry (NYC Ferry, Sydney) | Fast craft (Hullo, TurboJET) | Ro-pax (Megastar, BC Ferries) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Speed | 25-30 knots top, 19 km/h door to door with stops | 35-42 knots, 65-78 km/h | 20-27 knots, 37-50 km/h |
| Seats | 150-350 (Staten Island 4,500) | 300-1,000 | 1,000-2,800 plus cars |
| Boarding | walk on | 15-30 min check-in | 30-45 min check-in for foot passengers [10] |
| Service | every 15-60 min | hourly or so | a few to 20 sailings a day |
| Fare | US$4 NYC Ferry, free Staten Island | HK$194 (about US$25) for 64 km; Hullo from C$20 | Jumbo 1,990 yen for 116 km; Helsinki - Tallinn about EUR 30 |

Our fare curve charges about $22.50 for 100 km in the US: inside the real range.

## Options

**(a) Recalibrate the one boat.** Top speed 32 to 50 km/h for every ferry. Players: nothing new to learn. Riders:
Hong Kong - Macau about 1,800 to 6,700; Helsinki - Tallinn unchanged. Cost: city ferries get quicker than real ones
(Staten Island 19 min to about 14, real 25), and fast craft are still out of reach.

**(b) A second kind, chosen per line like normal and high-speed trains.** "Fast ferry": 65 km/h, the same 320
seats, about US$25M a boat (my guess; no clean new-build price found; a 1,006-seat 40-knot ferry cost A$109M [11])
and about US$100 a boat-km. Same Ferry fare row. Players: one choice on the ferry panel and in the Construct quote.
Riders: Hong Kong - Macau about 9,900 (real 19,000); city ferries untouched. Its own stats panel line, save field and
check: about 4-6 hours.

**(c) Fans for long ferries.** Give the inter-city trips fans too (a tenth at 0.1 a minute). The most it can add is
a tenth of a corridor's trips: Helsinki - Tallinn up to about +2,700, Hong Kong - Macau up to about +1,600. A new
demand factor, and at 0.1 a 4 hour crossing feels like 24 minutes, so a fan would take it over a train beside it.
Helsinki - Tallinn, the one route already close to real, needs none.

## Recommendation

T-2216 first, since without it no ferry to an island without a bridge gets inter-city riders whatever the boat. Then
(b), a Fast ferry kind at 65 km/h, US$25M a boat, US$100 a boat-km. Leave the fans as they are for now and look again
once fast ferries are in.
