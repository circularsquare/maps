## Commute fans (comfans, 2026-11-20)

7.5% of commutes inside a zone are ferry fans, with the outings' fans' cost and assignment (rule:
docs/demand-mode.md "Ferry fans").

**Where**:
- `sim/src/demand/kernel/fans.rs` (new): `FanCosts`, `NO_FAN` and `FAN_RIDE_W` moved from everyday.rs unchanged;
  `COMMUTE_FAN_SHARE` 0.075; `CommuteFans`: per station the stations a fan reaches cheaper by a ferry (a list out
  of `FanCosts::via`), `route_all` (the fans' ferry trips as rides to and from the ports plus a ride per port
  pair), `both_ways` (the trips home: the ride back q to p, or the ordinary way between the ports where the line
  has no ride back), `add_rides` (the outings' `add_fan_rides` for one `Loads`). A pair whose way back would end a
  leg at a station no strategy is searched to (not in `want`, such as a port past every cell's walk) loses its
  `via`: the assignment drops flow towards such a station (a `debug_assert` only, off in the agent and shipped
  builds), so those fans' trips home would have lost their legs' loads.
- `kernel/mode.rs`: `mode_choice_fans` (`mode_choice_part` calls it with none), compiled twice by a const generic
  so the loop with no ferries is the old one. An origin any of whose stations has a `via` gets a third pair of
  rows (its two with each fan pair's exp in place of `er`); a lane with an egress station marked for its row sums
  those too (one gather a step), takes the fans' share beside everyone's at 0.075 (walk tier on everyone's
  costs, as everyday's `pr_t`), and spreads the fans' trips on the fans' rows. At the split the fans' trips go
  with everyone's, then each fan pair on a ferry moves from `od` to the new `ModeOut::od_fan` at the fans' exp.
  Walking rule: the refused pairs' fans' weights are taken off as everyone's are; where the rule refuses over
  half the fans' weight and not over half of everyone's (no list of kept pairs to load alone), the fans on that
  pair choose as everyone does. The fans' kept sum never drops below everyone's (rounding broke a test there once).
- `api.rs`: `DemandApi::set_ferry` (as everyday's, drops the periods on a change); `pass` builds `CommuteFans` from
  the period's skims, keeps `SubOut::od` on the true station pairs (`od` + `od_fan`), routes `od_fan`, mixes the
  trips home, adds the rides to the loads after the assignment.
- `app/src/workers/demand.worker.ts` line 2052: `set_ferry` for both city populations (was everyday only).
- Tests (api/tests.rs): `commute_fans_ride_where_a_ferry_is_their_way` (flags none, all 0, or a ferry 60 km out
  solve as before to the bit, free flow and crowded; line b as a ferry: its boardings and rail up; entries =
  exits = rail trips); `commute_fans_are_the_mix_of_two_choices` (the kernel against 0.925 x mode choice on `er`
  plus 0.075 x on `er` with the fans' exps written in: rail, fare sensitivity, walkers taken, by home subzone,
  station pair by station pair within 1e-4 with the walk tier and rule off, 2e-3 with the rule on);
  `commute_fans_go_back_by_stations_searched_to` (a ferry to a port no trip ends at, a train on: on the pass's
  own skims some fan way leaves the ferry there, none of the commute's does; entries = exits = rail trips). A
  first try comparing the train's riders out and back proved nothing: everyone's own trips there are lopsided
  (10,285 out, 0 back with no ferry at all).

## 3. Fans

Your call 11-20: a slight increase, broadened to every trip purpose, half as much for work, medical and airport.

**What "half" means**: half the share (7.5% of commutes are fans against 15% of the rest), not half the effect. One
weight for every fan keeps the rule to two numbers and a list.

**Local fans (option A, my pick)**: 15% of visits, attractions, errands, school and university trips, 7.5% of commutes,
medical and airport trips, each minute aboard at 0.1 as today. City ferries' rides are under half an hour, so no
floor is needed while fans stay inside a zone.

**Inter-city fans (a separate call)**: the same shares for the regional market's commutes and outings between zones
(they carry purposes) and for its longer trips, which carry no purpose: all longer trips at the full 15% is my guess
(most long trips are leisure: NHTS), and yours to set. This needs a floor so a 4-hour crossing doesn't beat a train
beside it: a ride never feels more than 30 minutes shorter than it is. A harbour ferry is as before; Helsinki -
Tallinn's 114 minutes feel like 84; a 4-hour crossing like 3.5 hours, so a 2-hour train keeps its riders.

**Measured** (weekday riders; the extra is linear in the share, measured from a build where everyone is a fan with
the 30-minute floor, mixed by each route's purposes; local purposes as Staten Island's on the real save, where today's
fans are already in the base and are taken off):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who | 10% of visits, attractions; inside a zone | 15% / 7.5% inside a zone | and between zones | 25% / 12.5% | all |
| Staten Island | 15,755 | +290 (+2%) | +290 | +520 (+3%) | +3,275 |
| Manly | 9,932 | +285 (+3%) | +285 | +515 (+5%) | +3,229 |

| Seattle - Victoria, Kobe - Takamatsu | 1,552; 65 | 0 | +1; +3 | +1; +6 | +6; +22 |

Inter-city fans are 3-5 hours, a new market rule and a new constant (the 30 minutes) for +14% on one route and 1-2%
elsewhere. Given "don't buff long routes a ton", I'd build local fans now and leave inter-city fans until a route
shows it needs them. Fans matter where a car competes with the boat (Hong Kong - Macau). On island crossings everyone near the ports rides
already (the islands rule), so fans add little. With a softer floor (every ride at half its minutes) Hong Kong -
Macau's ceiling was 16,086 instead of 11,849; with no floor it would be higher still, and a long scenic crossing could
beat a fast train beside it.

**In the code**: local fans take a share by purpose (one table; `fan_kind` becomes a share). Inter-city
fans are new: the market's longer trips, commutes and outings between zones get the same composed cost through the
ports as the local layer's (cost to the port, the fan's ride, cost on), only while a ferry line exists. Its solve time
needs a check on a big save with ferries (T-2196 found +1.3% for the local fans).

- **Pay**: Helsinki - Tallinn, Vancouver - Nanaimo, Vancouver - Victoria: a Large boat every 2 hours (+7-9M a day),
  or Medium hourly (+2.5-3.4M). Staten Island, as now.
- **Close**: Hong Kong - Macau: a Small boat hourly about -0.8M, Large every 2 hours -3.8M, Medium every 2 hours
  -0.3M; inter-city fans would add 14%. It competes with a road and a bus over the bridge, as the real one does.
- **Don't, and shouldn't**: Seattle - Victoria (5,800 trips a day between the two, nearly all ride already; the real
  Clipper carries about 550), Kobe - Takamatsu (port reach; rail feeding Kobe port would help). Long thin lines lose
  money in the real world too.
- **Your Okinawa line, for now**: its islands aren't places of their own. In the market rows Yaeyama, Miyako and the
  Okinawa islands are zones of the Okinawa place (`oka`), Amami, Yakushima and Tanegashima zones of Kagoshima (`kgs`);
  Taiwan's places are on another landmass. Trips between a place's own zones on two islands still go by an imagined
  car (T-2216 step 2, unbuilt, yours to approve), and Okinawa - Kagoshima is over 250 km with airports, so it flies.
  Step 2 is what would give this line its island trips.
- **Still missing**: a trip's length doesn't change how many trips there are (gravity ignores water, T-2216), so the
  island pairs carry about real numbers by luck of the 3-4x cut-off; ports' reach is a station's walk, so a ferry
  needs feeders as a train does; and the Okinawa line shows a long multi-stop line has no way to run its far legs
  less often than its busy middle (split it into lines).

## 5. Full-load fares over running cost (her question)

Default China fares (US$1.00 plus 8 cents a km to 20 km, then 7.9 cents; US$8.90 for 100 km, 16.78 for 200, 24.65
for 300), China's running scale 0.39. Every rider rides the whole trip at 100% load (the panels' 100%: 160 a car, 120
a tram section, 53 a bus). Running a vehicle-km at world prices times 0.39; the economy's x100 is on both sides, so
the ratios are the game's. Upkeep: track's US$350 a route-km a day (high speed 1.5x) shared over 112 runs a day (the
default service); ports' upkeep is nil a boat-km. Payback: the vehicle's price over its daily margin at 16 hours a day.

Every country comes out about the same: fares and running both follow income to the 0.5 power. A full train's fares
are about 14 times its running cost, a full ferry's under 2 times; trains pay for that with track (US$10-60M a km).
Real life agrees in direction: NYC Ferry covers 26% of its costs from fares, BC Ferries' one profitable route is
Vancouver - Victoria. If you want ferries to pay more easily, the lever is the running cost (Medium at US$30 a boat-km would
take Helsinki - Tallinn from -2.0M to +4.4M a day at today's default service), not the fans.
