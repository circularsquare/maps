# T-3892: ferry rework, a design (ferrydesign, 2026-11-20)

Design only; nothing in the game changed. Sources: research/ferries.md (new) and notes/T-3435.md. Measured in the game on
today's master (78c7f12f, islands fix in) with play.mjs, a new game each time, each line alone. Fans, boat sizes and the
before-islands build were measured in scratch trees (`.tw-trees/ferrydesign`, `ferrybefore`), removed afterwards.

## For Anita, in short

- **Long ferries aren't short of riders any more.** Since the islands fix: Vancouver - Nanaimo 17,800 a weekday,
  Vancouver - Victoria 16,700 (BC Ferries' real routes: 14,600 and 19,200 a day), Helsinki - Tallinn 17,900 (real
  20,000-25,000). They lose money because a 320-seat boat every 20 minutes over 70-130 km costs more than it takes.
  At a boat an hour they earn US$2.5-3.4M a day.
- **Your Okinawa line** (Ferry 72, 1,429 km, Keelung to Kagoshima, 40 boats): 1,370 riders a weekday before the
  islands fix, 1,398 after; US$-78.8M a day either way. The fix didn't reach it: the outer islands are zones of the
  Okinawa and Kagoshima places, which still drive across (T-2216 step 2, yours to approve), and Okinawa - Kagoshima flies.
- **Boat types (my numbers)**: Small 160 riders, 65 km/h, US$15M, US$40 a boat-km, fares 1.5x. Medium as today. Large
  1,280, 45 km/h, US$50M, US$100 a boat-km, 1.0x, 5 minutes at each port. Picked on the ferry panel where a train has
  its cars. A Large boat every 2 hours earns US$7-9M a day on the three routes above with 75-80% of today's riders;
  a Small one lifts Hong Kong - Macau from 6,200 to 8,000 and doubles its fares.
- **Fans, as you asked**: 15% of every purpose, 7.5% (half the share) for commutes, medical and airport. City ferries
  +2-3%. Giving inter-city trips fans too is a separate call: Hong Kong - Macau +14%, island routes +1-2%, 3-5 hours
  more work; I'd wait on it.
- **Full-load fares over running**, China's default fares, 200 km: train 13.8, high speed 11.7, tram 7.7, bus 1.9,
  ferry today 1.7, Small 1.3, Large 2.8.
- **Build**: types and per-type fares about 5-7 hours; local fans 1-2 hours.

## 1. Where ferries stand now

One boat: 320 riders at 100% load (480 at crush), 45 km/h in open water, 10 km/h in harbours, 0.15 m/s² to speed up.
Port US$10M, boat US$8M, US$40 a boat-km (times 100 in the game, times the country's running scale). A new line runs
a boat every 20 minutes at high demand, 30 at medium, 60 at night. Fare: the curve, the part above the base times the
Ferry multiple (1.00). Fans: a tenth of visits and attractions trips inside one zone count each minute aboard at 0.1;
inter-city trips have none.

An inter-city rider's wait is planned (half the headway, at most 5 minutes plus a sixth of it, at most 30), so an
hourly boat costs only 7 minutes more than one every 20. Where riders halve at hourly service it is crowding: the busy
hours reach 90-130% of crush and riders mind a packed boat (nobody is left behind; demand prices the crowding), so
bigger boats win them back (section 2). Seattle - Victoria and Kobe - Takamatsu are short of trips, not boats.

**Walking's head start (T-3888)** doesn't touch these: no one walks across 8 km of harbour. On short river crossings
with a bridge nearby, dropping it would win a ferry a few more riders.

## 2. Boat types

Your call 11-20: ferry types go ahead, small and large beside today's medium. Real classes in research/ferries.md.

| | Small | Medium (today) | Large |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real ones | fast craft: Kitsap, Hullo, TurboJET (250-400 seats, 35-42 knots) | NYC Ferry, Sydney (150-350, 25 knots) | Staten Island Ollis (4,500), ro-pax (1,600-2,800 plus cars, 20-27 knots) |
| Riders at 100% / crush | 160 / 240 (one car) | 320 / 480 (two cars) | 1,280 / 1,920 (eight cars) |
| Open water speed | 65 km/h | 45 km/h | 45 km/h |
| Boat | US$15M (a new 250-seat fast ferry: US$17.5M) | US$8M | US$50M (Ollis US$105M for 4,500) |
| Running a boat-km | US$40 (fast craft burn far more fuel a seat) | US$40 (NYC Ferry about US$54 real) | US$100 (Staten Island about US$330 real for 4,500 seats) |
| Running a seat-km | 0.25 | 0.125 | 0.078 |
| Dwell at a port between ends / turnaround | 60 s / 5 min | 60 s / 5 min | 5 min / 20 min |
| Ferry fare multiple (default) | 1.50 (TurboJET charges about 1.7x our US curve) | 1.00 | 1.00 (real ro-pax fares are at or above our curve) |
| Drawn as | 25 m hull | 35 m, as now | 90 m |

Large is not slower: real ro-pax run 37-50 km/h and one speed for both keeps it simple. Its trade-off is the boat's
price, the minutes in port and needing full boats. Small trades seats for speed and fare. A Large line's riders
don't pay a check-in time: that would be a new demand factor, and the dwell stands in for it.

So: Large every 2 hours is the long-route boat (its riders fall 20%, its running cost by 60%); Small is for routes
where the speed wins riders off the car (Hong Kong - Macau: fares 5.4M to 10.3M a day) and otherwise earns about what
Medium does with fewer riders. Neither is a free win, which is what we want. Small is the one I'm least sure of:
at US$30 a boat-km it beats Medium everywhere (Helsinki +4.9M), at US$40 it ties.

**Where the player picks**: the ferry line panel, a row "Boat" with Small, Medium and Large, where a train line has
its cars stepper. Changing it is one undoable edit: the old boats go to the spares, new ones are bought as with cars;
the Construct quote and the edit note price it. New ferry lines start Medium. Each type is its own fleet with its own
spares (Money tab: boats owned, needed and spare by type). Advanced fares: the one Ferry row becomes three (Small
ferry 1.50x, Ferry 1.00x, Large ferry 1.00x), each with its own stepper and default, as you asked. A save's
existing Ferry multiple becomes Medium's; Small and Large start at their defaults.

**In the code**: the line's boat type replaces the fixed two cars (`BOAT_DEMAND_CARS`, `BOAT_CRUSH`): demand and the
boarding account already take cars per line. Speed: the route keeps its speeds as searched (10-45 km/h by room); a
Small boat runs each point over 10 km/h at the same share of the way from 10 to 65 (as old saves' 32 km/h routes load
today), its bends' limits rising with it. Running, price, dwell and turnaround by type in the sim's ferry module.

**Saves**: one new trailing block (each ferry line's type; save level +1). Older saves load every ferry as Medium,
exactly as now. An older build can't read the new block's types (its boats load as Medium). The three fleets go in
the game header beside `fleetFerry` (absent when zero).

**Default service for long lines**: a new ferry line over 40 km starts at a boat every 60 minutes at high and medium
demand and none at night, instead of 20/30/60 (the service measured above). A sensible default I'd make and tell you;
it is why today's long lines look broke.

## 3. Fans

Your call 11-20: a slight increase, broadened to every trip purpose, half as much for work, medical and airport.

**What "half" means**: half the share (7.5% of commutes are fans against 15% of the rest), not half the effect. One
weight for every fan keeps the rule to two numbers and a list.

**Local fans (option A, my pick)**: 15% of visits, attractions, errands, school and university trips, 7.5% of commutes,
medical and airport trips, each minute aboard at 0.1 as today. City ferries' rides are under half an hour, so no
floor is needed while fans stay inside a zone.

**Inter-city fans (a separate call)**: the same shares for the regional market's commutes and outings between zones
(they carry purposes) and for its longer trips, which carry no purpose: all longer trips at the full 15% is my guess
(most long trips are leisure: NHTS), and yours to set. This needs a floor so a 4-hour crossing doesn't beat a train
beside it: a ride never feels more than 30 minutes shorter than it is. A harbour ferry is as before; Helsinki -
Tallinn's 114 minutes feel like 84; a 4-hour crossing like 3.5 hours, so a 2-hour train keeps its riders.

**Measured** (weekday riders; the extra is linear in the share, measured from a build where everyone is a fan with
the 30-minute floor, mixed by each route's purposes; local purposes as Staten Island's on the real save, where today's
fans are already in the base and are taken off):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who | 10% of visits, attractions; inside a zone | 15% / 7.5% inside a zone | and between zones | 25% / 12.5% | all |
| Staten Island | 15,755 | +290 (+2%) | +290 | +520 (+3%) | +3,275 |
| Manly | 9,932 | +285 (+3%) | +285 | +515 (+5%) | +3,229 |

| Vancouver - Nanaimo | 17,804 | 0 | +175 (+1%) | +290 (+2%) | +1,153 |
| Vancouver - Victoria | 16,664 | 0 | +245 (+1.5%) | +410 (+2.5%) | +1,643 |
| Seattle - Victoria, Kobe - Takamatsu | 1,552; 65 | 0 | +1; +3 | +1; +6 | +6; +22 |

Inter-city fans are 3-5 hours, a new market rule and a new constant (the 30 minutes) for +14% on one route and 1-2%
elsewhere. Given "don't buff long routes a ton", I'd build local fans now and leave inter-city fans until a route
shows it needs them. Fans matter where a car competes with the boat (Hong Kong - Macau). On island crossings everyone near the ports rides
already (the islands rule), so fans add little. With a softer floor (every ride at half its minutes) Hong Kong -
Macau's ceiling was 16,086 instead of 11,849; with no floor it would be higher still, and a long scenic crossing could
beat a fast train beside it.

**In the code**: local fans take a share by purpose (one table; `fan_kind` becomes a share). Inter-city
fans are new: the market's longer trips, commutes and outings between zones get the same composed cost through the
ports as the local layer's (cost to the port, the fan's ride, cost on), only while a ferry line exists. Its solve time
needs a check on a big save with ferries (T-2196 found +1.3% for the local fans).

## 4. Long routes: which pay, and what's still missing

At the default service only Staten Island pays. With the changes:

- **Pay**: Helsinki - Tallinn, Vancouver - Nanaimo, Vancouver - Victoria: a Large boat every 2 hours (+7-9M a day),
  or Medium hourly (+2.5-3.4M). Staten Island, as now.
- **Close**: Hong Kong - Macau: a Small boat hourly about -0.8M, Large every 2 hours -3.8M, Medium every 2 hours
  -0.3M; inter-city fans would add 14%. It competes with a road and a bus over the bridge, as the real one does.
- **Don't, and shouldn't**: Seattle - Victoria (5,800 trips a day between the two, nearly all ride already; the real
  Clipper carries about 550), Kobe - Takamatsu (port reach; rail feeding Kobe port would help). Long thin lines lose
  money in the real world too.
- **Your Okinawa line, for now**: its islands aren't places of their own. In the market rows Yaeyama, Miyako and the
  Okinawa islands are zones of the Okinawa place (`oka`), Amami, Yakushima and Tanegashima zones of Kagoshima (`kgs`);
  Taiwan's places are on another landmass. Trips between a place's own zones on two islands still go by an imagined
  car (T-2216 step 2, unbuilt, yours to approve), and Okinawa - Kagoshima is over 250 km with airports, so it flies.
  Step 2 is what would give this line its island trips.
- **Still missing**: a trip's length doesn't change how many trips there are (gravity ignores water, T-2216), so the
  island pairs carry about real numbers by luck of the 3-4x cut-off; ports' reach is a station's walk, so a ferry
  needs feeders as a train does; and the Okinawa line shows a long multi-stop line has no way to run its far legs
  less often than its busy middle (split it into lines).

**Okinawa, in detail** (her save `t3600-asianita-1852`, day 557, 221 lines): Ferry 72, 1,429 km, 17 ports from Keelung
by Yonaguni, Ishigaki, Miyako, Naha, Amami and Tanegashima to Kagoshima, 33.7 hours end to end, 40 boats, a boat about
every 100 minutes in the day. Weekday 1,370 riders before the islands fix, 1,398 after; Sunday 864 and 906; fares
US$1.11M and 1.19M a day against running US$79.8M; busiest hour 17% of crush, about 64 riders a sailing. Boardings
are mostly at Naha's two ports (231 and 297). Her three short ferries (Ferry 61-63) didn't change either.

## 5. Full-load fares over running cost (her question)

Default China fares (US$1.00 plus 8 cents a km to 20 km, then 7.9 cents; US$8.90 for 100 km, 16.78 for 200, 24.65
for 300), China's running scale 0.39. Every rider rides the whole trip at 100% load (the panels' 100%: 160 a car, 120
a tram section, 53 a bus). Running a vehicle-km at world prices times 0.39; the economy's x100 is on both sides, so
the ratios are the game's. Upkeep: track's US$350 a route-km a day (high speed 1.5x) shared over 112 runs a day (the
default service); ports' upkeep is nil a boat-km. Payback: the vehicle's price over its daily margin at 16 hours a day.

| Vehicle | Riders at 100% | Fares a vehicle-km, 100 / 200 / 300 km | Running a vehicle-km | Fares over running, 100 / 200 / 300 km | Upkeep a vehicle-km | Days to pay for the vehicle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Train, 8 cars | 1,280 | $113.9 / $107.4 / $105.2 | $7.8 | 14.6 / 13.8 / 13.5 | $3.1 | 1.4 |
| High speed, 8 cars | 1,280 | $139.2 / $132.6 / $130.4 | $11.3 | 12.3 / 11.7 / 11.5 | $4.7 | 0.7 |
| Tram, 3 sections | 360 | $32.0 / $30.2 / $29.6 | $3.9 | 8.2 / 7.7 / 7.6 | $3.1 | 5.4 |
| Bus | 53 | $4.7 / $4.4 / $4.4 | $2.3 | 2.0 / 1.9 / 1.9 | $0.3 | 4.1 |
| Ferry today (Medium) | 320 | $28.5 / $26.8 / $26.3 | $15.6 | 1.8 / 1.7 / 1.7 | 0 | 11 |
| Small (proposed) | 160 | $20.6 / $19.7 / $19.5 | $15.6 | 1.3 / 1.3 / 1.2 | 0 | 39 |
| Large (proposed) | 1,280 | $113.9 / $107.4 / $105.2 | $39.0 | 2.9 / 2.8 / 2.7 | 0 | 11 |

Every country comes out about the same: fares and running both follow income to the 0.5 power. A full train's fares
are about 14 times its running cost, a full ferry's under 2 times; trains pay for that with track (US$10-60M a km).
Real life agrees in direction: NYC Ferry covers 26% of its costs from fares, BC Ferries' one profitable route is
Vancouver - Victoria. If you want ferries to pay more easily, the lever is the running cost (Medium at US$30 a boat-km would
take Helsinki - Tallinn from -2.0M to +4.4M a day at today's default service), not the fans.

## 6. Recommendation and build

1. **Boat types** as section 2's table, with per-type Ferry fare multiples (1.50, 1.00, 1.00) in advanced fares, and new
   ferry lines over 40 km starting at a boat every 60 minutes, none at night. Sim (types, speed mapping, running, prices,
   dwell, fleets, save block), app (panel row, quote, Money tab rows, three fare rows, boats drawn by length, demand's
   cars per line), docs and wiki. **About 5-7 hours.** One worktree.
2. **Local fans**: 15% of every purpose, 7.5% of commutes, medical and airport trips. A share table; re-run t2196 on
   the real save. **About 1-2 hours.** Can run beside 1 (demand files only).
3. **Inter-city fans, only if you want them**: the same shares for longer trips, commutes and outings between zones,
   composed through the ports, with the 30-minute floor. **About 3-5 hours** with a solve-time check on a big save.

Hours are real agent time, guessed from T-2193 (the ferry line kind, guessed at a day) and T-2196 (local fans).

**For you to decide**: the type table's numbers (mainly Small: fast at US$40 a boat-km, or a cheap slow 160-seat boat
for quiet routes instead), the fare defaults 1.50 / 1.00 / 1.00, local fans option A, whether inter-city trips get
fans (and at what share for longer trips, which have no purpose), and T-2216 step 2 if you want the Okinawa line's
islands to ride.

**Strings I'd propose** (for later review, when built): panel row "Boat", choices "Small", "Medium", "Large"; their
hover "160 riders, 65 km/h", "320 riders, 45 km/h", "1,280 riders, 45 km/h"; advanced fares rows "Small ferry",
"Ferry", "Large ferry".
