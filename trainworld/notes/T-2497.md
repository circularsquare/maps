# T-2497 Diagram water: fewer gaps and stubs, and a cleaner coast

Agent diagwater2, tree `.tw-trees/diagwater2`, 2026-11-05. Follows T-2169 (notes/T-2169.md). Water still draws only
with `?water`. The rule as built is in docs/ui-diagram.md "Water".

## What changed

- **Coast** (`waterSea.ts`): the sea grid is opened and closed by 4 pitches before fitting, and the fit is much
  freer: up to 12 pitches seaward, and landward up to the coast's clearance from the lines and pills less a pitch (1
  to 8; a chamfer from the casings and the land round the pills). Each ring keeps every pill on its own side
  (borders.ts' `keepsSides`, copied), so the old refit-at-0.3 is needed only where two rings fitted near each other
  cross; it now fires on none of the four test saves. The old `pillsIn` test took a pill on an island (inside a
  clockwise ring) for a wet one and refitted New York's whole outer ring at 0.3 pitches: that was the fine staircase
  on the Jersey Shore. Corner radius 3 -> 4.
- **Rivers** (`water.ts`): a section with no clean way, on a river at least 150 m each side of its middle, takes the
  way crossing the fewest other lines (40 pitches of length per pitch inside a casing; never a pill, never a hop the
  same river crosses elsewhere; at most 3 lines, else a gap). A crossing beside a gap now ends free with a short run
  on, not bare. Pieces touching end to end are taken together and a whole under 16 pitches is dropped, unless it is a
  wide river's and crosses a hop (those keep 5-pitch free ends, narrow ones 3).
- **Checks**: `t2169-water.mjs` accepts the declared crossings (`falseKeys` per run) and reports the coast's bends
  per 100 pitches, runs under 4 pitches, the rivers' pieces and bands; `t2169-shots.mjs` takes `[name, dx, dy]` and
  marks false crossings magenta in debug; all three t2169 checks take `WATER_OUT=<folder>/`.

## Why false crossings (and only for wide rivers)

`faces.mjs` (scratch, not kept) rasterised New York's drawing (casings and pills as walls, 0.5 pitch) and flooded
from both ends of each of the 28 sections the router found no way for: **27 of 28 have their ends in different
faces** (the 28th ended in a wall). So no router change could close them: the layout has put Manhattan stations on
both sides of New Jersey ones. The only ways to a continuous Hudson are crossing a line the river does not, or
changing the layout. T-2146's plan said "left out rather than drawn wrong"; the brief asked for continuous bands, so
wide rivers may now cross up to 3 lines a section. Narrow rivers keep the old rule: in Osaka the fallback put about
nine false crossings round Namba for canals, which read worse than dropping them.

## Found on the way

- **Japan's water masks miss the big rivers** (T-2510): along the hops that cross them the mask is dry at the Tama
  (二子玉川-二子新地, 多摩川-新丸子), the Sumida (浅草橋-両国) and the Yodo (新大阪-大阪, 中津-西中島南方, 十三-中津); the
  Arakawa at 赤羽-川口 is 175 m wet. So Tokyo's and Osaka's drawn rivers are mostly canals and small rivers, and the
  biggest ones are missing. Likely the OSM river-area multipolygons in `pipeline/osm_water.py`'s read; not looked into.
- **Jamaica Bay** stays land: tried counting islands under 3 km² as water for the sea's core test (coarse grid);
  the ground still read no sea between Howard Beach and Broad Channel, and it slowed the coarse pass by half, so it
  was dropped. The **Boso side of Tokyo Bay** was not tried. Both, and the sea's straight cut 40 pitches past the
  network's box, are T-2511.

## How published diagrams draw water (from memory, no sources fetched)

London's Tube map draws the Thames as one band of constant width at 0/45/90 degrees with rounded corners and very
few bends, ignoring every small creek; Vignelli's 1972 New York map and the current MTA diagram draw water as large
flat areas with octilinear edges; Tokyo Metro's map draws the bay and the big rivers (Sumida, Arakawa) only. What this
took from them: a coast with few long runs and generous rounding, and leaving small rivers out rather than ticking
every one under the lines.
