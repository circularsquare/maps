# T-3518 national tertiary figures, Venezuela and Nicaragua (agent tertiary, 2026-11-18)

Added to `pipeline/tier0.py` NATIONAL_TERTIARY; taken up at the next rebuild of VE and NI rows (no rebuild done).
The share is students over the World Bank's 2023 total population (SP.POP.TOTL), as the pipeline's own denominator.

## Nicaragua: 193,116 students, 2023 -> 0.0283 per resident
- CNU (Consejo Nacional de Universidades) statistical yearbook, 2023 enrolment 193,116 (9,519 teachers), as reported by
  Confidencial and La Prensa (2024-11-18). The yearbook itself was not fetched (La Prensa returns 403 to automated
  fetches; URL kept for Anita: https://www.laprensani.com/2024/11/18/nacionales/3401896-eduacion-universitaria-crisis-nicaragua).
- Series (La Prensa, from CNU yearbooks): 2017 241,113; 2018 194,876; 2023 193,116. Regime claims: 137,162 (2021, as
  quoted by Infobae), 180,220 (2024, CNU president), over 200,000 (2026, SECET). UNAN-Managua alone claims 239,242 for 2024.
  The CNU was dissolved and replaced by a Consejo Nacional de Rectores, so no yearbook after 2023.
- Picked the 2023 yearbook: the last count by the statistics office of the body itself, and the pre-crisis series is
  consistent with it. Range of plausible totals 180k-240k; the pick is mid-low.

## Venezuela: about 653,000 students, 2023 -> 0.0231 per resident (estimate)
- The government's last published count: 2,230,023 (2010 peak), 1,666,157 (2018), nothing since (Cotejo.info, citing the
  government's report of 2022-03-30). UNESCO's gross ratio was last measured in 2009 (78.27%), so UIS has no usable
  total either (El Nacional column on the 2026 UNESCO-IESALC report; page 403s).
- Independent: Observatorio de Universidades (OBU) says about 17% of ages 18-24 are in university; ENCOVI 2023 (UCAB)
  has 14% of men and 21% of women aged 18-24 still studying (any level, 10 states). Institution counts agree on a collapse
  (UCV 44,735 in 2016 -> 20,476 in 2021; UPEL 105,000 in 2012 -> under 45,000 in 2021; LUZ 65,000 -> 15,000).
- Ages 18-24 in 2023 from UN WPP via the World Bank (SP.POP.1519 and 2024, male plus female): 3/5 of 2,706,262 plus
  2,220,181 = 3.84M. 17% of that is about 653,000. (ENCOVI's mean 17.5% gives 673,000: same answer.)
- Range: 650,000 (independent) to 1,666,157 (government, 2018). Picked the independent figure. The government count
  includes Misión Sucre and other municipalised courses, is eight years old, and is the number the OBU says fell 60%;
  the game wants people attending a campus, which the OBU ratio is closer to. A 0.0231 share is under Nicaragua's 0.0283
  and Colombia-like countries' 0.04-0.05, which fits a country in collapse; the government figure (0.058) would put
  Venezuela above Colombia.
- Caveat: 17% is ages 18-24 only (older students missing, partly offset by part-time and non-university tertiary
  counted by nobody), so true tertiary may be 10-20% higher. Revisit if the OBU publishes a count.
