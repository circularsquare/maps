# T-1445: buses, a design

Design 2026-10-26, for Anita. Nothing built. Asked for: draw a line, stops place themselves (at train
stations it meets, otherwise every 400 m or so), maybe no stops at all; a cheap slow feeder.

Mockups: [the map](../../artifacts/trainworld/t1445/map.png) and
[the line panel](../../artifacts/trainworld/t1445/panel.png).

## The short version

- **The player draws a bus line like a rail line, but with no track.** Click points; the route
  between them is the drawn line in stage 1 and follows the roads in stage 2. No stops to place.
- **Stops place themselves**: one at every train station the route passes within 200 m, one at
  each end, and small street stops every 400 m between. The street stops are for looks and for the
  bus's speed; demand does not see each of them (below).
- **In demand a bus is just another line**, slow and frequent, over a few stops per 2 km zone, so
  the existing rules (walk, wait, ride, change, fare, crowding, the 30-minute queue) do the work.
  It feeds rail and carries short trips of its own with no new factor. Two small changes to how
  cells reach stops, and no bus dislike or liking, as rail has none.
- **Cheap**: US$1M a km of route, US$0.6M a bus, US$3 a bus-km to run. A 10 km line with 10 buses
  is about $16M, against $4B for a first metro line.

## Drawing

**What road data there is.** The packs hold no road shapes: `routes.py` keeps only zone-to-zone
detour factors and deletes the OSM extracts after reading them (8 of about 270 cached). T-279's
GRIP4 rasters are 9 km cells of road density, no use for snapping. The one worldwide road layer the
game already has is the **basemap**: OpenFreeMap's OpenMapTiles tiles carry every OSM road in their
`transportation` layer at zoom 14 (motorway down to minor and service), and `stationNames.ts`
already reads that layer's tiles for street names (T-724). So snapping costs no data build and no
download beyond tiles the map fetches anyway.

**Stage 1, free drawn.** The player clicks points as for track; the route is straight between them.
Players will click at corners, so most routes follow streets anyway. Its road length is the drawn
length times 1.2 (a diagonal across a grid really runs 1.27-1.41 times, a route clicked along the
streets 1.0). That length sets the run time, the price and the running cost.

**Stage 2, along roads.** Between two clicks, a shortest path over the basemap's roads: fetch the
zoom 14 tiles under the two points' box (a 10 km line is about 20 tiles of 50 KB), join the road
pieces where tiles cut them, search primary to minor roads (motorways and service ways left out,
bigger roads a little cheaper), and fall back to the straight segment where the path is over 3
times the straight line or the tiles hold no road. No one-way or turn rules; a bus can use any
public road. The road length then counts at 1.0. A day or two of work, all in the app.

## Stops

**Where they go.** At each train station the route passes within 200 m: the bus stops there and
changes to the train cost the ordinary change (2 minutes plus the walk, here a fixed 1 minute from
the bus to the platforms). At both ends. Then street stops every 400 m between, never within 200 m
of another stop. Where two bus lines cross they share a street stop at the crossing, so people can
change between them. The player never places or moves a stop; moving the route moves them.

**Why demand should not see every street stop.** Demand prices homes in groups that share their
nearest station (the access subzones), and searches once towards every station. A stop every 400 m
would split the subzones along every bus route and add 25 stations for a 10 km line; a city with 30
bus lines would double or triple the solve. It would also crowd rail stations out of each cell's
6 nearest stations.

**What demand sees instead: the corridor.** Anita's "maybe no concept of stops" fits the demand
model best, at its own 2 km grain:
- **One demand stop per 2 km zone the route crosses** (at the street stop nearest the middle of
  the route's stretch in that zone, or at the crossing with another bus line), plus every train
  station met and both ends. A 10 km route has about 7.
- **Anyone within 400 m of the route** in that zone can use it. Their walk is their straight
  distance to the route plus 100 m (the mean walk along it to the nearest 400 m stop), at the
  ordinary walk rule: 13 minutes a km, each minute 1.5. So a home 200 m from the route walks
  about 4 minutes, 6 perceived.
- **Cells keep their 6 nearest train stations and add the stop of up to 2 bus lines**, so a bus
  never hides a station. The subzone key gains "which bus stop, if any", which adds a subzone or two
  per zone a bus crosses, not one per street stop.

The cost of this: a trip that starts and ends in the same zone gets no bus (it is under 2 km and
mostly walked anyway), and a home at the far edge of a zone rides as if from the zone's stop (up to
about 3 minutes off). Both at the grain the rest of demand already works at.

## In demand

The bus is a line in the same network, so nothing new decides who rides it:
- **Feeder.** A home 1.5 km from a station walks 29 perceived minutes to it. By bus, 200 m off the
  route and 8 minutes between buses: walk 6, wait 4, ride 5, change 3, about 18. At 3 km it is 59
  against about 23, and the drive to the station (32-43 in a suburb) loses too. So the suburbs'
  walk problem (demand-plain, "Why rail is hard to win in the suburbs") gets a player answer.
- **Short trips of its own.** A 4 km trip along a suburban route comes to about 37 perceived by bus
  against 23 by car: the bus gets about 1 in 5 of those not walking. In a dense place, where the
  car pays to park, it gets most. A bus never beats a train on the same trip; it is slower.
- **Speed**: a flat 18 km/h door to door, street stops included (London's buses average about
  15, suburban ones 20-25). The run is the road length at that speed plus the dwell at train
  stations. No accelerating, no traffic, no waiting for other buses.
- **Fare**: the network's fare curve, paid once a trip on its whole length, bus kilometres
  included, as for a change between trains. So a bus to the train is a free change.
- **Frequency** like trains: buses per demand level, or a headway (1 minute to 2 hours).
- **Capacity**: 80 a bus at crush; the crowding rule (from 70% full) and the 30-minute queue apply
  as they are.
- **The layers**: commutes, everyday trips and the regional layer's access all find the bus
  through the same stations. Walking still also stands for short bus rides, and the car for longer
  ones, as now; no change to either unless buses turn out to carry too many short trips.

## What the player sees and pays

- **On the map** ([mockup](../../artifacts/trainworld/t1445/map.png)): a bus line is half the
  width of a rail line, in its colour on a white casing; street stops are small rings from zoom 14;
  ends are small rounded squares; at a train station it meets the station's own circle. Its badge
  is a rounded square, a rail line's a circle. Hovering a line shades its 400 m corridor. Buses
  are drawn as short single cars.
- **The line panel** ([mockup](../../artifacts/trainworld/t1445/panel.png)): the rail panel, cut
  down. Schedule (buses or headway, per demand level), average speed, the stops list naming only
  the ends and the stations met with street stops folded into a row, and the past 24 hours with a
  new row: riders changing to trains. No train kind, train length or stop editor. "Edit route"
  takes the place of "Edit stops".
- **Prices**: US$1M a km of route (stops, shelters, signs), paid on Construct like track; US$0.6M
  a bus, its own fleet kind with spares shared between bus lines; running US$3 a bus-km (a 4-car
  train is US$12 a train-km for 8 times the room, so a bus costs more per place, as in life, and
  much less per line); upkeep US$3k a route-km a day. Removing a route refunds nothing, as track.
- **New line**: "New line" gets a choice of train or bus.

## Size and stages

- **B1, the smallest useful thing (about 3 tasks, a few days in all):** (a) the sim: a bus line
  kind over a drawn route with no track (run times from length, the stops placed, departures, the
  boarding account and money as trains; no track capacity); (b) demand: the corridor stops and
  access above, and a measured cost on the real save with 30 bus lines, where demand time should
  rise by a third at most; (c) the app: drawing, the map style, the cut-down panel, the price.
  Free drawn at 1.2. Most of the risk is in (a): the line code assumes track edges, so the agent
  decides early between a "road" edge kind trains cannot use and a separate path type.
- **B2: along roads**, the basemap snapping above. Built 2026-10-26 (T-1465, [notes](T-1465.md)): the app
  fetches and searches, the sim takes the points with a corner bit and a road bit each (so not app only).
- **B3, if wanted after playing:** speed by place (from the car's street speeds, slower in dense
  centres), a bus lane upgrade bought per km that raises it, and ferries (T-280) on the same line
  kind with piers for stops.

## Questions for Anita

1. Free drawn in stage 1 (click at corners, length times 1.2) and along roads in stage 2? My pick:
   yes. Snapping is a separate chunk of work and stage 1 plays fine without it.
2. Fare: one fare a trip on the network's curve, bus km included, so bus to train is a free change,
   or a flat bus fare of its own? My pick: the one curve. Nothing new to set, and the feeder works.
3. Do trips by bus alone count in the train share (top bar, stars) and in points? My pick: yes, as
   the player's network; the top bar's hover says trains and buses.
4. Speed: a flat 18 km/h everywhere, or by place (about 13 in dense centres to 25 in suburbs)? My
   pick: flat in B1, by place in B3 only if suburban buses feel too slow.
