### What was built

- **Head start**: `walk_bonus_min` 0 (one constant, as before; `asc_rail` stays 0).
- **2 + 2**: `train_in_min` 2 where a trip's first boarding is a train, `train_out_min` 2 where its
  last ride is one; never at a change (which keeps its fixed 2). Buses (`Network::bus_line`), trams
  and ferries (`Network::line_level`, a new per-line flag the app sends every API as
  `set_level_lines`; empty = every non-bus line is a train) pay neither. A trip from a bus or tram
  onto a train pays the way out only, from a train onto a bus the way in only. Added to each station
  pair's cost after the search (`search::trip_ends`, expected over the departures the strategy
  accepts by frequency, as the fare curve's km are), so it counts for mode choice, the walk tier,
  the regional market and the explorer; route choice between a station's lines does not see it (the
  fare curve's lie) and the walking rule's real minutes don't include it. The trip explorer's legs
  carry it (`Leg::ends`, LEG_LEN 16) and a new cost term "stairs" (`trip.stairs`).
- **A**: `walk_tier` 0.1 of a trip whose walk costs more than 800 straight metres' (`walk_tier_m`;
  by the walk cost, so round water counts) never walk it: rail = (1 - t) x the usual three-way
  choice on the pairs the rule keeps + t x rail against the car alone on every pair
  (`Params::tier_choice`); no-rail walking is (1 - t) x (`walk_no_rail`). Folded into the commute
  lanes (only lanes the rule touches need the every-pair sum, which they already had), the everyday
  pass, both explorer choices; the regional outings only once their packs are rebuilt (below). A trip
  the rule refuses whole now carries the tier's riders on every pair.

## 1. How walking works now

Every local trip (commutes, everyday trips, the regional layer's commutes and outings, the trip
explorer) chooses its mode in two steps (docs/demand-mode.md "Cost and the rail-against-car
curve", "Walking and land parts"; docs/demand-plain.md "Walking the whole way"):

1. **Walking's share** comes from one logit among rail (the player's network, buses included), the
   car and walking. Scale `beta` 0.10 a perceived minute, damped past 60 car-minutes
   (`Params::mode_beta`).
2. **Rail and the car split the rest** on their cost ratio, rail's share 1 / (1 + q^3), with a
   fade from 3 to 4 times (`Params::ratio_share`). Walking does not enter this.

The deciding numbers:

| What | Value | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Walking the whole way, real minutes | 13 a straight km (6 km/h on a path 1.3x the line) | `walk_mps`, `walk_detour` |
| Weight of each walk minute (whole way) | 1.5, one rate at every length | `walk_trip_w` |
| Walking's head start | 10 perceived minutes, `asc_walk` = 1.0 in the logit | `walk_bonus_min` |
| Longest walk offered | 10 km straight | `WALK_REACH_KM` |
| Walk to a station or stop | same speed and path, weight 1.5 (a knee at 10 minutes exists, both sides 1.5) | `w_walk`, `walk_easy_min`, `w_walk_far` |
| Bus stop reach | homes within 300 m of a route, walk = distance to the route + 100 m | `BUS_REACH_M`, `BUS_WALK_EXTRA_M` (kernel/access.rs) |
| Bus boarding | 4 perceived minutes (T-3856 removes it) | docs/demand-worldwide.md "Bus corridors" |
| A fare dollar | 4.4 perceived minutes in the US, so the $2.50 starting base is 11 minutes | `fare_w` |
| No train slower than walking | a station pair whose real minutes (walks, waits, rides) exceed walking's is no option, within 10 km | `walk_rule` (T-1059, T-2265, T-2307) |

Code: `Params::car_choice`, `walk_no_rail`, `ratio_share`, `asc_walk` in sim/src/demand/kernel.rs;
the commute lanes in kernel/mode.rs (the `share` closure near line 819); everyday.rs near line
1568; the explorer in trip.rs near line 208; commutes' walking between sampled cells (T-587) in
kernel/gravity.rs near line 429, which turns the cell pairs' mean walking share back into one walk
cost; the regional layer in intercity/pass.rs near line 636.

**Three things that hold the bus back on short trips**, from the formulas (section 5 has the
numbers):

- **The walking rule.** A bus trip's real minutes include both walks to the stops and half the
  headway (`Skims::walk` holds the expected wait, rides and walks between, search.rs; the access
  walks are real minutes, access.rs). For a home 150 m from the route (250 m of walking each end), stops every 400 m at
  about 19 km/h, it is 6.5 + half the headway + 4.1 a km against walking's 13 a km. So the bus is
  refused for everyone under **1.3 km at a bus every 10 minutes, 1.0 km every 5 minutes**. No walk
  tier changes this unless the tier is let off the rule.
- **The head start counts against the network too.** In the three-way logit the car's utility is
  0, rail's is beta x (car - rail) and walking's is beta x (car - walk) + 1.0, so walking against
  rail is beta x (rail - walk) + 1.0: the 10 minutes apply to the bus and the train as well. The
  docs give the head start's reason as what a short drive costs beyond its road time (getting to
  the car, the walk from the space), which has no counterpart on the network side, whose walks
  are already counted. In code this is `asc_rail` (0); it moves only walking's share, never rail
  against the car (docs/demand-mode.md says so).
- **Everyone walks alike**, which is her question: one weight and one head start for all, so on a
  short trip where walking is a little cheaper than the bus, most people walk.

## 2. Real figures (sources: research/walk-tiers.md)

- **Can't walk far**: 6.7% of Americans have serious difficulty walking or climbing stairs (ACS,
  via USAFacts), 28.3% over 75. In England, walking 100 yards is hard for 8% at 50-64, 14% at 65-79,
  35% at 80+ (ELSA 2014-15); a mobility impairment for 13%, 21% and 39% (Understanding Society
  2015-17). WHO: 16% of the world has some significant disability (all kinds).
- **Fewer trips**: disabled adults in England make 21% fewer trips (31% fewer at 60+; NTS 2025).
  So people who can't walk far make **about 5% of trips** (more in Japan or Italy, less in Nigeria;
  people 65+ are 10.2% of the world, 29% of Japan, 3% of Nigeria, World Bank from UN WPP).
- **How their trips differ** (NTS 2021, a year, with / without a mobility difficulty): walking 131
  / 243, bus 28 / 27, car passenger 119 / 97, driving 177 / 389. They walk half as much and ride
  the bus as much; much of what they don't walk goes to lifts.
- **How far**: in the US, people 65+ walk on fewer days (11.8% against 16.5%), but their walks are
  nearly as long (mean 0.56 against 0.61 miles; Yang and Diez-Roux 2012). The median walk of
  anyone is 800 m. Without a rest: 50-100 m for people with a stick or without an aid, 150 m in a
  wheelchair (Inclusive Mobility 2005). People 65+ walk 0.8-0.9 m/s, against the game's 1.67 m/s.
- **Walk weights by group**: the big meta-analyses put a walk minute at 1.5-2 minutes aboard and
  do not split by age or disability; I found no published weight by group. One study of older
  Americans: 5 more minutes of walk to transit cuts their riding about 5% (Hess 2009).
- **Other games and models**: the subway-building game has one walk weight and spreads riders by income only;
  the newer city-builder compares walking by time, comfort and money with no age rule found; strategic
  models split by car availability and income, not by walking ability; TfL's PTAL uses one walker
  (640 m to a bus, 960 m to rail).

What this says for the design: the real effect is a small group (about 5% of trips) that walks
much less, and what they don't walk goes to lifts and the bus, not a group that walks a bit less.
A share that stops walking past a length fits that better than a heavier walk weight.

## 3. Options

All four were priced in the hand model on two places (section 5): **dense** (every driver pays a
space and home parking, streets at 16.5 km/h) and **suburb** (parks free, 30 km/h). A bus every 10
minutes, T-3856's rules.

### A. Some people stop walking past 800 m (the cheap tier; my pick)

- **Rule**: 1 in 10 of every trip's people walk the whole way only up to 800 m straight; past it
  they choose between the network and the car alone, and the walking rule doesn't refuse their
  station pairs. Their walks to stations and stops are everyone's.
- **Share and figures**: 10% (about 5% can't, from the sources; the rest stands for people who
  won't). 800 m is a pick, not a fitted number: it is the median walk (NHTS), and it sits under the
  bus's 1.0-1.3 km threshold so the rule exemption matters. The sources say older people walk less
  often rather than shorter, so no survey pins the length. 500 m is the alternative, nearer the
  rest distances; it differs only between 500 and 800 m.
- **Solve cost: none in the choice, a little for the rule.** Walking's share past 800 m is times
  0.9 and the rest is as now: for both kinds of driver rail's share is (1 - 0.9w) x the ratio,
  `walk_no_rail` is times 0.9, and the commutes' cell pairs take the factor before their mean is
  turned into one walk cost. No second pass over station pairs. Letting the tier off the walking
  rule needs the station-pair sum from before the rule takes refused pairs off (one more number
  per lane, priced into the tier's rail cost) and the tier's share loaded on refused pairs too: the
  rule costs 3-8% of a solve today (T-2307, T-2324), this a part of that, under 3% by my guess.
  Without that part the tier gets no bus under 1.3 km; they would drive.
- **A player would see**: more bus riders on 1-3 km trips, the first ones near 1 km; walking a
  little lower there. Nothing new to click. The trip explorer's Walk hover could say who doesn't
  walk (a string for later).
- **Hand model** (bus % of all trips, dense): 1.0 km 0 to 7.2; 1.5 km 17.4 to 22.8; 2 km 28.1 to
  32.5; 3 km 52.1 to 54.1; 5 km unchanged. Suburb: +1.5 points at 1 km, under 1 point beyond (the
  tier mostly drives where parking is free). At 20% instead: dense 1.5 km 28.3, 2 km 36.9.
- **Ceiling tests**: both are rail of rail + car, so the tier's would-be walkers past 800 m join
  that pool at the test train's share, 69-84% at 0.5-2 km in a suburb, a little under Houston's
  86%. They are about 0.5% of trips (10% x walking's share x the 1-3 km trips): **under 0.1 point**
  by this estimate, test A; test B's train is slower and dearer, so its short-trip share is lower
  and the move likely a little larger, still small. Not run: the `checks/ceiling` run on its five
  cities is the check. Trips under 460 m are not touched (the tier walks them).

- **A player would see**: a bus with stops at the door winning that tier from a station 800 m

- **Hand model**: dense 1.5 km 17.4 to 19.7, 2 km 28.1 to 30.5: less than A, because the tier's

### C. A stretched curve for everyone

- **Rule**: a walk the whole way counts 1.5 a minute for 10 minutes and 2.5 a minute after (T-558

- **Share and figures**: everyone. T-558 found the flat rate puts 20% of 3-4 km everyday trips on

- **Solve cost**: none (a knee in `walk_trip_cost`, as the walk to stations already has).
- **A player would see**: much less walking at 1-3 km everywhere (dense 2 km 61% to 24%, suburb

### Folding a tier into what exists

- **Income groups**: the three groups exist only on the regional market's longer trips (T-1431);
  local trips have one group (T-3246 is still open), so there is nothing to fold a walk tier into.
  If local income groups are built later with their own station-pair pass, a walk tier inside them

## Fix (walkfix)

**Cause**: the demand worker never solves on `packService`'s network as sent. Each population gets a
cluster's sub-network or a city's reach of it (`demandClusters.ts` `subNetwork`, `reachView`), each a
new `DemandNetwork` listing its fields by name, and neither listed `pierLines` or `gradeStops`. So
`set_stairs` always got two empty arrays, `pier_lines` gave none, and every non-bus stop paid, ferries
included. Calib never goes through the clusters, so its figures were right.

**Fix** (`app/src/workers/demandClusters.ts`): `stairsOf` slices both by the view's own lines and stops
(the same whole-network stop list `catchesOf` takes, so a reach view's trimmed spans keep their own
flags), each only where any flag is set. Both go into the cluster's `sig`, `shape` and period
signatures, so a level change alone solves again instead of keeping a held result. The cached-result
key (`demandCache.ts` `canonical`) now has a station's level where it is not 0, for the same reason.
No Rust change.
