## The arithmetic

With two solving workers (3 cores), worker 0 holds periods 0, 3 and 4 and worker 1 periods 1 and 2
(`periodsOfWorker`). A high edit re-solves periods 0, 1 and 2 (T-124 keeps 3 and 4), so worker 1 holds two of them and
the edit waits on it. Each period is four passes (free flow and three crowding rounds) and a capacity fit; no high
period on this save settles early (T-434). Everything else in the wait is about 2 s.

- **The slow worker runs 8 passes.** At T-3512's 4-6 s a pass that is 32-48 s; 20 s needs 2.4 s a pass.
- **A perfect split still leaves 6 passes a worker** (three periods' 12 over two workers): 24-36 s at 4-6 s a pass;
  20 s needs 3.3 s a pass.
- **With three workers** (a desktop with 12 or more threads) a high edit is one period a worker: 16-24 s at 4-6 s a
  pass, so those machines may already be near 20 s (not measured cleanly here: `c4w3-1` below ran on a full machine).

So on two workers 20 s needs passes 1.5-2.5x faster than T-3512 measured, with the work dealt perfectly.

## What changes convergence

1. **Two crowding rounds instead of three** (T-2830's B2, measured there): 3 passes a period, about a quarter off
   every solve, load and edit. T-2830 found New York lines moving by up to 14% (13 of 92 lines over 10,000 riders past
   5%). A high edit on two workers at 4-6 s a pass: 24-36 s. Alone it does not reach 20 s, and Anita has already
   declined it (2026-11-09: she likes three rounds; T-2879 shows two as an early result, then three).
2. **Not measured: an edited period's crowding starting from the last solve's loads** (a warm start), one or two
   rounds. Each population already keeps its last whole solve's loads per round (T-1733's `trail`), but
   `crowd_with_external` needs a free-flow `solve` first and its averaging (`msa`) counts that pass in, so it needs a
   new Rust entry point. Fewer passes, so it could reach 20 s, but nothing about its time or drift is measured. The
   figures would then depend on the history of edits (a reload gives other numbers than the edit showed; today a
   network's figures are the same to the bit however it was reached, T-392). The nearest figure: crowding the
   touched populations on their last final loads sent all of Japan's lines 6-16% astray (DECISIONS 2026-10-28,
   T-1733). A prototype behind a dev flag, measured against the cold solve on NYC and NA, is about 2-3 h.

Meanwhile T-2624 stage 2 already moves the edited lines at the first period's final (about 34 s on 2 workers).
