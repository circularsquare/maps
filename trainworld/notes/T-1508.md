# T-1508: the market's totals on very big far pairs (2026-10-27)

Analysis only, no game code changed. Proposal waits on Anita.

From T-1504: Beijing - Shanghai has about 21,000 longer trips a weekday both ways by every mode;
published figures put rail and air alone well above that. The brief: calibrate against national rail
totals, every country.

## What was measured

`notes/T-1508/t1508_test.rs` (a test added to a copy of master's crate, d746b717) opens the market
from the world's rows alone and writes, for every ordered pair of places 100 km or more apart, the
trips a weekday from the ground curve and from the tail apart. `t1508_bands.py` adds them up by
country and band, `t1508_more.py` lists the biggest far pairs and a place's departures by band,
`t1508_scen.py` recomputes the same from the rows under changed curves (it reproduces the test's
pairs: Beijing - Shanghai 21.3k). Year = weekday x 425 (the longer trips' weekend factor 1.58).
To rerun: `notes/T-1546/t1546_copy.py <folder>`, add `t1508_test.rs` as a `#[cfg(test)]` module at the
end of the copy's `intercity.rs` as that script adds its own, build with `cargo test --release
--features demand --no-run`, run the binary with `t1508_dump --ignored` and `T1521_ROOT`, `T1508_OUT`
set (9 seconds, rows only, no packs).

## The causes

1. **The ground curve runs on past 300 km and double counts with the tail.** The ground's weight
   is plain population times `max(d, 25)^-2.37`, with no end; the tail starts at 300-700 km on top.
   Of China's trips of 300 km and more, 5.5bn of 7.2bn a year are the ground's; in the US 2.3bn of
   2.7bn. Because the ground weighs every town by its people alone, those trips go to every town
   within reach, not to the big cities: Beijing's residents make 226k trips a weekday of 300 km and
   more, close to what Beijing's airports and long-distance stations handle (about 260k departures a
   day, my estimate from published throughput), but only 4.4% of them go to Shanghai, where the
   published figures say 12-30%. This is a plain error: T-376 fitted the tail on top of a ground
   curve that already filled the 300-1,000 km bands 2-2.7x (its notes say so) and left it.
2. **The tail was fitted to flights where rail carries the trips.** T-376's targets over 1,000 km
   were 0.6 x flight segments, treated as all modes. On China's trunk pairs (Shanghai - Beijing's
   target 14,858 a day) that misses the 80-90% that go by rail, and the fit read those pairs as
   "1.5-2.2x high". The tail's onset (300-700 km) was set the same way ("NHTS Northeast air trips are
   near 0 under 500 km"), though the tail is all modes. Stale inputs, not a missing factor.
3. **The 300-700 km valley.** Between 300 and 700 km the ground has decayed and the tail is only
   partly on (half at 500 km). Berlin - Munich (499 km) and Seoul - Jeju (458) sit in it. With the
   ground spread over small towns, the big pairs there get little from either curve.
4. Not a cause: the market's sampling (T-1504), or a place's own departures (Beijing's are about
   right).

Seoul - Jeju is also a resort island (15M visitors a year); the rule has no pull for touristy places
(T-1374's idea for superholidays), so part of that gap is that.

## What the changes do (`t1508_scen.py`)

Domestic pairs from the rows; the tail's normaliser rescaled by its domestic weight (abroad held),
the ground's kept (its trips past the fade dropped); then one world factor on `Rt` so the US's trips
of 300 km and more match NHTS. Europe's figures are the roughest (its trips abroad are not rescaled).

| Change | Rt | US 300+, 700+, 1500+ against NHTS (a person a day) | China 300+ a weekday | Japan 300+ | Beijing - Shanghai | Berlin - Munich | Seoul - Busan | Tokyo - Osaka | NY - Chicago | NY - LA |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| as built | 1.00 | 0.0107 / 0.0056 / 0.0032 (0.0052 / 0.0025 / 0.0021) | 0.0120 | 0.0066 | 21.3k | 4.4k | 35.1k | 160.8k | 29.1k | 31.4k |
| ground fades over the tail's 300-700 km | 0.99 | 0.0069 / 0.0012 / 0.0018 | 0.0065 | 0.0046 | 19.5k | 2.9k | 33.9k | 145.5k | 25.9k | 31.0k |
| both over 150-400 km | 2.73 | 0.0030 / 0.0027 / 0.0041 | 0.0078 | 0.0059 | 45.5k | 2.7k | 17.1k | 346.7k | 59.4k | 70.8k |
| both over 100-300 km | 3.17 | 0.0024 / 0.0030 / 0.0045 | 0.0081 | 0.0059 | 50.0k | 2.6k | 6.5k | 355.8k | 63.8k | 75.6k |

What it says:
- **Fading the ground over the tail's own window fixes the national totals with no refit**: the US's
  300 km and more comes to NHTS's total (0.0099 against 0.0098, Rt x 0.99), China's halves (to about
  1.5x the rough published), Japan's falls to 0.6x MLIT. It does nothing for the big pairs.
- **Moving the window lower and refitting Rt lifts China's big pairs to the published range**
  (Beijing - Shanghai 45-50k) but overshoots everything the tail already carries well: the US's long
  bands 1.5-2x, New York - Chicago and Los Angeles 6-7x, Tokyo - Osaka 2x; and Europe's and Korea's
  300-700 km pairs fall further.
- **No reshaping of the two curves with one world rate fixes China, Germany and Korea's big pairs
  while keeping the US and Japan.** The US's big far pairs are already 2-3x high under the same rule.
  What China and Germany need is more of a city's long trips going to the biggest cities: a
  stronger pull by destination size in the tail (its exponent `TH`, today 0.75 on the r5 cell's
  density), which is in the fit already but cannot be tried from the rows (it needs the world field).

## Options, ranked by fix against complication

1. **Fade the ground over the tail's onset (300-700 km), no other change.** Fixes a plain double
   count; national totals right in the US, nearer in China; no new constant (it reuses the tail's
   window). Moves every country's 300-700 km pairs down 10-35% (Berlin - Munich 4.4k to 2.9k, Madrid -
   Barcelona about 17k to 12k, the Northeast's pairs unchanged since they are under 350 km). Does not
   fix the big pairs. Small code change in `Rule::ground` and the pipeline's normaliser (unchanged by
   choice: the trips past the fade are dropped, so near trips stay as they are).
2. **Option 1, then refit the tail's existing constants (Rt, G, TH, THo, the onset) against
   corrected targets**: NHTS bands, MLIT, China's rail plus air over 300 km, and the big pairs with
   rail counted (Beijing - Shanghai, Berlin - Munich, Tokyo - Osaka, Seoul - Busan, the Northeast);
   flights pairs kept only where rail is small. Same form, no new constant per country;
   `pipeline/longhaul_fit.py`, about 6 minutes on 3 cores, then the rows and every market pack's
   normalisers rebuilt (a data rebuild for every country). Whether one rule then fits both the US's
   and China's big pairs is unknown: T-376 found the fit poorly identified. Expected to lift the big
   pairs where destination size matters most.
3. **A factor per country on the tail** (China x3, Korea x2...). Fits by construction; a constant per
   country, which the brief and Anita's rule count as a complication. Not recommended.
4. **Leave it.** It caps rail on the biggest pairs (Beijing - Shanghai cannot carry more than 21k a
   day both ways however good the line), not its share. A player building China's high-speed spine
   would see too few riders on it.

**Recommendation: 1 then 2, as one task (T-1599)**, measured on these tables and T-1546's corridor
cases before the data rebuild. Option 1 alone is safe to land first if 2 slips.
