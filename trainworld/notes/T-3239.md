# T-3239: high-speed trains losing to the slower parallel line

Agent hsr, 2026-11-11. Player report via Anita: with HSR beside a normal line (Omiya - Tokyo), people
take the normal line, and only a 1.0x high-speed multiplier makes them prefer HSR.

## Verdict

1. **A bug, fixed**: the regional market's route search weighed every fare km 11.6 times too heavy in
   a US-start game (7.3 in a Japan start). Since T-1906 (2026-11-02) a game's curve is in its start
   country's dollars, but the market kept India's weight for a dollar of India's units (`curveWeight`,
   50.8 perceived minutes). Mode choice was corrected by `fareFactors` (every country 1/11.6), route
   choice was not. With every line at 1x, all lines charge the same per km and nothing shows; at any
   multiplier above 1 the high-speed line's extra fare looked like 5 perceived minutes a km to the
   market's riders (longer trips and commutes between zones), so they never took it, however much
   faster. Tokyo - Koriyama, HSR 110 minutes faster: 0% at 1.5x before, 100% after.
   Fix: `marketFareWeight(scales)` = `curveWeight()` / the start key's scale (`app/src/game/income.ts`),
   set on the market in the demand worker; `fareFactors` now over that, so the market's mode choice is
   unchanged to float rounding. A save from before T-1906 (no start key) is unchanged. The everyday
   layer and the city commutes never had it: they are set at their country's weight and scale.
2. **Working as designed but harsh: the 1.5x default loses at every distance against a 160 km/h line.**
   The default curve's per-km part weighs about 0.88 perceived minutes a km everywhere (US $0.20 a km at
   4.40 min a dollar; Japan $0.1275 at 6.96). 1.5x adds 0.44 min a km. A 320 km/h train saves at most
   0.19 min a km over a 160 km/h one (0.31 over 120, 0.56 over 80). So for riders weighing money as a
   local trip does, the break-even multiplier is 1.21x against a 160 km/h ordinary line with the same
   stops, 1.36x against 120, 1.64x against 80, at any distance. That is the player's "1.0x".
3. **Riders take the first useful train.** At a shared station the strategy search (docs/demand-kernel.md
   step 4) has three regimes: the HSR alone when it beats the ordinary line by more than half the
   HSR's headway; both, split by trains an hour, when they are closer than that; the ordinary line
   alone when it is cheaper by more than half its own headway. With HSR at 4 an hour and the ordinary
   line at 8, the middle regime gives the HSR a third. There is no taste spread beyond that, so the
   multiplier works as a cliff: a few minutes either way flips a corridor whole.

Nothing else changed since 10-25 that bears on this (git log over the fare, search, income and market
setup code): T-1430 (planned first wait, helps HSR), T-1521 and T-1431 (0.4 and income groups, mode
choice only), T-1801 (transfer fare, 0 by default), T-2670 (income^0.5, weights move with scales so a
default fare weighs the same). The multiplier is charged once, on the high-speed line's km only, on the
part above the base, as T-1626 says: the Omiya break-even falls where its arithmetic puts it.

## Real shares

No published split by origin and destination between the Shinkansen and the conventional lines turned
up; MLIT's Inter-Regional Travel Survey counts rail as one mode, and JR East's section counts page is
refused to automated fetches (`jreast.co.jp/rosen_avr/`, 403). Section counts on Japanese Wikipedia
(from JR East): the Takasaki line Kumagaya - Takasaki carried 47,033 a day in FY2019, against about
97,000-105,000 on the Joetsu Shinkansen Omiya - Takasaki (much of which goes on past Takasaki). For
scale: Tokyo - Utsunomiya (109.5 km) is about 1 h 40 by the conventional rapid and 50 minutes by
Shinkansen for a non-reserved surcharge of about ¥2,700-2,900 on a ¥1,980 fare, so riders pay about
2.4x the fare to save about 50 minutes and many still do (employer-paid commuter passes and business
trips). Tokyo - Omiya by Shinkansen saves about 10 minutes for about ¥1,000 more, and most riders take
the conventional lines, which the game reproduces at 1.5x. The game's ordinary trains at 160 km/h are
about twice a real conventional line's average, which is the larger reason HSR's edge is small here.

## Proposal (for Anita, not built)

- **A. Longer trips choose their route at their own money weight.** The market's route search weighs
  fares at 0.4 of the local weight (the trip-weighted mean of a country's three groups is close to
  0.4). Longer trips then take the HSR at 1.5x against any ordinary line averaging about 150 km/h or less over a 200 km trip, and
  split by trains an hour against a 160 km/h one. The lie: commutes and outings between zones share
  the market's search, so they would choose routes at 0.4 too (their mode choice stays local). Two
  searches would remove the lie and roughly double the market's search time; not measured.
- **B. A lower default multiplier, 1.2x or 1.25x**, the local break-even against a 160 km/h line. Fare
  income on high-speed lines falls by most of the surcharge.
- **C. Leave it**: commuters ride the ordinary line, long trips the HSR once it is clearly faster
  (with the fix, against an ordinary line averaging under about 90 km/h at 1.5x, as a real conventional line does). Closest to Japan, but a
  player who builds both at full speed sees the HSR empty.

My pick: A, keeping 1.5x. It makes the long trips weigh money the same way in both choices, which is
what T-1521 meant, and keeps commuters on the cheaper line, as in Omiya.
