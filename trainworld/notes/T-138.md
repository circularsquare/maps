# T-138 — First non-work local demand proposal

2026-10-11, Codex. Research/design only; no runtime or pack changes.

Anita reopened T-066, suggesting NYC first or a general rule where inputs permit, with
leisure/shopping/errands, school, airport and hospital travel, and separate daily timing.
She explicitly does not want to force an approximately 60% commute share while coverage is
incomplete. This proposal bounds an implementation; trial constants below are not fitted data.

## Evidence and denominators

- [NYC DOT 2024 CMS results](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/2024-cms-report.pdf),
  report pp. 9–11: work-related purpose 20%, social/recreation 21%, shopping 17%, meals 14%,
  errands/other 9%. Commuting transit mode share is 52.9%; these are different denominators.
  The published purpose table alone does not establish our two-leg home/work commute share.
- [CMS user guide](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/2024-cms-user-guide.pdf),
  pp. 18–21: the sample excludes children's trips, nonresident travel and some group-quarter
  residents. The trip weights represent a typical Monday–Sunday day; weekday analysis requires
  the appropriate filter. Purpose is recorded at each trip end. For our comparison, classify
  both outward and homeward commute legs consistently, and retain mixed work/non-work chains
  as a distinct class. Do not interpret the report's 1% school share as all-age school demand.
- [2010/11 NYMTC/NJTPA RHTS](https://njtpa.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/RHTS_FinalReport-10-6-2014.pdf),
  tables 4-13–15: NYC median straight-line distances are 3.2 miles for work, 0.9 for social/
  recreation, 0.5 shopping, 0.7 other. Non-work has a midday hump. Overall study-area weekday
  trip rates are about 1 work and 3.1 non-work legs/person. Older, broader geography, with school
  inside non-work: useful scale/shape checks, not a current NYC target or category-specific fit.
- [MTA customer survey](https://www.mta.info/document/67846), pp. 8, 20, reports work as the
  most frequent trip purpose for 62% of lapsed vs 42% of current customers. This is another
  example of a percentage that is not a trip-weighted share of all NYC journeys; it is not
  identified as the source of Anita's figure.
- [LODES 8 technical documentation](https://lehd.ces.census.gov/data/lodes/LODES8/LODESTechDoc8.0.pdf),
  p. 6: CNS07 retail, CNS17 arts/entertainment/recreation, CNS18 accommodation/food, CNS19 other
  services. These count employees, not customers. CNS15 education and CNS16 healthcare/social
  assistance are too broad to identify schools or hospitals faithfully by themselves.

## Proposed first slice

Enable only in the NYC pack initially (the game's 25-county metro, not the five boroughs alone).
Use a general purpose-layer schema and the existing US inputs so enabling other metros later
does not require a new model. Avoid a flat multiplier on commute trips: residents who do not
commute also shop and visit people, and customer destinations differ from office destinations.

Two categories:

1. **Shopping and errands:** population to retail/service employment. First destination weight
   is the sum of CNS07 and CNS19, normalized across the city. Include basic personal services;
   do not claim explicit medical, school, airport or park coverage.
2. **Social and leisure:** population to a mixture of food/recreation employment and population
   (visiting friends/family). Trial mixture: 50% of destination mass from normalized CNS17+CNS18,
   50% from normalized population. Normalize components independently so employee and resident
   counts do not accidentally set the mixture. Food jobs include accommodation: a documented proxy.

Ordinary workplace employees, including school/airport/hospital staff, already have commutes.
Additional school attendance, passengers and patients must represent different activities.
Each category generates all-mode demand; rail wins a share through the existing service/fare/
walk/drive choice, contributing actual loads, boardings and fare income.

Use origin-constrained destination gravity, not destination job capacities. Each resident
generates an outing rate; nearby attractors receive weights by distance, normalized separately
for each origin. A shop's jobs control its relative draw, not a compulsory number of customers.
Candidate decay scales: 3 km errands, 6 km social, with finite weight at zero distance. Check
the resulting median/upper-tail distances, rather than equating a decay scale to a mean.
This proposal originally retained very short walks. Anita superseded that suggestion when
authorizing implementation: discard short outings entirely, including all-mode totals.
The initial 300 m threshold was subsequently lowered to 150 m at Anita's request (T-141).
T-066 applies this cutoff to represented cell-centre distances.

Anita, 2026-10-11: work trips are generally longer than other trips, which affects the meaning
of travel statistics. Keep purpose-specific distance distributions and check both trip-leg shares
and passenger-distance/time shares; none is interchangeable with the share of commuters using
transit or rail boardings. A smaller work share by trip count may still supply much more of the
travel distance and sustained train loads. Compare like geography, modes and day type, and use
the RHTS purpose-specific length checks above when inspecting the first prototype.

Tentative total: **0.5 outings/resident/day** (0.3 errands + 0.2 social), each with outward and
return legs: 1 additional local trip leg/resident/day. This is a deliberately partial gameplay
starting point, not a survey estimate. Check 0.25/0.5/0.75 outings to expose sensitivity before
settling the default. Never increase rates merely to make commuting 60% of train ridership.

First approximation is home-based outings, not full activity chains. Work→lunch, work→shop→home
and tourist origins are not explicitly represented. Those can later replace part of the daily
budget, rather than being added on top of an already complete non-work total.

## Timing proposal

Share of each category's daily outward/return legs; every row sums to 100%. These are trial
weekday shapes, not measured purpose-specific NYC distributions.

| Category/direction | 6–10 | 10–16 | 16–20 | 20–24 | 0–6 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Errands outward | 10% | 55% | 30% | 4% | 1% |
| Errands return | 6% | 49% | 36% | 8% | 1% |
| Social outward | 5% | 35% | 42% | 17% | 1% |
| Social return | 2% | 25% | 43% | 27% | 3% |

Use separate outward and return schedules, retaining total-trip conservation. Within a period,
trips are flows, not individual departures. Compare hourly demand (period totals divided by
4/6/4/4/6 hours), not percentages alone: a large six-hour midday total need not be a peak.
The combined category loads participate in the same frozen crowding rounds as commutes and
regional trips. Initially retain the model's shared per-period busiest-hour factors consistently;
these approximate mixed demand and do not assert that leisure has the same within-hour peak as
commuting. Finer category-specific peak treatment needs a consistent combined-load definition.

## Purpose and timing breakdown — required alongside the model

Anita, 2026-10-11: build the breakdown together with non-work demand. Selecting a destination
bubble should explain how much of its travel is work, school, leisure, etc., and show a rough
time-of-day distribution for each purpose. This is part of T-066's first implementation.

- Show each implemented purpose's daily trip count and percentage of that bubble's total,
  followed by a compact five-period chart. Start with Work, Shopping and errands, Social and
  leisure; add School and facility purposes when their demand exists. Unmodelled categories
  are absent or explicitly labelled not modelled, never displayed as measured zero.
- Counts describe trip legs, not unique people, and use the same selected origin/destination
  relationship and mode scope as the bubble. All-mode destination bubbles show all-mode
  purpose totals; rail-only views show rail totals. Make the scope clear in the inspector.
- Time bars show departures for the represented direction (outgoing trips to that destination,
  or incoming trips from the shown origin). Label this explicitly; arrival times and return
  journeys must not be silently folded into the same chart. Where both directions are shown,
  offer separate To destination / Return rows.
- Use the actual purpose-by-period query results, including period-specific mode choice for
  rail views, rather than attaching the city's generic timing template to every bubble.
  Period tooltips show trip counts/shares and hours. If bar height represents intensity, use
  trips/hour so the six-hour midday/night periods are comparable with four-hour periods.
- Purpose totals must sum to the displayed bubble total, and period totals to each purpose's
  daily count, including any documented omitted/cut totals. Work includes the appropriate
  return leg when inspecting returns; Returning home is a direction, not a separate purpose.
- Preserve purpose and period dimensions through generation, assignment and selection queries.
  Summing them must reproduce combined demand, loads and fare income. Additive query checks
  and a browser inspection of a mixed-purpose destination are required acceptance checks.

## What comes after

| Category | Inputs/behavior needed |
| --- | --- |
| Schools and universities | Locations and enrollment/capacity; school-age/student origin weights; strong morning arrivals and earlier afternoon returns. A 15:00 departure is midday in this game. Separate K–12 from university where practical. |
| Airports | Passenger volumes and terminal access points; distribute local-resident and visitor ends; broad/flight-related timing. Use originating/terminating passengers, excluding connecting passengers who never leave the airport. |
| Hospitals/medical | Actual facilities and visits or plausible capacity; appointments mostly daytime, visitors broader. Do not turn home-care jobs into hospital destinations. |
| Parks and major attractions | Real entrances or reachable access points and visit weights, not polygon area or employee counts alone. |

## Implementation observations and checks for T-066

- `build_city.py` already reads all CNS columns and caches them in `data/work/nyc.blocks.csv.gz`.
  Confirmed those columns and positive weights in all five NYC counties. No new jobs download is
  required. Current final packs retain total population/jobs and commute weights but not sectors.
- `spread.spread(..., cols=...)` already supports extra conserved weight arrays using the block
  land/water geometry. Add attractor columns there; do not use internal-point-only placement.
  Review adjustments: the cached block CSV is written before `adjust_jobs`; relocations can leave
  original sector columns inconsistent with adjusted totals. Avoid using healthcare as a facility
  proxy; preserve documented true retail/recreation locations.
- `pack::parse` replaces its internal pop/jobs with commute_home/work. A new layer must retain
  true resident population separately and must not reuse the commute gravity factors.
- `DemandApi` hardcodes TO_WORK/TO_HOME and commuter semantics in period summaries and queries.
  Merely adding new demand objects to workers would leave the bubbles/top-bar denominator wrong.
  Integrate purpose-aware totals/queries and label destinations honestly. Keep commute inspection
  available and expose everyday trips distinctly; combined line loads include both.
- Return assignment is currently derived by reversing outward station pairs. For the new layer,
  price/route each direction using the period's service; do not silently grant the outward route's
  costs to the reverse direction. Preserve existing commuting behavior during this addition.
- Prevent overlap with the existing regional non-work market: partition local vs regional by the
  complement of its 40–80 km onset for same-market OD. Normalize rates over the eligible local
  destinations and document that they generate the local component only. Cross-pack everyday
  trips remain outside this first slice; the existing regional layer continues unchanged.
- Validate generation/conservation, directional periods, attraction location/short-trip shape,
  zero-service mode totals, midday vs peak service response, common crowding/fares and query
  totals. Compare old commute-only results with the new category disabled. Browser-check the real
  NYC save and a small neighborhood line; verify all other cities and old saves still load.
