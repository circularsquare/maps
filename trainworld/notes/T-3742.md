# T-3742: nudge a line's departure times

Stage 1 built 2026-11-27 by agent nudge (tree nudge): the sim offset, the save block and the schedule window's
Offset column, with the anchor rule and weekends. The approved design (agent nudgedesign, 11-21) follows below it;
"Where the build differs" lists what changed and why. Other games: research/departure-offsets-2026-11-18.md.

## Built (stage 1)

**What the player gets.** The schedule window of a rail or tram line has an Offset column after Headway: a stepper
in each Weekdays row ("+4 min", "−1m15s", "0 min", grey while the row is not held), 1 min a click, 15 s with
Shift, 10 min with Ctrl, at most half the row's headway either way. One click is one undoable edit (clicks while
one is out go as the next). The board, chart and drawn trains move at once; nothing settles and no trips are
calculated. Weekend rows show the weekday value greyed. A row with no trains is empty. Bus and ferry windows have
no column. The window's left side is 430 px for a rail line (330 before).

**Code.** `sim/src/track/nudge.rs` (new): `TrackWorld::nudge_op` builds the edit, `row_nudge` the readout,
`apply`/`release` the op's application. `Network::line_offset` (net.rs) per line and slot, None where not
held; `spacing::place_pass` and `seed_split` skip a held slot; `Op::Nudge(Vec<NudgeSet>)` (world.rs) sets
offsets and phases and its inverse sets back what it found; `Ahead::nudged` reports the lines as changed with no
settle or placement; a removed line releases its held slots into its undo (`Op::Line` arm).
`Capacity::follow_pairs` gives the timed pairs without working out plans. Save: trailing `LOF1` (count, then line
index, slot, offset f32 bits), level 43; `SAVE_LEVEL` 43 in saveFile.ts. WASM: `nudge_row`, `line_nudges`. App:
`game/nudge.ts` (steps folded one edit in flight per row, the stepped value held on screen until a readout from a
world holding it), `schedule.tsx` `OffsetCell`, the `lineNudges` clock query, `nudgeRow` in protocol,
clockEdits and `isServiceOp` (the edit lead), `Stepper`'s new `steps` prop (widgets.tsx).

**Where the build differs from the design below, and why.**
- *Storage*: `line_phase` holds the nudged times (where the spacing put it plus the offset) and `line_offset`
  marks the held slots with the offset, rather than a frozen base the lattice adds the offset to. Every reader of
  the phase (lattice, other lines' spacing, `pin_twin_phases`, the weekend copy, boarding's key, `retime`) gets
  the nudge with no change of its own, so none can be missed. A save with nothing held is byte for byte as before;
  undo is exact because the op sets values. Release at 0 lands within 1 ms of the old base (f32).
- *Rows and slots*: a row's slots are those whose timetable is that row's (`Network::place`); a slot split off
  later (more periods) copies its offset with its phase (`seed_slots`).
- *Anchor rule*: per slot, the nudged line's anchor (the slower line, up the chain) holds the offset, and every
  line timed with it, either way and through others, moves by the same seconds; a partner itself held has its
  offset moved too. Both panels show the anchor's offset; the value's hover names the others in text ("Express is
  timed with this line and moves with it"), since a hover can't hold a chip.
- *Weekends*: a line with its own weekend rates has its weekend phase moved with the weekday one and held by the
  same `line_offset` (its weekend placement skips it too); every other line copies its weekday phases already.
- *Demand*: no solve per nudge. Waits are by headway; a stop's first and last trains move by up to the offset and
  reach demand at the line's next edit (the lie: up to half a headway on the span's ends, within a tolerance of
  30 min or more). In DECISIONS.md.
- *Control*: the panel's own −/+ stepper rather than arrows, as every other control there.
- *Save level*: 43 (master was at 42, not 36).

## For Anita (the design, approved 11-21)

**What the player gets.** In the schedule window, each weekday row of a rail line gets an Offset: "< +4 min >".
1 min a click, 15 s with Shift, 10 min with Ctrl (your Exact mode steps). It moves that row's departures, both
directions, earlier or later than the game put them. The board, the chart and the trains on the map move at once.
One click is one edit with one undo. Weekend rows follow the weekday ones. Back at 0, the game times the row again.

**How it meets the game's own timing.** Two things keep lines on shared track apart today. Pacing gives count lines
at a merge one headway; a nudge never touches headways, so pacing is unaffected. Spacing picks when each line leaves
in each row so its trains miss other lines'; that is the "default" the player wants to move. A nudged row is held:
the game stops re-placing it and adds the offset to where it had it. Other lines are placed around its real times
on their next edit. Without holding, the next train or stop edit would re-place the row and undo the fix.

**Can it break something?** Both directions move together, so no train loses its turnaround. An express timed
behind its local (or passing it at passing tracks) is moved by the same seconds as the local, so passes and waits
stay as sized. A bad nudge makes two lines' trains reach a merge together, and the map holds one back, as it does
now where the game can't space them. If a later edit still leaves a pass that doesn't fit, the game does what it
does today: those locals run the averaged waits, and the map holds an express that would catch its local.

**The catch to know.** A nudge changes the timetable and the map; riders and money barely move (the first and last
train at a stop shift a little). The game counts delay on shared track from how many trains run, not when, so the
delay numbers the player hopes to cut won't drop until Exact mode (T-3346 stage 6). And a nudge only lasts between
lines on the same or matching headways (10 and 20 min); others drift through each other whatever the offset.

**Options.**
1. One offset per row, held (my pick). Rows have different headways and the game times each row on its own, so a
   fix for the peak shouldn't move the night. About 5-7 h.
2. One offset per line, held: one control, by the chart. Simpler, but +4 fixes one row and moves the others for no
   reason. About 4-6 h.
3. Nothing now; wait for Exact mode (24-34 h), which nudges single trains.

**Track rework.** No need to wait. The nudge changes when trains leave, not track; the rework's parity checks hold on
any save without a nudge. Build it after track stage 1 (T-3948, running now) lands, since both touch the save code.

**Text (drafts):** column "Offset"; its hover "Earlier or later than the game would run them. The game won't move
this row while it's set."; values "+4 min", "-1m15s", "0 min"; arrows "1 min earlier" / "1 min later" with
"Shift for 15 s, Ctrl for 10 min" under each; at the end of the range "Half a headway at most"; on a line timed with
another, "(chip) is timed with this line and moves with it"; weekend rows "Follows weekdays".

## How the game times departures now (for the builder)

Rules: docs/track-and-service.md 6.3 "Scheduling" (pacing) and "Departures" (spacing, spare time, weekends);
wiki/read/scheduling.md.

- **Pacing** (`TrackWorld::pace_merges`, T-2973, T-3388, weekends T-3584): count lines meeting at a merge with
  headways within 3% run the longest, `line_pace` per slot, derived every settle. Headways only, never phases.
- **Spacing** (`spacing::place`, T-1925, T-2960, T-2972, T-3929; called from `TrackWorld::place_departures`,
  world.rs:455): per line and slot a phase in seconds (`Network::line_phase: Vec<[f32; SLOTS]>`, outside undo,
  `LPH1`) and for headway lines the share of spare time at run 0's end (`line_split`, `LSP1`). Greedy: only the
  lines an edit changes, then the lines they meet that have spare time move their split, then the edit's lines once
  more. A line keeps its phase unless another cuts the score by more than 10%. Every departure of a slot, both runs,
  leaves `phase` after the lattice time, so the reverse offset `off` (forward trip, plus a share of the spare) is
  untouched by phase: shifting the phase keeps every turnaround.
- **Weekends** (T-3735): lines with own weekend rates have `line_phase_rest`, `line_split_rest` (`LWP1`), placed by
  `place_weekend`; every other line copies its weekday phases.
- **Twins** (T-3333): `pin_twin_phases` keeps a quad twin half a headway behind its line.
- **Pairs timed together**: a faster line behind a slower one with nowhere to pass is placed `s` before the next
  slower train (T-2419); a pair overtaking at passing stations is placed by the overtake plan's cost per offset
  (T-3929). After every placement `timing::retime` works out each train's own waits and following losses at the
  phase it runs (T-3346 stage 2); `timing::floor` reverts a run to the averaged waits where trains would miss their
  next departure, and `timing::following` caps a train's loss at the average plus its far-end stand.
- **What reads the phase**: departures (`Service::run_deps`), the drawn trains, the board (`station_departures`),
  the chart and train table, boarding's per-train account (its key hashes `line_phase`), `retime`. Not read by
  capacity (Kingman delays from rates) or money. Demand's waits are by headway, but it reads each stop's service
  span from a steady day's departures (span.rs), so a nudge moves a stop's first and last train by up to the offset;
  past `DEMAND_TIME_EPS_S`-style tolerance that re-solves the periods touched. So a nudge needs no settle; the builder
  measures whether it triggers a demand solve on the real save and, if so, how much riders move (expected: a little
  at the ends of the day).

## The design

**Storage.** A new field, not `line_phase`: `Network::line_offset: Vec<[f32; SLOTS]>`, seconds, NaN (or a flag
array) where the row is not held. A held row's `line_phase` is frozen at its value when first nudged (the base), and
the lattice adds the offset. This keeps `line_phase`'s meaning, so saves without a nudge are byte for byte today's
(the rework's gate A compares phases). In undo: the op carries old and new offset per row, plus the frozen base so an
undo of the first nudge releases the row exactly.

**Range and wrap.** The stepper runs from minus to plus half the row's headway. The headway can change later (a
count edit, a slower round trip); the offset is stored raw and applied modulo the row's headway, shown wrapped into
the half-headway range. A whole headway's shift is the same pattern, so wrapping never changes the relative timing
of lines.

**Held rows.** `spacing::place_pass` and `seed_split` skip a held (line, slot): neither its phase nor its split moves.
Every other line is scored against its real, nudged times. A nudge edit re-places no neighbour and moves none
except the timed partners below (an edit elsewhere moves no one). Setting a row back to 0 releases it; it keeps its times until its
line's next edit places it again.

**Pairs timed together: the anchor rule.** The offset lives on the slower line of a timed pair (the anchor; up the
chain if that one is itself timed behind another). Nudging the faster line nudges its anchor. The same edit shifts
the anchor's timed partners in that slot by the same seconds, straight into their `line_phase` (and the op keeps
their old phases for undo, since `line_phase` is outside undo); they are not held. Re-placing them instead would not
follow: spacing tries 24 phases a headway and keeps a line's phase unless another scores 10% better (`STEPS`,
`KEEP`), so a 1 min nudge could leave the express where it was or snap it a step away. Shifted, their relative
place is exact, their score against the anchor is unchanged, and on their own later edits the game places them
against the held anchor's real times as usual. So retime's waits and the figures they are sized on stay consistent
and `floor` doesn't trip. The pair list is `Capacity::follow_targets(net, slot)` (following and passing pairs). Both
lines' panels show the anchor's offset, with the "(chip) is timed with this line and moves with it" note.

**Weekends.** The weekday row's offset applies to the weekend row: lines running their weekday departures on
weekends get it for free; lines with own weekend rates hold their weekend row too (`line_phase_rest` frozen, offset
added). No separate weekend offset in this version.

**Twins.** A twin follows its line's offset through `pin_twin_phases` (removed with twins at rework stage 6c).

**Buses and ferries.** Not offered: spacing places rail lines only.

**Edits and eras.** A nudge is a service edit like a train count step: the edit lead (T-1071), an era at the edit
time, trains join and leave at the ends (T-206). A negative nudge brings a few trains in at the terminals for one
cycle; nothing else differs from a count edit.

**What can still go wrong, and what the game does.**
- Two lines' trains at a merge, a flat junction or a one-place end together: figures unchanged, the map's guard
  holds the later one (T-3264's guard; T-3231 for one-place ends). The player sees it on the chart with other lines
  shown and nudges again. No warning in this version; Exact mode's conflict marks are the place for one.
- A later edit (a partner's count, a stop) changes the pair so the held anchor's place no longer suits: the partner
  is placed against it on its own edit; where a pass still doesn't fit, `timing::floor` puts that run back on the
  averaged waits (T-3343's local leaving just ahead of its express can show there), and a following loss past
  `timing::following`'s cap is held by the map's guard. As today for any pair the spacing can't suit.
- A line timed with a held line that the anchor rule doesn't cover (a line timed behind several slower
  lines runs the random-meeting average, not a timed pair): as today.
- If T-3346's leftover fix (size round trips on the trains' real waits, 4-6 h, needs a design note) lands, nothing
  here changes: the anchor rule keeps pairs at their placed relative offset either way.

**Saves.** A trailing block `LOF1`: count, then per held (line, slot) its line index, slot, base and offset (f32s),
weekdays and weekends. Written only when a row is held. The next free save level on master (37 while master is at
36), and the
shonei-server save check learns it (`twSaveLevel`, `twTrackBlocks`, an `LOF1` parser in trainworld_savecheck.go;
commit in shonei-server, deploy on her word before the publish).

**The control.** In the schedule panel's Weekdays rows (schedule.tsx `DaySections`, `LevelCells`), a compact column
after Headway: two small arrows around the value. The panel's left side is 330 px with four columns; it widens by
about 70 px (the panel is 1320 px). Weekend rows show the weekday value greyed. The dock line panel is unchanged.
Typing not needed (arrows with Shift and Ctrl cover 15 s to 10 min). Copy and paste of a schedule doesn't carry
offsets (they are relative to another line's timing).

**Into Exact mode (T-3346 stage 6).** Entering Exact makes the line's departure lists from its lattice, offsets
included; the row offsets then become a read-only summary, and the arrows move single trains. The held-row idea,
the op with era and undo, and the step sizes all carry over.

**Station timetables (T-3780).** Read `station_departures`, so they show nudged times with no extra work.

## Track rework (T-3444, tw/track)

The nudge lands on master and reaches tw/track with the regular master merges. Where they meet:
- **Gate A** (part 5: phases on the real NYC save "to the bit"): unaffected, the offset is its own field and a save
  without one is today's.
- **Stages 3a-3c** rekey capacity and paths; spacing.rs, service.rs and timing.rs are on their list of consumers to
  adapt. The nudge adds a skip in `place_pass`, an add in the lattice and the anchor lookup, a few lines each.
- **Stage 6c** removes twins: the nudge's twin line goes with `pin_twin_phases`.
- **Stage 7b** (real passing tracks, the rejoin not a merge): changes which pairs are timed together and the
  spacing weights. The anchor rule reads the same capacity resources, so it follows.
- **Save levels**: master takes one more. The rework's level table (07-saves.md: 34 for stage 1, 35-37 for stages
  2-4, 38 on) already overlaps what master has used since (36), with or without this; for the orchestrator to
  settle with the rework's save stages, not something this task changes.
- Order: after track stage 1 (T-3948), which is in the save code now. Not after any later stage.
