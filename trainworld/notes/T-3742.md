# T-3742: nudge a line's departure times (design, agent offsetdesign, 2026-11-18)

Design only; nothing built. Outward look: research/departure-offsets-2026-11-18.md.

## What the player sees and does

In the schedule window, in the small tool row by the chart (beside the other lines toggle), a rail line gets one
control:

    Departures   <   +4 min   >

- The arrows move all of the line's departures, both directions, every row, weekdays and weekends: 1 min a click,
  15 s with Shift, 10 min with Ctrl (your 11-12 steps for Exact). The value can also be typed.
- It runs from minus to plus half the line's longest headway, so every timing has one value. A row with a shorter
  headway takes what is left over (on a line at 6 min in the peak and 20 min in the evening, +4 min is the same as 2 min earlier in the peak); the board and the chart show the real
  times at once.
- The board, the chart and the trains on the map move with each click. With other lines shown on the chart, the
  player sees two lines' trains reaching a merge, a flat junction or a shared terminus together, and nudges one
  apart.
- A nudge is one edit with one undo, like a train count step. Back at 0, the line runs where the game had it.

## How it works

**What the game does today.** Two separate things keep lines sharing track apart:

1. **Pacing** (`pace_merges`, T-2973, T-3584) makes count lines at a merge run one headway. It sets headways and
   never looks at when trains leave. A nudge leaves it alone, and it is what makes a nudge last: two lines at 9:47
   and 10:03 drift through each other whatever the offset, two at one headway (or 10 and 20, 15 and 30) keep it.
2. **Spacing** (`spacing::place`, T-1925, T-2960, T-2972) picks a phase per line and row, one of 24 steps of a
   headway, so its trains meet other lines' as far apart as they can, merges first. It runs only for the lines an edit
   changed (or whose track it touched), against the others as they stand. This is the "whatever the game defaults
   to" the player asked to move from.

**The rule: a nudged line is held.** While a line's offset is not 0, spacing never places it again: it keeps the
phase the game had given it, and the offset is added on top. Other lines, whenever the game next places them, place
against its real (nudged) times, so the game doesn't drop another line on top of it. A nudge moves only that line;
it never re-places its neighbours, since the player is steering against lines that should stay still ("an edit
elsewhere moves no one"). Setting it back to 0 frees the line; it keeps its times until its next edit, when the
game places it again.

Why held rather than added on top of a placing that keeps running: spacing re-places a line at every edit to it or
its track (a train more, a stop moved). With "+4 on top" the base would quietly move to the best spot and the +4
would then push the line off it, so the player's fix would come undone after any edit. NIMBY Rails does the same
split: a shift's offset is either auto or typed, and typed means the player owns it.

**Lines timed together** (an express timetabled right behind a local it can't pass, T-2419, or a pair that
overtakes at passing stations, T-2231): spacing places the faster line against the slower, and the figures (the
express's following loss, the local's waits) assume that place. My pick: a nudge on either moves the whole pair
(and any line timed with them) by the same amount, and all of them are held. Their timing together, and so the
figures, stay right. The alternative (move one alone) widens the T-2239 gap between what the figures assume and
what runs, and once T-3346 stage 2's leftover fix makes the figures follow the real phase, a nudge there would move
round trips and riders.

**A quad twin** (T-3333) follows its line's offset.

**What a nudge changes.** The departures, the board, the chart and the drawn trains (the map's guard holds trains
that would run through each other, so a good nudge means fewer trains held at a merge). The line's boarding account
is worked out again from its new departures (riders left behind and crowding per train, which barely move).
**Riders, delay figures, capacity and money do not change**: delays where lines share track are a queue worked out
from rates, not from when trains leave, and riders' waits are by headway. So the edit needs no settle and no
demand solve, and costs a few ms. The player who asked may expect delay numbers to drop; they won't, until the
game works out conflicts train by train (T-3346 Exact mode's first-come engine, or T-3264 B). Said in the wiki's
scheduling page, not in the game.

**Saves.** A new trailing block `LDO1`: count, then per held line its index and offset (seconds, i32). Written only
when some line is held, so every other save is byte for byte as today. The held line's phase is already saved
(`LPH1`). That is a **save level bump** (22 to 23, or the next free one if T-3542 stage 1 lands a block first) and a
**shonei-server save-check change before publish**: `twSaveLevel`, `twTrackBlocks` and an `LDO1` parser in
trainworld_savecheck.go (commit in shonei-server; the deploy waits for your word).

**Code it touches** (for the builder): `Service::lattice` adds the offset to each row's phase, weekends included
(`rest_phase`); `spacing::place_pass` and `seed_split` skip held lines; `pin_twin_phases` reads the parent's nudged
phase; a new op (offset per line, with its inverse for undo; the auto phases sit outside undo, the offset may not)
that marks only the departures changed: an era at the edit time, no settle; save and load; the boarding key reads the
offset with the phases. App: the control in `TrainTable`'s `tools` slot from SchedulePanel's `LineBody`, its keys
and translations, `changelog.py` item. The timed-together closure comes from `Capacity::follow_targets` at every row.

## How it sits with T-3542 (five periods, stage 0 being built now)

No collision in the model: one number per line, not per row, so nothing depends on `LEVELS` (stage 0 is rewriting
every `[_; 3]`). The code meets stage 0 in two places, `Service::lattice` and `spacing.rs`, both a few lines; build
this after stage 0 lands. With 4 or 5 rows (stage 1) and tweakable hours (stage 2) the offset still applies to every
row, each modulo its headway. The control sits by the chart, away from the rows that stages 1-2 rework.

## Stepping stone or dead end

A stepping stone for Exact mode (T-3346 stage 6): the held line, the op with its era and undo, the step sizes and
arrows, and "others place around what the player set" are all what Exact needs per train. Entering Exact will bake
the offset into the line's departure lists (they come from the lattice), and the line control becomes "move all
trains" there. Nothing here is thrown away.

## Open questions (my pick first)

1. **One offset per line, or one per row?** Per line. Per row fits better where a line conflicts only at the peak
   (spacing already picks each row's phase on its own, so +4 moves the evening too), but it is a control per row,
   and stage 1 changes how many rows a line has. Per row later if players ask; Exact covers finer control.
2. **Lines timed together move together?** Yes, as above. The other choices: the nudge moves only the line (and the
   figures are a little off where it is an express behind a local), or the control is greyed on such lines.
3. **Per direction?** No. A count line's trains turn straight back, so one direction alone would need extra
   standing time, which only a headway line with spare time has, and the game already places that share.
