# T-1354 The market's corridor figures depend on pack order

## The task as filed (2026-11-04)

The market's corridor figures depended on which market packs were open at a worker's first composition:
`MarketPacks` keeps the corridors worked out then for every later composition (T-1204). On the real save a
worker that composed once from all 13 packs and workers that composed as packs arrived had corridor tables ten
rows apart (1,530,357 and 1,530,367). Goal: longer trips depend only on the world's rows, whatever order packs
arrive in. T-1225's float-sized load-to-load differences were suspected to share the cause.

## What master did (2026-11-22, 65c5292b)

Two separate things, measured on the real save (now 9 market packs, 8,523 places, 11,682,392 table rows):

- **The table itself is already order-free.** `probe-order.js` (scratch) built one `MarketPacks` per order with
  the real rows, drives and packs: all 9 at once; one pack, then the other 8 (twice, with different first packs);
  one at a time in reverse. Row count, each row's corridor, longer trips and commute legs came out bit-identical
  in every order. With rows, `corridors_of` reads only the rows (every place field the pack loop sets is set again
  by the row loop, `near` comes from the rows). The old ten-row gap was not reproduced; its cause was not found
  (the T-1174-era `corridors_of` and row loop have the same shape, so it may have been the rows file of the day).
- **The book was not.** The worker sums the table into a book for the page (`table_summary`: key, per-place
  longer trips, commute legs, longer trips times share flying, own rows, own-row share on two lands). Its shares
  flying and driving come from the composition (an open pair's rule from the table, `Market::apart` from the open
  zones), but `corridorBook` kept the book by `corridors_id` alone, and that id survives recomposition (the kept
  corridors). So the book stayed at whatever packs were open at the worker's first market solve. In the probe the
  one-pack composition's modes, flying totals and `apart` differ from the nine-pack one; the full composition's
  are the same whatever the order.

End to end (`e2e-order.mjs`, scratch: a load with phl and pvd's market downloads aborted until the first final,
so they arrive through the 10 s retry, against a normal load; fresh profile, `perfOff=nocache`, 2 solving
workers): after all 9 packs were open, every rail figure (view, lines, stations, market, railAt, longerRail,
commuteRail) matched the normal load, but the book's key, flying totals and `apart` stayed at the 7-pack values
(key `5el6if` against `jzljr9`). The same with `?split=1` (`d1wbcq` against `1pl04ff`, with the key now holding
`apart`). From the code, not run: a worker that composed later (a grown or replaced worker) would also carry a
key different from the others', and `dayCorridors` drops periods whose key differs ("periods with different
corridor tables").

## The fix

- `demand.worker.ts` `corridorBook`: the book is made again when the composition changes (`composedOf`, the
  packs it was made of), not only when the corridors change; `rowsFor` answers from a market of that composition.
  `railMemo` (each rail row's share flying) is cleared with it.
- `exports.rs` `table_summary`: the key also hashes each place's own-row share on two lands (`apart.own`), which
  no row's shares carry, so a composition that changes only those makes a new key and the page takes the new book.
- `build.rs`: `forget_corridors`'s comment said the corridors differ with the packs open; they don't.
- Docs: `demand-market.md` (the book per composition, its cost), `architecture.md` (the T-1174 line no longer
  claims the compose-afresh keeps books equal).

Cost: one book is about 3.6 s on the real save with its 9 packs open (11.7M rows; `probe-cost.js` on the page's
main thread, agent WASM build), made by a worker that solves the market at its first result after each
composition (read from the code, not counted). A key can come back (a pack opened, then closed): the page keeps
its last 3 books by key and asks the workers again (`corridorsAgain`) for one it lacks, so that is covered. A load composes once (T-889 batches the
packs), so a load's cost is unchanged; each later pack opening or closing (an edit reaching a new zone) costs a
worker about 3 s more than before, beside the 1.5-3.5 s composition itself.
