# T-839 and T-838: what a snap can reach, no detour limit, high-speed preferred

Anita, 2026-10-21: huge detours are fine (the player sees the ghost before clicking, and a mountain
line can be twice the straight distance); the harder problem is not knowing what is connected, so
highlight what a snap can reach, as stop editing marks reachable stops. And high-speed track should
count about 20% shorter.

## What changed

- **No detour limit** (T-839a): `DETOUR`, `DETOUR_M` and the search's cost cap are gone from
  `workers/realRoute.worker.ts`. The cap stopped the search at three times the limit, which refused
  routes a full search would find, so it went too. The search's only early stop now is A*'s own
  (the cheapest open state already costs more than the best route found), which refuses nothing.
- **High-speed at 0.8** (T-838): `HSR`, a chain's cost per m when its flags have 8; the A*
  heuristic is scaled by the same 0.8 so it stays a lower bound. Lengths shown and built are real.
- **The reach** (T-839b): a `reach` message to the router worker after each first click (and on
  each move or zoom while the segment is open). The worker walks the search's moves without costs
  (from every candidate chain at the click, through junctions with no turn over 60 degrees), keeps
  the result per start, and sends the reachable chains whose box crosses the view plus a quarter
  each side, vertices thinned to 1.5 px and chains smaller than that left out. `map/realNetwork.ts`
  draws them (`realReach` signal, a GeoJSON line layer `tw-reach` above the `tw-real-*` layers) in
  the layer's colours at 0.9 opacity and a little wider, with the layer dimmed from 0.4 to 0.25.
  `RealSnap.reset` (placing, Escape, a click on the start, snap off) clears it.

## Timings (dev server, headless Chrome, 2026-10-21; scratch `probe.mjs`)

Click at zoom 13, then the view moved to the zoom given. "Answer" is click to the worker's reply on
the main thread, "drawn" to the GeoJSON source loaded and a frame rendered. No main-thread long
task over 50 ms in any of them.

With vertices 0.75 px apart and every chain sent, zoom 7 and 9 views took 250-430 ms to draw with
95-147 ms long tasks; 1.5 px and the small chains left out brought them under 100 ms.
