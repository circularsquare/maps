# T-4086: a lighter load for phones (design, agent phonedesign, 2026-11-23)

## Summary for Anita

- **Choice: don't build "coarser further out".** The game already does it (T-1223), and on a big Tokyo game the far
  zones cost 2 MB more fine than coarse: the memory is Tokyo itself, always in view. All coarse saves 44 MB (3%).
- **Instead, a phone load that changes no figure**: no aux worker (65-80 MB); cut what a far jump of the view costs
  (+280 MB for a minute in the clock worker and page, +420 with the refine, above every load peak measured; no refine
  on a pan on phones); later the solve's working space (about 200 MB of the load peak, T-3160).
- **Players see** no difference. Optional, your call: Tokyo coarse on phones too: trips calculated a third to two
  thirds faster after a load or edit, totals within 0.013%, lines within 6.6%, big stations within 7% (small ones up to
  21%); no region star or goal flips, so stars and goals could count coarse data (section below).
- **Build**: aux 3-5 h; the jump 3-6 h; no refine on pan 0.5-1 h; the solve's space after this week's sim/ work.
- **PWA**: no source shows an installed app getting more memory on iOS or Android (fairly sure). No banner: on iPhone
  the app also starts with its own empty storage, so Safari saves don't carry over unless signed in.

## The design: a phone load

For a device where `tabLimited()` is true (phone or tablet with no `deviceMemory`: every iPhone and iPad browser,
Firefox on Android). Android Chrome reports memory and keeps today's rules.

1. **No aux worker.** The pool is the solving worker alone; the aux worker's questions (bare zones, bubble sums) go to
   it, answered between its populations, the path the focus pool already uses (`demandFocus.ts`, T-2495, where an aux of
   its own measured about 0.1 GB more during a load). Saves the aux worker's 65-81 MB plus its JS engine. The lie: bare
   zones and bubble answers wait behind a solve's population on phones (seconds on a big game, not measured). Figures
   identical. 3-5 h, with a run of this harness and the bare-zone check (`t325-bare-check.mjs`).
2. **The jump spike.** First name it (heap snapshots of the clock worker and page across a jump, as T-3939 did:
   suspects are the view-dependent work there, `lineBands`, `corridors`, place names and the outlines copy); then cut
   the biggest piece. 1-2 h to name, 2-4 h to cut. Up to 280 MB of the in-play peak, 0 at the load peak. Figures identical.
   Also on phones: **no refine on a pan** (levels fixed at load), a one-line rule in `demandLod.ts`, worth 140 MB of the
   spike; the lie is that a far zone looked at stays coarse (its figures within sample noise of fine). 0.5-1 h.
3. **The solve's working space**, about 200 MB of the solving worker's high water, which sets the load peak. T-3160's
   "Next" list (the five periods' held results, period 4's skims). Open-ended, and the sim/ and solve code belongs to
   the track rework and solve speedup agents this week; after them.

Together (1)+(2) give about 65-80 MB at the load peak and 300-420 MB at a jump's peak. The load-time kills need (3) or
T-3939 (the clock worker) as well: no change found that takes 250-300 MB off the load peak short of trips off, which the
game already offers after an out-of-memory load (T-3940).

### Optional, a demand change (Anita's call): Tokyo coarse on phones

Every zone coarse on phones, the zone in view too.
- Saves 44 MB of WASM (38-74 MB settled; the load peak moves within run-to-run spread). Loads 40-60% faster: R 206 s
  against 355 s, S 199 s against 466-490 s, which also shortens the time a phone sits near its limit.
- What players see: the same model at fewer samples. Total rail trips -0.002% (17,428,605 against 17,428,960 on R);
  the busiest line (Keihin-Tohoku Line) 1,456,459 riders against 1,462,413 (-0.4%); smaller lines and single stations
  move more (not measured per line). Money follows riders, so fares move the same way.
- Coarse zones are `roughZones` today: no star or goal comes from them, and the zone in view shows the "rough" status
  while fine is on its way. On phones that rule would have to treat the phone level as normal, or phone players would
  earn no stars or goals.
- 1.5-3 h with the stars and goals rule and a run of this harness.
- Not recommended for memory alone; worth it only if you want the faster loads.

## Home city coarse on phones: how much it changes, and how fast (Anita's question, 11-23)

Her words: "how much does this impact line ridership and/or station ridership in the worst case? and total trip count?
if its not that much maybe we can just give stars / goals / everything for coarse data. 50% faster load does seem pretty
nice, especially if trips are faster to compute too (are they?)"

Same harness, both saves, home city fine (today's phone load) against every zone coarse, weekday figures (the average
day gives the same picture). Lists: scratch `phonedesign/measure/ridership-q-{r,s}-{fine,coarse}.json`, `cmp.py`.

- **Totals**: all trips -0.009 to -0.013%, rail trips -0.003 to -0.006% (R 17,427,762 -> 17,427,302).
- **Lines** (113 with 10,000+ riders/day): median change 0.9%, 90th percentile 3.5%, worst 6.6%.
  - By percent: Tamagawa Line 41,705 -> 38,971 (-6.6%), Daishi Line +6.3%, Kodomonokuni Line +5.3%, an Isesaki Line
    +5.1%, Enoshima Electric Railway +4.9%.
  - By riders: Oedo Line -15.7k, Blue Line +11.0k, Yamanote Line -10.2k, Saikyo Line +8.3k, Hibiya Line +7.3k (each
    under 2% of its riders).
- **Stations** (1,399 with 5,000+ boardings/day): median 2.1%, 90th percentile 6.0%, worst 21%.
  - By percent: Higashi-Nagasaki +21%, Funabashi-Keibajo +16%, Akiyama +16%, Otsuka-Teikyo-Daigaku -16%, Shinanomachi -15%.
  - By riders: Shinjuku +3.9k (+2.9%), Kamata -3.2k (-4.4%), Otsuka -3.1k (-7.3%), Machida -3.0k (-3.7%), Tokyo -2.6k (-2.2%).
- The 13-zone save gives the same lines and stations to about 1 rider: its far zones carry no rail trips.
- **Faster, yes.** One core each with other jobs on the machine, so the ratios count, not the seconds. Calculating
  trips after the load: 63% off on R (215 of 340 s), 16% off on S (34 of 213 s); the earlier 2-core runs gave 42% (149 of
  355 s) and 59% (291 of 490 s). After an edit (one more train on the Keihin-Tohoku Line, then another): 66% and 68% off
  on R (387 of 583 s, 266 of 390 s), 31% and 33% off on S (68 of 220 s, 68 of 209 s). So roughly a third to two thirds
  off both waits; the spread between runs is large.

**Stars and goals on coarse data.** What coarse blocks today (`roughZones`): a region's star and goals (train share of
10, 20, 30, 40%, `clockClient.ts`) and the city first (a city's train share, `achievements.ts`). The trips, station
and line tiers already count coarse figures.
- Region shares: every zone the same fine or coarse to 0.01 points (Tokyo 24.56 -> 24.57% weekday, 23.99 -> 24.00% on
  the average day). None crosses a mark on these saves.
- Line and station tiers (500k, 1M, 3M, 10M a line; 500k, 1M, 2M, 5M a station): the lines and stations that big move
  under 3% (busiest line 1,462,413 -> 1,456,459, -0.4%), so a tier flips only for one sitting within a few percent of
  it, and goals are never taken back: at most earned a little sooner or later.
- So giving stars and goals for coarse data would look the same to a player on these saves. Where it could differ is a
  small zone (fewer trips, more noise) sitting right at 10-40%; not measured, since these saves have no small zone with
  rail.
- A phone player would see small stations move by up to about 15-20% against the same game on desktop, and lines by up
  to about 6%. Totals and the big lines and stations match to a few percent or better.

## The PWA question

The full notes with quotes: scratch `phonedesign/pwa-research/notes.txt`.

- **The game already**: `app/public/manifest.webmanifest` (standalone, icons 192, 512 and maskable) and the
  `apple-mobile-web-app-*` tags, no service worker (T-3815, DECISIONS 2026-11-19: no offline cache, so no stale build).
- **iOS: no more memory, as far as anyone has published** (weak to medium confidence). The page runs in the same WebKit
  web content process whatever the host is; WebKit reads its limit from the kernel for that process
  (`Source/WTF/wtf/AvailableMemory.cpp`, "WebKit processes currently use the same limit for FG and BG"). An Apple
  engineer on bugs.webkit.org 277848: "WebContent processes on iPhone are subject to a "soft" limit of around 1.5GB",
  allowed above it "if memory demands from other processes are low". A developer forum log (thread 823061, iOS 26) shows
  the 2,048 MB ActiveHard kill in Safari and in an app's web view alike. Nobody published a home screen app measured
  against a tab. Every iOS browser, Chrome and the Google app included, is the same WebKit.
- **Android: no more memory** (medium). An installed app is a thin shell that loads Chrome to draw the page
  (Chromium's `android_architecture.md`); the same renderer, killed by Android's low memory killer by device pressure.
  What matters more there is 64-bit Chrome (8 GB devices and up, by 2021 reports).
- **Other differences** (strong): on iOS an installed app has "separate cookies and storage from the browser" (WWDC23
  10120), its own 7-day counter (webkit.org/blog/10218, so its saves are not cleared after a week away) and its
  `persist()` granted more readily. The cost: it starts with empty storage, so a player's Safari saves and played hours
  are not there unless signed in (the account's copies are). Android shares Chrome's storage.
- Since Chrome 108 on Android a manifest with no service worker is a real install from the menu
  (developer.chrome.com/blog/update-install-criteria); whether `beforeinstallprompt` fires without one is unsettled.
  On iOS 26 every site added to the Home Screen opens as a web app by default.
- **Recommendation: no banner.** It would not stop the crashes, and on iPhone it would split a player's saves between
  Safari and the app. If you want one anyway for the storage reason (saves kept past Safari's 7 days), I'd draft it:
  shown after 2 hours from `hoursPlayed.ts`, never when already installed (`display-mode: standalone` or
  `navigator.standalone`), dismissed forever in localStorage, with the iOS step telling players to sign in first. About
  2-3 h.

- One run per arm; S's load peak is a built save, not a player's.
- The jump spike's owner inside the clock worker is not named (design step 2).
- Per-line and per-station differences under coarse not measured beyond the busiest line.
- Advisor called once, after the first measurements: it asked for the S all-fine arm (which showed the far zones cost
