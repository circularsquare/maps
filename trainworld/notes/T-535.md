## The idea in one paragraph

Split the work into **country onboarding** (attended, once per country, optional) and **city
building** (unattended, every city). A country's row in the data register becomes a small machine
readable entry: its archetype, its unit source, and which country module hooks it has. A city's
config is then generated from a world plan file, not hand-written: extent, origin, name, id,
extracts, time zone and curve all come from rules. The build runs, a gate of checks with numeric
thresholds decides ship or park, and parked cities wait with a written reason. Every place where
an agent decides something today becomes a rule, a check, a fallback, or a listed exception that
an agent handles in a batch.

## Automatic city and zone choice

**Sources** (all global, all GHSL or open):

- *Which cities*: GHSL Urban Centre Database R2024A (on disk already, from citybrowser): 11,422
  urban centres with 2025 population. Over 1M: 524 centres in 127 countries (25 in the US); over
  2M: 254; over 0.5M: 1,114.
- *How far each reaches*: GHSL Functional Urban Areas (GHS-FUA R2019A, the urban centre plus its
  modelled commuting zone, 2015 epoch; about 9,000 areas). Older than the UCDB, but the commuting
  shape changes slowly.
- *Units*: geoBoundaries gbOpen (CC BY or ODbL per country), at the finest level whose median
  unit has at least 20,000 people. Where a country module exists, its own units instead (Japan's
  N03 municipalities, Germany's Kreise, England's ITL3), so its census tables join without a
  crosswalk. OSM admin relations as the fallback where geoBoundaries is coarse or old (China, as
  T-291 found). GADM is ruled out (non-commercial licence).

**The world plan** (`pipeline/world_plan.json`, written by a new `world_plan.py`): one row per
candidate metro, with its units, origin, name, id, extracts, time zone, archetype, curve source,
order and status (planned, built, shipped, parked with reason). Every unit in the world has at
most one owner: a city pack, a non-city zone, or nobody (dead space). Built packs claim their units
first; a new pack takes only free units, so packs never overlap. Where two functional areas claim
one unit, the one holding more of its people wins.

**Order**: decision 1. Within a run, cities are grouped by Geofabrik region so each extract is
downloaded and read once.

**Non-city zones**: decision 5's rule, applied from the unit table: a free unit touching two
different city packs becomes a zone; one under 100,000 people joins the neighbouring zone it
shares the longest border with (docs/zones.md 2, unchanged).

**Commuting between packs**: today only the Northeast and Japan are joint gravity groups; every
other tier 0 pack stands alone, so a commuter crossing a pack line between two new neighbours
would be lost. The runner forms groups from packs within 150 km of each other and re-solves a
group when a member is added. That rewrites existing members' balancing factors (as Japan's did,
T-463), so the gate's existing-pack checks run on them too.

## Checks a pack must pass

`checks/pack_gate.py`, one line per check, PASS, WARN or FAIL. A pack ships (is registered) only
when nothing FAILs. Thresholds are first set from the 67 existing packs (stage 6): each must pass
or its failure must be a real fault.

**Structural (FAIL)**

- `read_pack.py` OK: cells, totals, gravity balance under 2e-3, water arrays, students where
  campuses are.
- Water: every automatic water point in water and every station point on land, at least 95%.
- `every_pack_everyday` rates in range (school 0.08-0.35 a resident, university 0.001-0.08).
- Browser: the pack opens, ground track over water refused, a test line through the centre solves
  with work, shopping, social, school and medical riders, no page errors.
- No file of another pack changed except gravity factors in its own group.

**Plausibility (FAIL beyond the outer bound, WARN beyond the inner)**

- Population against the functional urban area's GHSL total: 0.7-1.5 (catches a bad snap).
- Outline: the main piece holds 95% of the people; area under 40,000 km².
- Jobs a resident 0.25-0.7; no cell over 1% of jobs; no 2 km zone over 5%.
- Mean commute (straight line) 3-25 km.
- Pupils per school site under 5,000 (WARN only: Lagos's 5,800 is OSM's gap, not a bug).
- Largest hospital under 5,000 beds.
- Median routed drive factor 1.1-2.0; median cell within 300 m of a road (else WARN: OSM roads
  thin).
- Each figure inside the existing fleet's range widened by half (WARN), to catch what nobody
  thought to bound.

**On a fail**, in order:

1. A known fallback, automatic: retry downloads; cached API answers; a degenerate curve fit
   falls back to the borrowed curve; a too-large outline drops its outermost units until it fits.
2. Otherwise **park**: not registered, its status and reason in the world plan, its log and
   pictures kept. The batch report lists parked cities. Nothing parked is ever registered
   silently, and a WARN is listed in the batch report, not hidden.

## Cost

**Machine time per city**, from the retrospectives (Lagos, Berlin, Seoul, Sao Paulo): build 1-10
minutes, routes 1-5, merge and the small steps seconds, gate with browser check about 2; plus the
region's extract (download 4 minutes, reads 2-10) shared by every city in it. About 20 minutes a
city on 3 cores, two cities at once inside the 5-core cap: **200 cities about 2-3 days, 500 about
a week**. Large packs (40M) take an hour or more each.

**Tokens per city**: the runner needs no agent while it runs. An agent starts a batch, waits once,
reads the one-screen report and the contact sheet: about 0.3-0.5M weighted a batch of 10-20,
so 20-50k a city. Parked cities are the real cost: if 15% park and each costs about 1M to fix
(that fix then becoming a rule), that adds about 0.15M a city on average. **About 0.2M a city
in all, against 1.6-3.4M today**, falling as parked reasons turn into rules. 500 cities about
100M, against 300-700M for 200 by hand.

**One-off**: the stages below, about 8 tasks at 2-4M each, so 25-35M. Onboarding a country with
a module (optional, for tailoring) stays at about 3-6M a country.

## How a country module fits *(detail)*

A country's archetype is what its best city can have; a given city takes the first archetype its
own data supports. A Brazilian city other than Sao Paulo has no travel survey, so it falls to C
(census tracts, a borrowed curve) or D. A module therefore offers hooks (population, jobs,
schools, curve, units), each either working for any city in the country or for named cities
only, and the generated config uses the ones that apply. Archetype A modules that fit a curve per
city must do so with a fixed grid and a tie rule (as `japan_zones.py fit` does), not by an agent
choosing. Archetype C countries need their published commute figures as a table per city (China's
CAUPD-Baidu report covers about 45 cities), entered once at onboarding.
