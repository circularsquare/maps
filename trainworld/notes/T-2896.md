# T-2896: Chinese airports and railway stations (investigation, 2026-11-09, agent airports)

Read-only. No code or data changed. Scripts were one-offs in the agent's scratch folder.

Anita's note: "someone mentioned chinese airport traffic is low, like nanjing lukou. also that chinese railway
stations are low (we really shouldnt have railway stations as attractions? if we have that, we should undo it)".

## Short answer

- Airports: China is low, but not evenly. There are two separate causes, neither a Chinese factor. Stale Wikidata
  figures (213 of China's 258 airports carry a 2022 figure, the zero-Covid year) and the pack share (the part of an
  airport's catchment outside its own zone is dropped, and airport trips do not cross zone lines). Nanjing Lukou has
  a good figure and loses only the pack share: 0.61 of published passengers, about like Frankfurt, Heathrow or CDG.
- Stations: not attractions. `attractions_json.py` excludes them by class. One leak in the world file (Moscow
  Kiyevsky) and it is not in China. No station anywhere generates trips of its own.

## 1. How an airport gets its trips

`pipeline/tier0.py` `airports()` (line 1369) for every world-run zone, US zones included; `enrich_airports.py` for the
hand-built packs (the 13 US cities on FAA, Ontario on StatCan).

    daily outings = boardings x O&D share x pack share / 366        legs a day = 2 x outings

- Sites: OurAirports `scheduled_service=yes` inside the zone's boundary (`airports_in`).
- Boardings: `cfg["boardings"]` if the zone has one, else Wikidata P3872 / 2, the newest dated statement
  (`wikidata_boardings`, `ORDER BY DESC(?t) LIMIT 1`, cached in `data/work/wikidata_boardings.json`). In China only
  Shanghai has a config figure (PVG, SHA: CAAC 2024 via the press, `build_city.py:332`).
- O&D share: 1 unless configured (PVG 0.85, ICN 0.85, NRT 0.8, CDG 0.75, LHR 0.7, Pearson 0.75; judgement). There is no China factor.
- Pack share: GHSL people within 300 km weighted exp(-d / 20 km), the part inside the zone's boundary.
- The kernel (`everyday.rs` kind 4, `RATE` 1.0) spreads those outings over the pack's residents and jobs, 20 km decay,
  normalised per airport. The 150 m exclusion and 40-80 km taper keep 92-100% (measured below). Trips do not cross
  zone lines (docs/zones.md section 4a: "Airports and attractions keep each site's own total and do not cross"), so
  the dropped share appears nowhere.
- The legs at the airport are this purpose. The market's long trips do not place anyone at an airport.

Coverage is fine: every OurAirports CN large or medium airport with scheduled service is in an audit except Yueyang
(YYA); 4 are skipped for no figure (Bengbu, Leshan, two small). No join or code mismatch found.

### Why China is low

1. **Stale figures.** 213 of 258 airports use a 2022 figure, when China's air travel was well under half of normal.
   The big ones on 2024-25 figures are fine; the second tier is 2 to 3.6 times too low: Xi'an 13.6M against 48.5M,
   Zhengzhou 9.2M against 29.2M, Wuhan 11.6M against 31.3M, Chongqing 21.7M against 50.1M, Xiamen 10.1M against 29.2M,
   Tianjin 5.8M against 19.9M. China's figures add to 1,058M passengers both ways against CAAC's 1,529M for 2025.
   Wikidata's latest figure wins however old; nothing treats the pandemic years specially (the attractions file does:
   `PANDEMIC` in `attractions_json.py`).
2. **Pack share.** 57 Chinese airports have a share under 0.5 (80M boardings), because a Chinese zone can be a county
   holding the airport while the city is another zone: Wuhan 0.10 (Huangpi), Hefei 0.09 (Feixi), Shijiazhuang 0.17,
   Changzhou 0.19, Huizhou 0.12, Zhengzhou 0.26, Tianfu 0.27, Zhuhai 0.30. Boardings-weighted share: China 0.69, against
   the US 0.94, Japan 0.91, Korea 0.93, France 0.88, Spain 0.94; Germany 0.60, Italy 0.78, India 0.75, the UK 0.75.
   So this is world-wide: Istanbul 0.04, Bengaluru 0.09, Munich 0.11, Dusseldorf 0.28, Luton 0.12.

Together: 1.95M legs a day (about 1.9M after the taper) against 4.19M passengers a day, about 0.45 nationwide.

### Smaller faults found

- **Hong Kong's figure is a month's**: HKG boardings 2.55M dated 2024-12-01 (5.1M, one month; the year is about 53M),
  with pack share 0.42. Any figure with a month's date is suspect.
- **Beijing Capital's 52.9M** is labelled 2024 but looks like the 2023 total, from memory of that year (2025 published 70.7M).
- **Shijiazhuang (goe, the Gaocheng zone)**: audit 2,615 outings, pack `airport_trips` 0. The airport's nearest cell is
  1,423 m away and the limit is 1 km (`SNAP_M`), so `tier0.airports` (line 1405) drops it silently. Only two zones in the
  world lose more than a tenth of over 200 outings a day: this and Hinds County, Mississippi (`hit`, 0.87).
- Other countries carry the same staleness: Brazil 103 airports at 2022, India 91, France 44, Colombia 42, Greece 39.

## 2. Railway stations

**Not attractions.** `attractions_json.py` line 85 lists `Q55488` railway station and `Q928830` metro station under
`NOT` ("Never an attraction... places people pass through"). The check compares an item's direct P31 classes only.

- The world file has 13,304 rows. By class: 2 with a station-like class. One is a museum in an old station building
  (Museum of the City of Skopje, `Q1339195` station building, right to keep). The other is **Moscow Kiyevsky railway
  station** (Q666886): 874,531 visits a year by the budget, 2,396 a day, 4,800 legs a day. It came in as a historic site
  (`Q1081138`) and its other class is `Q55485`, dead-end railway station, a subclass of `Q55488`, which the direct check
  misses. A name search found no other station. Two other items with `Q55485` were in the raw query and did not reach the file.
- No Chinese station is in the file. Within 1.5 km of Nanjing South, Shanghai Hongqiao and Nanjing main: nothing;
  of Beijing South: Yongdingmen (a real gate, 1.8M).
- No OSM or pipeline rule treats a station as a destination: `train_station` appears nowhere in `pipeline/`; OSM stations are
  read for the gate's checks and the real-network build only.

Removing Kiyevsky changes that one Moscow zone by 4,800 legs a day. Nothing changes in China.

What the three stations hold in the packs, as ordinary local destinations (commute jobs from the general proxy: GHSL
non-residential volume, OSM job places, population):

| Station | Jobs within 500 m | Errand jobs | Leisure jobs | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nanjing South (nan) | 5,023 | 2,075 | 648 | nearest cell's jobs rank 5,622 of 16,754 |
| Beijing South (bei) | 8,744 | 1,857 | 624 | rank 6,696 of 34,765 |
| Shanghai Hongqiao (sha) | 8,606 | 1,095 | 730 | one cell 6,132 jobs, rank 138 of 36,007 |

Published: Hongqiao station "transported 155 million passengers in 2024" (Shanghai government, about 425,000 a day; it does
not say whether arrivals count, but Shanghai's whole outbound rail figure for 2024 is 259M, so it reads as departures). I
found no trustworthy annual figure for Nanjing South (10M+ in the 2023 summer season) or Beijing South (only capacity
figures and unsourced numbers).

**"Stations are low" in the intercity sense: yes, and it is by design.** A station has no trips of its own. Long trips are
the market's, zone to zone (docs/regional.md 5.1-5.2), by place; rail takes its share only on lines the player builds, and
those riders then walk or ride to the player's stations. A map of real-world stations in a new game shows the ordinary
jobs and homes around them, not the 425,000 a day. The bubble at Hongqiao is a few thousand jobs. Rail passengers
are in the model; they reach a station only as an output of what the player built, and I did not run a network to compare.

Market size, for scale (departures a day, all modes, `market-rows.json`): Shanghai 1.41M, Beijing 1.14M, Nanjing 459,000.
Per resident a year: China about 20, Tokyo 32, London 34, Paris 35, New York 43. Shanghai's real outbound rail is 259M a
year, 710,000 a day, about half of the market's 1.41M: enough room for the real rail flows, so the market is not obviously
short. Not a like-for-like test (the market's trips start at a shorter distance, and I ran no network).

## 3. Recommendations (nothing done; demand changes go to Anita first)

1. **Replace Chinese airport figures with CAAC's 2025 bulletin** (small, data only). The `boardings` config already exists
   (`tier0.py:1392`, used for Shanghai). Needs a table of 270 airports by IATA code: the CAAC PDF, or the Wikipedia table above,
   which reproduces it (the data register lists this as N, T-897). Rerun the airports step for 219 China units. Effect: the
   national figure goes from 1,058M to 1,529M; second-tier airports up 2 to 3.5 times. No new rule.
2. **Stop trusting Wikidata's newest statement anywhere** (small, the general version of 1): ask for every dated statement
   with a year precision, prefer a year outside 2020-2022 (as `PANDEMIC` does for attractions), and reject month-dated
   figures. Fixes Hong Kong, and helps Brazil, India, Colombia, Greece, France. Needs a re-query, then the airports step
   for those units.
3. **Let airport trips cross zone lines** (medium, a real demand change, needs her). Add airports to `cross_everyday` as
   T-1175 did for errands, so the people in the next zone reach the airport through the regional layer and the pack share goes.
   Biggest effect on Wuhan, Hefei, Zhengzhou, Tianfu, Istanbul, Munich, Bengaluru, Dusseldorf. Rerun `cross_everyday` and
   `market_zones` for affected units. I would not simply set pack share to 1: all of Wuhan's passengers would then come from
   the people of a county zone.
4. **Shijiazhuang's lost airport** (trivial): snap to the nearest cell with a wider limit (it is 1,423 m off), and warn when
   an airport places nothing.
5. **Stations as attractions**: nothing to undo in China. Test `NOT` against subclasses as well, so `Q55485` and its like
   count as `Q55488`, the next time the attractions file is rebuilt; it removes one Moscow row.
6. **Stations "low"**: no change proposed. Giving stations their own trips would double-count the market's riders and go against
   rail being what the player builds. If the complaint is about what the station looks like on the map, that is a display
   question.
