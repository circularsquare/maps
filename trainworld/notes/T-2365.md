## The kernel query (built, in the tree)

- **Interface.** `DemandApi.trip_lengths_day(day, mask)` and `EverydayApi.trip_lengths_day(day, mask)`
  (WASM, `sim/src/demand/lengths.rs` with the bins and the spread; the commute pass adds
  `ModeOut::len`, `Period::len`). Each answers its solved periods in `mask`, the day's load factors
  applied per purpose: eight blocks of 58 bins, trips, rail, walking, no route, then the km of each;
  driving is trips less the rest. `trip_length_edges()` gives the bins' upper edges. Worker query
  `tripLengths` (demandProtocol, demand.worker), client `DemandClient.tripLengths(zone?)` (cached
  per zone, solve, round and day; a zone open bare asks the aux worker), type and assembly in
  `game/tripLengths.ts`; `twDemand.tripLengths(id)` under `?debug=1`. Nothing in the UI calls it yet.
- **Bins.** 0.25 km to 3 km, 0.5 km to 10, 1 km to 20, 2.5 km to 50, then 60, 70, 80, 90, 100,
  125, 150, 200, 300 km and over: smooth enough for a curve on a log or linear axis.
- **How lengths are counted.** Commutes are counted between 2 km zones, so their lengths come in
  steps (1.04 km within a zone, 2 km to the next...). Each zone pair's trips are spread over the
  bins as the distances between points spread evenly over the two squares (a table made once per
  worker), out to 24 km; past that, the centres' distance. The zone's own pair keeps the kernel's
  mean (0.52 of its side). The trips, walking with no rail and no-route trips are summed once per
  zone on first ask, the rail each pass. Everyday outings take each sample's own cell-to-cell
  distance; their rail goes by its subzone pair's mean distance. The lie: a curve smoother than the
  model's own zone steps, and an everyday pair's rail at its mean length (pairs span a km or two).
- **Exact sums.** On the New York (T-007) and London saves, average day, every zone with a view:
  each mode's bins add up to the zone's split within float rounding (trips 0, rail under 3.2e-7,
  walking under 3.5e-8, driving under 2.8e-6 relative). No route: New York 9,099 against 9,203 a day
  on the average day, because the split scales no-route trips by all trips together while the bins
  scale them per purpose (exact on a weekday). No bin's driving goes below 0 (smallest share 0.5%).
  The zone pairs' sum before scaling matches the gravity's total to 3e-9 (New York 6,407 zones, 293
  ms native; London 82 ms), mean commute 18.7 km against the gravity's 18.9 (London 10.5 against
  10.2; the gravity's mean is between centres): `t2365_commute_lengths_of_real_packs` (ignored).
  Rust tests: `trip_lengths_add_up_to_the_summary` in api/tests.rs and everyday/tests.rs (rounds, the
  capacity cap, an estimated period, three kinds of day).
- **Cost** (page, two demand workers, after the final): the first ask of a zone works out the
  commutes' table, New York 0.4 s of work (1.9 s round trip while the workers were still finishing),
  London 0.37 s, small zones under 0.1 s; every ask after, about 36 ms for New York or London, 1 ms
  for small zones. Memory: 58 x 6 numbers per period and population, the spread table under 1 MB a
  worker. Stage 2 could work the table out at a zone's warm-up, so the first ask is quick too.
- **Next to T-2364's numbers.** New York's km share comes out 26.2% (T-2364 26.5%), London 37.2%
  (37.6%). The bands lean shorter than T-2364's: New York under 1 km 8.0M against 4.8M, over 20 km
  6.9M against 9.8M. T-2364 measured from a group of cells' centre to the far cells, which blurs
  short trips outward; these are the model's own lengths.
- **Other cities** is not in the kernel: the corridors are on the page already. The mockups take
  the city view's own To other places figure (corridors to another place: New York 2.82M, London
  2.28M), its plane part split out. T-2364's 5.2M counted the place's own corridor too, which the
  city view leaves out.

## My view

- **The log graph reads best.** At about 245 px wide, a linear axis to 50 km puts all of walking and
  the 1-2 km trips in the first 10 px: a spike, then a long flat tail where the train's long trips
  are a blue sliver you cannot judge. Linear down the side gives the short trips more room in a
  tall panel, but past 15 km everything is a hairline, and the train's best band (New York 40%,
  London 50% at 10-20 km) does not show. On the log axis the three humps read at once: walking's
  peak at 1-2 km, driving's at 2-5, the train's plateau from 5 to 30 km.
- **The share strip helps**, and I would keep it: the areas show how many trips, the strip shows
  what share of each length the train takes, which is the "where can I still win" question behind
  "50% feels unattainable". Without it the train's share far out is hard to read off any area.
- **The bars scaled by trips** are the plainest to read exact numbers from and need no axis; they
  lose the shape within a band. A fair fallback if the graph feels too busy.
- **Hover on the graph** (stage 2): the bin under the pointer, as the bars' row hovers had it
  ("Trips of 5-5.5 km per day in New York: Train ...").
- **Top bar hover:** the small bars and a two-row table replace the sentence; it needs the hover
  tag to draw a strip with weighted cells (it draws equal cells today, `tipStrip`, T-1926) and a
  small grid, a modest change to `ui/tip.ts`.
- **The label.** The game says "Within New York"; T-2364's mockup shortened it to "Within", which is
  what read unclear. I'd write "Trips within New York": it says what is counted and where, both
  ends inside the city. "Intracity" is a planner's word, and "All trips" would wrongly take in the
  trips to other places listed under it.

- **Polish, looked up**: the dataviz skill's mark specs (a 2 px "surface gap" between touching fills, hairline
  recessive grid), a thin white stroke on each stacked layer's top kept apart from its fill
  (https://community.plotly.com/t/how-to-edit-lines-on-a-plotly-stacked-area-chart/80798), round joins against
  seams, and the biggest, steadiest layer at the bottom with few layers
  (https://designsystem.maersk.com/guidelines/data-visualisation/chart-types/stacked-area-chart/). Built: soft
  fills, a gap in the panel's colour and a line in the layer's own colour along each top, faint vertical lines at
  the labels, smoothing that also hides the zone-step artefacts. No gradient: the train is the bottom layer and
  would fade most.
- **Check** `checks/t2365-trip-graph.mjs`: New York, new game and the real save. Bins against the split and To
  other places within 0.1% (both exact to the printed 1k); each layer's drawn area against its bins within 1%
  (driving 39.124M against 39.133M: No route's 9k is its own layer); New York's 3,389 corridors all have a distance.
- **New York, real save**: 2.83M trips to other places, planes 532k; the 100-200 km car bump (Philadelphia, New
  Haven, Hartford) shows; flights are about 1% of the plot's height and show in the share strip.
