# T-509: demand calibration, stage 1 (design)

Design only, 2026-10-16. No parameter changed. For Anita's review: her decisions are collected
in section 6.

**Calibration** here means fitting a short list of worldwide demand parameters jointly on several
cities that have a real rail network and measured ridership, then writing the fitted values as
rules in docs/demand-worldwide.md. No per-city tuning: a parameter that wants very different
values in different cities is a finding (a missing rule or a country knob), not something to fit
city by city.

## 1. Cities and targets

have data on disk from Anita's riders projects.

Definitions to match (each comparison states its own):

- **Entries** are first boardings from the street. The model has no bus, so a station fed by buses
  (Times Square, the Seoul transfer hubs) and a commuter rail terminal whose riders change to the
  metro have entries the model cannot make. The station objective drops hubs where measured
  bus-to-rail or rail-to-rail transfers dominate, or compares them separately.
- **Operator or line totals** count trips using a line (boardings less changes within it), the way
  operators count (T-269; T-171 showed the old count put PATH at a quarter of its real figure).
- **Days**: every target is an average weekday (Tue-Thu where the source allows). Annual figures
  become weekdays by the operator's own weekday ratio where published, else one documented factor.
- **Years**: post-COVID targets where they exist (NYC 2024, London 2024, Seoul 2023). Tokyo's 2015
  census and FY2019 counts are pre-COVID; the packs already carry 2024 telework, so Tokyo's
  levels are scaled by each operator's FY2019 to FY2024 ridership ratio (MLIT rail statistics)
  and Tokyo weighs on shape (which stations, which lines) more than level.
- **Mode share** is rail's share of commuters who travel, by home area. The model's other mode
  includes the bus, so "rail" never includes bus.
- **All purposes vs commutes**: entries and line totals are all purposes, so they test the
  commute and everyday layers together; census mode shares test commutes alone. That pair is what
  separates the everyday layer's rail level (T-272) from the commute's.

## 2. Real networks

One converter for every city: each source becomes a list of **trips** (operator, route, stops
with coordinates, times), then realnet.py's existing logic makes lines (stopping patterns over
15% or 5% of a route's trips, short turns folded in), trains per hour per level (high = 7-9 and
17-19, medium = 10-16, low = 0-6) and run times (median per hop and level). Today that logic is
GTFS-only and New York-only (feed list, MTA complexes, city boundary in the code). The change:

- `realnet.py net <city>` reads a small per-city config: sources, the pack, the count point for
  trains per hour (the busiest central station), the station grouping rule.
- **Adapters** to trips: GTFS (New York, Paris, Toronto), TfL API cache (London, londonriders'
  `fetch_schedules.py` output), NR CIF (London's National Rail, londonriders' `cif.py`), the
  seoulriders timetable, OSM route relations (Tokyo, Sao Paulo; run times from distance at a speed
  per kind of line, checked against a few timetables).
- **Stations** grouped the way the target counts them (MTA complexes, NUMBAT stations, Seoul
  complexes, S12 group codes, GTFS parent stations), with a join table from network stations to
  target stations (name and distance, overrides listed). Stations outside the pack are dropped,
  lines cut at the boundary, as now.
- The game save (`realsave`, `gamesave`) is optional per city: useful for Anita to look at, not
  needed to fit. About an hour more each.

## 3. Parameters

Three blocks, fitted in order, each on the targets that identify it. Everything else stays fixed.

**Block A: how many trips and where to (no network needed).** Fitted directly on surveys and
census flows, so cheap and outside the rail loop.

| Parameter | Now | Identified by |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday rates by purpose (T-450): errands 0.3, social 0.2 a resident (`everyday.rs` RATE) | New York's | survey trip rates by purpose: NHTS, NTS, EGT, PT 2018, OD 2017 |
| Everyday destination decay lengths: errands 3 km, social 6 km (`DECAY`) | judgement | survey trip lengths by purpose, same surveys |
| Commute destination term (T-438): big job centres drawing from further | none | census flows by destination size (LODES, Japan table 4, WU01EW, MOBPRO) |

**Block B: mode choice (needs the real networks).**

| Parameter | Now | Identified by |
|---|---|---|
| Walk share curve, `walk_d0_km` 1.7 / `walk_s_km` 0.4 | judgement (T-026) | census walk share by area (Manhattan, central London, Paris) |
| Walk weight to stations, `w_walk` 1.5 | fitted on NYC (T-371) | entries by station against distance from dense housing; mode shares near and far from stations |
| Drive weight `w_drive` 1.33 and parking `park_paid_min` 15, `park_free_d` 8,000 | NYC (T-090, T-380) | suburban vs central rail shares (NYC, London, Paris differ most here) |
| Ride to a station, `p_ride` 15 | NYC (T-372) | commuter rail entries at outer stations; LIRR, MNR, NR suburban totals (T-271) |
| Logit scale `beta` 0.05 and damping from `damp_min` 60 (T-495, T-502) | literature, not fitted | rail share by trip length; `damp_min` stays fixed unless long-trip targets join (T-437) |
| Rail propensity `asc_rail` 0 | 0 since T-078 | overall rail level per city; see the car note below |
| Waiting and changing, `w_wait` 1.37, `transfer_min` 5 | literature | fixed; too entangled with the others to fit on counts |

**Block C: route choice and crowding.**

| Parameter | Now | Identified by |
|---|---|---|
| Crowding penalty `crowd_alpha` 2 (from seats) | literature, T-216 open | split between parallel lines and express/local: Seoul line 9 (published split), NYC Lexington vs others, London Central vs Elizabeth; peak link loads against NUMBAT and congestion |
| Station choice spread `station_theta` 0.2 | T-020 | entries at neighbouring stations; fixed unless the fit shows a pattern |

**Fixed**: road speeds (fitted on ACS car times), walk speed, period shares and timing (T-058's
job), weekend factors, border factor (T-273), market parameters (T-429, T-437 and T-438's long
trips belong to a separate corridor fit on Eurostar, NEC and Shinkansen counts, after this one).

**The car note.** The driving alternative assumes everyone can drive (demand-worldwide.md
"Driving alternative"). One worldwide `asc_rail` then has to explain both New York's suburbs and
Tokyo, and the fit will split the difference: Tokyo and Seoul under, New York over. Two ways out:
(a) fit `asc_rail` alone and accept the residual; (b) add one structural input before fitting, a
share of people with no car to drive, from a general source (vehicles per 1,000 people by country
from the World Bank or OICA; car-free households by small area where the census has it: ACS
B08201, England TS045, Japan's census). (b) is a rule change, so it is Anita's call (decision
6.3). My recommendation is (b), because without it the other mode-choice values would absorb a
gap they do not cause.

## 4. Method

**One run** = a full weekday on a city's real network with its pack, every in-pack layer
(commutes and everyday purposes; the regional market off), three crowding rounds, natively.
Measured today: New York's commutes alone, three rounds, 15 s on one core (`realnet`, a copy of
the current pack). With the everyday layer about 40-60 s; Tokyo perhaps 2-3 times New York. The
`zones_day` bin already solves every layer on New York's network; the calibration bin is
`zones_day` with realnet's outputs (entries, link loads, home-area rail) and T-269's operator
count, plus `--set` for any parameter (block A's rates and decays need `set_extra` names).

**Objective**, per city, normalised so each city weighs the same:

- station entries: mean squared log ratio over stations with at least 2,000 entries a day, hubs
  dominated by transfers left out or compared separately;
- line or operator totals: squared log ratio each;
- mode shares: squared difference of the logit of rail share by area, weighted by commuters;
- link loads (block C only): squared log ratio at the busiest hour.

Plus a small penalty for moving away from today's values (in units of a plausible range), so a
weakly identified parameter stays put rather than drifting.

**Solver**: coordinate search first: one parameter at a time, three to five values, all cities in
parallel on 4 cores, two sweeps. About 6-8 parameters x 4 values x 2 sweeps = 50-60
evaluations, each 2-3 minutes wall with Tokyo bounding it: 2-3 hours of machine. It shows each
parameter's curve per city, which is what tells a worldwide value from a country knob. A
Nelder-Mead polish over the 3-4 most sensitive parameters only if the sweeps leave clear gains
(100-150 evaluations, 4-6 hours, overnight).

**Against overfitting**:

- Toronto (and Sao Paulo if built) never enters the fit; the final values are checked there once.
- Leave one city out: refit with each training city dropped. A parameter that moves by more than
  its plausible range is reported as unstable, not shipped.
- Few parameters (8 at most in block B and C) against hundreds of targets per city; but the
  real information is a few levels per city, so the penalty term and fixed blocks matter more
  than the count.
- Crowding rounds are not fully converged (T-434), so a run is slightly rough in its parameters;
  steps are kept larger than that roughness (checked by repeating one evaluation at a nearby value).

**Becoming rules**: each fitted value goes into its entry in docs/demand-worldwide.md (the value,
the cities fitted on, each city's residual, the hold-out result), and its sim default changes in
one task with the usual checks (smoke, the real save's totals, rail per zone before and after in
every pack). A parameter whose per-city optimum differs widely becomes a listed country knob with
the evidence, for Anita to decide. A DECISIONS line records the fit.

## 6. Decisions for Anita

1. **The cities**: New York, London, Paris, Seoul, Tokyo to fit; Toronto held out; Sao Paulo as an
   optional second hold-out. Swap or drop any?
2. **Tokyo's frequencies**: Tokyo has no open GTFS for JR East or most private lines. (a) OSM
   relations' `interval` tags where present, else a default by kind of line (rough, general); (b)
   the ODPT open data (Tokyo Metro, Toei, some JR; needs a free developer account in her name).
   I'd start with (a) and fit Tokyo more on shape than level.
3. **Car availability** before fitting (section 3, the car note): (a) fit one rail constant and
   accept the residual, or (b) add a no-car share from vehicles per 1,000 by country and census
   car-free households where available. I recommend (b).
4. **Everyday rates** (T-450): one worldwide set fitted on all surveys, or a country's own survey
   where it has one with the worldwide set as default. Her rule (general methods) suggests the
   first; T-450 asks for the second for Japan.
5. **Which layers**: the fit covers commutes and everyday trips inside a pack. The regional market
   (T-429, T-437, T-438's long trips) is a later corridor fit. Agree?
