## What was built (agent split)

- `terminalBays.ts`: `splitsAt` (whether single track splits at a station: two-way, no crossover, not
  tram, not joining one track of a double there), `singleSplitEnds` (per single edge its `SplitEnd`s:
  two tracks for p m from an end, easing to one over `lead`; carried on through plain nodes along
  two-way single track; a split that reaches double track within its ease is two tracks all the way
  (p = Infinity) and that plain node is in `full`; one that reaches a junction, track end or other
  station first has its lead cut to the room there), `splitValue` (smoothstep ease, as T-3202's).
  `terminalEnds` gives two places only where `splitsAt` holds.
- `network.ts`: `splitPoints` (a single edge's points with a cut at each platform end and every ~10 m
  of its ease, cuts placed as shares of `lengthM` so they fall where the train samples do); the edge
  cache key has the splits (`EdgeEntry.split`); `taperAt` skips split stations and `full` nodes;
  station flag 16 also for a split station; `singleSplits` and its `LineSamples.splits` are gone, so
  `sampleLane` comes from `laneAlong` on every edge (one source for track, lines and trains).
- Trams: tram track is never single (docs/track-and-service.md), so no tram rule was needed; a tram
  stop on its double tram track is unchanged (pixel-identical before and after).

## Follow-up: no thickening zoomed out (2026-11-12)

Anita, on 0503b008: "the line seems to thicken substantially over a long stretch before we actually
enter the station ... when zoomed out we should have the whole thing just look like a single track,
and be thin". Two causes, both in the stroke shader's width blend by lane (`lfm`, the larger of a
segment's two end lanes):

- Zoomed out (below 15) the strokes are drawn simplified (strokeLod, T-1639), and a merged run keeps
  its first and last lanes: on the test line A - M - B the simplified line was two segments, each
  lane 255 at both ends, so the whole stretch between stations drew at double track's width (zoom
  13: 7 px against 3 px for the plain ends; zoom 14.5: 9 px by M against 5).
- Between where double track starts growing and where it parts (4 m = 4-7 px, about zoom 16-16.7),
  double track is a solid wider band; a split did the same with no tracks to see.

Fix: line and track strokes on a split edge carry `F_SPLIT` (8192); the shader takes a split's lane
times the parting (`lfw = lfm * t`) for the width blend, the growth and the band under both tracks,
gives it single track's track widths, and parts its two tracks by `t` too, so it opens out of single
track's width once the tracks part and is single-track thin before. Fully parted (zoom ~17 up) it
draws as before. The turn-back crossover at a split dead end, while the tracks are part way apart,
meets double track's offsets, not the split's (grey, close in only; at full parting they agree).

## The proposal (agent meet, kept for the reasoning)

Anita 2026-11-11: "stations on single track lines dont really look like they have 2 tracks, but
conceptually they do / they act as passing points. we should draw single tracks as like splitting
into a station so it looks correct".

## Where it starts from

T-3202 already draws the *trains* this way: at a station on single track each train stands on its own
side, as on double track, from where double track's tracks part on screen (zoom ~16.7 in New York).
The lane rises from the middle to its side within 60 m of the platform (`SINGLE_SPLIT_LEAD_M`) and is 1
along the platform, a dead end's platform running in from the end (`network.ts` `singleSplits`). A
line's end there has two places (`terminalBays.ts`, `single`). Only the track and the station still
look single, so close in the trains stand half beside the one drawn track (the DECISIONS lie, T-3202).

## Proposal

Draw the track the same way the trains already are, from the same numbers:

1. **Track and line strokes.** A single-track edge that ends at a station gets lane values like double
   track's taper (T-658) run backwards: 0 (one track) out on the edge, easing to 255 (two tracks)
   over the 60 m before the platform, 255 along the platform (half of it from a through station's
   node, all of it in from a dead end). Points added every ~8 m on the lead so the ease is smooth.
   The stroke shader already draws any lane value between 0 and 255 (grow, then part), and line
   strokes take the edge's drawn lanes, so lines split with their track. Two new `lanes` bits in the
   edge cache key (split at a, split at b), as the taper bits are.
2. **Train lanes from the drawing.** Then `sampleLane` comes from the edge's drawn lanes (`laneAlong`)
   as it does for double track, and `singleSplits` goes: one source for track and trains, so they
   can't drift apart.
3. **The station.** Set station flag 16 (double-track station: a platform each side close in, T-658c)
   for a station on single track, so its riders stand on the two platforms either side of the two
   tracks, as at a double-track station.
4. **Where it doesn't split:** one-way single track (no trains meet there; drawn as now), buses, and
   tram stops in the street (to check: a split in a street-running tram stop may look wrong; leave
   them single at first). Zoomed out nothing changes: the split only shows once double track would
   part.
5. **Joins.** `taperAt` must not taper double track down at a station whose single track splits (the
   station is two tracks there already). Two stations closer than their platforms and leads stay
   split all the way between them (drawn as double track for that stretch), which reads fine.
6. **The dead end's crossover.** The mock shows the turn-back scissors (T-658d) at a split dead end,
   just where the two tracks join; T-3202's crossover for a train going to the other side is 60 m past
   the join, so the two line up. Keep it.

No sim change, no figure moves: drawing only, like T-3202.
