# T-054 home end weighted by resident job holders

Done 2026-10-09 by the demand realism agent. The gravity's home end was population x (1 - the
tract's work-from-home share) (T-042), so every child, student and retiree counted as a would-be
commuter. It is now **LODES RAC S000 JT00 2023 (jobs by the block their holder lives in) x (1 -
the tract's work-from-home share)**: the home end of the very jobs WAC counts at work. Code:
`pipeline/build_city.py` (`residents_for_state`, the `rac` column), `pipeline/wfh.py`
(`commuters`). Pack rebuilt (format unchanged; `commute_home` holds the new weights, `pop` is
still population). Build log: `data/work/build_t054.log`.

## Why RAC rather than ACS workers by tract

RAC is per block (ACS B08301 is per tract and would be spread over a tract's blocks by
population), it comes from the same administrative records as the WAC work end and the OD the
decay is fitted to, and it counts jobs, as WAC does (someone with two jobs makes two). It leaves out
the self-employed, as WAC does. Checked against ACS commuters (workers less work from home) by
county: every county within 10%; Queens -10%, Fairfield and Westchester -9 to -10% (the
self-employed and people working outside the region), the Bronx +5%. With population as the
weight the Bronx was 23% over and Kings 7% over.

| county | ACS commuters | home end before (population) | after (RAC) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kings | 1,007k | 1,073k | 1,027k |
| Queens | 975k | 989k | 880k |
| New York | 667k | 610k | 615k |
| Bronx | 511k | 630k | 538k |
| Suffolk | 686k | 628k | 700k |
| Nassau | 588k | 555k | 613k |
| Bergen | 406k | 369k | 402k |

(The "before" column is the pack's home end scaled to the work-end total, notes/T-042.md.)

## Refit of the decay (T-006's method, `pipeline/fit_decay.py`)

| decay | mean km | misplaced by length | county pairs log r | county share misplaced | own county (LODES 42.8%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| d^-1 exp(-d/29), before T-054 | 18.4 | 2.9% | 0.881 | 15.5% | 39.0% |
| **d^-1 exp(-d/29), after** | 18.7 | 3.2% | 0.886 | 15.1% | 39.3% |
| best after: d^-0.94 exp(-d/24) | 17.8 | 3.1% | 0.879 | 14.9% | 40.3% |

The optimum moved along the same ridge (T-006 noted it) by less than the ridge's own spread: 0.2
points of the objective. **The decay stays d^-1 exp(-d / 29 km)**, which spares a churn of every
constant fitted on top of it. Plot of the full search: `T-054-decay.png`.

Own-county commuting is still 39% against 43%: the home weights were not the cause. What is left
is likely geography the crow-fly distance cannot see (the Hudson and East River make Manhattan
far from New Jersey and Long Island in time, not in km), and the counties' own labour markets.
Not chased: it is a 4-point gap, and a time-based decay needs a road network.

## Effect on the real-network comparison (notes/T-007.md, "Comparison log")

Rail 26.8% -> 26.7% of commutes; subway AM station entries log correlation 0.66 -> 0.73 (homes now
sit where commuters live); suburban counties a little further over (their home end grew: Nassau
+10%, Suffolk +12%), weighted county error 6.2 -> 6.7 points. Raw files: the OD files were
fetched again for the fit and deleted; the RAC files (`data/raw/*_rac_S000_JT00_2023.csv.gz`,
14 MB) stay, every rebuild reads them.
