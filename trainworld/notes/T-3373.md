# T-3373: demand workers share one copy of the zone data (stage A, 2026-11-16, agent memA)

Stage A as Anita chose it: fewer solving workers on big saves, capped by memory (reverses T-3072's "a reported 8 GB
keeps its workers"). Stage B (zones and the market dealt between workers, T-1560) is next; the CPU speedups of PR 12
are T-3473, after this by her 2026-11-16 call.

## The rule (as built)

`workers/demandClient.ts`: the solving workers together may take `SOLVING_SHARE` = 17.5% of `memBudget` (1.4 GB
where Chrome reports 8 GB; 1.05 GB where memory is not reported), each counted as its need plus 0.15 GB of JS
(`MEM_WORKER`, was 0.7 GB, a T-1205 figure from before T-2122 and T-2485; T-3472 measured 110-150 MB). Checked:

- at a save's load (`stationsAhead` -> `capAhead`), once its stations' reach is known and before its packs open,
  from `memoryCheck.memoryNeed`'s estimate of a worker's WASM for those zones (New York 0.38 GB, all-China 1.29);
- as each zone opens in a solving worker while no solve is out (`capPool` from the `opened` message);
- before each solve (`capPool`, as before, which also keeps the old whole-budget test).

`?demandWorkers=N` and Settings' "Use more cores" skip the share (the player or check asked for a count or for
speed). `?demandWorkers=auto` is the page's own rule in a check whose core plan would add a number.
`workersFor(perWorker)` gives the same count for the estimates: the saves card (`memoryEstimate.estimateGB`, each
extra worker 0.2 GB + 0.2 GB per 1,000 stations) and the load refusal (`memoryCheck.memoryNeed`).

What runs where (reported 8 GB, 12+ threads): small cities (WASM about 250 MB) keep 3; New York, London, Paris,
Berlin, Tokyo (380-545 MB) get 2; all of China 1. Where memory is not reported (desktop Firefox, Safari; already at
most 2), New York-sized saves get 1.

- **Saved**: NYC -0.5 GB settled, -0.6 GB at the peak; all-China -2.9 GB settled and at the peak.
- **Cost**: loads slower. Timings on this machine swung by 20 s between identical runs (other agents' work):
  NYC about +15 s (50 to 65), all-China +30 to +50 s (92 to 124-142). An edit's solve on NYC deals 5 periods

- **Figures**: identical to the rider in every run (rail trips 12,368,756 NYC, 34,535,853 all-China; every

- The load-time estimate matters for the peak: cutting only as zones opened (first build) left all-China's peak at
  5.7 GB, since three workers had opened a third of China by then.
