# T-1810: the clock worker's transfers step after a demand solve (2026-10-29, agent t1810)

Measuring: `checks/old/t1810-transfers.mjs [url] [save]` (headless, one demand worker): loads the save,
then ROUNDS times sets the busiest line's trains up by two (then back), and moves the track tool's
ghost every frame until SETTLE ms after the solve's transfers reach the clock worker; per round the
preview asks' ask-to-answer ms and a hash of each drawn hour's last boarding slice.

## What the step did

`transfers` was `set_transfers` (1-2 ms: 11-12k platform pairs into a hash map) and then
`sendTrips`: the trips and the boarding account's slice for this hour and the next
(`Boarding::hour`, ~15-25 ms each on shared New York, its pulses about half). Nothing per pair
was slow. The 450-560 ms came from the order of messages: a solve sends each kind of day's riders
(`riders`, three messages), which redo the account three lines a step between other messages,
and the transfers usually arrive while those steps are under way. `hour()` brings the account up
to date first (`ensure`), so the transfers' `sendTrips` worked out every line still left in one
go, and then the stepping chain sent the same hours again when it ended. A solve's view also
often brings the same transfers twice.

## Fix (clock.worker.ts only; no Rust, no WASM interface change)

1. Transfers that arrive while the account still has lines to redo (`boarding_step(0)`) only
   set; the step that finishes sends the hours.
2. Transfers equal to the last ones set (every float) are skipped; nothing drawn changes.
3. After riders or transfers, `sendTrips(true)` sends the next hour's slice a task after this
   hour's, so a preview waits for one slice only; dropped when trips are sent again, or riders
   come first (their last step sends both). Edits and the hourly tick still send both at once.

where before the whole block finished first).

## T-1814: Tokyo's transfers (2026-10-29, agent t1814)

No game bug. T-1810's check opened a New York page (`?new=1`, the default zone) and imported
`tyo.save` into it, so the zone in view (`activeCity`) stayed `nyc`. Demand's `transfers` question goes to
the zone in view, and a worker with no part of the solve in that zone answers mask 0 / no pairs;
`platformTransfersAt` then returns null. A real Tokyo start (start menu, sandbox, Tokyo, Real
network; or `?new=1&city=tyo` and an import) has `activeCity` = `tyo`.
