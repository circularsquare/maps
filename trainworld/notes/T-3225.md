networks for cities? ... or we can work on a way to import geojson or somtehing, then they can just share. worth

Survey only; nothing in app/ or sim/ changed. Scripts and their outputs are in `notes/T-3225/` (`autonet.py`,
`world_rels.py`, `find_agents.py`, `report_<city>.txt`, `world_rels.txt`).

## 1. Today

**How a network gets in.** All offline, in `pipeline/` (docs/code-map.md "Real networks and calibration";
architecture.md "Real networks"):

1. `realnet.py net <city>`: a `CITIES` entry lists the city's sources; an adapter turns each into stations and
   timetabled trips (`gtfs`; `osm` from Overpass route relations; `trips`, a CSV any timetable can be written to;
   `n02` for Japan). Lines are stopping patterns, trains an hour per demand level, run times per hop.
2. `colours` (inside `net`): GTFS `route_color`, else the OSM `colour` tag, else noritetsu's colour.
3. `route`: every hop routed over the real-network graph (`packs/realgraph/<cc>.rgr`, built from noritetsu's OSM
   extract for all 118 regions), the track tool's router ported to Python.
4. `corridors` (`realcorridors.py`, `realshared.py`): lines on the same real track share one double track, pairs
   by need, station and junction nodes.
5. The sim's `realsave --shared` bin builds the track model, `gamesave` wraps it, `ship` copies it to
   `data/packs/real/<id>.save`. Every imported edge is `sep` (grade-separated from other imported track whatever
   its level, T-1751), and every piece sits on level -1 (T-1773, open, would take levels from tunnel and bridge
   flags).

The game only loads the finished file: the new game sheet's Real world network box, sandbox only, for the seven
cities in `REAL_NETWORKS`.

**Cities**: New York, London, Paris, Tokyo, Seoul, Toronto, Sendai (plus the all-Japan N02 stress saves `jpn`,
`jpn7`, not offered). Sources: New York, Toronto and Paris from GTFS; London from londonriders' TfL timetables and
the GB GTFS; Seoul from seoulriders' timetable; Tokyo from the 2015 transport census lines with default
frequencies; Sendai from OSM relations with a hand table of frequencies. Four of seven leaned on one of Anita's
earlier riders projects.

**What it cost.** Weighted tokens from the agents' transcripts (`checks/token_usage.py`'s weights; script
`notes/T-3225/find_agents.py`), orchestrator not counted:

At notes/city-costs.md's 9-14M weighted per percent of her weekly limit, the whole real-network feature so far
was about 4-6% of a week. **Per city**, the T-509 stage 3 agents did two cities each in 30-44 minutes for 4-5M,
so about 2-2.5M and 15-22 minutes a city, and half or more of that was the calibration targets (station counts,
census shares), which a playable network does not need. Every later pass (real track, shared track, colours) was
general and reran all seven at no per-city cost, except London, which needed a task of its own (T-1774, 5.6M).

**Adding an eighth city by hand today**: a `CITIES` entry and its sources (a GTFS city about half an hour; an OSM
city needs a hand frequency table like Sendai's), then `net`, `route`, `corridors`, `realsave --shared`,
`gamesave` (under two minutes of machine), a look in the game, and the app's `REAL_NETWORKS` list. My estimate:
**1-2 agent hours and 2-5M weighted a city** (about 0.2-0.5% of a week), more for a dense city that trips the
shared build as London did. Nobody has added an eighth yet, so the per-city hand bits in `realcorridors.py`
(`SPLITS`, W 4 St) are untested on a new city.

## 2. Automation

### What is already on disk

noritetsu's extract (`noritetsu/data/proc/<cc>/`, 118 regions, read only) keeps every rail route relation and
route master with name, ref, colour, interval, wikidata and members in order; every railway way with `tunnel`,
`bridge`, `layer`, `tracks`, `highspeed`, `usage`, `service`; and every stop node with its name and wikidata.
So an automatic build needs no Overpass fetch (which failed three times for Tokyo in T-509) and no download.

World wide in those extracts (`world_rels.txt`; one relation per direction):

| Kind | Relations | Stops in order | Colour | Interval tag | PTv2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| subway | 2,565 | 98% | 97% | 16% | 96% |
| light rail | 1,067 | 94% | 87% | 20% | 93% |
| train | 19,303 | 94% | 42% | 12% | 90% |
| tram | 5,067 | 92% | 56% | 9% | 90% |
| monorail | 216 | 85% | 57% | 6% | 78% |

254 (region, network) pairs have subway relations, in 65 regions (China 767 relations, US 225, Korea 181, Japan
143, Germany 131, India 121, Britain 99). That is close to every metro system in the world.

### The test: five hand-made cities rebuilt with no hand work

`autonet.py <cc> <city>` takes the pack's boundary, keeps route relations not tagged night, long distance, high
speed or tourism with two or more stops inside, makes one line per route master, one station per stop name within
400 m, and compares with the shipped network JSON (stations within 200 m; a hand line counts as found when 80% of
its stops lie on one automatic line). No filters by name, no frequency table, nothing by hand.

| City (hand source) | Auto lines / hand | Hand stations found | Auto stations that are real | Hand lines found | Lines with an interval tag |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sendai (OSM + hand table) | 21 / 17 | 97% | 98% | 16 of 17 | 1 of 16 |
| Toronto (GTFS) | 25 / 40 | 89% | 96% | 30 of 40 | 18 of 30 |
| Paris (GTFS) | 93 / 97 | 98% | 95% | 96 of 97 | 2 of 96 |
| London (timetables + GTFS) | 142 / 171 | 93% | 94% | 146 of 171 | 48 of 146 |
| New York (GTFS) | 74 / 111 | 85% | 95% | 76 of 111 | 35 of 76 |

What broke, and what fixes it without hand work:

- **Intercity and tourist trains come through** when not tagged: Sendai's Shinkansen and Hitachi limited
  expresses (exactly what Sendai's hand `skip_name` removes), Paris's TGV, Ouigo, TER, the Disneyland Railroad and
  the horse-drawn tram. General fixes: drop a route whose ways are mostly `highspeed=yes`, whose mean stop spacing
  is over about 15 km, or most of whose length lies outside the pack; drop `railway=preserved` and `usage=tourism`
  track.
- **Commuter rail patterns differ, not missing**: New York's LIRR and Metro-North branches and London's
  Southeastern and SWR services are mapped as fewer, longer relations than GTFS's stopping patterns, so a hand line
  matches only 20-78% of one relation. The track and stations are there; the automatic network has fewer patterns
  (an express pattern is often missing). Fine for a sandbox start.
- **Frequencies are the real gap**: interval tags on 2-60% of lines (Paris 2%, Sendai 6%, London 33%, New York
  46%, Toronto 60%), and where present they are the midday headway. The rest gets the default by kind
  (`OSM_TPH`: metro 20/10, commuter 8/4 an hour), which is far off for rural JR lines (Sendai's real 1-2) and for
  big trunk lines. GTFS where open fixes it (the Mobility Database lists 6,000+ feeds; it would find New York,
  Toronto and Paris's feeds by bounding box); elsewhere a default by kind, scaled by the line's stop spacing and
  length, is the honest guess. In a sandbox the player changes trains anyway.
- **Station complexes**: the extract keeps no `stop_area` relations, so stations are grouped by name within 400 m.
  85-98% land within 200 m of the hand station; New York is lowest because its hand stations are the MTA's
  complex centroids (Times Sq, Fulton St). Different names in one complex stay apart (as most of the hand ones did
  before the source's own grouping). Adding `stop_area` to noritetsu's extract would close it, but that is
  noritetsu's code and a re-extract.
- **Branches and shared track**: already general (`route`, `corridors`); the risk is London-style throats, where
  the shared build's holds went to 26% (T-1774). An automatic batch should check holds and fall back to the
  per-line build (each line its own track, T-1303) for a city over a threshold.
- **Depots and yards**: not drawn and not needed (the game has none); service track is outside the relations.
- **Missing relations**: cities whose lines are mapped as ways only (some of Africa, South Asia, small systems)
  get nothing; noritetsu's own line model (`ways.json`) has lines without stops there, not enough for a network.

**How close, per region** (judgement from the table above and the counts): Western Europe, North America, Japan,
Korea, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Australia: stations and lines 85-98% with no hand work; frequencies from GTFS
in North America, Europe and Australia, defaults in Japan (no open JR GTFS) and most of Asia. China: many
relations (767 subway), stop nodes present; GTFS none, so defaults. Latin America and the Middle East: metros
mapped, commuter rail patchier. Africa and South Asia outside the big metros: often no relations.

**The hand check left**, per city about 10 minutes: one screenshot at zoom 12, the lines list against the system's
Wikipedia list (could be Wikidata: line items with `part of` the system), the trimmed-lines and holds report, and
any line kept or dropped wrongly by the filters (a per-city `skip` and `keep` list, as Sendai's).

## 3. Import: what players would upload

Facts gathered by a research helper and checked where load-bearing (sources below).

| Format | Who has one | What is in it | Ease | Rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GeoJSON** | anyone exporting from Overpass turbo, geojson.io, QGIS, a city's open data, Citylines; NIMBY Rails has a built-in Export GeoJSON | LineStrings and Points with free properties; OSM exports carry `tunnel`, `bridge`, `layer` | easy: one JSON parse | 1 |
| **KML / KMZ** | fans who draw fantasy maps in Google My Maps or Google Earth (My Maps exports KML/KMZ, and a layer as CSV) | Placemarks with LineStrings and Points, folders as layers, line colour in the style; no depth | easy: XML, KMZ is a zip | 2 |
| **GTFS** | every real system with open data, 6,000+ feeds (Mobility Database) | stops, `route_color`, optional `shapes.txt`, timetables; no depth (`level_id` is a floor in a station) | medium: a large zip, frequencies counted from `stop_times` | 3 |
| OSM relation by id | OSM mappers | everything above with levels | needs a live Overpass call from the game; better done by us offline | - |
| NIMBY Rails export | NIMBY players (about 1,300 Steam reviews) | stations, lines, colours confirmed; track geometry not confirmed | medium, test on a real file first | - |
| Subway Builder save | Subway Builder players (game out July 2026, about 460 reviews) | the mod API's model has track `coords` with `startElevation`/`endElevation` in metres and route colours, but the save format is undocumented; a community mod exports KML | hard; take its KML | - |
| Metro Dreamin' | many fantasy maps on the site | no export at all (checked in its open source: lines are station id lists drawn straight) | none | - |
| Mini Metro, Cities: Skylines | many players | no geographic export of transit lines | none | - |

**"subwayworld"**: no site or app by that name turned up in two searches. Candidates: Citylines (citylines.co,
historical networks as GeoJSON), Metro Dreamin' (no export), Subway Builder. Worth asking Anita for the link.

**The GeoJSON shape the game would take** (and write, if it gets an export): a FeatureCollection; each LineString
or MultiLineString is one line (properties `name`, a colour in any of `colour`, `color`, `stroke`, the last being
geojson.io's simplestyle; optional `kind` metro/commuter/tram, `tph`); each Point is a station (`name`); optional
per feature `level` (-3 to 3), or OSM's `tunnel`, `bridge`, `layer`; stations join the line they lie within 100 m
of, in order along it. KML maps onto the same shape (Placemark name, LineStyle colour, which KML writes as
aabbggrr). An OSM-flavoured export (railway ways plus route relations from Overpass turbo) maps onto the
automatic build in section 2 instead.

## 4. Elevation

**With OSM tags** (the automatic build, and an Overpass GeoJSON import). Measured on the ways the kept lines run
over (`autonet.py`): level from `layer` where set, else -1 in a tunnel and +1 on a bridge, else 0. Crossings of
different lines' ways that share no node:

| City | Crossings | Flat if all on one level | Flat with levels from tags |
|---|---|---|---|
| Toronto | 220 | 220 | 0 |
| Sendai | 185 | 185 | 6 |
| New York | 1,098 | 1,098 | 2 |
| London | 1,741 | 1,741 | 26 |
| Paris | 1,377 | 1,377 | 42 |

So OSM's own rule (ways that cross without a shared node must differ in layer, bridge or tunnel) leaves 0-3% of
crossings flat. Crossings at a shared node are real flat crossings or junctions and stay as they are.

What still goes wrong, and the rule for each:

- **`layer` is order, not depth** (OSM wiki): layer -1 under a road is not a deep tube, and London's ways run from
  -7 to +5 while the game has -3 to +3. Rule: rank the levels met at each crossing and keep only the order, mapped
  to the game's -3..+3 (metro in tunnel -1 or -2, deep tube -3).
- **Small bridges and culverts**: 30-60% of ways carry +1 for a road bridge or a creek. The game has no roads to
  clear, and each level needs a 200 m ramp. Rule: a bridge or tunnel under about 400 m with ground both sides
  goes to ground, unless it is where another kept line crosses; long viaducts stay +1.
- **Ramps that do not fit**: two stations closer than 200 m a level at different levels. Rule: the piece takes its
  neighbour's level (T-1773's rule).
- **Stacked lines in one corridor** (W 4 St's two IND levels): the routing graph has tunnel and bridge flags but not
  `layer`; adding `layer` to `realgraph.py` (a 5-minute rebuild of data/packs/realgraph) separates them, and
  `SPLITS` in `realcorridors.py` goes away.
- **The safety net stays**: every imported edge `sep`, so a crossing the levels miss is never a flat crossing
  between imported lines (DECISIONS, T-1751). Track the player draws still crosses imported track by the usual
  rule, which is where the levels matter.

**Without tags** (KML, Metro Dreamin'-style drawings, GTFS): a level per line from its kind (metro -2, commuter
and main line 0, tram and light rail 0, monorail +1), or `level` in the file where given; then at each crossing of
two lines on one level, the line of the kind that usually goes under (metro under commuter, commuter under main
line, else the one with fewer crossings) dips one level for the crossing, with its ramps, if they fit between its
stations, else the whole hop moves; `sep` as above for what is left. Wrong where a real metro runs elevated (much
of Asia's) or a fantasy map's author meant it to; the player can rebuild a piece's level in place.

## 5. Proposal

Smallest first. Hours of agent time, checked against T-509 stage 3 (two cities with targets in 30-44 min) and
T-1305 (a stage about 1-1.5 h).

| # | What | Hours | Kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Export GeoJSON** of a game (lines with colours, stations, track with levels), so players can take a network to other tools and the import has a reference format | 1-2 | game feature, small: Anita's OK on the button |
| 2 | **Station names on the real network layer** (her TODO "bare bones"): noritetsu's station model per region as points with names from zoom 11 | 2-3 | visible, a layer setting; her OK on the look |
| 3 | **Levels from tags** (T-1773): `layer` into the routing graph, the rules in section 4 for each shared piece, the seven saves rebuilt | 2-3 | plumbing |
| 4 | **Automatic real networks from the local extract**: an `osm` adapter reading noritetsu's extract (no Overpass), the filters in section 2, frequencies from interval tags else defaults, a batch command that builds, checks (stations, lines, holds, load) and writes a one-page report per city, and the app's `REAL_NETWORKS` read from `packs/real/index.json`. Then about 10-20 agent minutes and well under 1M tokens a city in batches of ten, minutes of machine | 3-5 to build | plumbing, but the new game sheet's list grows: **Anita's call** on rough frequencies and how many cities |
| 5 | **Frequencies from open GTFS**: look up the Mobility Database catalogue by the pack's box, take trains an hour per route, match routes to relations by name, ref and stops | 2-4 | plumbing |
| 6 | **Player import of GeoJSON and KML/KMZ** through Open a file, into a new sandbox: each line its own double track along the drawn line (as the saves were after T-1303), stations placed on it, levels from the file or by kind with crossings resolved, imported track `sep`, a summary of what was skipped | 6-10, after a design pass | **changes the game**: sandbox only or not, leaderboards, the `sep` lie for players' track |
| 7 | Player import of GTFS | 6-10 more, on top of 6 | game feature; later, real systems are better built by 4 and 5 |

**Recommendation: 4 with 3, as one task of about 5-8 hours.** It is the whole world from data already on disk,
it is what "streamline/automate" asks for, and it drops a new city from 1-2 agent hours and 2-5M tokens to about
a quarter hour and well under 1M, almost all of it the look. 5 can follow for the cities with open GTFS. For
"they can just share", Community already shares a .save and Open a file loads one; 6 is the step that lets a
player bring a network from My Maps or GeoJSON, and it wants Anita's design call first. 1 is cheap and worth doing
before 6 so the import and export use one shape.
