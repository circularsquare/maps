# T-2168: faint zone borders in the network diagram

Rule: docs/ui-diagram.md "Zone borders". Code: `app/src/diagram/warp.ts` (real place to drawn), `diagram/borders.ts`
(the borders), `workers/diagram.worker.ts` (drawn after the names), `diagram/view.ts` (sends the outlines, colour),
`diagram/draw.ts` (painted under the lines). Check: `checks/t2168-borders.mjs`.

## What it does

The zones holding stations have their coarse outlines (`packs/outlines.json`, the map's own) warped into the drawing
and drawn faint grey under the lines, fading out away from the blocks' stations. Each shared border is one arc, drawn
once. The warp: each station carries a place near it by its own linear map (its hops' drawn vectors against their
real ones), blended over the 8 nearest stations at 1/d^4, each map reaching at most 20 pitches.

## Tried and dropped

- **The block's affine fit plus an inverse-distance blend of the stations' residuals** (notes/T-2407.md's
  suggestion), fit weighted as one station at 1.5 or 4 median hops: far parts of a zone (its outline runs 5-10 km
  offshore in New York, prefecture edges in Japan) were extrapolated by the fit past other blocks, and offshore
  points landed at the centroid of their neighbours (the Rockaways and Coney Island are drawn far apart), cutting
  through Brooklyn. 92.6% (New York) and 88.5% (Japan) of stations inside their zone's whole ring, 11.6 s for Japan
  before a k-d tree replaced the grid search (0.3 s after).
- **A Delaunay triangulation of the stations, each triangle mapped onto the drawn one** (piecewise affine, exact at
  stations, continuous; the far outside closed by three corners drawn by the overall fit): 11% of New York's
  triangles and 21% of Japan's are turned over by the drawing, and every turned triangle folds the border through
  it; outside the stations' hull the drawn hull cuts across the network. 86.5% (New York) and 88.2% (Japan) inside
  their whole rings; by the nearest drawn border, 97.9% and 92.8%.
- **Borders shown by every station** (between blocks too): Japan 95.2% by the nearest drawn border, but of the 2,385
  stations with a border beside them 437 were on the wrong side, nearly all on stretches between blocks (Gunma,
  Tochigi, Ehime and Kagawa's lines), where the blocks' stage and the lonely hops move stations far from their real
  arrangement. Borders now show by the blocks' stations only.
- Variants of the blend (reach 10 to 40 pitches, 4 to 16 neighbours, 1/d^4 or 1/d^6) all gave Japan 95.0 to 95.5%
  with every station counted; 8 neighbours, 1/d^4 and 20 pitches kept.

Worst zones, Japan: Osaka 32 of 1,268, Nagoya 21 of 896, Shizuoka 16 of 199, Toyama 13 of 189, Kochi 9 of 161, Shiga 6
of 92. Of Japan's stations with a border beside them, a fifth are on the wrong side of it: few in all, but a border near a

Danbury, line ends a few hundred metres from the zone's edge.

The layout is untouched (the borders are drawn after the names, in the same worker turn): Node layout times are the
layout's own. In the browser, the real save: borders 27 ms after a 3.5 s layout; a frame's paint 1.36 ms with borders,
1.35 without (30 frames each). The Northeast: 30 ms after a 3.4 s layout; 0.70 ms a frame against 0.62. All Japan:
borders 0.52-0.60 s after a 52-58 s layout (drawing 0.15-0.19 s, names 1.4 s); 5.1-6.7 ms a frame against 5.0-6.2.
Japan draws 2,931 border points: its zones are prefecture-sized and most of their borders run between cities, so few
show (Osaka's with Wakayama and Nara, the Osaka Bay coast by Hagoromo, Shimonoseki with Kitakyushu). Screenshots in
artifacts/trainworld/diagborders/ (jpn-v0-3.png is Osaka's south, at 3 px a pitch).

Whole rings are looser: a zone's ring far from every station is still warped (each map clipped at 20 pitches), and
folds there never show.

## Looking outward

- London's Tube map draws fare zones as alternating light grey and white bands (TfL's tint values: zones at 10%, the
  2/3 shared area at 15%) behind the lines; the bands are a drawing of which zone each station is in, not of ground
  boundaries ("it's not possible to draw a line round the edge of zone 4 in real life, only on a tube map",
  [Diamond Geezer](https://diamondgeezer.blogspot.com/2021/04/london-fare-zone-map.html); [Mapping London, Tube
  Zones](https://mappinglondon.co.uk/?p=7222); [London fare zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_fare_zones)).
  Boundary stations sit on the line between bands. That is the same promise as here (a station on its zone's side),
  with the shape taken from the real outline rather than drawn freehand.
- Octilinear map research ([EuroVis octi-maps slides](https://ad-publications.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/EuroVis_octi-maps_slides.pdf))
  treats zones and coasts as an afterthought to the lines; nothing found on warping real outlines into a schematic.
