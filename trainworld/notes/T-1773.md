## The rule as built

`pipeline/reallevels.py` (called by `realcorridors.py` after `realshared.track_model`), on the track model's
nodes and edges:

1. **Tag level** of every 10 m of every edge: the nearest real-network chain within 15 m running the same way
   (25 degrees). OSM `layer` where set, else -1 in a tunnel, +1 on a bridge, else 0; a tunnel never above -1, a
   bridge never below +1; clamped to -3..3 (layer is an order at a crossing, not a depth, so the clamp keeps the
   order except below -3 and above 3).
2. **Node level**: the commonest tag level within 60 m of the node along its edges; else the nearest tag along
   its edges; else its neighbours'.
3. **Along an edge**: the stretch past each end's lead and platform reach (half the platform, all of it at a
   terminus, plus 10 m; a platform reaching past a short edge carries into the next node's edges) is at the node's
   level, plus 20 m slack. Then, until nothing changes:
   - a stretch under 400 m between two stretches of one other level takes theirs (road bridges, culverts, short
     cuts), unless another edge crosses it there;
   - a stretch too short for its ramps (200 m a level, half each side of the change) takes its longer
     neighbour's level; a change too near a flat end moves into the next stretch while that stays long enough.
4. **An edge too short to ramp between its two nodes**: the less sure node (samples near it, a station's
   counted 10 times) moves towards the other's level only as far as the edge needs, and becomes a little surer
   than the node it followed (so it is not pulled back next round). Any left after 20 rounds go on one level per
   group of such nodes (groups of at most 8 nodes in the seven cities). Tried and dropped: not passing the
   sureness on (ping-pong between stacked platforms, then groups of 55 nodes on one level), and union from the
   first round (a 553-node group in New York).
5. Every edge stays `sep`, so a crossing the levels miss is still no flat crossing between imported lines.

Output in the corridors file: each node's `level`, each edge's `levels` ([from m, to m, level] along a, lead a,
line, lead b, b) and `flat` (metres level at each end).

**The save** (`realsave --shared`): each edge is traced once as before; every corner takes its stretch's
level. The game lays a ramp centred between two corners of different levels, spreading over the corners
beside at its two levels when it is longer than its leg (geom.rs `vertical`). Each change's ramp is centred
where the stretches put it, moved into the room it has (past the flat ends and the ramp before, 20 m short of
each, since the alignment's chainage runs up to about 20 m shorter than the traced line on 1% of edges), by, in
order: a new corner at each end on a leg with 30 m clear of its corners; one new corner making a leg centred
there with an existing corner, both 30 m clear; the existing leg whose middle is nearest inside the room; the
same one new corner nearer a curve (5 m); else its own corners at the change anyway. Counts per city are in
the realsave log ("level changes: ..."); New York 160 / 188 / 151 / 6 / 0.

## What still goes wrong

- **Real grades steeper than 4%.** Smith-9 Sts (+3) to 4 Av-9 St (+2) to 7 Av (-3 in OSM) on the F/G: the
  game's 200 m a level cannot drop five levels in 900 m, so 7 Av and 15 St-Prospect Park follow their
  neighbour up and are drawn at +2, really underground (the clearest wrong thing in New York; visible in
  `nyc_after_-73.99_40.675_z14.png`). Where a real line ramps faster than the game can, one end moves; which
  one is the sureness tie-break. A variant that did not pass sureness on put 4 Av-9 St at -2 instead and made
  55-node groups elsewhere, so it was dropped.
- **Stacked platforms joined by a short junction** (a station's local and express on different OSM layers,
  joined 90-120 m past the platforms): they must share a level, so one moves.
- **Layer is an order, not a depth**: Tokyo and Seoul tag many subways -3 to -6, so most of Seoul's subway is
  drawn at -3 (36% of its km) and costs more than before.
- **Crossings left flat**: 5-37% of the ones OSM separates in the big cities, mostly short edges between close
  stations in Manhattan, Paris and London, where no ramp fits between two platforms. `sep` keeps them from
  acting as flat crossings.
- **Off-graph pieces** (trams, Toronto's streetcars, people movers) take their neighbours' levels; streetcars
  stay at ground, which is right.
- **A ramp moved off its spot** when no leg has room: 0-4 per city "with corners anyway"; these are where the
  two platforms on a ramp come from.
