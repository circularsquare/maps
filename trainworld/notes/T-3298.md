# T-3298: tram then train shows as a walk

Player report (Caio, feedback 1794440480-9c44ca08, build mwc9777k, 2026-11-11/12, read through
`tools/ops/feedback.py`): "if i have a tram that goes from point A to point B, and a train that goes from point B
to point C, it calculates the path from A to C as 'Walk to B, then grab a train to C'"; later "it doesnt happen
all the time". No picture; he has since changed the layout.

## Cause

The route search only lets a rider walk from one station to another between rides when the two are within
`Params::xfer_walk_m` (300 m, straight line between station positions) or in one complex (a passage). Tram track
and train track can't share a node, so a tram stop is always a separate station from the train station it serves.
Placed more than 300 m from it, the tram stop has no change to the train at all, and the only transit route left
is to walk from home to the train station (up to `walk_cutoff_m`, 3 km). His layout is gone, so this is the cause
that reproduces his symptom, not one seen in his game; it would fit "not all the time" if some of his tram stops
were within 300 m of a station and others not.

Not the trip explorer hiding a leg (the choice follows the same search: transit loses to the car in the 450 m
scene), and
not a tram rule (tram lines board like rail; nothing in `build_graph` looks at tram flags). The same holds for any
two lines' stations 300 m or more apart; trams just meet it most, since a tram stop beside a station is never one
with it unless within the 100 m merge.

## Not changed

No sim edit. Raising `xfer_walk_m` changes a stated rule (SPEC: passages are "the one way to link stations"),
reaches every pair of stations 300 m or more apart in every city, adds walk edges with the square of the radius
(600 m is about 4x the change walks), and moves real-save totals; Anita's call. Tests that assume the limit:
`a_line_bent_back_near_itself_is_changed_onto_across_the_bend` (a 600 m passage "past the walking limit").

Options for her:
1. Keep 300 m; players link with a passage. The explorer gives no hint why.
2. Raise change walks to 600 m (recommended), measured first on the real save for search time and rider totals.
   Long changes cost their minutes x 1.39, so they win only where the alternative is worse; passages keep 2 m/s
   and reach 1 km. Bus stops get the longer change to rail too (nothing in the walk-edge loop skips them), and
   buses are otherwise settled.
3. Only a tram-only stop gets the longer reach to non-tram stations (narrower, but a special case).
