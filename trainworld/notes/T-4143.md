# T-4143 The first click of a track takes seconds to register (agent firstclick, 2026-11-23)

Anita, 11-23 on 8800 after T-4136: "for the initial click it can take a couple seconds to register sometimes, but after
the initial click the ghost feels significantly better."

## What changed

- **A stand-in ghost before the run's first answer** (`drawStandIn`, `trackHeading`, `heightOf` in map/tools.ts,
  `standInPts` in map/toolParts.ts). T-4136 bends the last answer to the pointer, but a new run has no answer yet, so
  the ghost waited for the clock worker's first one. Now, until it lands, the ghost is drawn from what the page has: from
  a start on track (or chained on with a heading) the circular arc that leaves along the track toward the pointer and
  ends at it (straight when the pointer is behind); from open ground a straight line; heights from the start's level to
  the end's. No figures (the tag shows only the spinner after 300 ms, as before). The first answer replaces it.
- Unchanged: a click places only what the worker checked (the click's quote is still only the answer for its own spot,
  T-830; with none, the edit is checked by the worker as before); a refused spot is red once its answer lands; the start
  mark was already drawn at the click.

## Step 1: what the first click waits on (master)

Method: play.mjs on my Vite (2 claimed cores, machine at the ledger's cap), track tool, snap switches as a new profile has
them (all off), the pointer pressed at a spot, then moved every 16 ms. Times from the press (pointerdown) to: the click
handler, the start mark's first frame, the first preview asked, its answer, the first ghost drawn. Worker profiles by CDP.
Scripts and raw results: scratchpad firstclick/ (probe.js, loop.js, r-*.json).

The click itself and the start mark were never slow: click handled 3-144 ms after the press (Playwright's own press/release
gap included), start mark on the next frame. The ghost is what waited, for the run's first answer:

| case | first ghost after the press, ms |
|---|---|
| Asia, warm area, free or station, idle or calculating | 35-476 |
| Asia, right after a pan into a new area, while calculating (Seoul, Busan, Taipei) | 1,806 / 2,613 / 3,584 |
| Asia, after a pan, later (Shanghai, Bangkok; Osaka, Hong Kong not hit) | 812 / 778 (282, 21) |
| Asia, clock running, 15 clicks 3 s apart | 1,650 once, others 20-290 |
| NYC, station or free, calculating | 151-364 |
| NYC, after a pan (Newark, Jamaica, Stamford) | 142-216 |
| NYC, clock running, 15 clicks | 24-535 |

Why, from the worker profiles (Busan: 4,209 of 5,065 ms profiled; Taipei 4,425 of 5,528): after a pan, the street speeds of
the new zones land (`ensureStreets`), every bus line is timed again (`rework_buses`), and `sendStateOutside` sends a whole
state with both hours' trips and boarding accounts (`sendTrips` -> `boarding_hour`, 3.9-4.4 s on Asia while trips
calculate, about 0.75 s later). It runs in one go, so the first preview waits behind it. The clock crossing an hour
(`turnHour`, an hour's trips and boarding) is the same kind of wait, shorter. Previews already jump ahead of queued
messages (T-1407) and only one is in flight (T-1039), so the queue is not the cause; the worker is simply busy.

What makes it slow or fast: whether the click lands while the clock worker is building a whole state or an hour's trips.
That happens after a pan into an area whose street speeds are not in yet (only when the game has bus lines; the fetch
decides when, so the same pan is slow one time and fast the next), more while trips calculate (riders in make the
boarding account dearer), and at an hour's turn with the clock running. NYC was never slow in my runs (its streets are in
after the load; its boarding hours are cheaper). Not causes: a cold snap cache (the click handler takes under 1 ms, 26 ms
once), the worker's queue, the page's own work (no long task over 172 ms near a click).

## After

The natural block is a race I could not hit on demand (20 more pans on a fresh page all missed it), so before/after are
measured with every preview answer held until 2 s after the press, as a worker busy for 2 s would (the same page, the
stand-in switched off for "before"):

| run | first ghost before (master) | first ghost after |
|---|---|---|
| Asia, Tokyo, free, 3 runs | 2,025 / 2,026 / 2,028 | 47 / 19 / 54 |
| Asia, Tokyo, from a station, 3 runs | 2,029 / 2,027 / 2,013 | 8 / 12 / 95 |
| NYC, free (start on track), 3 runs | 2,000 / 2,030 | 16 / 20 / 11 |
| NYC, from Times Sq, 2 runs | 2,030 / 2,044 | 17 / 13 |

The ghost now shows at the first pointer move after the click: 97-99.5% off the first click's wait (2,013-2,044 ms to
8-95 ms). For the natural waits above (0.8-3.6 s on Asia after a pan) it is the same: the stand-in does not wait on the
worker at all. When no worker is busy the answer lands a few ms after the stand-in and nothing changes.

Frames (Asia, from the station east of Ryogoku, answers held 2.5 s, zoom 15): artifacts/trainworld/t4143/
after-station-330ms.png (the start and the stand-in arc leaving east along the track to the pointer, 0.33 s after the
click), after-station-900ms.png, after-station-1600ms.png, after-station-2700ms.png (the checked answer); before-station-
330/900/1600 ms show only the start and the tag's spinner, before-station-2700ms the first answer.

## Not done (cause left)

The worker's whole-state send after street speeds land is the cause of the long waits and also holds every edit and
preview behind it, not only the first click: `ensureStreets` times every bus line again and resends everything whenever any
zone's streets land and any bus line exists, even where no bus runs. Skipping the send when no bus line's timing changed
(or sending it in pieces like an edit's follow-up) would remove most of it. Sim timing, not this task; no task filed (asked).
