## What was found

- **Colliers International, Global CBD Parking Rate Survey 2009**, as charted by Paul Barter in
  *Parking Policy in Asian Cities* (ADB, 2011), Figure 11, page 64: monthly non-reserved CBD season
  parking for about 60 cities, $ per m² (the report divides by 19.5 m² a space).
  [ADB page](https://www.adb.org/publications/parking-policy-asian-cities),
  [PDF copy](https://www.locjkt.or.id/application/docliblcjkt/upload/parking-policy-asia.pdf).
  Read off the chart, times 19.5: Delhi about $27 a month, Mumbai $25, Bangalore $31, Jakarta $23,
  Johannesburg $68, Mexico City $117, Beijing $156, Shanghai $214, Moscow $254, Tokyo $526, Hong Kong
  $751, New York Midtown $546, London City $1,014, Dallas $98. The same report's own surveys
  (Figures 9 and 10) find work parking in Ha Noi, Dhaka, Bangkok and Manila at $3-50 a month.
- **City of Calgary, Briefing Note 8** (November 2010), a log-log regression of Colliers 2008-09 CBD
  rates over 44 mostly rich cities: elasticity of the monthly rate to GDP per head (PPP) 0.56-0.62,
  0.80 without the US cities (t about 2.5).
  [PDF](https://www-prd.calgary.ca/content/dam/www/cfod/finance/documents/corporate-economics/other-reports/briefing-note-08-parking.pdf)
- Monteria, Colombia (income elasticity of parking *demand*, not price, -0.10 and -0.32) and a
  meta-analysis of parking price elasticities turned up too; neither gives a price-by-income slope.

My fit of the Figure 11 points against GDP per head at market rates (game/countryIncome.json `usd`,
the fares' own income figure), `fit_parking.py` in the agent's scratch, 57 cities:

| | slope of log price on log GDP | R² | cities |
|---|---|---|---|
| all | 0.53 | 0.41 | 57 |
| under $40,000 | 1.00 | 0.82 | 17 |
| $40,000 and over | -0.45 | 0.03 | 40 |

So prices rise about in proportion to income up to roughly $35-40,000 a head and are flat (a wide
scatter: Dallas $98, London $1,014) above it, which is where all of the cities the parking rule was
checked on sit.

## The rule

The game counts paid parking in perceived minutes, the same everywhere. A local rider weighs a
dollar of fare at GDP^-0.7 minutes (game/income.ts). Parking dollars going as GDP^1.0 then make its
minutes go as GDP^0.3. So:

    factor = (GDP per head / 35,000)^0.3 below $35,000, 1 above

on the paid space at the destination and the parking at home (`Params::park_income`,
`kernel::park_income_of`, set from the pack's country in `DemandApi::new`; cross.rs's cross-zone
outings too). The 7 minutes to park on every trip are time and stay. The market's longer trips keep
their own parking.

| country | GDP a head (market US$) | factor |
|---|---|---|
| India | 2,702 | 0.46 |
| Vietnam | 5,066 | 0.56 |
| China | 13,862 | 0.76 |
| Turkey | 18,599 | 0.83 |
| Poland | 28,420 | 0.94 |
| Japan, Korea and richer | from 35,951 | 1.00 |

## Done when

Car share of all local trips with no rail at all (commutes and everyday outings together), each pack
as built against the same pack with its country read as the US (`api::tests::t2278_car_shares_by_income`):

| pack | factor | before | after |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ho Chi Minh City | 0.56 | 53.7% | 60.6% (+6.9 points; commutes 69.8 to 75.6, outings 45.3 to 52.8) |
| Delhi | 0.46 | 59.7% | 60.9% (+1.2 points; commutes 73.9 to 74.8, outings 54.4 to 55.6) |
| New York, London, Tokyo, Paris, Seoul | 1.00 | unchanged | unchanged |

Delhi moves less, likely because fewer of its zones' trips reach paid parking (over 3,000 jobs a
km²) than Ho Chi Minh City's; not checked zone by zone. Rail's share follows the car's: cheaper
parking makes the car a little better against a train in both.

The lie: "car" stands for mopeds too; the rule cheapens the car's parking rather than adding a moped.
