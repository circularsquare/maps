## What it is

Each zone's populations put the stations in their own pack's frame, so two zones' search graphs differ in the last
metre of every walk and never share a search. Now every population of a cluster (both layers of every zone, and the
market) is given the stations in one shared frame (`set_graph_xy`): the pack frame of the cluster's first zone in
registry order. In that frame the stations are merged (100 m, T-4094's bus merge), and the search graph's walks
between stations and platforms are laid out, so every population of the cluster gets the same graph to the bit and
`with_skims` searches it once a period and round. Walks are measured in ground metres (the frame's east-west scale
at each station's latitude, `GraphXy::ew`), so which zone's frame is the shared one hardly matters. Station access
(homes to stations, bus stretches, `Fold::weigh`) stays in each zone's own frame. In game a city's layers then solve
on the whole cluster (one graph needs the same stations everywhere), not on its reach (T-756).
`?perfOff=onegraph` gives the old behaviour (own frames, reach views).

Code: `network::GraphXy`, `Service::build` (api.rs, its `graph` argument), `build_graph` (search.rs: positions,
`ew`, the grid), `set_graph_xy` on DemandApi, EverydayApi and IntercityApi, `demand.worker.ts` `setUpPops` and the
reach view line.

## Identical figures: not possible with one table

One table means one set of walk costs for every population, so the zones whose frame is not the shared one cannot keep
their figures. In plane metres (no ground correction) the shared frame's own zone keeps its figures to the bit
(checked: nyc's layers and the market hash the same) and every other zone moves by its frame's offset. With ground
metres every zone moves a little, towards the true walk lengths. With no shared frame given the code is bit-identical
to master (real NYC loads hash f8534e9db44a048d, T-4123's).

**Which frame, and why ground metres.** The same NYC run with the shared frame put on nhv (a degree north) in plane
metres: rail trips +0.01%, NJ Transit NEC -1.8% and -1.7%, Port Jefferson +0.66%, Hempstead -0.65%. In ground metres
with the frame on nhv or on nyc the lines are within 0.02% of each other. Plane metres would keep the shared frame's
zone bit-identical but make the shift grow with how far the cluster spans in latitude; ground metres keep it at each
zone's own frame error.

Walks near the cut (`flips.py`, Tokyo community network, 2,594 station pairs within 400 m, every zone's population
sees all of them): own frame -> ground metres, 9-19 pairs a zone change side of the 300 m cut and 8-9 of the 150 m bus
cut; own frame -> Tokyo's frame in plane metres, 0-11 and 0-4. Walk lengths: own frame against ground 0.5-0.9% off on
average; Tokyo's frame against a zone's own 0.4-0.6%.

## The shape that would show it worst

Ground metres: a tall zone at high latitude (its stations far north or south of its pack origin, where its own frame
is most off) with many changes on foot or bus stops at 280-320 m (150 m for bus stops) apart east-west: those walks
come and go. Also the merges are still measured in the shared frame's plane metres: in a cluster spanning about 3
degrees of latitude at 60 N (Oslo to Trondheim, say), east-west gaps in the farthest zone read up to 10% off, so bus
stops 270-300 m apart merge or split differently from today. Cheap measured case: the real NYC network in a frame a
degree north in plane metres moved NEC 1.8%; in ground metres 0.03% at most.

## Verdict

T-4057's 41% one-off share is partly the native harness's: `demand_mem` gives every zone the whole network, while the
game's zones solve on their own reach (T-756), so their graphs differ by stations as well as by frame. Natively one
graph is a clear cut on a big multi-zone network (Tokyo community -12.8%) and nothing on NYC. In game, sharing one
graph means giving every zone the whole cluster: no save's CPU moved beyond noise (NYC -4.5%, bench Tokyo +2%,
Belgrade +0.6%), and demand-worker memory went up 15% on the bench Tokyo and 13% on Belgrade, the same on every run
(over the 10% line). The community Tokyo network itself was never run in game: that run is the one that could
overturn this. Rider shifts are tiny everywhere (rail trips within 0.001%, no line
past 0.12%). Not worth landing as built. Ways on, not built: share only between populations whose reach is the same
stations (no memory cost, smaller gain), or one graph only for clusters small enough that the whole cluster is about
each zone's reach; or land the ground-metre walks alone (a correctness change, the shift above) and leave the search
as it is.
