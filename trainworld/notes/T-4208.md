# T-4208 Track ghost jumps before the last click lands (agent ghostjump, 2026-11-24)

## Her words

"sometimes it seems like before the last click is fully registered we dont have a good sense of how to draw the new
ghost segment. and the ghost segment can appear at wild angles / positions, sometimes disjoint with the track we were
previously placing ... what lasts ~ a second is the period during which if i move my mouse around quickly it can cause
divergences" (scratch pad 11-24, picture artifacts/trainworld/board/20261124-093053-85f1.png).

## Cause

From a click until the worker answers it (`segmentClick` awaiting the early `route` edit), the tool's start was still the
start being left. Every pointer move meanwhile asked a ghost from that old start and bent the last answer to the
pointer, so the ghost swung about the previous segment's start (its start mark there too), over the segment just
placed. The answer is quick when the worker is free (30 ms on NYC), but when it is still building the network of the
run's earlier segments (released on the pointer's rest, 0.5-2 s on Asia) the click waits behind it: that is the second.
Answers for ghosts asked from the old start in that window also landed and were drawn (`start === from` still held).

## Fix (map/tools.ts, map/toolMarks.ts, map/renderer.ts)

- At the click (plain track, not a real or street route, and not a ghost already refused): the ghost of this very
  request (or the ghost as shown, when the click beat its answer) is kept as the placed stand-in at once
  (`clickAhead`), and `clicked.onward` holds the click's end, its heading from the answer, and its height.
- Until the answer, the ghost is a stand-in from that end to the pointer (`drawOnward`, the same `standInPts` as before a
  run's first answer); no ghost is asked or bent from the old start (`requestPreview`, `drawGhost` return), and the
  start mark, curve labels and tag go with `ghostStart` (`shownPreview` is null meanwhile, so the tag shows only the
  spinner after WAIT_MS). A run that ends at the click shows no ghost from the click on.
- At the answer: the stand-in kept at the click is taken back (`renderer.dropPlacedSince`, a no-op once a network has
  dropped the stand-ins) and drawn again as before (from the answer, or from the held edges when the click beat its
  ghost). A refused click takes it back and the ghost goes on from the old start.
- Not covered: a real-network or street-snapped route; its snap draws its own ghost from the old start until the answer.

## Also asked: the ghost pointing far off after zooming (her 11-24)

"after zooming ... it seems to think the cursor is somewhere way off the screen and draws the ghost diagonally across the
whole screen ... moving the mouse at all updates it". The ghost's end is the pointer's place on the map, taken at the
last pointer move. A wheel zoom keeps that place under the pointer, so it was right; a zoom about the centre (the keys,
the zoom buttons) or any other camera move without a pointer move left the end where the pointer used to be on the
map. Measured (zoom.js, pointer 70 px from the centre): one key zoom out left the end 35 px from the pointer; zooming in
two steps 500 px from the centre would leave it 1,500 px off. Now `Tools.cameraMoved` (map `move`) takes the hover again
at the pointer's screen point whenever the map spot under it changed (track and station tools), and the pointer's
screen point is kept up while a button is held (a drag of the map). After: wheel out and in, key out and in, a drag:
0-1 px.

## Also asked: the "wiggly" ghost (her 11-24)

"if i move my mouse the change sorta seems to propagate from my mouse to the origin point". Which it is: neither a curve
solved over several frames nor a smoothed start. Between answers the page draws the last answer bent to the pointer
(T-4136 `bend`): turned and scaled about the start with a weight that eases from 0 at the start to 1 at the far end, so
the heading out of the start holds. So the far end is on the pointer at once and the part near the start keeps the old
answer's shape until the next answer lands, a frame or a few later, and redraws it: the change reads as travelling from
the pointer back to the start. How big the correction is at each landing (wiggle.js, NYC, start with a heading, 4 runs of
4 s of moving): 2-12 px between the bent ghost and the answer for the same spot (median 5). Two other stand-ins
measured on the same samples, a circle arc tangent to the heading (as before a run's first answer) and the answer
moved by how such arcs move, were no better (2-9 and 3-11 px), so `bend` is kept. The lever is how often answers land:
in this tree's game they came 1-4 a second while moving (45 of 50 asks dropped as stale, each answered one taking about
1 s), while t2403 in the same tree (shorter segments, same area) had answers in 8 ms median; the game's status also said "New
York could not load", and I did not pin down why these took ~1 s (water masks being fetched again is one guess). Worth a look on 8800 with a big save: if answers there are slow while the pointer
moves, that is the wiggle's cause and a task of its own.
