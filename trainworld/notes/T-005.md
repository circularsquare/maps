# T-005 notes: demand kernel spike

Question: can one city's full demand recompute run in a background worker in a few seconds at New
York scale, and how much memory does it need? **Yes: under a second per period in WASM, about
25 MB.** The design that gets there is in SPEC 4.4; this file keeps the method, all the numbers
and what I would change.

## Code

All in `sim/`, behind the cargo feature `demand` so the app's `wasm-pack build` is unchanged.

- `src/demand/pack.rs`: format v0 reader (ignores unknown arrays) and writer.
- `src/demand/synth.rs`: synthetic city on New York's own coordinates (km from the pack origin).
- `src/demand/network.rs`: network arrays and the hand-made network (25 lines from real place
  coordinates: 12 subway/light rail, 10 commuter, 3 orbital; stops within 350 m merged into one
  station, giving 434 stations, 1,032 route nodes).
- `src/demand/kernel.rs`: the stages. `bench.rs`: timings and heap. `alloc.rs`: counting
  allocator (same heap numbers natively and in wasm). `wasm.rs`: `demand_bench()` export (feature `demand-bench` since T-026).
- `src/bin/demand_bench.rs`: native driver.

Run (CARGO_TARGET_DIR anywhere outside `sim/target` avoids blocking the app's builds):

    cargo run --release --features demand --bin demand_bench --manifest-path sim/Cargo.toml -- [--pack data/packs/nyc.json] [--exact] [--sweep] [--prune 0.5] [--band 5] [--zone-m 2000]
    wasm-pack build sim --target nodejs --release --out-dir <scratch>/pkg --out-name sim -- --features demand-bench
    node bench.cjs   # require('<scratch>/pkg/sim.js').demand_bench(headerText, binBytes, "exact")

`cargo test --release --features demand` covers the loader (round trip, unknown arrays skipped),
`fast_exp`, and a small end-to-end run (trip conservation, boardings >= rail trips).

## Inputs

| | synthetic | real New York (T-004) |
|---|---|---|
| cells | 152,683 (324 m squares = H3 res-9 area) | 74,920 H3 res 9 |
| people / jobs | 20.0M / 10.0M | 21.7M / 10.2M |
| 2 km zones | 4,662 | 5,710 (extent 280 x 283 km) |
| mean commute (gravity) | 13.5 km | 15.4 km |

The brief said "~170 m spacing"; that is H3 res 9's edge length in old docs. Res-9 cells average
0.105 km², so centre spacing is ~350 m (hex) or 324 m (square). The real pack has half the
synthetic's cells, so the synthetic is a safe upper bound.

Parameters: walk cutoff 1.5 km straight line, x 1.3 at 1.2 m/s; Subway Builder weights (walk 1.39,
platform wait 1.37 for the first 5 min of half-headway then 0.4, driving 1.33, 3 min to park);
transfer 5 min; fare 6 min; parking cost one minute per 2,500 jobs/km² in the destination zone,
capped at 40; road 28 km/h x 1.3; logit 0.05 per minute; rail constant 1.5 (picked so rail gets
~15-18% of all commutes on this small network, enough to load the crowding code); decay
exp(-d/9 km); 45% of commutes in the 3-hour morning peak, peak-hour factor 1.25.

## Timings

One thread, Windows, a 16-core desktop shared with two other agents and Anita, so the same run varies
by up to 40% natively. WASM: Node 22.20 (V8), wasm-pack 0.15 with wasm-opt, no SIMD. "Warm" is a
later call in the same process; the first call runs partly on V8's baseline tier.

Mode choice is ~90% of every recompute. In WASM it was 2.5x native until libm's `exp` was replaced
by a 5-term polynomial (`fast_exp`, 2e-4 relative): wasm has no exp instruction. After that, warm
WASM is within ~10-40% of native. Gravity is still 2.6x native in WASM (f64 accumulation over a
gathered f32 table).

**Edit** (the Second Av-Bronx line's 20 own stations moved 400 m): reruns everything but gravity.
New York 510-690 ms native, 775 ms WASM; synthetic 700-1,050 ms native, 990-1,040 ms WASM. Access
and subzones are under 10 ms of that, so making them incremental is not worth it; the cost is the
pair loop, and a moved line changes the best path for most pairs.

## Gravity scheme: what was tried

- **Cell x cell**: 75k-150k cells = 5.6-23 billion pairs. Out.
- **Distance-truncated sparse cell pairs**: commutes average 13.5-15.4 km; a 30 km radius around a
  core cell holds ~20k cells, so ~2 billion pairs (estimate, not run). Truncating short enough to
  be cheap deletes the long commutes rail is for. Out.
- **Cell x cell restricted to station-served cells** (run as the "exact" reference): New York
  9,976 served cells, 88M pairs, 1.3 s native; synthetic 13,317 cells, 177M pairs, 2.6-3.5 s
  native, 5.6 s WASM, 47 MB. Workable for this network, but it grows with the square of the
  area the player's network covers: a 1,000-station network serving ~40k cells is ~1.6B pairs.
- **Chosen: gravity between 2 km zones; mode choice between access subzones** (zone x nearest
  station x 5-min walk band). Pairs grow with the square of the station count (~11 subzones per
  station here), not with cells.

Subzones against the exact per-cell reference (free-flow round, same gravity; sum of absolute
differences over sum):

| walk band | NY subzones | NY pairs | NY free-flow ms native | rail total | station entries | exits | segment loads |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| none | 1,313 | 1.7M | 63 | -1.6% | 8.5% | 11.0% | 4.0% |
| 10 min | 2,526 | 6.0M | 126 | -1.2% | 7.6% | 9.8% | 3.6% |
| **5 min** | 3,951 | 14.6M | 250-330 | -1.0% | 5.8% | 6.5% | 3.0% |
| 2.5 min | 5,578 | 28.6M | 479 | -0.7% | 5.1% | 4.5% | 2.1% |

(Synthetic: 5 min gives -1.3% / 5.7% / 6.6% / 2.2%.) The station error that remains with fine
bands comes from all-or-nothing access-station choice: a subzone sends everyone to one station
pair, cells at a boundary do not. A logit split over the access stations would fix both that
and the lumpiness.

Zone size (New York; gravity time native): 1 km = 18,423 zones, 27.6 s; 2 km = 5,710, 2.9 s;
3 km = 2,727, 0.67 s; 4 km = 1,612, 0.21 s. Mean commute barely moves (15.3-15.5 km). Rail trips
move ±4% (837k at 1 km, 805k at 2 km, 782k at 4 km), from intrazonal trips and subzone shape.
2 km keeps intrazonal trips (mostly walk) small while gravity stays a load-time cost.

Pruning zone pairs (synthetic): under 0.5 trips skips 82% of zone pairs, 21.5M -> 9.8M subzone
pairs, halves mode choice, drops 1.3% of station-served trips (mostly long ones, so rail -1.3%).
Under 2 trips: 7.3M pairs, drops 4%. Left off by default; it is the first lever for a big network.

## Crowding round

New York, morning peak, free flow: 196 of 982 segments over seats at the peak hour, worst 132% of
crush, penalty p50 +12%, p90 +104% (rider-minute weighted). After one MSA round: 192 segments,
worst 112%, p50 +7%, p90 +65%; rail 805k -> 769k (riders lost to the car when trains are full).
One round is what the brief asked for; the memory note says three rounds still move, so the game
should keep refining in the background after the first estimate lands.

## What parallelises (each worker owns its memory, no SharedArrayBuffer needed)

- Periods: fully independent. A pool of 3-5 workers runs five periods in about one period's time.
- Mode choice: split origin zones across workers; each returns an S x S rail table (0.75 MB at 434
  stations) to sum. Every worker needs the subzones and skims (a few MB).
- Station-to-station search: per source station.
- Gravity: per row; but better computed offline (below).
- Assignment and access are too cheap to bother.

## What I would change

- Ship the gravity balancing factors (two f32 per zone) in the city pack, computed by the
  pipeline; 8 s in WASM is too long for opening a city. Or an FFT convolution (the zones are a
  regular grid and the decay is by grid offset, so each Furness pass is a 2-D convolution).
- Logit over access/egress station pairs instead of all-or-nothing (station entries, lumpiness).
- The "other" access leg (drive or bus to a station). With walk-only access, cells beyond 1.5 km
  of a station get no rail at all: only a third of New York's morning commutes are between
  station-served subzones on this network (33%; synthetic 35%).
- The second demand layer (shopping, school, leisure) doubles the mode-choice loop per period.
- Zone-centre distances for gravity (a lookup by grid offset) are crude within ~1.4 km; fine
  for a 15 km mean trip, revisit when fitting the decay (T-006).
- `pair` count is the number to watch in game: log it and fall back to pruning or no walk bands
  when it passes ~30M.
