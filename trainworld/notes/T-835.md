# T-835: frequency at mid-line stops across a change of period

Anita, 2026-10-21: "if train frequency is determined by headway when they start at the beginning of
the line... isnt frequency a bit too low in the early morning for long lines".

## How it works now

- `Service::run_deps` / `lattice_deps_local`: each period's departures start at the period's start at
  both terminals (run 1 offset by the forward trip modulo the headway, always under one headway).
  Periods: night 0-6 low, 6-10 high, 10-16 medium, 16-20 high, 20-24 medium.
- A stop a run time `tau` from its direction's first stop sees a new pattern `tau` late. Every stop's
  two directions average a lag of half the one-way run T. So at 06:00 and 16:00 (the switches to
  more trains) the middle of a long line keeps night or midday frequency for up to T/2 (one direction
  up to T); at 10:00, 20:00 and 00:00 it keeps the busier service as long (a bonus).
- The boarding account (6.6) uses the drawn departures to the bit, and riders arrive at a stop by
  the clock at that stop. So the account already shows the lag.
- Demand assumes the period's headway at every stop for the whole period: wait = half the headway
  (demand-kernel step 4), and its left-behind rule runs on even headways per period, each standing
  alone (step 7). It never sees the lag.
- Money: running cost follows the timetable's departures, so trains drawn are trains charged; fares
  follow demand's riders. The only mismatch is the service demand prices for riders in the lag.

## Why it is smaller than it looks

The peaks' arrival shape is a tent (c = 0.8 at the 1.4 busiest-hour factor): riders arrive at 20%
of the period's mean rate at 06:00, rising to 180% at 08:00. Share of a stop's morning-peak boarders
who arrive before the busier trains reach it (`x + 0.8 (2x^2 - x)`, x = lag / 4 h):

| Lag at the stop | Example | Share of the peak's boarders |
|---|---|---:|
| 16 min | middle of the 7 (31 min run) | 2% |
| 40 min | middle of the 2 (80 min run) | 8% |
| 70 min | near the end of the 2 | 19% |
| 75 min | middle of a 2.5 h London - Paris line | 22% |
| 150 min | far end of London - Paris, outbound | 70% |

The 8 frequent lines with no night service (B, W, 6, 6X, PATH...): 06h 5.88 against 4.57 minutes

Riders left behind (whole network): 06h 1,169 now, 0 with any option; 16h 4,391 now, 3,076 half,
2,145 a run early, 3,515 half a run early. The day's 391k left behind is mostly 17-19h and unrelated.

Money: on the frequent lines the lag is about 11,000 rider-hours a day of wait demand does not price,
on about 1.1M riders arriving in the 06h and 16h. Even if each such minute lost 1% of those riders,
that is about 6,000 rides of 17M boardings (0.04% of fares). The cost is not in the money; it is
what the player sees: sparse trains mid-line at 06:00 while the panel says peak.

Car-km a day (whole network, 3.92M now): half early +0.0%, up a run early +7.1% (frequent lines
+4.2%, the 2 +6.5%, Hudson 64 min +14%), up half a run early +3.6%.

Not this task: lines with one or two trains a period (80 sparse lines, mostly LIRR and NJT, 3.8M
boardings) wait 29% over demand over the day, nearly all from riders arriving after the period's
last train passed (late peak, 23h) who wait for the next period's first train. Demand prices them
at half a 2.5 h headway. That is the end of service, not the lag, and wants its own task.

## Options

1. **Half early**: every period starts T/2 early at the terminals, so the line's middle switches on
   the hour. Same car-km. But it moves the error to the ends and makes the downswitches worse (the
   00:00 switch to night service reaches near-start stops at 23:xx: +5,586 rider-h). Net day barely
   better (+0.7%). Not worth it.
2. **Busier periods start a run early** (`up a run early`): a period that runs more trains than the one
   before starts its departures one one-way run early (06:00 - T, 16:00 - T); a quieter one starts
   at its start, as now, so the trains already out finish their trips. Every stop then has at least
   the period's frequency all period, so demand's assumption becomes a floor. +7.1% car-km (running
   cost) on the real save. Fleet unchanged: the trains in service at the switch are the busier
   pattern's count, as now, just earlier; the hand-over at the terminals works as at any switch.
3. **Busier periods start half a run early**: the middle of the line gets the busier service on the
   hour; removes 88% of the 06h gap and 75% of the 16h one for +3.6% car-km.
4. **Per-stop periods in demand**: give demand each stop's wait from the drawn departures (lagged).
   Honest without running more trains, but it touches the strategy search's combined headways and
   the left-behind rule, for a 0.9% wait error. Too much for the gain.
5. **Leave it**, and say in the schedule's hover that the busier service takes a run to reach the
   far end. Costs nothing; the morning on long lines keeps looking thin.

What 2 or 3 changes in code: `Lattice` gains a lead per level (from the forward trip at the busier
level, one figure for both runs so a whole-line fleet stays aligned; capped at the period before's
length), `period_points` starts a busier period that much early. Eras keep their own lead, so edits
and the hand-over need nothing new. The account and the drawn trains follow `run_deps` as they are.
Money: the running cost per hour can no longer be the period's level everywhere: the lead hours of
each line run at the busier level, so `running_cost_in` / `moneyTiming` take each line's lead.
Saves: nothing saved changes (the pattern is derived); an old save's trains take the new times
after load. The schedule's hover would say when the busier service leaves the ends ("from 05:00").
Not a small change; it waits for Anita's pick.

## Recommendation

Option 2, busier periods start a whole run early: the only one where no stop ever gets less than
the panel says, which is the thing Anita noticed, at +7% running cost on the real save, honest
because the trains run. Option 3 if that cost feels too high for what it buys. Option 1 is no better
than now. File the sparse-line end-of-service gap separately.
