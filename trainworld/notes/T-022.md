# T-022 crowding convergence

Done 2026-10-09 by the demand realism agent. Ran 8 crowding rounds (method of successive
averages, SPEC 4.4 step 7) on New York's real network (T-007, 109 lines, 20M subzone pairs) and on
the batch-3 single Manhattan line, logging per round the rail trips, the segments over seats, the
worst segment's busiest-hour load as a share of crush, the rider-weighted p50 and p90 penalty
and how far the averaged segment loads moved (sum of absolute changes over the sum of loads).
Tool: `realnet --rounds 8` (notes/T-007.md); the moved figure is the bin's 12th summary column.

## What the numbers say

- **One round is the worst place to stop.** The first round moves half of everything the penalty
  pushes, all at once (route choice between stations is all or nothing, only access and egress
  are a logit), so the segment that takes the overflow is at its most overloaded right after it:
  963% on the real network against 310% before. The game showed exactly that state (one round).
  Averaging then shrinks it by about 1/n. Starting the averaging later (weights 1/3, 1/4, ...
  instead of 1/2, 1/3, ..., tried) only shifts the same sequence by a round.
- **The worst segments on the real network after 8 rounds are 0.4-train-an-hour LIRR express
  patterns** (Ronkonkoma express at Hicksville 214%, then 164%, 150%): separate lines in the
  converter, each with its own tiny capacity, which the main patterns' crowding pushes riders onto.
  Frequent lines stay under ~120% of crush. That is the common-lines gap (T-069), not a crowding
  problem.
- **Rail falls 17% from free flow to round 8 in the morning peak** (riders who give up on full
  trains), 6% after one round would suggest; the first round's figure is not an estimate of the
  converged one.

## Decisions

- **The app runs three crowding rounds** (`ROUNDS` in `workers/demandClient.ts`), each landing as
  its own update. On a player-sized network the third round moves loads under 1%; on the real
  network 10%, with rail within 3% of round 8. Cost per edit: four passes per period instead of
  two. On the hand-made 434-station network (T-026) that is about 2.8 s to the final result with
  three workers, the split still at ~0.7 s; on the real 867-station network a pass is ~1 s native
  (20M pairs), so ~10 s in the browser to the last round (T-061 is the lever if that matters).
- **No crush cap.** The memory note's post-hoc cap fixes single trains in a timetable model that a
  penalty table cannot reach. Here, converged, frequent lines stay under ~120% of crush, and the
  remaining extremes are the converter's 0.4-train patterns (T-069). In the game, a line at 160%
  is the player's signal to add trains or a second line, so it should show.
- MSA kept as it is (weight 1/(r+1)).

The single-line case's 162% evening peak is real in the model's terms: 73% of its riders arrive by
the other access leg (T-007), and nothing a crowding penalty does makes driving into Manhattan
attractive enough to leave. That is T-034's business.
