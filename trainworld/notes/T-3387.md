# T-3387 Contributor PR #2 (peanutmow): settle and outline speedups

circularsquare/trainsworld #2, "Keep big networks quick: fewer run-profile rebuilds in the settle, numeric
outline keys". Tested 2026-11-13 by pr2 in tree tw/pr2 (from master aae022a2).

Three parts:
1. `app/src/map/boundaryGeometry.ts`: `OutlineEdges` keys each border segment by a number made from two
   dense vertex numbers instead of a `"a:b"` string.
2. `sim/src/track/world.rs` `settle_counts`: the 1% probe runs `probe_trips` on the bumped lines only;
   from round 9 (`SETTLE_EXACT_ROUNDS = 8`) the round trips come from the capacity solve alone
   ("cheap rounds") and the profiles are rebuilt once after the loop for the lines whose rate moved.
3. `settle_train_counts`: merge pacing (T-2973) only after a settle that came to rest.

## Outcome

Part 1 taken, with one change. Parts 2 and 3 not taken as they stand.

## Part 1: outlines

- Same output. Every zone's coarse outline (4,045 zones, 1.34 M vertices, `data/packs/outlines.json`)
  through master's and the PR's class: identical features for no focus, three focuses, a 300-zone
  `only` set, a third removed, re-added in reverse, and a 300-zone window walked across the world and
  back (50 steps, as a small device does). Script: scratchpad `pr2/outline/run.mjs`.
- Time and memory (Node 22, heap plus array buffers):

  | | add all | `lines()` | held, all zones | held, small-device window |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | master | 1.5-1.6 s | 0.45 s | 160 MB | 14.0 MB |
  | PR | 1.07-1.10 s | 0.41-0.46 s | 133 MB | 47.2 MB |
  | PR + change | 1.14-1.36 s | 0.38-0.44 s | 145 MB | 14.3 MB |

- The change: the PR's vertex-number map never shrank, so a small device (T-2794, which keeps only the
  zones near the view and adds and removes them as it moves) ended up holding every vertex it had
  passed: 47 MB after a walk across the world against 14 MB on master, the memory T-2794 saved. Each
  vertex number now counts the segment ends using it and is given back (and reused) when none do.

## Part 2: the settle

**The probe on bumped lines is exact.** `Service::probe_trips` clones the capacity, calls
`Capacity::update` with the given lines as rescheduled (re-solving every existing resource any of them
uses, from the rates in `net`, each resource on its own), then works out holds for the lines whose
resources changed delay. A bumped line's resources are all re-solved either way and from the same
rates, so its probe is the same whether the other lines are passed or not. The step reads a probe
value only where `active[lev] && off[j][lev]`, exactly the bumped lines; the others get `trips[j]` from
`align`, which nothing reads. Measured: a build with only this shortcut gave every save below
identical to master, to the bit (52 cases by 4 stages). It saves nothing measurable (London edit 4.78 s
vs 4.75 s, London x2 edit 23.0 vs 23.0 s): the cost of a round is the cap clone and the profiles,
not the re-solve. Left out for that reason.

**The cheap rounds change settles that come to rest.** The PR says a settle that comes to rest does so
as before; that holds only for settles of 8 rounds or fewer. Our saves often take 9-30 rounds and come
to rest (London's edits 12, the Northeast 9+, world-map day 73, the Japan cities save), and those now
end elsewhere: up to 256 s on a round trip and 0.9 trains/h on world-map day 73's load.

**They leave the service stale.** After the loop only the lines whose rate moved are rebuilt; a line
sharing track with them whose holds moved is not (`Capacity::update` was already run on the real cap
during the cheap rounds, so the final `update_scoped` sees no delay change for it). In-memory round
trips then differ from a service built from scratch at the same rates: up to 35 s (world-map x2), 9 s
(London x2 full), on 2-68 lines. Master: 0 on every case.

**The end is not at rest, so later edits and reloads pay.** A reload of the edited state no longer
reproduces it (master reproduces it on every save except the Tokyo day-77 fixture and the North America
x2), and edits on networks that master leaves at rest got much slower. A version of the cheap rounds
that also rebuilds every neighbour (scratchpad `world_fix_traced.rs`) made the stale count 0 but not
the slowdowns, since the state still ends off rest.

## Part 3: pacing only after rest

In our saves only the Tokyo day-77 fixture and Paris x3 have settles that hit the 80-round cap, so it
changes little measured. From the code: a settle that does not come to rest leaves `line_pace` as it
was, and a paced line runs `min(trains, round trip / pace)` (`Network::running_trains`), so on such a
network trains added to a paced line would stand at the ends until some settle comes to rest. Also
`settle_unpaced` reports only its last `settle_counts`, so a first settle that hit the cap followed by
a resting trim settle still counts as at rest (seen on Asia full: 80-round settles followed by pacing).
