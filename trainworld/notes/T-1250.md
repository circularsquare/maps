# T-1250: crowding on the line's schedule no longer turns red (2026-10-24)

Design only, for Anita. Anita: "since we made people not go to stations and wait for trains that
can't fit them, the crowding indicators on the line scheduling don't really go red when you should
raise the amount of service ... maybe we let people come and wait if they would only wait less than
10-30min?"

## For Anita

Anita picked (a), with the wait tuned to track overload; built as T-1255 (section 4).

- **Why the dots stay green.** The dot before High, Medium and Low is the riders' average wait
  for a train with room. Riders who would not fit are removed before they reach a platform, in
  two steps: the crowding rounds make a packed line look worse in mode choice (most of the
  effect), then the cap (T-1070) removes whoever is still over the trains' room. Whoever is left
  fits, so nobody waits long, and the average over every rider on the line keeps even a bad stop
  small. On the real network nothing is above yellow-green at High (58 of 59 lines green). With
  the 7 cut to a third of its trains it loses 44% of its peak riders, and its dot reads 1.1 minutes.
- **Your idea (a)** (riders come if their wait for a train with room is under 10-30 minutes) is
  realistic and fills the worst platforms (10-15 minute queues at Union Square), but it does not
  turn the dots red. At 30 minutes the worst line averages 4.2 minutes and the worst subway line
  1.8 (both yellow). It changes a demand rule and adds about 1% more rail trips.
- **What I'd do instead (c):** colour the dot by how many riders want the line against what its
  trains hold. At the busiest stretch, in the busiest hour, count the riders who would ride if
  every train had room, divided by the trains' crush load. The hover adds how many go another way
  each day. The 7 at full service reads 0.86 (green); cut to a third it reads 2.1 (red). No demand
  rule changes, and the ceiling tests are untouched. One stage, filed as T-1255, waiting on your pick.

## 1. The mechanism today

The schedule's dots (docs/ui-inspectors.md, "Sections"; `CrowdDots` in LineInspector.tsx) read the
boarding account: rider-seconds waited after full trains over riders arriving, for the level's
periods, each rider weighted by fare (`Boarding::crowding`), coloured green to 0.2 min, yellow at
2.5, orange at 9, red at 15 (T-1160). The account only gets riders who fit:

1. **The crowding rounds** (SPEC 4) charge a crowded ride (from 70% of crush; 3 perceived minutes
   a minute at crush) and the wait for a train with room (`kernel::left_behind`, up to 60
   minutes). Three rounds averaged with free flow already take most of an overload away.
2. **The cap** (T-1070, `kernel::capacity_fit`) then holds every segment to its trains' crush load
   in the busiest hour. So the busiest hour of the worst stretch is exactly full, and the rest of
   the period has spare room.
3. **The account** spreads those riders over the period by its tent shape. Only the minutes just
   around the peak of the tent overflow a train, so a few riders wait one extra headway. T-1067's
   90-minute give-up and 5,000 platform cap almost never act.

What used to make dots red was mostly step 3 without steps 1-2's hold: before T-1067 the account
carried queues round the clock (waits of 10.7 hours on the Lexington test, notes/T-1067.md). Even
without the cap (pre-T-1070 demand, with the give-up at 90 minutes) the real network's High dots
were 39 green, 9 yellow-green, 9 yellow and 2 orange (NJ Transit's Montclair-Boonton 12.6 min and
Northeast Corridor 12.2, both a few trains an hour).

### Worked example: the 7 cut to a third

Real network, the 7 (and the 7X beside it) cut to a third of their trains: 6.1 trains an hour at
peak instead of 18.5, a train about every 10 minutes. Weekday boardings in the High periods (06-10
and 16-20):

| | 7 at full service | 7 at a third |
|---|---|---|
| riders who would ride if every train had room (free flow) | 589,393 | 421,147 |
| after the crowding rounds | 586,514 | 343,791 |
| after the cap (what demand and fares count) | 581,691 | 330,197 |
| worst stretch's busiest hour, free flow / after rounds / after cap, x crush | 0.86 / 1.06 / 1.0 | 2.14 / 1.24 / 1.0 |
| average wait for a train with room (the dot) | 0.4 min, green | 1.1 min, yellow-green |
| the stop where most riders wait | 6.7 min, 5 Av/42 St | 19.4 min, 90 St-Elmhurst Av |

What riders do: 77,000 of the 421,000 decide against the 7 in the crowding rounds, the cap turns
away another 14,000, and the 330,000 left fit in the trains, so the account sees short queues at a
few stops around 08:00 and 17:30 and nothing else. What the panel shows: a yellow-green dot, "Riders
wait 1.1 min on average for a train with room". The line lost 44% of its peak riders, against full
service, and the panel shows nothing worth acting on. (The Trains to add count does turn orange:
its score divides the wait by the square root of the ride, 1.1 / sqrt(7.3) = 0.39. It is the
schedule dots that cannot reach red.)

### (a) Riders come and wait if the wait for a train with room is under W (Anita's)

- **Demand rule change.** The cap becomes a queue: at each stop a rider joins the platform only if
  the riders ahead all get on within W (the room of the next W minutes of trains), else goes by
  car or on foot. Built for the measurement as `t1250_wait_sim` (the cap's walk along the line,
  with `left_behind`'s trains and arrivals). The account would take the same rule in place of the
  5,000 cap, so the two agree.
- **What the player sees.** The worst platforms fill: busiest-stop waits of 10.5 min (W 20) and
  14.8 min (W 30) at 14 St-Union Sq on the L, 8.9 min at 74 St on the cut 7. Trains leave full
  through more of the peak. The dots barely move: the worst line, NJ Transit's Montclair-Boonton
  (about a train an hour at peak), averages 3.2 min at W 20 and 4.2 at W 30 (yellow);
  the subway's worst (the L and the 4) 1.5 and 1.8; the cut 7 0.9 min. Nothing goes orange.
- **Why so little.** Only the cap's riders come back (the cap removes 210,000 boardings at High
  today; W 30 brings back 120,000 of them). The 670,000 the crowding rounds put off stay away, and
  one long queue at one stop is still averaged over every rider of the line and period.
- **Ceiling tests:** unaffected (their made-up train has no network, no cap). **Cost:** small, a
  pass like `left_behind` once per period, and the same rule in `simulate_carry`. Rail +0.7% (10
  min) to +1.3% (30 min).

### (b) Show the turned-away riders as their own figure

- **No demand change.** The cap knows exactly who it turned away (`Fit::board`), per line, stop and
  period.
- **Measured, it is too small to signal.** The cap turns away at most 10% of a line's peak riders
  (PATH 9%, the NEC 10%, the L 4%, the cut 7 4%), because the crowding rounds did most of it
  first. Counting everyone the line would carry with room (free flow less the final) is the
  better figure: 21 lines lose at least 5% at High, 11 at least 15%, 1 at least 30%; the L 28%, the
  4 20%, the cut 7 22%, the 7 at full service 1%.
- **Ceiling tests:** unaffected. **Cost:** free flow is the solve's round 0, already computed;
  keeping its loads per period is a copy.

### (c) Colour by riders wanting the line against its room (my pick)

- **No demand change.** Per line and period: on its fullest stretch, the riders free flow puts
  there in the busiest hour, over the trains' crush load then (the room the cap and
  `load_of_crush` already use). Below 0.7 nobody minds the crowd; 1.0 is full at crush; 2 means
  the line needs about twice the trains to carry everyone who wants it.
- **Measured.** Of 97 line ids with riders, at High: 55 at least 1.0, 21 at least 1.5, 8 at least
  2.0 (the HBLR's three branches 2.6-3.4, the L 2.41, NJ Transit's North Jersey Coast 2.17 and
  Main/Bergen 2.16, the G 2.11, LIRR Babylon 2.07); the 4 1.90; the 7 0.86, cut to a third 2.14.
  The spread is wide, so a ramp separates fine from short: green to 0.7, yellow-green 0.85,
  yellow 1.0, orange 1.4, red from 2.0.
- **What the player sees.** The same dot, coloured by the ratio. Hover, roughly (stretch names
  illustrative): "2.1 times as
  many riders want this line between 82 St and 74 St as its trains hold at the busiest hour", then
  "91,000 riders a day go another way" (free flow less the final, the (b) figure), then today's
  wait line. The Trains to add count could take the same colour for the period now. Adding trains
  raises the riders who want the line (a shorter wait), so the ratio falls a little less than the
  trains rise; it tells the player which way to go, not an exact count.
- **Ceiling tests:** unaffected. **Cost:** demand keeps each period's free-flow segment loads (every
  population summed, as the crowding rounds' `total`) and sends them with the result: a new WASM
  getter, so its own worktree (CLAUDE.md). The panel computes the ratio and its stretch. About one
  agent task; no solve time.

### Considered and dropped

- **The wait demand charged in the last round** (what the riders priced, and what the trip
  explorer's "Waiting for a train with room" shows): reaches only orange on 2 lines; it measures the
  overload after the rounds have damped it.
- **The worst stop's wait instead of the line's average**: 5-24 min on busy lines today, but noisy
  (the J reads 17.9 at Kosciuszko St with free flow at 0.82 of room, riders pushed there by crowding
  elsewhere).
- **Lowering the dot's thresholds** to the waits that happen now: the cut 7 (1.1 min) and the L at
  full service (0.84) would read the same, so it cannot tell short service from fine.

## 3. Recommendation and stages

(c), with (b)'s count in the hover, keeping the cap and the account as they are. It answers the
player's question ("would more trains carry more riders here?") from a figure the solve already
has, and changes no rule. (a) is a fair realism change for the platforms, but it does not fix the
signal and adds a demand rule; I would leave it unless the map's platforms look too empty.

- **Stage 1 (T-1255):** demand keeps and sends each period's free-flow loads; the schedule dots and
  the Trains to add colour read the ratio, with the hover above; docs/ui-inspectors.md and the
  line panel's hover text follow. Own worktree (WASM getter).
- **Stage 2 (only if she wants it):** (a) at W 20 in both the cap and the account, replacing the
  5,000 platform cap; docs/demand-plain.md and demand-worldwide.md updated.

## 4. Built as T-1255 (Anita picked (a), 2026-10-25)

Anita: riders come and wait when the wait for a train with room is under a threshold, tuned under
the hood so the wait tracks how overloaded a line is, and red at a 15-minute wait. Built in
worktree t1255 (DECISIONS 2026-10-25, T-1255).

- **The queue** (`WAIT_FOR_ROOM_S` 30 min, track/boarding.rs; `kernel::capacity_fit`): a rider
  joins a platform only while the riders ahead get on this train or a whole train due within 30
  minutes, each with this train's room. Demand's cap and the account run the same rule; demand's
  riders coming are the free-flow pass's boardings (at least the rounds'), kept per population in
  the demand worker and sent to the cap as a fourth block of `total`. The account gets them as
  `arrive` (`TrackApi.set_arrivals`) only where demand's queue turned riders away, so its
  boardings stay demand's.
- **The dot** (`Boarding::crowding` slots 9-10, `BUSY_PLATFORM_S`): the average wait for a train
  with room at the platforms where trains come full (a direction at a stop whose riders wait a
  minute or more in the period), each rider turned away counted at 30 minutes. The line-wide
  average (slots 4 and 2) diluted the cut 7 to 5 minutes. Colours: green to 0.2, yellow-green 3,
  yellow 6, orange 9, red 15, deep red 25; Trains to add takes the same wait (orange 9, red 15).

Measured in the app on the real save (`artifacts/trainworld/t1255/survey3.json`, `shots-*.json`;
High's dot):

| line | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| the 7, full service | 0.1 min, green | 1.3 min, green |
| the 7 cut to a third (and the 7X) | 0.9 min, yellow-green | 9.8 min, orange; 14k a day turned away |
| the L | 0.4 min, green | 4.0 min, yellow-green; nobody turned away |
| red lines at High (of 101) | 0 | 6 (the HBLR's branches, PATH, the D) |

The L does not reach orange: in the real save the crowding rounds already keep its riders within
what its 23 trains carry (21% of its peak trains full, none of its riders turned away by the
queue). The free-flow ratio of section 2 (2.41) counts riders who avoid it for the crowd, not for
want of room. Sending the free-flow riders to every platform read the L at 13 min, but those riders
then boarded in the account on lines with room, 10-15% over demand's fares (the cut 7: 539k a
day against 480k), so they go only where the queue turns riders away.

Rail trips (native calib, nyc_real.json): 11,690,112 to 11,774,509 a day (+0.7%). The ceiling
tests (notes/T-636.md) have no network, so neither the queue nor the account enters them. Native
candidate runs (`artifacts/trainworld/t1255/f*.json`, `w*.json`, `meas.py`): a 20-minute
threshold left lines at twice their room short of orange; the rounds' 60-minute wait charge
against a 20-minute one changed nothing (11,815,532 against 11,815,875).
