## Where the time went

Measured in node on the inputs of New York's real network (new game) and of T-007 (lines, edges, edge points
dumped from the clock worker; the module bundled with rolldown, `--cpu-prof`), uncontended, cold first call:

| | New York real | T-007 |
|---|---|---|
| `lineBands` | 0.8 s | 1.4 s |
| `besideSamples` in it | 0.30 s | 0.6 s (search of each sample against nearby segments 0.3 s of it) |
| ribbon at each sample (`ribbonAt`) | 0.18 s | 0.3 s |
| smoothing knots per line (`smoothKnots` later closure) | 0.19 s | 0.25 s |
| `bandPoints`, all lines (not in `lineBands`) | about 0.19 s | about 0.68 s |

`bandPoints` ran in `networkFromState` after `lineBands`, once per line per path entry: for each segment of an
edge it looped over every knot cut (edge points x knots), and per added point `profileAt` scanned the knots
from the start and spread a fresh array. On T-007 that was the biggest single piece of the pass (4,716
calls); in the browser trace it was 0.5 s of the 2.9 s.

## What changed

`bandPoints`: the cuts are walked with a pointer (they are sorted, segments ascend), and the band at a point
and at a segment's middle is read by bisection over the knots (a profile whose knot places are out of order
falls back to `profileAt`'s scan), written straight into the array. Same arithmetic.

Not changed, tried: `besideSamples` (a flat segment table, stamps for candidates, no per-hit `Math.hypot`,
fewer cell insertions) gave nothing measurable (811 -> 796 ms New York, 1370 -> 1384 ms T-007, best of five);
its search is 4.4 million segment tests over 154k samples on T-007 (bundles of tracks), no waste left to cut
without changing the order the records come in. The rest of `lineBands` is the work itself (150k ribbons,
190k knots on T-007).
