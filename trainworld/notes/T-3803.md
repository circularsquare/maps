# T-3803 The next edit waits behind the last edit's full state

Found by T-3801 (notes/T-3801.md): on Anita's 2,647-station save, after an edit the clock worker builds the full
state for 0.6-2.7 s, and an edit made meanwhile waits behind it. Goal: an edit made right after another shows up
within about 0.3 s on that save.

## Setup

- Save: `artifacts/trainworld/t3600-asianita-1852.save` (221 lines, 2,647 stations), clock at speed 2, Build tab, no
  panel, after the final. The machine had 9-13 of 14 cores claimed by other agents throughout (`cores.py list` at
  each run), so costs swing about 2x between runs: shares and same-game A/B are the evidence, not single ms.
- "Shows up": a track piece at its early answer (its stand-ins draw); a stop click or a rename at its rows state
  taken on the page; anything else (a station, a headway, an undo) at the first full state taken that holds it (the
  map needs the network). "Wait": the edit's post to the worker starting it.
- Probes (scratch `editwait\`): `pairs.js` (an edit sequence, each `gap` ms after the last showed up, or each `gap`
  ms after the last was posted, with what the worker ran during each wait), `abname.js` (the A/B below),
  `profile.js` + `prof.mjs` (a CPU profile of the clock worker), `clonecost.js`; the temporary worker stamps as
  `probe-stamps.diff`; results `res-*.json`, `abname-*.json`.

## Where the time goes

**The wait is the previous edit's follow-up** (its full state, trips and boarding hour). Per edit, the worker's work
during the wait (30 edits, show mode, 200 ms after each showed): the previous follow-up, run on its own or forced by
whatever message came next (the hour's `epoch`, `demandFine`, a `blueprintQuote` or `today` read), was 555-1,526 ms
in 8 of the 11 waits over 300 ms. The rest: a `today` read (140-525 ms under this load), the hour turning itself,
demand's snapshot timer (90-420 ms), the next hour's trips (about 190 ms).

It reaches the next edit because some edits show up before their follow-up runs: a stop click and a rename show with
their rows, a track piece with its stand-ins, so the player acts again while the worker is still building. A station,
headway or undo shows only after its own full state, so nothing reordered can bring those under that state's own
cost (0.6-1.8 s here).

**One full state** (CPU profiles of the clock worker, paused clock: `worker.cpuprofile`, 12 mixed edits;
`rename.cpuprofile`, 10 renames):
- the renderer's network (`networkFromState`): about 55% of the full states' time in the mixed run, about 0.3 s a
  rename. Of that, the waiting rooms and car parks (`placeFacilities`, a pass over every edge point near any complex
  with one) about a third, then `legShifts`, `lineBands`, the train samples, the station buildings, one-way marks.
- the rows (`buildState`): about 20% (stations, lines, complex names), about 0.1 s each.
- posting (`postState`, rows states and full ones): about 0.19 s a post in the rename run: `rowsDelta`'s
  field-by-field compare of every row (about 45 ms a post) and the structured clone, the money record alone 25-35 ms
  (it goes with every state).
- the trips and boarding hour: usually 30-600 ms. Twice the boarding hour alone took 1.4 and 3.5 s (a stop change
  and an undo of one; cause not found), and the longest wait seen (3.1 s) was behind one of them.

A rename's follow-up cost 0.6-0.7 s (median, A/B below), most of it the network, which has no names in it.

## The change

`clock.worker.ts`: a name edit (`nameOnly`: renames, binds, a name-only `lineLook` or `stationProps`) already sent its
rows first (T-3793); its follow-up state now takes the train count's path (`sendState(service)`): rows and run tables,
the renderer's network left as it is, no trips, and the schematic's graph sent again when it is open (it reads its
names off the world when a graph comes). A full one still goes if the drawn pieces are to move to a new anchor. The
service path also no longer sends trips when asked for none (every service edit asks for them).

## Not fixed: the general case, options

After a stop click or a track piece the next edit still waits behind a full state of 0.5-1.8 s. Making one full
state cheap enough (under 0.3 s here) is out of reach by caching alone, so the options are:

1. **The follow-up in pieces, a waiting edit first (4-6 h).** The full state built in tasks of about 100 ms
   (the rows, then the network as a generator with a few yields, the post, the trips), outside the message queue. An
   edit arriving at a yield runs next: the rows already built go out as the earlier edit's rows with its answer (so a
   charged edit's money split, `chargeShares`, stays its own, and its answer comes with a world holding it), then one
   full state covers both. The next edit waits at most one piece, except the boarding hour, one WASM call, when it
   spikes (1.4-3.5 s seen after stop changes). Work: `networkFromState` into a generator (its
   caches look self-checking; prove with `?patch=check`), the follow-up merge with its flags (service, trips,
   effective time, ghost first), `sendTrips`' forced run kept whole. Risk: the T-1407 and T-2766 ordering rules.
2. **The network built in a worker of its own (8-12 h).** The clock worker posts the rows and run tables to a
   network worker, which keeps the build caches (T-496, T-1170 shifts) and the posted-changes bookkeeping (T-1029)
   and posts to the page. The clock worker is free after the rows (0.15-0.45 s here). Also takes the network off the
   time every other message waits. The diagram and `?patch=check` move with it.
3. **A cheaper full state (2-3 h, with 1 or alone).** `placeFacilities` per complex, kept while its stations and the
   track near it are unchanged; `rowsDelta` only over rows the edit touched; the money record out of the state
   (it goes with `money` anyway). Perhaps 30-40% off each state; alone it does not reach 0.3 s.

I would do 1, then 3.
