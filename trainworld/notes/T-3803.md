# T-3803 The next edit waits behind the last edit's full state

Found by T-3801 (notes/T-3801.md): on Anita's 2,647-station save, after an edit the clock worker builds the full
state for 0.6-2.7 s, and an edit made meanwhile waits behind it. Goal: an edit made right after another shows up
within about 0.3 s on that save.

## Setup

- Save: `artifacts/trainworld/t3600-asianita-1852.save` (221 lines, 2,647 stations), clock at speed 2, Build tab, no
  panel, after the final. The machine had 9-13 of 14 cores claimed by other agents throughout (`cores.py list` at
  each run), so costs swing about 2x between runs: shares and same-game A/B are the evidence, not single ms.
- "Shows up": a track piece at its early answer (its stand-ins draw); a stop click or a rename at its rows state
  taken on the page; anything else (a station, a headway, an undo) at the first full state taken that holds it (the
  map needs the network). "Wait": the edit's post to the worker starting it.
- Probes (scratch `editwait\`): `pairs.js` (an edit sequence, each `gap` ms after the last showed up, or each `gap`
  ms after the last was posted, with what the worker ran during each wait), `abname.js` (the A/B below),
  `profile.js` + `prof.mjs` (a CPU profile of the clock worker), `clonecost.js`; the temporary worker stamps as
  `probe-stamps.diff`; results `res-*.json`, `abname-*.json`.

## Where the time goes

**The wait is the previous edit's follow-up** (its full state, trips and boarding hour). Per edit, the worker's work
during the wait (30 edits, show mode, 200 ms after each showed): the previous follow-up, run on its own or forced by
whatever message came next (the hour's `epoch`, `demandFine`, a `blueprintQuote` or `today` read), was 555-1,526 ms
in 8 of the 11 waits over 300 ms. The rest: a `today` read (140-525 ms under this load), the hour turning itself,
demand's snapshot timer (90-420 ms), the next hour's trips (about 190 ms).

It reaches the next edit because some edits show up before their follow-up runs: a stop click and a rename show with
their rows, a track piece with its stand-ins, so the player acts again while the worker is still building. A station,
headway or undo shows only after its own full state, so nothing reordered can bring those under that state's own
cost (0.6-1.8 s here).

**One full state** (CPU profiles of the clock worker, paused clock: `worker.cpuprofile`, 12 mixed edits;
`rename.cpuprofile`, 10 renames):
- the renderer's network (`networkFromState`): about 55% of the full states' time in the mixed run, about 0.3 s a
  rename. Of that, the waiting rooms and car parks (`placeFacilities`, a pass over every edge point near any complex
  with one) about a third, then `legShifts`, `lineBands`, the train samples, the station buildings, one-way marks.
- the rows (`buildState`): about 20% (stations, lines, complex names), about 0.1 s each.
- posting (`postState`, rows states and full ones): about 0.19 s a post in the rename run: `rowsDelta`'s
  field-by-field compare of every row (about 45 ms a post) and the structured clone, the money record alone 25-35 ms
  (it goes with every state).
- the trips and boarding hour: usually 30-600 ms. Twice the boarding hour alone took 1.4 and 3.5 s (a stop change
  and an undo of one; cause not found), and the longest wait seen (3.1 s) was behind one of them.

A rename's follow-up cost 0.6-0.7 s (median, A/B below), most of it the network, which has no names in it.

## The change

`clock.worker.ts`: a name edit (`nameOnly`: renames, binds, a name-only `lineLook` or `stationProps`) already sent its
rows first (T-3793); its follow-up state now takes the train count's path (`sendState(service)`): rows and run tables,
the renderer's network left as it is, no trips, and the schematic's graph sent again when it is open (it reads its
names off the world when a graph comes). A full one still goes if the drawn pieces are to move to a new anchor. The
service path also no longer sends trips when asked for none (every service edit asks for them).

## Not fixed: the general case, options

After a stop click or a track piece the next edit still waits behind a full state of 0.5-1.8 s. Making one full
state cheap enough (under 0.3 s here) is out of reach by caching alone, so the options are:

1. **The follow-up in pieces, a waiting edit first (4-6 h).** The full state built in tasks of about 100 ms
   (the rows, then the network as a generator with a few yields, the post, the trips), outside the message queue. An
   edit arriving at a yield runs next: the rows already built go out as the earlier edit's rows with its answer (so a
   charged edit's money split, `chargeShares`, stays its own, and its answer comes with a world holding it), then one
   full state covers both. The next edit waits at most one piece, except the boarding hour, one WASM call, when it
   spikes (1.4-3.5 s seen after stop changes). Work: `networkFromState` into a generator (its
   caches look self-checking; prove with `?patch=check`), the follow-up merge with its flags (service, trips,
   effective time, ghost first), `sendTrips`' forced run kept whole. Risk: the T-1407 and T-2766 ordering rules.
2. **The network built in a worker of its own (8-12 h).** The clock worker posts the rows and run tables to a
   network worker, which keeps the build caches (T-496, T-1170 shifts) and the posted-changes bookkeeping (T-1029)
   and posts to the page. The clock worker is free after the rows (0.15-0.45 s here). Also takes the network off the
   time every other message waits. The diagram and `?patch=check` move with it.
3. **A cheaper full state (2-3 h, with 1 or alone).** `placeFacilities` per complex, kept while its stations and the
   track near it are unchanged; `rowsDelta` only over rows the edit touched; the money record out of the state
   (it goes with `money` anyway). Perhaps 30-40% off each state; alone it does not reach 0.3 s.

I would do 1, then 3.

## Option 3 (fullstate)

A cheaper full state, all three pieces done, nothing approximated (the outputs are the same bytes; no DECISIONS line).

- **Waiting rooms and car parks kept per complex** (`map/facilityPlace.ts` `FacilityCache`, held in the network cache
  `NetCache.facilities`). The segment grid is kept and updated edge by edge (an edge's points are the same array while
  it is unchanged) and platform by platform; a complex is placed again only when its stations' inputs changed, a
  segment went in or out of a grid cell its last search read, the station buildings changed, or a room or lot placed
  before it (in the build's order) moved near its tries (twice the rects' half sizes plus the pad, which bounds
  `rectsMeet`'s separating-axes test). The near cells (what goes in the grid) and the scale are compared too; when
  they move (a facility complex gains, loses or moves a station; the draw anchor moves) everything is placed from
  scratch. Station segments are owned by station id now, not index, so a station added earlier in the list moves
  nothing. Every comparison is against the cache's own last inputs, so an undo, a load or a merge is only more
  changed inputs: nothing can go stale. On the A/B save, 0 of 72 complexes were placed again in every one of 192
  builds (the probes' edits are away from the complexes); the edits beside them were proved by the checks below.
- **rowsDelta** no longer clears and refills its maps: a row that is the same object as last posted (the rows keep
  unchanged rows, stateRows.ts) is passed with one lookup, `by` is written only for changed rows, and gone ids are
  dropped only when the map holds more than the rows. Small: 5 -> 3.5 ms a post (it was 45 ms a post in the rename
  profile above, on a busier machine; most of what is left is the 30,000 lookups).
- **The money record as its changes** (`workers/valuePatch.ts`, `moneyPatch` on states and "money" messages). Whole it
  is 2.2 MB of JSON on this save, 2.16 MB of it the regions' ledgers; its structured clone was 35-65 ms a post (170 ms
  for a rows state under load). The worker keeps a private copy of the last record posted and sends a patch: per key,
  per array entry after dropping the entries a capped ledger lost from its start, keys in order. The page applies it
  to its last record (a serial each side; a patch against a record it does not hold asks for a whole state, as a rows
  delta that cannot be taken does). The diff costs 12-21 ms a post (walking the record; `moneyState()` still builds
  it whole each time, about 8 ms); with the clock running a patch is about 72 KB (the regions' entries for the day and
  hour), 97 KB for the hourly "money" message.

## Option 1 (editfirst)

Built on option 3. The follow-up state goes in pieces and an edit goes ahead of it; nothing is approximated (the same
network, money and trips; an earlier edit's rows and answer now go before a later edit's, by design), so no DECISIONS
line. Advisor called three times: before building (the single cache commit, no yield between a post and its trips, the
carry surviving a refused edit, money taken again at the post, a narrow jump), before the final measurements (the two
holes under "Three holes found on the way", the T-1540 rerun, the mutation test), and before handing back (the third
hole, and a proof with charged edits).

### What changed

- `map/network.ts`: `networkSteps`, a generator; `networkFromState` runs it to the end. Yields after `legShifts`, the
  edge pass, the lines, the speeds and track parts, and the stations; the buildings, waiting rooms, passages, marks and
  path tables are the last piece. The network cache is written only in the last piece (it was written pass by pass),
  so a build dropped at a yield leaves it as the last finished build did. No cached entry is changed in place anywhere
  in the build (checked), and the module memos the build calls (lineBands' `memo` and `last`, corridors' `cache`) are
  kept against their last call's own inputs, as the facility cache is.
- `workers/clock.worker.ts`:
  - `stateSteps`: `sendState` in pieces (the rows, then the network's pieces, then the post with its trips and boarding
    hour, never split, T-648). It yields only with a network posted and no anchor move pending. The cash and money
    record are taken again at the post (the money tick and demand's money messages run between pieces).
  - The follow-up is a `FollowUp` record (`StateAsk`: network or not, name, train count, its moment, trips, ghost
    first; the edits' work after it; who still waits for a world) instead of a closure, so two can be one (`joined`).
    After its boarding steps it runs a piece a timer task outside the message queue (`stepThenRun`, `pump`). Previews
    are answered between pieces; any other message, and trips from a timer, run the rest of it whole first (T-1407 as
    before, `sendTrips`' forced run included).
  - `goesAhead`, `standAside`: an edit of the main path (not fares, loans, links or buildings, which post their own
    state) that changes no service (`isServiceOp`: a train count's lead is sized to a train count's state), coming to
    a follow-up that builds the network. An edit in that follow-up still waiting for a world holding it (its answer not
    sent, or an early answer's money split the page reads off the next state) gets the rows built so far, or new ones,
    as a rows state and then its answer; the rest becomes a carry, taken into the new edit's follow-up (one state for
    both, never held: the earlier edit's network does not wait for this one's pointer to rest), or started on its own
    after the message when the new edit made none (refused, a stale game).
  - Held follow-ups (T-2404, T-2766) join as before; a join drops a state already being built. A joined follow-up
    runs every edit's work after the state (its snapshot timer, `keep`, an autosave when it paid); the old held join
    ran only the last two edits' (a run of stop clicks lost the rest, harmless since each does the same). `keep`, a
    whole save of the track model, is 3-5 ms here.
  - `release` starts the held state's pieces at once and ends its hold, where it ran it whole.

### Three holes found on the way (all fixed)

- A follow-up's 5 s cap could be dodged: a joining click or a going-ahead edit dropped a state already being built
  even past `until`, so quick clicks for ever would never get their network. Now an edit coming 5 s or more after the
  follow-up's first edit neither joins nor goes ahead: the state is run first, as the old cap did. In the bursts below
  a full state came at least every 7.5-7.8 s (5 s plus the state) in both arms.
- `release` only set a timer, so the next ghost's `holdOn` could hold the state again; it now starts it at once and
  sets `until` to now.
- (After the measurements.) `later()`'s "run the earlier follow-up now" branch, reached when an edit passes a held
  follow-up and is then not held itself (an early piece that paid, or one that changed a line for demand), could finish
  a state already begun, its rows from before the edit, with trips from after it. `later()` now drops whatever was
  begun, so that state is built afresh holding both edits, as the old code did.

What piles up is the clicks' own cost, outside option 1: each click is about 0.4-0.8 s of the worker's time on this
save (the edit itself 220-470 ms, its rows 100-180 ms, the rows post 50-170 ms), and the page sends the next only once
the last is answered, so at a click every 0.35 s the page's queue grows by 0.1-0.5 s a click whatever happens to the
full states. Option 1 takes the full states out of the clicks' way (the worker's oldest unanswered stayed under 3 s
in every new-arm burst). On a save as big as the player's (a browser at 6 GB) each click presumably costs several
times this, which at quick clicks reaches the notice's 3 minutes only if the clicks run for a long time or something
else stalls; the long first burst after the final (old arm) is the likelier match.
No leak seen: the clock worker's WASM memory stayed at 325 MB over 144 clicks (one growth, 289 -> 325 MB, in the first
burst of a game), the held charges at 0; the page's heap went up and down between 520 and 880 MB. The worker's JS heap
could not be read (no `performance.memory` in a worker, and the page is not cross-origin isolated, so no
`measureUserAgentSpecificMemory`).

one fix made here** (a paid edit's charge could land in the unassigned region; below). Network counts match HEAD, the

### 2. Refusal: the slip is the page's queue, on HEAD too

The refusal answer, click to notice: option 1 median 81 ms, over 300 ms in 1 of 10 (404); HEAD median 64 ms, over 300
in 2 of 10 (342, 1,042). Split three ways (click to sent, sent to answered, answered to notice): the over-300 cases are
the page holding the click until the free clicks before it are answered (311 ms on option 1; 279 and 540 on HEAD).
Inside the worker the refused edit waits at most one piece: sent to answered minus the worker's own time, 13-79 ms on
option 1; HEAD's 1,042 waited 488 ms behind a whole held state (run by a `riders` message). So yes, a refusal can wait
behind a piece, and that includes the last one (the post with its trips and boarding hour, 0.46-0.78 s at worst on the
2,647-station save, above). Not fixed: a refusal is known only once the edit is applied, so the soonest answer is the
end of the running piece, and shortening the last piece means splitting the post from its trips (T-648) or the
boarding hour (one WASM call), each a task of its own. HEAD waits for the whole state there, so option 1 is no worse.

### 3. Money split: a fix

There is no per-line record of charges (a line's days hold its fares and running cost only), so the split read is the
page's `chargeShares` per edit (build and trains by region, trains by the line's route km when the answer names a line)
and each region's build and trains for the day. The paid stop clicks (t2766's fixture: five stops out, the fleet
trimmed, put back until one buys trains; scratch `step3.mjs`) give nothing to compare: on both arms their train charges (20.0 and 22.5) stay in the unassigned region,
the page's split for them is empty (`chargeShares` gets no line for an `insertStop`; old code too). 4 pairs a side,
plain edits and `rows` clicks, 100 ms and 4 s apart: the same charges every time.

A blueprint piece constructed, then a paid stop click 100 ms or 4 s later, is the real test. On option 1 as handed over
the first piece built in an area put its whole charge (35.87) in the unassigned region where HEAD put it in `nyc`.
Cause, from the page's timelines: a second full state reached the page between the construct and its answer (states
at 244 and 393 ms, the answer at 502 ms); the page splits a paid edit's charge between the world before the last
state it took and that world, and both held the piece. On HEAD the same extra state came after the answer (271, the
answer 366, 425). The extra state is `sendState()` called outside the message queue when fetched ground (or streets)
reprice the blueprint (one of three: `ensureBlocks`, the roads loader, the bus streets; the stamp sat in the helper
they now share, so which one is not known): a temporary stamp there saw it land while the
construct's follow-up was begun and still owed its answer in 3 of 6 runs in one game, 2 of 6 in another. HEAD's whole
follow-up usually ran before such a callback came; option 1's pieces leave room between them (HEAD meets the same race
when its states are slow, below). The same state also
meant the begun build would later post rows older than the ones the page had just taken.

Fix (`clock.worker.ts` `sendStateOutside`, used by those three callers): as for trips sent from a timer, a waiting
follow-up goes first, whole, then that state; a state begun is begun again on the new model (a held one stays held,
its pieces restarted if they had begun, so `pump` never finds no state to build). HEAD's order back: the edit's state,
its answer, the extra state. Its cost: with a follow-up waiting, the fetch's task now builds two full states back to
back (the follow-up's, then the extra one), 0.3-0.5 s on the New York save here (the fixed runs' states 110-360 ms
apart), about a second on the 2,647-station save; HEAD pays the same whenever the fetch lands while its follow-up waits
(the second of its two states is redundant when the follow-up built the network; not trimmed, to keep the change
small). The held branch (a stop click's or a track piece's held state) was not met in the runs: the stamps saw only a
waiting follow-up or none, so its restart rests on reading.

After the fix, 6 construct pairs, each in an area of its own, the same areas in the same order on each arm (`cmp3.mjs`):
charge and split the same to the last digit on HEAD and two fixed games, every piece's charge in `nyc`; in the fixed
games A's answer went ahead of its state (a rows state, then the answer) in 3 and 4 of the 6 runs, on HEAD in none.

18 stop clicks 350 ms apart through the line tool on line 2 (each of nine stops out, the one before it back in, the last
back), against the same clicks made slowly, on option 1 before the fix (it changes nothing a stop click meets): the same
46 stops in the same order.
