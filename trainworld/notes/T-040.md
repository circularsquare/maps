# T-040 track model in Rust

Done 2026-10-09 (started 2026-10-08, paused overnight). Code in `sim/src/track/`, in the default
build (the app's wasm carries it). Design from SPEC 6 and notes/T-008.md; where implementation
forced a choice, it is under "Design calls" and in DECISIONS 2026-10-09.

## Data layout

- **Ids** are stable `u32` per kind (node, edge, line) with free lists; deleting and undoing keeps
  ids. Allocation (`alloc_*`) hands out an id; the op using it claims it. Saves renumber densely.
- **Inputs**, one `Vec` per field: nodes (x, y, level, flying, platform length, name), edges (a, b,
  tracks, PIs span), lines (name, colour, stops span, path span, tph[3], dwell, turnaround, cars).
- **Variable-length data** (PIs, pieces, height breakpoints, water spans, stops, paths) lives in
  `Pool<T>`: one flat vector, each owner holding a span; a replaced run becomes garbage, and a pool
  compacts itself when more than half is garbage.
- **Derived per edge**: pieces (straight or arc: start chainage, length, start point, heading,
  signed curvature), height breakpoints, water spans, length, cost, free running time. **Per
  node**: ports (up to 8 edge ends: side A/B against the node heading, right-to-left rank by
  lateral offset 200 m out) and heading. **Network**: crossings (derived records), the 1 km grid,
  the edge-to-lines index (CSR).
- **Coordinates**: f64 local metres, x east, y north of the city origin (the pack's equirectangular
  frame). Positions and set radii are quantised to 1 mm on input, so derived geometry is a
  deterministic function of the save and a reload is bit-identical (tested).
- **Per line** (`service.rs`): two `Run`s (path in travel order, node and stop offsets, speed
  limits widened by half the train length, free profile, holds and profile per demand level).

## How an edit runs

`TrackWorld::apply(op)`: apply raw (setters record what they touched) -> `derive` only that (re-fit
touched edges, grid out/in, ports of their nodes, crossings of touched edges against the grid,
broken flags) -> validate the touched scope -> if it adds problems, roll back and return them ->
`Service::update` rebuilds the lines on touched track or stations, runs the capacity pass if
anything it reads changed, and recomputes profiles only where holds changed -> dirty sets and
cost. The inverse op goes on the undo stack.

Ops: `Node`, `Edge`, `Station`, `Line` (whole-object setters; `None` removes), `Schedule`,
`Split { edge, s, node, new_edge }` (geometry unchanged: inside an arc a new PI each side with
the arc's radius; neighbouring auto radii frozen to their value; line paths rewritten),
`DeleteEdge` (each line using it rerouted by the fastest path or left broken), `Batch`.

**Dirty** (`world::Dirty`): zoom-12 tiles of the old and new extent, edges, nodes, positions of
crossings removed or added, resources on touched track or used by changed lines, lines changed,
and from the service: lines rebuilt, profiles recomputed, lines sharing a resource with a changed
line (SPEC 6.5's one step), and lines whose service changed for demand (stops, frequency, or a
stop-to-stop time moving 5 s or more).

**Cost**: `cost_before`, `cost_after` (whole network, US$M), `built_before`, `built_after` (what
is constructed) and `charge` (the increase in constructed cost; nothing is refunded). Blueprint
and construct came with T-055: notes/T-025.md.

## Profiles

Minimum time between standstills = pointwise minimum of the speed limit, the forward
acceleration envelope and the backward braking envelope; in v² against distance all three are
straight lines, so every switch point is exact. Two traction bands (1.0 m/s² to 60 km/h, 0.5
above), braking 0.9 m/s². The train's reference point is its middle; a curve limit applies from
half a train before the curve to half a train after it. Stops: the train's middle at the station
node, or at the platform's middle at a dead end. Holds: within 50 m of a stop (or at a platform)
they become extra dwell there; elsewhere a stop in front of the point if the delay is at least
what stopping costs, else a 300 m slow zone whose speed is bisected to within 0.1 s.

## Capacity

As SPEC 6.2. Resources: track sections (directed double track merged across two-ended nodes
while the set of line directions is the same), single-track sections (merged across two-ended
non-station nodes, run time + 60 s, both directions), platform tracks (by arrival side; single
track stations have one), termini, junction moves (track positions: ports right to left, each
double-track port's two tracks by running side; moves cross when `(pA1-pA2)(pB1-pB2) < 0`; a flying
junction has none), flat crossings (each direction of each route, loaded by the whole other
route). Delay rounded to whole seconds, dropped under 2 s.

Not modelled yet: a short-turn reversing at a through station does not conflict with the other
direction there; a single-track station has one platform track for both directions.

## Keyframes: the format for T-025

**What changes from T-002.** T-002 gave each train one `(line, offset, speed, t_ref)` and the
shader extrapolated at constant speed. Trains that stop and accelerate need more than that. Now:

1. **Phase tables**, one per profile `p = (line * 2 + run) * 3 + level` (run 0 = the line's stops
   in order, run 1 = reversed; level 0/1/2 = high/medium/low demand). `TrackApi.phases()`:
   `Float32Array`, 4 floats per phase, `(t, s, v, a)`:
   - `t`: seconds since the trip's departure from its first stop (the first phase has t = 0);
   - `s`: metres along the run's path from the first stop's node;
   - `v`: speed at `t`, m/s; `a`: acceleration until the next phase, m/s² (0 cruising or standing,
     negative braking).
   The phase in force at trip time τ is the last with `t <= τ`; then, with `τ' = τ - t`,
   `s(τ) = s + v τ' + a τ'² / 2` (for `a < 0` clamp `τ'` to `v / -a`). A trip ends with braking
   into the last stop; after `duration` it stands there for the turnaround.
2. **Profile meta**, `TrackApi.profile_meta()`: `Float32Array`, 4 floats per profile:
   `(first phase index, phase count, trip seconds incl. turnaround, path length m)`. Count 0 = no
   service (line broken or absent). Indices stay exact in f32 below 16.7M phases.
3. **Trips**, `TrackApi.trips(epoch)`: `Float32Array`, 2 floats per trip: `(profile index,
   departure - epoch)`, for every trip on the track at any time in `[epoch, epoch + 3600)`
   (departures may be negative: trips still running from the previous hour). Computed in f64 from
   the schedule (SPEC 4.2 periods in `params::PERIOD_HOURS`, both directions leave their first stop
   on the same clock). The hourly re-base is: call `trips(next_epoch)`, transfer, upload. Phase
   tables and meta change only after an edit (`dirty_lines()`).
4. **Path geometry** for the renderer: `TrackApi.run_polyline(line, run, step)` samples a run's
   path every `step` m (x, y local metres). Run 1 is run 0 reversed, so one texture per line works:
   run 1's offset `s` is `length - s` along run 0.

In the vertex shader: `τ = u_time - dep` (both relative to the epoch, f32), skip the instance if
`τ < 0` or `τ > trip seconds`, binary-search the profile's phases by `t` (8 steps for 256
phases), evaluate `s`, map to the line texture. The `(edge, direction, offset)` form of SPEC 2.1 is
`Service::segment_at(line, run, s)` (not exported yet).

## API for the clock worker (`wasm.rs`, `TrackApi`)

- `new(origin_lon, origin_lat, right_hand)`, `set_water(cell, x0, y0, w, h, water_row,
  water_x)` (the pack arrays as they are), `load(bytes)`, `save()`.
- Ids: `new_node_id`, `new_edge_id`, `new_line_id`.
- Edits, each returning 0 or the number of problems (`issues()` as debug text): `set_node`,
  `remove_node`, `set_edge(id, a, b, tracks, pis)` (pis: x, y, radius 0 = auto, level per PI),
  `delete_edge`, `split_edge`, `set_station`, `remove_station`, `set_line(id, stops, high,
  medium, low, dwell, turnaround, cars, colour, name)` (path = fastest route), `remove_line`,
  `set_schedule`, `undo`, `redo`.
- After an edit: `last_charge`, `total_cost`, `dirty_tiles` (x, y pairs), `dirty_edges`,
  `dirty_lines`, `dirty_demand_lines`.
- Drawing: `edge_polyline(e, lateral)` (±2 m for the two tracks), `preview(vertices, tracks)` ->
  `[cost, ok, length, x, y, ...]`.
- Service: `stop_times(line, run, level)` (time of each stop from the start of the line, SPEC
  6.3), `line_summary(line)` (per level: round trip, trains needed, delay), `phases`,
  `profile_meta`, `trips(epoch)`, `run_polyline`, `train_at` (check), `markers(level)` (x, y,
  utilisation, delay, kind for resources at 75%+).

Typed arrays come back as copies the worker can transfer. Missing on purpose: the station
inspector and junction inspector queries (T-031 will want users per resource), names, stop-to-stop
times (`LineSvc::stop_to_stop` exists in Rust).

## Tests (`cargo test --release --manifest-path sim/Cargo.toml`, 28 pass)

- Geometry: auto radius (half legs, whole leg at a node, cap 1,796 m), set radius too small or not
  fitting, continuity and tangency, speeds against the SPEC table, ramps centred and direction-
  independent, ramp too short, arc bounding boxes.
- Costs: SPEC 6.4's example (20 km at -2 with 18 stations = 1,980 + 1,800 = US$3.78B), level
  multipliers, ramps at the dearer level, water 2x, single track, stations, junctions, crossings.
- Crossings: straight/arc/arc hits, collinear and same-circle overlap, the grid.
- Profiles: 1 km straight stop to stop = 67.51 s, equal to the hand calculation to 1e-9;
  limits respected with train-length widening; dwell; holds of 5-120 s add their delay within
  0.5 s (short ones as a slow approach, long ones as a stop).
- Capacity: SPEC 6.2's table (27 s at 75%, 81 s at 90%, 171 s at 100%, 425 s at 110%); end to end
  on a Y junction with two lines at 15/18 tph: exactly one crossing pair (towards the branch
  against in from the main line, right-hand running), 75% / 27 s and 90% / 81 s, the hold in the
  profile; a flying junction removes it.
- Edits: a line's times, fleet and trips; split keeps every point within 1 cm, times within
  0.05 s, wakes no demand, undo restores the edge exactly, redo works; refused edits change nothing
  (radius, kink at a node, station in use, platform too long); flat / separated / ramp crossings;
  schedule change dirties sharing lines and demand; delete breaks a line and undo restores it;
  save round trip byte-identical with equal cost and times.
- **Water, real pack**: a straight from Times Square west across the Hudson at ground level is
  refused (ground over water); at -1 with ramps on land it builds, 1,475 m of 3,500 m priced as
  water, US$429M. Skipped if `data/packs/nyc.*` is missing.

## Benchmarks

Synthetic network (`bench.rs`): random-walk corridors, branches at junctions curving away at the
node, stations every ~4 km, one stopping line per corridor (branch lines start on the trunk) and
express lines; same-level corridors cross flat. Median of 21 apply+undo pairs; one thread;
desktop shared with other agents. WASM: Node 22.20, `wasm-pack --target nodejs --release`,
warm (second call in the process).

2,068 route-km, 494 edges, 4,011 pieces, 480 stations, 60 lines, 38 flat crossings, 1,247
resources (394 at 75%+ in the peak: the synthetic schedules are heavy), 31k phases:

| | native | WASM |
|---|---|---|
| derive everything from inputs (geometry, crossings, all profiles, capacity) | 25 ms | 21 ms |
| capacity pass alone | 1.4 ms | 1.3 ms |
| move a PI 3 m on the busiest edge (10 lines, +12 sharing) | 4.2 ms | 3.6 ms |
| move a PI on a 1-line edge | 1.4 ms | 1.3 ms |
| change one line's trains per hour | 1.7 ms | 1.4 ms |
| split the busiest edge | 4.3 ms | 3.8 ms |
| 5 km of track no line uses | 0.02 ms | 0.03 ms |
| delete the busiest edge (lines reroute or break); its undo | 1.3; 4.3 ms | 1.1; 3.7 ms |
| load a save (decode + derive) | 21 ms | 27 ms |

WASM is as fast as native here (likely the Windows allocator; many small vectors). At 10,429
route-km, 3,085 stations, 400 lines (11,392 resources, 292k phases): derive 337 ms, capacity pass
9.4-10.7 ms (7 ms building resources for 50k users), moving a PI on a 23-line edge 34-38 ms, one
line's schedule 12-13 ms, unused track 0.09 ms. The global capacity pass is the floor there;
incremental resources are follow-up T-050.

**Save size**: 28,065 bytes raw for 2,068 km (19.8 KB deflated); 163,572 bytes raw for 10,429 km
with 3,085 stations and 400 lines, 104 KB deflated (~100 KB per 10,000 route-km). T-008 estimated
~170 KB with a PI every 0.77 km against ~1.1 km here: in line.

**App wasm**: 18.65 kB -> 378 kB (146 kB gzipped) with the track model in the default build.

## Design calls (DECISIONS 2026-10-09)

- Flat crossings derived from geometry, not stored nodes.
- Keyframes as shared phase tables (above).
- Traction 1.0 / 0.5 m/s², braking 0.9 m/s².
- Hold as slow approach vs stop by what stopping costs, not a fixed 30 s.
- Edits refused only for problems they add; ground over water = within 4 m of the ground; a
  station may lose its track (lines break); forks (two ends leaving one way) allowed.
- Capacity pass global, skipped when nothing it reads changed.
- Smaller ones (not in DECISIONS): R_CAP is the exact 1,796 m; positions quantised to 1 mm (not
  10 cm: a PI near a node must keep the node's heading within 3 mrad); intersections within 25 m
  of a node both edges share are the node; branches must curve away at the node (a straight on
  top of the main line is an overlap); water sampled every 12.5 m; periods AM peak 6-10, midday
  10-16, PM peak 16-20, evening 20-24, night 0-6 (in `params.rs`; the demand kernel has no period
  hours yet, it uses 3 h for the peak hour factor).
