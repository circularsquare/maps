# T-1647: cheaper market searches per node (2026-11-05)

**Outcome: the native Japan pass is 17.6% faster (CPU), every figure identical to the bit.** Exact
changes only; the WASM interface is unchanged.

## What changed

In `sim/src/demand/kernel/search.rs` (`Search`):

- **The queue keeps copies** (`IdHeap`): a lower cost for a queued node pushes a copy and the stale
  one is skipped when it pops (a node or change node is settled by then; a boarding's turn is queued
  once, shown in the code's three push sites). No place per id to keep up on every move. A pop takes
  the hole down to a leaf by the least of four children (no compare with the moved entry on the
  way), then lets the last entry rise. Same pops in the same order: keys are unique (cost bits, id).
- **A route node's edges in, in one record** (`RnStep`, made in `Search::new`): whether riders
  board it, its left-behind wait, and the ride into it with its cost, the previous node's wait and
  whether riders board there. The settle reads it instead of the incoming-edge lists.
- **Incoming edge costs in edge order** (`rev_cost`), not through `rev_edge` into `adj_cost`.
- **A station whose arrivals are all settled** returns at once from `settle_station` (16% of
  station settles on Japan).
- **`ban`** reads, per route node, the departures of its line that an arrival off it may not take
  (`banned`, `banned_all`, made with the `Search`, once a pass), not every departure of the station.
- **Arrivals barred their station's only line whole** are dropped when met, not filed under a key
  that then makes no change node.

In `sim/src/demand/intercity/pass.rs` (`pass_window`, the longer trips): each pair's entries in the
far-zone table (`st`) are looked up once per start station run and once per end station, not twice
per station pair; the adds go in the same order, so the table is the same to the bit.

`t1545_dump` prints the solve's own CPU milliseconds (`thread_cpu_ms`, Windows' thread times).

## Where the time went (probe builds, rdtsc, reverted)

- On base, the searches were about 57% of the pass: `Search::run` 104 G ticks of the pass's ~180,
  `entry_costs` 8, `settle_station` 31 of the run, the assignment walks 14, the searches again 3.
  `trip_km` and the fare columns cost nothing here (a straight fare line, no income groups).
- Per search on Japan: about 30,300 pops (9,200 stations, 18,500 route nodes, 1,800 boarding
  turns, 760 change nodes, 1,900 stale copies), the queue about 370 long.
- `settle_station`: 2.7 arrivals a station, 1.4 not settled; 71% of those barred (mostly from their
  only line), 7.1M change nodes made in all.
- After the search changes, by `pass_window`'s sections: searching 60%, the longer trips 27% (the
  logsums about a quarter of that, the far-zone table's lookups most of the rest: 127M station
  pairs, two galloping lookups each), assignment 11%, commutes 2%.

## Tried and dropped

- **A front slot** (one entry below the whole queue, pushed and popped with no sifting): it caught
  one push a search, the destination's; a settled node's next is almost never below the queue.
- **`ban` from per-arrival departure lists**, no per-station list of boarded lines: no faster
  (1.5-2.6% slower in each round of set b).

## What else could go

- The queue is still about a sixth of the search (pops ~66 ticks each); a radix queue would need
  every push at or above the last pop, true in exact arithmetic but not proven under rounding.
- `entry_costs` (about 4% of the pass) sorts each station's offers; an exact skip needs the pooled
  cost's rounding bounded, which it is not.
- 215 of 8,394 searches are repeats (`WINDOW_STRATEGY_BYTES`, 192 MB); more room would drop them,
  exact, for memory.
- The commutes and everyday outings put four table entries per station pair the same way; not hot
  on Japan (2%), so left.
