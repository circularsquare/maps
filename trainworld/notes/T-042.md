# T-042 notes: work from home makes no commute

SPEC 4.1 (Anita): people who work at their own home make no commute. LODES places a remote
worker at the employer's office, and the gravity used every resident and every job, so it made
commutes for people who do not travel. The pack now carries commuters per cell and the gravity
balances on them. Code: `pipeline/wfh.py`, `build_city.py` (`commute_by_county`), the Rust reader
(`sim/src/demand/pack.rs`); format: notes/T-004.md, "Commuters".

## Source: ACS 2019-2023 5-year, table-based summary files

The Census API needs a key now; the table-based summary files at
`www2.census.gov/programs-surveys/acs/summary_file/2023/table-based-SF/data/5YRData/` do not. One
pipe-delimited file per table for every geography in the country:

- **B08301** (means of transportation to work, 88 MB): workers 16+ (`_001`) and worked from home
  (`_021`) by tract. **Home end**: each block's population x (1 - its tract's share).
- **B08126** (means of transportation to work by industry, 185 MB): workers by 14 industries
  (`_002`-`_015`) and the same industries working from home (`_092`-`_105`), summed over the 25
  counties' tracts. **Work end**: each LODES sector's jobs x (1 - its industry's share), mapping the
  20 CNS sectors onto the 14 ACS industries (`wfh.CNS_TO_ACS`; utilities go with transport,
  management and admin with professional, education with health care, as ACS groups them).

The pipeline cuts each file down to the city's tracts once (`data/raw/acs2023_5y_b08301_tracts.csv`,
`..._b08126_tracts.csv`, under 1 MB each); the nationwide files are not needed for a rebuild.

**Why by sector, and what it assumes.** The work end needs to know which jobs are done from home,
and only the employer's side knows that. ACS asks residents, so B08126 gives the share of the
region's residents in each industry who worked from home; applying it to the jobs located here
assumes the region's jobs in an industry are remote at the rate its resident workers in that
industry are. That is a published split (information 31%, professional 28%, finance 25%, retail
10%, construction 6.5%), and it moves commutes out of Midtown more than out of the Bronx, which a
uniform cut would not. A finer split (by tract of work, or by employer) has no open source.

**Joins.** ACS 2023 uses 2020 tracts, but Connecticut's planning regions as county equivalents
(tract codes unchanged), so Connecticut tracts join on state + tract code. Suffolk renumbered
some tracts after 2020 (no 1456xx or 1461xx, some 1224xx in ACS 2023): those 771 blocks (76,505
people) take Suffolk's county-wide share.

**T-014's moves carry their commuters**: n moved jobs of a sector take n x (1 - its share)
commuters with them (home care and Northwell at the health care rate, JetBlue at transport's).
Home care aides are counted as commuters: their work is at other people's homes.

## Shares (resident workers in the 25 counties, ACS 2019-2023)

15.0% of 10.26M resident workers worked from home. By industry: agriculture and mining 17.0%,
construction 6.5%, manufacturing 15.5%, wholesale 15.6%, retail 9.7%, transport and utilities
6.9%, information 31.0%, finance and real estate 25.3%, professional/management/admin 27.9%,
education/health care/social assistance 10.7%, arts/accommodation/food 8.7%, other services
13.1%, public administration 10.0%, armed forces 12.6%.

The 2019-2023 window straddles the pandemic (2020-2021 at the peak of remote work), so these
shares are above 2023 alone; the 2024 5-year release would lean later. Noted, not corrected.
