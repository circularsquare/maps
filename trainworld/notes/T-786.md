# T-786: parallel country batches, the joint group split (T-781), runner checks (T-775) (2026-10-20)

Anita asked for parallel Europe runs. A batch's regions used about 4 of its 9 cores while its joint
solve used them all (T-779), so several countries should build at once and join once.

## What was built

1. **Locks** (`pipeline/shared_lock.py`): one lock file per name in `data/work/world_run/locks/`,
   held with `msvcrt.locking` (fcntl elsewhere), so the OS frees it when the process dies;
   re-entrant within a process (register.py holds `registry` and calls market_zones, which takes it
   too); `write_atomic` (temp file and rename, retried while Windows has the file open) and
   `read_json`. Every read-modify-write of a shared file holds one:
   - `plan`: world_plan.json: `Run.set_row`, row claims, world_plan.py (a replan or zone rows hold
     it whole, a few minutes).
   - `registry`: registry.json, market-rows.json, data/work/market_rows.json and the .market.json
     files: register.py (whole, market and trips included), market_zones.py, zones.py,
     zone_boxes.py, `Run.unregister`.
   - `made`: data/work/world_run/made.json (the runner's record of caches it made).
   - `extract-<name>`: a region holds its extracts from download to clean-up, so a run needing the
     same extract waits instead of finding it deleted (data/raw is shared, so this is the main tree's
     lock even for a scratch tree).
   - `batch-<batch>`: held for a run's life; a row's `run.claim` names its batch and counts while
     that lock is held, so `select` leaves out rows another live run claimed. A second run started in
     the same second gets its pid added to the batch name.
   - `gate-<batch>` and `packs`: gates of several runs overlap, a joint solve overlaps none. The
     solve holds `packs` and waits until no other `gate-*` lock is held; a gate takes its own lock,
     and backs off while `packs` is held (writer first, so a waiting solve is not starved). A gate's
     `other_packs_unchanged` now skips packs that left the registry since its snapshot (another run's
     park or rebuild).
   Other shared outputs checked and left alone: gate results, snapshots and logs are per batch; the
   joint files are written by solves only (under `packs`); `data/work/world_run/preview.log` is the
   dry run's only.
2. **Run modes**: `run --no-join` stops after the regions, gated rows registered with status `gated`
   (added to `plan_config.ROW_STATES`); `join [--countries ..] [--jobs N]` takes every gated row
   through the joint solve, the market step and `shipped`. A gated row no longer registered goes back
   to `built`. `status` lists gated rows, live claims and every lock held, with its holder.
3. **The split (T-781)**: `intercity.solve_units`. A group of one country is solved whole as before.
   A group spanning countries is solved a country at a time: the country's packs are balanced, and
   the group's packs of other countries within the 150 km cut take part at their current factors
   (`joint_gravity --fixed`; `--init` takes factors from a `--dump`, for the experiments). The unit
   file `<first member>-<cc>.joint_commutes.json` lists `solved` and `solved_at` and holds every
   commute with an end in a solved pack, the fixed neighbours' into the country too.
   `market_zones.Groups` takes each pack pair from the newest file covering it (both packs in its
   list, one solved); a file a newer one covers whole is removed. The runner's join solves the units
   holding new packs in turn (a later unit sees an earlier one's factors) and rebuilds the market
   packs of the members and of the fixed neighbours. A unit test checks that a block solved with the
   rest held at a joint solution gives the joint flows back.
4. **T-775**: three parks in a row on one reason stop the run (the reason with the row id taken out,
   and for a failed step its numbers, so Liguria's five drive factors would not have stopped
   T-779's run; tested on stand-in rows: three identical gate FAILs stopped it after the region). The
   `TW_SIM_DIR` copy is compared with `git ls-tree HEAD sim` (blob hashes, CRLF folded) at the start
   of a run or join; differences are printed and go in the report's problems, each saying whether
   the tree's own file matches the copy (DECISIONS: a warning, not a stop). The proof run printed
   `sim/src/bin/joint_gravity.rs differs (as in the tree: not committed yet)`.

Read so: inside each country the split changes commutes across pack lines by 0.1-2%; a re-solve of
one country against neighbours from a joint solve reproduces the joint flows to a few percent (the
one group itself stopped at 3.1e-4, not converged). Across a border the 0.05 national factor leaves
a few thousand commuters in all, and the first solve after a cold neighbour is off by tens of
percent until that neighbour is solved again (Italy-France: Italy solved against France's factors,
which were balanced against Italy alone). With each pair taken only from the home's own last solve
(the first version), France-Spain was +170%: the pair rule fixes the common case, a new country next
to one already solved. Margin errors of the fixed packs are not meaningful as measured (`--check`
sees only the packs loaded, so a fixed pack's partners beyond the unit are missing).

Chosen: by country, neighbours fixed (DECISIONS 2026-10-20). Not chosen: one group with a higher cap
(each country pays for the continent; Germany, Switzerland and Austria pass 400k), a split by size
(cuts inside countries, where pack lines take 0.4). Not built: a second sweep of the neighbours
after a new country (would cost their solves, France's 19 minutes, for a few hundred commuters).

**CPU** (machine-wide, 16 cores, other agents included): the two region runs averaged 3.1 cores
(median 2.8, 90th percentile 5.3, max 7.7), against France's single run at 3.9 (T-779). The gates
were the long phase and ran one at a time, and Greece's builds were short; with gates now
overlapping, Portugal would have ended about 16 minutes sooner. The join averaged 4.7 cores (90th
percentile 10.4) over 6 minutes. Run one after the other the two countries would have taken about
70 minutes of regions plus a join each; T-779's France join was 45 minutes because it re-solved the
continent, where Portugal's, now inside the same chain, took 48 seconds of solve.

## Retrospective

- **The gate is the phase that decides a parallel run.** Two countries at once used fewer cores
  than one France run, because the gates were exclusive and a gate is one Vite and one browser. Now
  they overlap; T-719's speedup 8 (the browser on a sample of zones) would shorten each.
- **One country's join is no longer everyone's.** Portugal joined the continent's chain in under a
  minute of solve. A new country's border commutes are taken from its own solve; the neighbour's
  side catches up when the neighbour is next solved.
- **Measure the one-sided rule before trusting it.** The first version took each pack's commutes
  from its home's last solve, which looked right in design and was +170% on France-Spain from a
  cold start; the pair rule came from that number.
- **Outlines are the new countries' weak point**: all three unexplained parks were outline matches,
  none were runner faults.
- **Tokens**: one background wait a long job (experiments, the two region runs, the re-gate, the
  join, the checks); the lock test and the T-775 test ran in the foreground in under 2 minutes each.
