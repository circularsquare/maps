# T-1140: compact zones, one merge rule worldwide (design, 2026-10-24)

Anita (2026-10-24): "some regions are awkwardly big and long. for instance, yakima/bellingham and
pueblo/colorado springs. when merging regions i think the criteria we should be using should be closer
to like... be willing to merge region A into B if A's biggest travel partner is B, or thats close to
being the case, or at least the region geometrically looks fairly compact ... also for regions that are
a whole state we can just use the state name, like south dakota." Then (via the orchestrator): one
merge rule worldwide, so T-903's European small-zone merges, T-1089's ring repair and Taiwan's Zhubei
split (T-1002) fold into it. A design measured on dry runs; nothing in `pipeline/`, `data/` or the plan
changed.

## For Anita

**The rule in plain words.** A zone under the floor joins a neighbour only if that neighbour is its
biggest travel partner or gets at least half as much travel as the biggest one, counting every zone and
metro around it. If no neighbour is a partner, it may still join one when the joined zone stays compact
(it fills at least 75% of its convex hull, or is no less compact than the bigger part already was). A
partner always comes before a join by shape alone. If nothing passes, the zone stays as it is, under the
floor. Travel is commuting both ways: the ACS county flows in the US, and elsewhere a gravity estimate
from GHSL people and the game's commute curve (the same everywhere). Everything else stays as today:
the floor (0.5M; 1M in China and Taiwan), the cap, no rings, no exclaves, joins within the state or
province first.

**What it does to your examples.** Whatcom's zone (Bellingham) stops at Skagit, Island and San Juan
(0.48M): 87% of its travel goes to Seattle and almost none east of the Cascades. Eastern Washington goes
to Yakima's and Spokane's zones. In Colorado, Pueblo joins Colorado Springs (its partner), the Western
Slope becomes its own zone round Grand Junction (0.64M), and the north-eastern plains join Fort Collins.
Picture: `t1140-colorado-washington.png` in the maps root.

**Choices for you**
1. **Europe's small zones: the plain floor, or T-903's area term?** T-903 counted a zone as big enough
   when people/0.5M + km²/10,000 ≥ 1, so large rural départements stay alone. With the plain floor the
   rule gives France 46 zones (T-903: 71), Italy 51 (58), Greece 13 (17) and Portugal 7 (8). With the
   area term, the rule gives almost exactly T-903's plan (France 71, Italy 59, Greece 17, Portugal 9).
   The area term can't be the worldwide rule, though: in the US it makes 484 zones with a median of
   0.25M, because western counties are huge. My default is the plain floor everywhere. France then gets
   whole old regions as zones (Auvergne 1.32M, Champagne-Ardenne 1.27M, Lower Normandy 1.43M).
2. **China: rebuild now or later?** The rule changes 537 of China's 660 rows, nearly a whole rebuild
   (about 15 hours at home). China has no commuting data, and gravity agrees with the real flows only
   about two times in three in the US. My default is to keep China as built for now and cut only its
   rings (T-1089's 123 rows). The rule would then rebuild China later in one go, on your word.

## 1. How the four zones came to be (T-1088's replan, wave 3)

Read from the plan's US merge record (`zones.USA.merges`) and replayed (`replay.py`).

- **Bellingham** (row `wha`, registry "Whatcom County", 1.00M, 14 counties). Small counties joined by
  longest border: San Juan into Skagit across 2 km of water, Island into Skagit, Garfield WA and Columbia
  and Asotin into Whitman, and Adams, Douglas and Chelan into Grant. Then by nearest centre: Skagit
  (0.24M) into Whatcom, and Walla Walla and Whitman with Franklin into Grant. Last, Whatcom's group
  (0.48M, 97% of the floor) joined Grant's (0.52M) by nearest centre. Its only neighbours were the
  Seattle metro, Spokane's zone (through Okanogan) and Grant's zone (through Chelan, along the Cascade
  crest). The 80% rule only keeps a zone as it is when nothing fits under the cap, and here something
  did. The result: Puget Sound joined to the Columbia Basin, with the Tri-Cities split between `wha`
  (Pasco) and `yat` (Kennewick).
- **Yakima** (`yat`, 0.57M: Yakima, Benton, Kittitas, Klickitat) is compact. It sits inside the bend of
  `wha`, which is why the two read as one problem.
- **Pueblo** (`pub`, 0.90M, 27 counties). Small mountain counties formed three groups by longest
  border: Gunnison, Saguache, Chaffee, Pitkin, Lake and Fremont (0.12M); Garfield with the north-west
  and the ski counties (0.23M); and Mesa with the south-west (0.37M). Then by nearest centre:
  Gunnison's group joined Pueblo County, Garfield's joined that (Aspen's workers live down the Roaring
  Fork, so in travel terms it was a partner), and Mesa's joined that. Each join pulled the zone's centre
  further west, so it chained from Pueblo to the Utah line. The origin ended up in Grand Junction.
- **Colorado Springs** (`els`, "El Paso County", 1.00M, 23 counties). El Paso and Teller, plus the whole
  eastern plains and the San Luis Valley, which had formed one strip by longest border. That strip
  (Prowers' group, 0.19M) joined El Paso by nearest centre, and so the zone wraps round Pueblo's zone to
  the east and south.

## 2. The rule

In `world_plan.zone_groups`, at the step where a zone of 100,000 or more under the floor picks a
neighbour (`notes/T-1140/rule1140.py`, a copy of the planner function with the test added):

1. **Travel.** `flow(a, b)` is commuters between the units of a and of b, both directions. In the US
   these are the ACS 2016-2020 county flows (`COMMUTE_FLOWS`, already read for the belts; LODES OD is on
   disk only for some states, and the `usa` module reads WAC, not OD). Elsewhere, flows come from
   gravity: GHSL people on 20 km cells, people × people × the zone curve d^-0.8 exp(-d/30 km), over
   pairs within 200 km (`common.gravity_flows`). The joint gravity's modelled flows are between zones,
   not units, so they can't score a unit's join.
2. **Partner test.** A join z → o passes when flow(z, o) ≥ **0.5** × z's flow with its biggest partner,
   counted over every area around it (zones of any region, metro rows, packs).
3. **Shape test.** Otherwise the join passes when the joined shape's area is at least **0.75** of its
   convex hull's, or no more than 0.05 below the bigger part's own ratio. The second clause covers a
   county added on the edge of a zone that was never convex, such as a coast.
4. **Partner first.** Among neighbours under the cap, those passing the partner test come first, by
   nearest centre. Only when none passes can a join by shape alone happen. Then come the wrap guard and
   the ring cut as today.
5. **Nothing passes:** the zone stays as it is, under the floor. One exception, decided here: a zone
   under 80% of the floor whose own state or province accepts nothing may join another region's
   neighbour that passes. This is today's "no zone left in its region" fallback, widened from "none
   left" to "none accepted". It puts Kootenai ID with Spokane (89% of its travel) and Washington
   County MD with Franklin PA (Hagerstown and Chambersburg, as now). Without it, 41 US zones would end
   up under the floor instead of 27.
6. **Units under 100,000** join the neighbour of their region that they travel with most (today: the
   longest border), with no test. This is how Logan County CO ends up with Fort Collins rather than in
   the plains strip.

**Why the convex hull ratio.** The four measures were computed on today's US zones. Deciles:
hull ratio 0.60 / 0.80 / 0.92 at 10 / 50 / 90%; Polsby-Popper 0.06 / 0.15 / 0.35 (it punishes river
and coast borders, so one-county zones fail it); Reock and span-against-equivalent-circle penalise long
zones whatever their people. None of them flags Pueblo's zone, which is a fat block (hull 0.80): its
problem is travel, not shape. So shape is only the backup test. Scored on the joins of T-1088's
replay (joins of 100,000 or more):

| Join | Partner ratio | Hull of the union (needed) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whatcom's group → Grant's | 0.01 (Seattle is the top) | 0.63 (0.70) | refused |
| Gunnison's group → Pueblo | 0.21 (Garfield is the top) | 0.73 (0.74) | refused |
| Garfield's group → Pueblo's zone | 1.00 (Pitkin is inside) | 0.72 | passes, but Pueblo's chain is already broken |
| Prowers' plains → El Paso | 0.17 | 0.74 (0.64) | passes by shape |
| Canyon → Ada (Boise) | 1.00 | 0.91 | passes |
| Weld → Larimer | 0.59 (Denver is the top) | 0.94 | passes |
| Malheur → Deschutes | 0.22 | 0.94 | passes by shape |
| Benton WA → Yakima | 0.17 | 0.87 | passes by shape |
| Skagit → Whatcom | 0.42 | 0.66 (0.75) | refused: a weak spot, because islands and inlets lower the hull ratio |

Of the replay's 124 joins: 88 pass as partners, 23 pass by shape, 13 are refused. Skagit and Whatcom
still end up together in the full runs, because the order there differs.

**Sensitivity** (US full replans): α = 0.33 / 0.5 / 0.67 gives 25 / 26 / 29 zones under the floor and
56-57% of county leavers kept; hull 0.70 / 0.75 / 0.80 moves little. Gravity in place of the ACS: each
county's top neighbour agrees 68% of the time; 42 of 181 zones come out identical, and 71% of people
land in a largely identical zone; 55% of leavers are kept against 58% with the real flows.

## 4. The built world (repair runs, `repair.py`)

For built countries the procedure has three steps:
1. A row fails when the rule, given only its own units (the rest of the country as it is), can't
   rebuild it whole. This includes the case where a part of it had a partner outside the zone. A row
   also fails when it wraps a zone, metro or pack (T-1087's test).
2. Failed rows go back to their units.
3. Every zone under the floor joins by the rule: the failed rows' units, and also kept rows under the
   floor, which are T-903's small zones. Kept rows stay whole. The ring cut runs after.

Lists: `artifacts/trainworld/t1140/evr_eu.txt`, `evr_eu_a.txt` (with the area term), `evr_nuts.txt`,
`evr_chn.txt`, `evr_twn.txt`.

## 5. Names: zones that hold a whole state or region

name in the game (`zone-names.json`; the planner's registry name too), where the region is a real state,
province or region people use. Where it is a statistical NUTS-2 grouping, the zone keeps its own name.

## 6. Cost and order

The planner change is a T-1088-sized code task (about two hours). It adds four things to
`zone_groups`: the partner and shape tests, the partner-first ordering, the small units' biggest-partner
join and the "none accepted" fallback. It adds gravity flows for countries without `COMMUTE_FLOWS`, a
replan switch that treats a country's built zone rows as planned, and the whole-region names. Then the
rows:

- **US** (162 rows plus Puerto Rico's 3, about 160 new): every state's extract and block cache again
  (about 10 GB of downloads; wave 4 fetched 2.2 GB for 12 states), builds at 1-3 min a zone, a gate of
  0.5-1.5 min a row, and the joins of all 8 divisions and Puerto Rico (wave 3: 45 min for two). At home
  on about 7 cores that is roughly 9 hours, one long night. With AWS for the builds (about 40 min of
  machine, under $1), about 6 hours at home.
- **Europe** (393 rows into about 220): tier-0 builds of 1-2 min, so about 3 hours of builds and
  gates on 7 cores, plus the joins of about 28 countries again: 7-9 hours in all.
- **Taiwan** (3 rows into 6): under an hour plus its join.
- **China** (537 rows into 485): about 15 hours at home, or one AWS run; held per choice 2.

**T-1089, T-903, T-1005 part 2 and T-1002** are all covered by these runs: the ring cut is part of the
rule, and so are the small-zone merges and Zhubei. Those tasks close into T-1140's rebuild, except
China's rings if China waits (then T-1089 keeps its 123 Chinese rows).

**The fix batch.** Stage 1 (the joins for T-1000 and T-1091) is running now. A country rebuilt by the
rule needs its join again afterwards, which carries T-1000 and T-1091 too, since their code is in. If
stage 1 has not yet reached the US or Europe, those joins can wait for the rebuild. Order: the planner
change, then each country's replan as a dry run and a look, then builds, gates and joins as a later
stage of the fix batch.

**Old saves.** Every removed row (about 165 in the US, 393 in Europe, 3 in Taiwan) needs a T-1053
successor: the new zone holding most of its people. Place ids are never reused.
