## Time

Node, one core (machine shared with Brazil's join): New York sheet 1.8 s, layout 1.4 s, carry 4.2 s, map 1.9 s; all
Japan sheet 4.6 s, layout 26 s, carry 20 s, map 4.1 s. In the page, New York's diagram opened in 9 s (sheet 1.1, layout
1.1, carry 4.2, map 1.4); all Japan (9,075 complexes) in 47 s (ground sea 1.9, sheet 1.5, layout 19, carry 19, map 3.2;
carry rounds 350, 169, 61, 17, 7, 3, 3, 2, 1, 0 stations off land; 0 off land and 0 on the wrong side of a border at the
end; 3,142 pieces straight, 1,148 bent). For comparison, today's flag-off New York takes about 4 s layout and 10 s water, all Japan 69 s
layout and 25 s water (notes/T-2566.md).

**The carry's solver.** The prototype's conjugate gradients took 8,000 iterations a solve (6 minutes for Japan). Plain
geometric multigrid with bilinear prolongation stalled here (55-60 iterations to 1e-6) because water links are 1,250
times weaker than land's: a land mass tied to others only through water is a near-free mode bilinear interpolation does
not see. The prolongation now follows the matrix (black box multigrid, Dendy 1982: a fine node takes its coarse
neighbours by its stencil's couplings toward each), and the solve takes 15-25 iterations from cold, a few after a round
that changed a few stations. Tolerance 1e-4 (residual over right side) gives the same drawing as 1e-6. The multigrid is
made once and remade only where a solve does not settle in 20 iterations; the stations met harder are changed in the
matrix in place. What is left is about 35 ms (New York) to 95 ms (Japan) an iteration in JavaScript.

## T-2589 (keep the sheet per network)

Not done. The brief's premise was that the sheet dominates a rebuild; on the sheet it no longer does: an edit's rebuild
re-runs the sheet (New York 1.1 s in the page, Japan about 2-4.5 s), the layout (1.1 s / 26 s), the carry (4.2 s / 20 s)
and the map (1.4 s / 4 s), so keeping the sheet would save at most 12% (New York) to 8% (Japan). Edit-rebuild time
measured as the first open: New York 9 s in the page. The carry is the part worth speeding up next (rounds after the
first touch a few stations but solve the whole lattice; a local solve, or the carry kept per network as the sheet would
be), then the layout's B and routing on a country map. T-2589's line carries these figures.

## Decisions

- The carry's lattice is the sheet's finest lattice (512 / 1024 a side), not a lattice of its own: the same sizes the
  prototype used (round 3: 512 for New York, 2.5 km for Japan) without a second grid.
- Black box multigrid for the carry (operator-dependent prolongation), solve tolerance 1e-4, multigrid made once.
- On the sheet the zone borders are the zones' real outlines carried with the map (round 3), replacing the pill
  regions of T-2464; the sea is drawn only with the water button, the borders always.
- City names take the towns' places through the sheet (their blocks are found on the sheet's places).
