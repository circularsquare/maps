# T-3280: attraction trips in Hakone, Atami and Izu are tiny (2026-11-12, agent jpattract)

Player report via Anita, 2026-11-12: "In Japan, attraction demand seems almost nonexistent (It's exist, but very
very small) even in major tourist destinations such as Hakone, Atami, and the Izu Peninsula." Anita: players are
about 2/3 Japanese now, so worth extra time.

Stage 1, diagnosis and options only; nothing built. Scripts in the agent's scratchpad (`attr_fate.py`,
`market_hakone.py`, `tour_weight.py`, `near.py`), all read-only on data/. The figures are worked from the packs
and attractions.json with the sim's own formulas (build.rs, everyday.rs), not read off a running game. Trips are
one way, a weekday, unless said.

## 1. Where the places are

- **Hakone** is in `tyo` (Greater Tokyo, one pack of 39.75M people, all of Kanagawa). Its 6 km market zones:
  Hakone-Yumoto (zone 573: 3,288 people, 5,643 jobs), Gora (587: 1,734 and 1,083), Moto-Hakone (572).
- **Atami and the whole Izu Peninsula** are in `szo`, the Shizuoka zone (the whole prefecture, 3.63M people).
  Atami's zone 202 holds 21,886 people and 13,637 jobs.

A resort town gets the long trips of an ordinary town its size. All Hakone: about 630 long trips a weekday plus

**Overnight tourism** has no model of its own. The market's ground curve counts every non-commute trip of 25 km
and more (NHTS, overnight ones included), so overnight trips are in its totals, but sent to places by headcount.
The superholiday lean (T-1374) sends more of them to zones with many attraction visits per resident, only on
superholidays, and only where attractions.json sees the visits.

**What a big-city attraction gets.** Tokyo Skytree, 5.76M a year: about 13,500 visits a weekday, 86% from within
25 km, 99.6% kept. Kamakura's temples (7.5M a year over five rows) and Kyoto (45.8M within 8 km of the centre)
do fine too: temples and museums are Wikidata attractions with plenty of sitelinks. The gap is the hot-spring,
lake and mountain resort whose draw is the town itself, and whose visitors live 50-150 km away.

Who visits Hakone and how (Kanagawa's visitor survey 2019, Hakone-Yugawara area, a sample of visitors
interviewed, https://www.pref.kanagawa.jp/documents/11726/2019shouhidoukou.pdf, pp.7-31): of the day visitors
50.9% live in Kanagawa and 49.1% outside it (Tokyo the most), of the overnight ones 25.1% and 74.9%; overnight
guests came most by JR conventional lines (48.8%), day visitors most by car (47.7%), the rest largely by rail.

Prefecture counts are "visits" by Japan's common standard (each municipality counts a visitor once), so 21M is
not 21M distinct people, but it is the right order for arrivals: about 58,000 a day on average, 11,400 hotel
guests a night. Taking about 80% of Hakone's visitors from 25 km or more, the game's ~630 long trips plus ~3,000
local visits a weekday are some 10x under; by rail alone the Hakone Tozan's 22,500 riders a day dwarf what a
player can earn there. Atami's real arrivals, about 16,000 a day, are 5x the game's.

**Outside Japan** (the same kind of place, for checks): Brighton and Hove 10.19M day trips and 1.62M staying
trips (2023, Visit Brighton's economic impact report, https://visitbrighton.com/dbimgs/Economic%20Impact%20of%20Tourism%20-%20%20Brighton%20and%20Hove%20Report%202023.pdf),
the game's rows 2.1M; Zion National Park 4,623,238 recreation visits in 2023 (NPS,
https://www.nps.gov/zion/planyourvisit/visitation.htm), the game's rows none near Springdale; Interlaken's
rows 2.1M a year, Zermatt's 0.2M; Venice's 42,000 listed visits a day against a 6 km zone of 31,000 people.
The gap is worldwide for nature and spa resorts; Japan's players meet it most because onsen towns are where a
Japanese railway goes on a weekend.

## 4. How others do it

- **SBB's SIMBA MOBi** (Danalet et al., STRC 2023, "Modelling foreign tourists in Switzerland",
  https://strc.ch/2023/Danalet_EtAl.pdf): tourists are a population living in accommodations, placed by the
  federal overnight-stays statistics (HESTA 2019) and OSM's hotels, who then make trips to attractions; foreign
  tourists are about 2% of Swiss rail demand.
- **Destination choice with hotel beds**: Hackney, STRC 2006 (https://www.strc.ch/2006/Hackney_STRC_2006.pdf),
  hotel beds explain leisure destination choice significantly.
- **Games** (from memory, not checked): the A-Train series (Japanese) has resort and leisure facilities (ski
  slopes, amusement parks, resort hotels) that add passengers at weekends and in season; Cities: Skylines sends
  tourists in through the outside connections by the city's attractiveness (unique buildings, parks, hotels in
  II); OpenTTD and Transport Fever have no tourism, passengers come from houses.
- **Japan's own data**: every prefecture publishes visitors by municipality on the common standard
  (観光入込客統計, 共通基準); MLIT's KSJ P09 lodging capacity mesh (宿泊容量メッシュ) gives the guests a night of
  about 18,000 lodgings by 1 km mesh (2010; licence not checked).

## 5. Options

Two things are wrong at once: **size** (the list misses places whose draw is the town: onsen, lakes, mountains)
and **reach** (attraction visits are a local purpose cut at 40-80 km and kept inside their pack, while long
trips weigh a place by its people). Fixing size alone sends more Odawara people to Hakone; fixing reach alone
moves a list that barely sees Hakone. So every useful option pairs a size source with a way in from far away.

### A. Resort towns in the long-trip weight, Japan-sized (recommended)

- **What changes**: a list of Japanese resort municipalities (onsen towns first: Hakone, Atami, Ito, Izu, Shimoda,
  Higashi-Izu, Kawazu, Yugawara, Nikko, Karuizawa, Kusatsu, Beppu, Yufu, Noboribetsu, Shirahama, Kinosaki, Gero,
  Takayama, Hakuba and so on, 60-100 of them) with each one's published visitors a year from its prefecture's
  report. In the market, a 6 km zone's destination weight becomes `people + 2 x jobs` rescaled, plus
  `beta x tourism visits a day` from that list, for trips within Japan. Each origin's departures are kept (the
  normalisers carry it), so Tokyo people take no more trips: more of their 25-300 km trips go to Hakone and Izu
  and a few percent fewer to the suburbs at the same distance. The local attractions purpose is untouched.
- **General?** A local factor for Japan backed by published figures (Anita's 2026-11-11 rule). The term itself is
  general and could take the worldwide list (attractions.json, or T-3247's survey) later.
- **Effect** (inbound long trips from tyo a weekday; beta to be fitted at or under the published counts, likely
  2-4):

  | Zone | Now | beta 1 | beta 3 | beta 5 | Published |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Hakone (three zones, 21.1M a year) | 630 | 4,200 | 11,200 | 18,400 | ~58,000 visits a day, Hakone Tozan 22,500 riders a day |
  | Atami (5.96M) | 1,160 | 1,930 | 3,470 | 5,010 | JR East 10,837 boardings a day (with locals) |
  | Ito (8.90M) | 660 | 1,520 | 3,270 | 5,000 | |
  | Shimoda (2.09M) | 270 | 360 | 550 | 740 | |
  | Shuzenji / Izu city (3.47M) | 160 | 430 | 980 | 1,520 | |

  Outside Japan nothing moves. Big Japanese cities hardly move (Shinjuku's zone x1.00 on its listed sites).
- **Cost**: list and figures 2-3 hours (47 prefecture reports, top resort municipalities first); market_zones.py
  (a tourism column in the sites, the field's normaliser) and build.rs's `a` 2-3 hours; the fit and checks
  (t1148-market-rule) 1 hour. About 6 hours.
- **Rebuild**: a market-only pass over Japan's packs (`market_zones.py` on the Japan ids, then `market_pack`
  rows); no cells, commutes or cross files.

### B. The same, worldwide, from attractions.json alone

- **What changes**: the market term of A for every country, sized by attractions.json's visits.
- **Effect**: little for Hakone (its rows are 1.6M a year: Gora's zone x1.9 at beta 1, Yumoto's none), Atami
  x1.09, Ito none. Elsewhere it lifts places with famous sites: Venice's zone x2.2 at beta 1, Nikko's x2.0, Kyoto
  Higashiyama x1.18, Brighton x1.03; US national parks none (no rows). Does not fix the report.
- **Cost**: 3-4 hours; a market pass over every pack (as the T-3240 pull's).

### C. Attraction visits that travel far

- **What changes**: Japanese resort sites added to attractions.json at their published sizes, and the
  attractions purpose draws a share of its visitors on a long tail (the market's ground curve from 25 km),
  handed across zone lines to the regional layer as airports' far passengers are (T-2896). Visits stay each
  site's published count; their origins move to Tokyo.
- **General?** The reach rule worldwide; the sizes a Japan factor.
- **Effect**: Hakone gets its full count, about 49,000 visits a weekday once at 21M, most from Tokyo and
  Yokohama, more than A at any beta here. Adds trips (on top of the market's, a double count of the far part),
  and every big listed site worldwide gets far visitors too.
- **Cost**: 6-8 hours (sim everyday draw, cross_everyday, market_zones); rebuild: cross_everyday and market over
  every pack. A new rule for a purpose: Anita's call.

### D. Hotel guests as residents (SIMBA MOBi's way)

- **What changes**: lodging capacity (KSJ P09 in Japan, OSM hotels elsewhere) becomes a guest population that
  makes local outings from its hotel and arrives and leaves by the market. Closest to how Hakone and Atami work
  (half to three quarters of their visitors stay the night).
- **General?** A new kind of person: a complicating factor.
- **Cost**: 10+ hours and a full rebuild of every pack's cells. Not now.

**Recommendation: A**, with beta fitted so Hakone's and Atami's inbound long trips stay under the rail counts
(likely beta 2-4: Hakone about 7,000-15,000 a weekday each way, Atami 2,700-4,200). It puts the trips where the
player builds (Tokyo - Odawara - Hakone, the Tokaido to Atami, the Izu lines), uses published Japanese figures,
changes nothing outside Japan, and needs no new purpose. T-3247's attraction-source survey stays the way to a
worldwide version (B or C) later.

Anita, 2026-11-12: "for japan tourism, i think A makes sense. lets do it".

## Stage 2: A as built (2026-11-12, agent jpattract)

**The table.** `pipeline/market_tourism.csv`, 79 municipalities, 274.5M visitors a year, each row its
prefecture's report (the town's own where the prefecture has no municipal table), year, basis, URL and page
or sheet. Gathered by three research agents and checked against the stage 1 figures (Hakone 19.51M 2023,
Kanagawa's Table 2; Atami 5.96M FY2023, Shizuoka p.17). Mostly 2023; Wakayama, Beppu, Yufu, Aso, Izumo,
Kotohira, Ishigaki 2024; Hokkaido, Hyogo, Yamagata, Miyakojima FY2023. Bases differ: most are summed visits
(延べ); Yamanashi and Unzen unique persons (the smaller where both are given); Nagano's are its spots summed
(Karuizawa 7.7M, Hakuba 2.4M); Gero and Shirakawa only their resort areas; Aso quoted by MLIT. No municipal
figure found: Kaga, Nanao, Wajima (Ishikawa reports 4 regions), Misasa, Minamioguni, Tonosho and Shodoshima
(one island area), Onna, Motobu, Yoshino (Nara's PDFs 403 to scripts). The research CSVs with every note are in
the agent's scratchpad (`res1`-`res3`).

**Why a file of its own**, not market_pull.json's resort list: the pull is a place's multiplier faded in at
300-700 km, read by place outline; this is a count of visitors added at 6 km zone level at every distance, and
needs each town's outline. `pipeline/market_tourism.py` turns the table into `market_tourism.json` (config `on`,
`beta`, and each town's N03 outline simplified to about 200 m, 1.5 MB).

**The path** (as the pull's, one level down): `market_zones.py` `site_tourism` shares each town's
`beta x visits / 365` over the pack's sites inside its outline by jobs (nearest site if none), only in the pack
whose outline holds the town's point, and writes the zone file's `zone_tw` (only where non-zero: no other
country's file changes); `Field` adds the same people to the r5 cell of the town's point, before the pull's
terms, ground only. Sim: `ZoneRaw.zone_tw`, `build.rs` adds it times `inc^eps` to the zone's `a`; rows
(market_pack) and the app's far pairs (marketRule.ts reads the row's `a`) follow with no code of their own.
Old WASM ignores the field (serde default), so the demand WASM must be rebuilt with the packs.

**The fit** (`trips.py`, a replica of build.rs and market.rs on the zone files: the scratch normalisers

zones 1.3% at the median (4 x 3.35e-3), Shizuoka's and Oita's about 3.3%.
