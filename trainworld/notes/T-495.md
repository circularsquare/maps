# T-495: mode choice scaled by trip length (cost damping)

2026-10-16. Rule now in docs/demand.md 4.3, docs/regional.md 5.1, docs/demand-worldwide.md "Cost
damping"; the choice in DECISIONS.md.

## The rule

The logit's scale is `Params::mode_beta(T)` = 0.05 x sqrt(min(1, 90 / T)), T the car's perceived
minutes door to door (drive x 1.33 plus parking locally; the regional car, computed even across
a sea where no car goes, so rail's share against the plane does not depend on whether a car can
make the trip, which `air_flies_far_pairs...` checks). The flat 0.025 of the longer trips is gone.

- Why the car's minutes: known before any track is built, so a pair's scale never moves with the
  network and needs no extra pass. The best mode's minutes would feed rail's improvements back
  into the scale.
- Why a square root: WebTAG's cost damping and the long-distance studies use powers about 0.5 on
  cost or distance; it keeps the SIMD lanes to a divide, a min and a sqrt (bit for bit the scalar
  `trip::pair_choice`).
- Why 90: New York's commutes and everyday trips have cars under about 90 perceived minutes, so
  they keep 0.05 (at 60, Manhattan-bound drives with paid parking, 90-130 minutes, would be
  damped; not measured).
- Local layers: only the mode level is damped (the access logsums at 0.05 are shared by every
  destination). Regional layer: the whole rail trip, each access weight raised to the power of
  scale / 0.05 (`intercity::damped`); with one station at each end the two are identical.

gaps that remain are not the scale: one station per city (corridor shares far below the stations'
own zones'), and the long car's cost (Paris - Berlin's car at 1,050 perceived minutes against the
plane's 650: 13 hours at 85 km/h and $82 of running cost each, so even 0.014 leaves it 0.2%).

The local layers barely move (their cars are mostly under 90 minutes; the small gains are crowding
relief). The regional layer falls by three quarters: its pairs within 250 km (New York - New
Haven and the like) have cars of roughly 130-200 perceived minutes, where the
scale is now 0.03-0.04 rather than the flat 0.025, and rail is worse than the car for most of their
zone pairs, so a steeper choice gives rail less. Before, the save's 115,665 regional rail trips a
day (T-378) were of the order of Metro-North's whole New Haven Line (very roughly 125,000 a
weekday, commutes included, from memory), so the drop is probably towards plausible; judgement,
not fitted.

## T-502: the damping starts at 60 (Anita, 2026-10-16)

`damp_min` 90 to 60 (kernel.rs `Params::default`; the SIMD lanes and `pair_choice` read the same
field, so they still agree; no test held a value). The scale is now 0.029 at a 3 hour car, 0.014 at
13. Real save, weekday final, same Vite and save as above:

| | 90 | 60 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Rail trips a day | 8,346,628 | 8,343,085 (-0.04%) |
| Boardings: work | 6,889,279 | 6,830,128 (-0.9%) |
| school / shopping / social | 1,170,225 / 3,221,583 / 2,765,867 | 1,170,054 / 3,215,769 / 2,763,228 |
| medical / airport | 473,214 / 68,345 | 473,674 / 68,032 |
| longer (regional) | 68,301 | 151,704 (+122%; 259,376 before T-495) |
| subway 1 / 7 | 842,141 / 649,839 | 840,017 / 651,506 |
| NJT Northeast Corridor | 181,666 | 179,031 (-1.4%) |
| MNR New Haven | 136,553 | 134,898 (-1.2%) |
| MNR Waterbury | 717 | 1,210 |

A lower start flattens the choice for every car over 60 minutes (the scale is smaller), so on the
regional pairs where rail is worse than the car rail gets more, and the commute loses a little in
the 60-90 minute drives. Rail trips a day hardly move because the regional layer is a small part
of them; its boardings count each leg.
