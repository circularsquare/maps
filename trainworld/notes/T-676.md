## T-676, the base map
`map/basemap.ts` (the Positron repaint, light or dark, faint place names in Zen Maru Gothic) and
`game/shades.ts` over `shadeTable.ts` (the map's light and dark colours, one table).
- **Light**: land a warm paper off-white, water a soft teal, parks and woods a sage green. **Dark**:
  the same three hues deep and muted (dark warm grey-brown land, deep teal, dark sage); the dark theme
  is not an afterthought (T-543).
- **Roads**: three weights only (major, minor, the rest), drawn low-contrast so the rail network is
  what the eye finds. Drop the rest of the road classes' extra widths and the casings that make roads
  busy at zoom 12-15.
- **POIs**: remove nearly all of the basemap's POI icons and labels (shops, bus stops, food); leave
  what helps a player place themselves (airports, stations as the style has them, large parks'
  names, water names). Place names keep the faint repaint (feedback: faint basemap labels).
- **Do not** touch the demand views' own colours, the line colours or the overlay's shades beyond
  what the new land and water need to stay legible (casing, outside shade, blueprint off).
- Make the palette a few named values at the top so Anita can retune it by hand.
- The experiment she is unsure of, **ground tinted by density of jobs and homes**: stage 2, behind a
  switch in Settings, off by default, not shipped until she has seen it. The pack cells carry homes
  and jobs per cell; a low-zoom raster or fill layer from the open zones' cells is the likely way;
  measure its cost before drawing it and say if it needs a pack-side raster.

### Stage 2, how and what it costs
Build the tint at run time from the cells the clock and demand workers already hold (homes and jobs
per H3 cell, 202,337 cells in New York), not as a pack-side raster: each open zone gets one small
canvas (about 1 pixel per 150 m, so 256 to 512 pixels a side for a city), each cell's homes plus
jobs added into its pixel, blurred, mapped through a two or three step warm ramp at low alpha, and
handed to MapLibre as an `image` source with four corner coordinates, placed under the roads. One
texture per zone, no per-frame cost, nothing in the packs to rebuild, and it works for every city
the same way. Cost: a few ms of one-off work per zone on the main thread (or a worker with an
OffscreenCanvas), well under 1 MB of GPU memory per city, a Settings switch (off by default) and
a redraw on theme change. Work: about half to one agent-day, mostly the ramp and fitting it to
both themes. A fill layer of hexagons is the alternative, and it is worse: 200,000 polygons per
city, heavy at zoom 12. A pack-side raster only pays if cell data is not loaded when the map is
first drawn (the world view at zoom 5, where no zone is open): that wants a coarse world raster
of about 50 to 100 KB per city.

## T-678, day and night tint (P3, after T-676)
A subtle tint over the map and not the panels, from the local time of the view's centre
(`localTime.ts` gives each region's), by season-free hour: night a cool dark blue at low opacity, dawn
and dusk a warm band. Settings switch, on by default only if Anita likes it. Trains and track stay
legible: tint the basemap's colours, not the overlay.

Done: screenshots of the same three places (a dense centre at zoom 12, a coast at 9, a region at 5),
light and dark, before and after, in the artifacts folder for Anita to judge. She decides the palette.
