# T-149: viewed-city-first startup

Anita asks to calculate the city being viewed first, prevent play/interaction until at least that city is fully loaded, and show Loading with a percentage. Preserve initial camera/save focus. Cities without construction need not load, with the viewed city necessarily an exception so an empty game works. Define readiness and honest progress milestones; retain loading/error recovery. Shared regional demand and crowding make this a scheduler/state task distinct from T-143's readiness appearance. Do not silently omit contributions from constructed cities or count partially solved demand as complete.

## Done 2026-10-12

### What loads

- **The viewed city** is `activeCity`, the city whose centre is nearest the camera (as the City
  panel and top bar already use). The camera always starts on New York and saves hold no
  camera, so at startup it is New York; nothing about the initial camera changed. If a save
  ever records a camera, the loader follows `activeCity` and needs no change.
- **Constructed cities**: a city pack is needed when a built station (the demand snapshot's
  station list) lies inside its exact footprint (`packs/<id>.boundary.geojson`) or within 4.5 km
  of it (`workers/cityReach.ts`). The walk's computational bound is 4 km from a cell
  (`walk_cutoff_m`); the 0.5 km margin covers edge cells whose centres sit slightly outside the
  boundary and the packs' equirectangular frames. Blueprint track and stations do not count:
  demand never sees them. If the footprints cannot be fetched, every city counts as reached.
- **The regional market** always loads: it is one pack for the whole region, and the City panel's
  corridors come from it even with no network.
- Once open, a city stays open (memory at most what every pack cost before).

**Why skipping is exact.** A city with no station within 4 km of any of its cells has no access
subzone, so every trip walks or drives, it adds nothing to any segment's load, boardings, fares or
the shared crowding vector. `checks/t149-worker-check.mjs` runs the real worker in Node: a Midtown
line solved with all nine packs open and with New York alone gives bit-identical line loads,
boardings, alightings, crowding, regional boardings, New York and market summaries and corridor
totals (max absolute difference 0); the other eight cities had zero rail in the full solve.
Opening Philadelphia later and solving again leaves New York identical and gives Philadelphia
the full solve's figures. Output: `../../artifacts/trainworld/notes-outputs/T-149-worker-check.json`.

Only one figure depends on which cities are open: the network-wide all-city trip total and split
in `demandView` (`tripsPerDay`, `split`), which nothing in the UI shows (the top bar and City panel
use per-city views; fares use rail trips, unchanged).

### Scheduling

- The pool's workers start their WASM at once with no pack (`init`); packs follow in `open`
  messages as each download lands, the viewed city requested first, then the market, then
  constructed cities once the first network snapshot and the footprints say which. Each worker
  opens in arrival order and answers `opened`.
- A network waits in `waiting` until every city it needs and the market are open in every
  worker; only then is it sent (`dispatch`). The last published result stays meanwhile. So no
  published result ever leaves out a constructed city's riders.
- A solve covers the cities open when it starts (the worker snapshots its city list per job); a
  city opened during a job waits for the next and answers no query (station, purposes, cells
  modes) until a solve covers it. A cells query for a city not open answers empty instead of
  failing the pool.
- **Pan to an unloaded city**: it loads without blocking, the clock keeps running, and the
  network is solved again once it is open so the city gets its view (its numbers are exact:
  walk and drive only). Top bar: "Loading Boston, 37%"; the selector's hover says the same.
- **Construction reaching a new city** (a line built into Boston while viewing New York): the
  new network's solve waits for Boston's pack; the last result stays meanwhile.

### Readiness and the clock

- **Ready** = the loaded game's network solved to its last crowding round (round 3) for every
  city it needs. Not the free-flow estimate: that is a first estimate and the crowding rounds
  change it.
- **Startup**: the clock is held (`clock.held`, separate from pause and speed) and a loading
  screen takes every pointer and key (window capture listeners) until ready. The map and track
  show dimmed behind it.
- **Later whole-game loads** (a file, a browser slot, a new game): the clock is held, without the
  screen, until that game's network is ready (`holdForGameLoad` from clockClient's `loaded`). An
  identical network is ready at once.
- **Failure**: at startup the screen says "Demand could not load" with the reason, Try again
  (reload) and Play without demand; after startup the clock is released and the top bar says
  Demand could not load (reason on hover). Every worker reporting the same failure does not
  release a startup hold.

### Progress percentage

Work done over work to do for the current load, weighted 10 / 30 / 60:

- downloading: bytes received over Content-Length of the load's pack files (a compressed
  response counts as done when it ends);
- opening: workers that have opened each item, weighted by the item's bytes;
- solving: period results landed for the covering solve, of 5 periods x 4 rounds.

It never goes down (more cities found after the first snapshot can only hold it still) and stops
at 99 until the result is published. Steps shown: Downloading city data, Opening city data,
Calculating demand. The weights are judgement from the timings below: a new game spends about
60% of its wait opening and 40% solving, the NYC save about 35% and 65%. The bar stalls during New
York's opening (one 5-12 s WASM call per worker, no progress inside it).
