# T-062 lines on shared track side by side

Done 2026-10-09 by the gameplay agent (batch 4). `T-batch4-lines.png`: three lines on one
trunk, the green one branching off to the east on its own side.

## How

- Every line stroke segment carries an offset in line widths (`Strokes.offset`), and the stroke
  shader moves both ends of the segment's quad sideways by `offset x u_slot` px in screen space,
  `u_slot` being the line width at the zoom. Adjacent lines touch; the white casings are all drawn
  first, so the bundle has one white outline and no gaps between colours.
- **Consistent order** (`layoutBundles` in `map/network.ts`): each edge gets a "forward"
  direction that agrees across nodes (a breadth-first walk over the nodes; at each node the edge
  ends are split by which way they leave, from the drawn polylines, so a straight run through a
  station keeps its direction and branches leaving one side of a junction point the same way).
  Lines on an edge take slots right to left looking forward. A segment's offset is the slot,
  flipped when the line runs against forward, so two lines keep their order along a stretch
  however each edge was drawn and whichever way each line runs.
- **Which side**: lines whose stops' middle lies more than 400 m to one side of an edge take that
  side (a branch line sits on the side it leaves to and does not cross the others); the rest go by
  line id.
- **Trains ride their own stroke**: the same offsets per 50 m sample of each line's run 0
  (`NetworkBuffers.sampleOff`, an R32F texture beside the samples), interpolated, so a train's
  sideways shift eases over 50 m where the bundle changes. Train picking applies the same shift.
- **Stations** span the bundle: the dot's radius grows to half the widest bundle at its node.
- Line picking shifts each segment like the shader, so a click lands on the line under it.
- Off in "colour by height" (no line strokes then).

## Limits

- Where the number of lines changes (at a junction or a station a line ends at), the others step
  sideways by half a width at the node rather than curving over.
- Both directions of a line share its one stroke, as on a metro map; trains in the two directions
  overlap on it.
- The side a line leans to is a rule of thumb; a line with stops on both sides of a trunk goes by
  id.
