## For Anita

**Short answer: don't cut. Keep T-1733's freeze plus slcload's T-2605 fix.** Three measured reasons:

1. **Her network is one cluster because of one line**, not the market. Line 92 (Los Angeles, Las Vegas, St George,
   Cedar City, Grand Junction, Denver, Salina, Kansas City, Peoria, Chicago; 3,091 km, 31 stops, 0.7/0.3/0.2 trains
   an hour, 6 cars) is the only line across any cut between -115 and -88 longitude. Without it the network falls
   into 5 clusters: the east (Chicago eastward, 1,218 stations), the west coast and Las Vegas (554), Denver (36),
   Utah (28), Kansas City and Peoria (24). No city zone and no market zone joins east and west (M). Salt Lake City
   is in the big cluster because Utah County's zone reaches line 92's St George and Cedar City stops.
2. **Few trips cross, but every one rides that line.** Cut at -100: 2.4-4.8% of the market's rail trips cross,
   3.6% over the day (91,000 of 2.53M at free flow), but **28-42% of its rail-km** (the coast-to-coast longer
   trips; no commute crosses) (M). Solved as separate clusters, line 92 would lose 55% of its market riders and
   the Chicago lines that feed it 27-74% (lines 69, 64, 68) (M, free flow). That is the player's own long line
   showing half its riders: the exactly visible lie Anita doesn't accept at the final.
3. **Cutting saves no work.** The market's pass costs about the same per station on a part as on the whole, so
   the parts add back up to the whole: all five periods 17.1 s whole, 4.3 s west + 12.6 s east = 16.8 s apart (M,
   one core, machine shared, about 10% noise; per period from -16% to +7%). T-1650's China case again: a clean cut,
   nothing bought. A whole solve's time is set by the periods dealt to each worker, so it stays at 104-131 s.

The one thing a cut does buy is the zone in view's wait when it sits in a small part (Salt Lake City's own
cluster would be the 28 Utah stations, solved in seconds), and that is what T-2605 is fixing without changing
any figure.

## 1. Measured

**Clusters** (`clusters.py`, the `splitClusters` rule in lon/lat, a city's cells' box taken as its registry box):
lines, complexes and the 1 km rule alone make 4 components, one of them 1,821 stations from -123.4 to -70.9; the
cities' and market's reach join the rest. With line 92 left out: 24 components, then 5 clusters after cities and
market (above).

**Stations by longitude** (5-degree bands from -125): 234, 319, 29, 22, 14, 4, 19, 119, 202, 255, 643. So 618
stations west of -100, 1,242 east.

**Market rail crossing a cut** (`cuts.py`, free flow, % of the market's rail trips (of its rail-km), period 1, the

| -110 to -96 (the plains) | 582-620 / 1,240-1,278 | 4.7-4.9% | 40-42% |

Three parts (-100, -84): 6-10% of trips, 42-53% of rail-km. Four (-110, -90, -80): 9-14%, 51-62%. The plains are
the one weak cut; everywhere else in the east is Japan-like.

**Each part's share of a round** (I): the market is about 60% of a round (T-2553: 4.1-4.6 s a period on 2
workers). The west is 25% of the market's pass (4.3 of 17.1 s natively) and 29 of 101 market places; the east
73%. Cities: about 30% west by zones (I, not timed).

**What separate parts lose** (`err.py`, market boardings over the day at free flow, whole against the two clipped
parts): 457,000 of 6.0M (7.6%). Line 92: 166,000 to 75,000 (-55%); 69 (Chicago to Philadelphia) -27%; 64 -74%;
68 -51%; 29 (Washington to Boston) -8%; 114 -7%. At free flow line 92's market boardings are 6 times its room at
crush (about 25,000 a day, `cap.py`), so after crowding the crossing riders are capped by line 92 near that; the
share it loses stays about half or more, since its through riders are what it carries (I).

## 2. The options

| | speed | error on lines | size | worldwide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) cut line 92, crossing pairs' loads frozen from the last solve, exchanged once or iterated | whole solve: none (parts add back to the whole; iterated adds a crossing pass searching the smaller side's 619 stations over the whole graph, about the west's cost again); zone in view in a small part: seconds to ~30 s | exchanged once: mid-solve within 10-20% except line 92's crossing riders stale; the final needs a whole solve anyway, so no gain there | 3-4 stages, Opus (a frozen background population per cut line, the window machinery of T-1735 reused, the split of a line between clusters) | rarely applies: needs a single weak line between two big parts |
| (b) parts as separate clusters when crossing rail is under a threshold | the same: none on the whole solve, the zone in view sooner | exact change: line 92 -55% of market riders, its feeders -27 to -74%; crossing trips go to plane or car in every figure | 2 stages (a weak-line rule in `splitClusters`, a line split into per-cluster pieces) | Europe: no weak line (I, Japan-like); China: cuts cleanly but cheap (T-1650); a whole-world save: oceans already split it exactly where no line crosses |
| (c) nothing new: T-1733/T-1735's freeze after an edit, slcload's T-2605 for the zone in view | edits: the touched zones in seconds; zone in view: what T-2605 gets | exact at the final; mid-solve T-1733's lies | 0 new stages | holds everywhere |

**Recommended: (c).** Neither cut makes the whole solve faster here, and both put their error on the one line the
player built across the country. The cluster rule already does the exact cut for free wherever no line crosses
(oceans, a separate country with no line between).

**The general shape.** A cut is either free (no line crosses, so the clusters are already split exactly) or it
falls on a player's line, and then every crossing rider is on that line: the lie concentrates on the thing the
cut touches. And since the market's pass is about linear in stations, splitting it does not shrink it; only
fewer stations or fewer passes would. If the market's 60% is to come down, that is where to look (for instance a
cheaper pass for the coast-to-coast longer trips, a few percent of the trips and 28-42% of the rail-km here; the
pass's time by trip length was not measured); not filed.
