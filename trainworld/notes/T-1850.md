# T-1850: keep far clusters' results after an edit elsewhere (design), 2026-10-30

Design only, agent t1850; no code in app/ or sim/ changed. Measurements: own Vite (6150), 2 demand
workers, headless Chrome; Node runs of the real demand worker. Scripts in `notes/T-1850/`, outputs in
`../artifacts/trainworld/t1850/`.

## Short answer

T-1847's reading was wrong about the cause. **Opening Ceuta's and Cadiz's market packs does not
make Tokyo dirty.** A market part keeps the composition it was made from while its key lasts
(T-409, T-1198), and a cluster with no rider in common is already kept to the bit across a
recomposition (Node check below). What re-solves Tokyo is that **building the Ceuta line moves
Tokyo's own inputs by float noise**: 5 of Tokyo's 133 lines came to the workers with run times
up to 0.12 s and trains an hour up to 0.08% different from the network Tokyo was last solved on.
The cluster's `sig` hashes those floats exactly, so the whole Tokyo cluster is solved again. That
happens on any edit anywhere, not just a first line in a new region.

Recommendation: let a cluster keep its result when its shape is unchanged and its service values
are within noise of the ones it was solved on (stage 1, demand worker only, TypeScript, no WASM
change). Separately, the clock's settle should stop moving lines that share no track with the edit
(stage 2, sim/src/track, a bug against what track-and-service.md already promises). The 3-worker
pool's market split (T-1607) re-solves every market cluster in a solve with the barrier. That is
a third and smaller problem (stage 3, measure first).

Since T-1851 (landed 2026-10-30) the tag and the 4x cap already lift once the zone in view is
final, so Ceuta-in-Tokyo no longer shows "Trips being recomputed..." for two minutes. What is
left is the hidden cost: 95-157 s of two workers re-solving Tokyo after every Ceuta edit.
During that time Tokyo clicks wait, the network-wide totals and the demand cache wait, and the
CPU stays busy. It is worth doing: stage 1 is small, and it also removes the same waste after
every edit in any multi-cluster game.

## What was measured

**Node, real demand worker** (`notes/T-1850/kept.mjs`, the t409/t301 harness): Boston solved alone,
then Philadelphia's city pack and then its market pack opened, as the page sends them. Both later
solves (with the barrier, as for a zone just opened) keep Boston's cluster
(`clusters kept/solved [1,1]`, plan `kept ["bos"]`). Boston's line loads and city summary are the
same to the bit as before the opening and as a fresh worker that opened everything at once. So
recomposition alone dirties nothing.

Boston-only composition. This is not caused by keeping: today a re-solve of that cluster runs on

depends on what was open when it was made. `kept2.mjs` showed it is not the corridor table.)

**Browser, Tokyo real-network save + a 3-station Ceuta line** (`notes/T-1850/tokyo.mjs`, which
records every solve, open, plan and done message between the page and the workers, and with
`QUICK=1` dumps each solve's network):

| | |
|---|---|
| load to final | 129-169 s (2 workers) |
| Ceuta's market packs (ceu, cad) and city pack opened | 1.6 s before the line's last edit |
| solves after the line | v3 (stations), v4 (trains): neither has the barrier; both plan `solving ["1:ceu", "5:tyo,tcg,gnm..."]`, `kept 0` |
| Tokyo's whole re-solve | `done` 95 s (worker with 2 periods) and 157 s (worker with 3) after the line |
| `demandUpdating` cleared | 158 s after the line |

`notes/T-1850/netdiff.mjs` compares the networks by line id. From the load's last solve (v2) to v3,
5 old lines changed: run times up to 0.12 s, trains an hour on 4 lines by 0.00-0.08% (line 128:
10.250 to 10.242 an hour at the high level). Stations, stops, cars, fares and the frame did not
change. From v3 to v4 (only the Ceuta line's trains set) the same lines moved again by about
0.001 s. That is T-1737's third finding: the settle steps every unconverged line of a level an
edit makes active. The spec says a held level is left "to the bit"
(track-and-service.md, "No resource carries more trains than its capacity") and it is not.

With 3 workers (Anita's machine) one more thing re-solves Tokyo in a solve with the barrier: the
market split (`sharing` in `demand.worker.ts`'s `dirty` filter marks every market cluster dirty).
Ceuta's solves had no barrier (the zone was already shown bare), so this did not apply here. It
does apply when construction reaches a zone that is not on screen.

## What a cluster's result depends on

A cluster's result is exactly a function of:

1. **Its populations, as objects**: each city's commute and everyday layers (pack and level: a
   level change reopens the pack, giving a new object) and its market part (one composition, kept
   while its key lasts). `held` is keyed by object, so a new object is always unsolved.
2. **Its sub-network** (`subNetwork` in `demandClusters.ts`): the shape (stations' positions,
   complex grouping, upgrades, passages, bus catchments, fare multipliers, each line's stops,
   cars and hop km) and the service values (run times, trains an hour, service span).
3. **The settings** in `sig`'s head: the cities in it, its market key, rounds, fares (base, per km,
   curve, country scales, transfer), the frame.
4. **The period layout**: which worker solved which period of each population (`mine`, and with
   T-1607 `mkt`), and whether each period came from a whole solve (`heldQ` psig, not `~local`).

Nothing outside the cluster enters: other clusters share no rider, and a pair whose ends lie in two
clusters is in neither (T-409). The current test (`held.get(p) === c.sig` for every population, plus
`sharedParts`) covers 1-4 exactly. It is correct, but too strict on the service values in 2.

## The rule (stage 1)

A dirty cluster whose only difference is noise keeps its result. It is **noise-only** when all of
these hold:

- **Same shape, exactly.** A second hash from `subNetwork`, `shape`: `sig`'s head plus `stXY`,
  complex, ups, passages, catchments, `fareW`, `lineN`, `stops`, `cars` and `hopKm`, without times,
  trains an hour and span. Every population of the cluster holds this shape (`heldShape`, set
  beside `held` in `solved()`).
- **Same populations, whole-solved.** Every population holds each of this worker's periods from a
  whole solve (`heldQ` entries equal to the cluster's last psig, no `~local`). The part is the one
  `assignParts` gives this key. There is no `freshParts` and no `job.fresh`.
- **Service values within noise of the values it was solved on.** Compare with the view each
  population was last whole-solved on (`cityOn` for cities, which T-1733 already keeps; a new
  `partOn` for the market part, its `clusterView`, set in `solved()`). Do not compare with the
  previous network, so drift over many edits cannot add up past the bound. Run times within
  **0.5 s** each; trains an hour within **0.2%** relative; span **exactly equal** (a span step is
  one train a day, which T-1764 judged noise only for the local solve; leave it out of a lasting
  keep until measured).
- Not `sharing` and not in `sharedParts` (stage 3 takes those).

Why these bounds: the noise seen is at most 0.12 s and 0.08%. The smallest real change a player
can make is one train on a line, which is 1/N of its trains an hour. That is past 0.2% for any
line with fewer than 500 trains at a level, which covers every line in the game. Track and
speed edits change run times by seconds. Real knock-ons (a crossing at grade, T-1737's first
finding) cut lines by tens of percent.

On a noise-only keep the worker re-keys the cluster to the new network without solving it:
`held.set(p, c.sig)`, `heldShape` as is, each `heldQ[q] = c.psig[q]`, and the `capRatio` entry
copied from the old `sig` to the new one (`keepCapRatio` is keyed by `sig`; T-1609's local refine
reads it). `cityOn`/`partOn` stay as the views it was solved on. The populations stay set on the
network they solved. Their results are in local indices of an identical shape, and the new
cluster's `nodeGlobal` maps them to the whole network as for any kept cluster. The next solve
that touches the cluster for real calls `set_network`, and Rust's `same_periods` decides from
what the population holds.

**The lie** (for DECISIONS.md): a cluster that is not edited keeps riders worked out on run
times up to 0.5 s and trains an hour up to 0.2% off what the clock now shows, until something
in it changes past that. Today those figures move by about as much at every re-solve anyway.

## What changes where (stage 1)

- `app/src/workers/demandClusters.ts` `subNetwork`: a `shape` hash beside `sig` on `Cluster`.
- `app/src/workers/demand.worker.ts` `run()`: before `dirty` (line 1504 as of 2026-10-30), a
  `noiseOnly(c)` test as above. Kept clusters are re-keyed and left out of `dirty`. `solved()`
  also sets `heldShape` and `partOn`. `forget`/`disturbed` drop both. A `?perfOff=noise` switch
  (job flag `exactKeep`) gives the exact rule back, for A/B and for the check.
- No WASM interface change, no protocol change (`plan.kept` already names kept clusters).
- docs/demand-runtime.md "Clusters": one sentence on noise-only keeps. DECISIONS.md: the lie.

The 3-worker non-barrier case comes along with it. A Tokyo cluster last solved with its market
split (`sharedParts`) and re-keyed by `noiseOnly` takes T-1733's "kept apart, solves nothing"
path (`localOf` with no fresh populations), because `held` now matches. The builder should
check this path on `?demandWorkers=3`.

## What the player sees afterwards (Ceuta in the Tokyo save)

- The line's first figures at once and Ceuta's final in about 1-6 s, as now.
- The tag and the 4x cap lift with Ceuta's final, as since T-1851.
- New: the solve itself ends a few seconds after the line, not after 95-157 s (2 workers) or about
  75 s (3). It reads Tokyo's kept figures, as a kept cluster's are read after any edit today. The
  network-wide totals, Tokyo clicks and the demand cache are ready at once, and the workers go
  idle. Each later Ceuta edit is the same, where today each one starts Tokyo's re-solve again.
- The same holds for an edit in any cluster of a game with several far-apart networks.

## Risks

1. **A stale result surviving (the one that matters).** It is ruled out by construction. The
   shape must be equal exactly, so any station, stop, line, car, fare or zone change re-solves.
   The populations must be the same objects, whole-solved, so a level change, a reopened pack or a
   new part re-solves. Service values are compared with what was solved on, not the last network,
   so creep cannot add up. The bounds are below any single player edit. Checks that would catch
   a mistake:
   - **Node** (a new `checks/t1850-noise-keep.mjs` from `notes/T-1850/kept.mjs`): Boston and
     Philadelphia lines. (a) Edit Philadelphia while moving Boston's times 0.1 s and trains an
     hour 0.05%: Boston is kept, and its figures are the same to the bit as before. (b) Boston's
     trains an hour +1%, its run times +1 s, its span one step, one car more, a stop moved: each
     re-solves Boston, and its figures equal a fresh worker's to the bit. (c) The same noise
     repeated 5 times, adding up past 0.2%: Boston re-solves on the edit that crosses. (d) A
     zone reopened at another level with only noise elsewhere: that cluster is still solved
     (T-1609's path).
   - **Browser**, Tokyo save + Ceuta line (`tokyo.mjs`): `plan.kept` names Tokyo's cluster.
     Tokyo's lines' riders in the final equal the load's final to the bit. Against a fresh load
     of the saved game with the Ceuta line (which solves on the new values), every line with
     10,000+ riders a day is within 0.5%. That is the size of the lie.
   - `smoke.mjs`, and `t409-worker-check`, `t301-worker-check` unchanged.
   - `calib --cells --exact` does not fit this check. It runs one city's network natively, with
     no clusters, worker or keep rule. The worker comparisons above are the equivalent.
2. **A period kept from a local solve.** The test requires whole-solve psig in `heldQ`, so a
   cluster with a `~local` period is never noise-kept.
3. **Questions about a kept cluster.** `isHeld` reads `held === sig`, so after re-keying, clicks are
   answered from the kept state at once, as for an exactly kept cluster.
4. **Cost.** `noiseOnly` compares the views of a cluster's populations. That is the same work as
   T-1733's `sameView`, milliseconds on all of Japan.

## Stages (each its own task)

1. **Noise-only keep in the demand worker** (above). About 100-150 lines in `demand.worker.ts`
   and `demandClusters.ts`, plus the Node check. Done when: in the Tokyo save + Ceuta line on 2
   workers, Tokyo's cluster is kept and `demandUpdating` clears within **10 s** of the line (today
   158 s). Tokyo's lines are equal to the bit to the load's final, and within 0.5% (lines with
   10,000+ riders) of a fresh load of the save with the Ceuta line. The Node check and smoke
   pass. Opus: the period, `capRatio` and `sharedParts` bookkeeping is easy to get quietly wrong.
2. **The settle leaves lines with no shared track to the bit** (sim/src/track, `TrackWorld::
   settle_train_counts`): step only the lines in capacity components the edit touched, or iterate
   held and unconverged lines to convergence at load (T-1737's third finding). Done when
   `netdiff.mjs` shows 0 changed old lines between the load's network and the Ceuta line's.
   After this, stage 1's tolerance is a safety net more than the main path. Opus, and only once
   the agents now in sim/src/track are done.
3. **Barrier solves on 3 workers keep clean market clusters** (T-1607's split made per cluster: a
   kept cluster's market periods stay where it last solved them; `Version.markets` per part for
   `icSel`, `icTrip`, `icStation`). First measure how often it happens: a station built in a zone
   not on screen, on a game with a big network elsewhere. If it is rare, a P3.

## Stage 1 built (T-1862, 2026-10-30)

Built as above, with one simplification: each population's record (`heldOn` in
`demand.worker.ts`, a WeakMap) holds the cluster's `sig`, `psig`, `shape` and the cluster network it
was last whole-solved on, so the noise test compares the cluster's network with that, not the
per-population `cityOn` views plus a `partOn`. A population's view is cut from its cluster's network,
so within noise on the cluster means within noise on every view. `cityOn` and `marketNet` are left as
they were. The keep runs before `dirty` in `run()` when the job is not sharing, not rough (`only`),
not `fresh` and not `freshParts`; a cluster last solved sharing (`sharedParts`) is re-keyed and stays
dirty, so T-1733's "kept apart, solves nothing" path takes it. `?perfOff=noise` (`exactKeep`).

Measured on own Vite (6161), Tokyo save + the 3-station Ceuta line (`T-1850/tokyo-keep.mjs`, figures by
`T-1850/keep-compare.mjs <out.json>`, outputs in `../artifacts/trainworld/t1862/`). Only 2 cores were free in the ledger (the Australia run held 9), so
the browser runs used **1 demand worker**, not 2; Tokyo is kept either way, so the edit's time does
not depend on the worker count.

| | run 1 | run 2 | run 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| load to final | 223 s | 241 s | 230 s |
| solves after the line's last edit | v3, v4 (the trains' settle, 1.1 s later) | v3 only | v3, v4 (1.0 s later) |
| plan | `solving ["1:ceu"]`, `kept` Tokyo's 5 zones, every solve | same | same |
| `demandUpdating` cleared for good | not timed (the timer took v3's 0.66 s) | **2.2 s** | **10.4 s** |

In every solve Tokyo's worker solves only Ceuta. Where a second solve comes (v4, the line's trains
landing after its stations), that one is an edit of the Ceuta cluster: T-1733's local solve of Ceuta
(its figures at 1.1 s), then the whole solve waits `EDIT_SETTLE_MS` (8 s, demandClient.ts) for a
next edit, so the final lands at about 10 s, 9.9 s in the worker. So the 10 s target holds on one
solve and is about met on two; the rest is that deliberate wait, not Tokyo. The design above
expected "a few seconds" and missed this wait. Today: 158 s on 2 workers (above).

Runs 1 and 2: the 133 old lines' riders and boardings after the line equal the load's final to the
bit; against a fresh load of the save with the line, 110 lines with 10,000+ riders a day, none past
0.5%, worst 0.038% (line 128, the line whose trains the settle moved most). No page errors. Node: `checks/t1862-noise-keep.mjs` PASS (26 lines), `t409-worker-check` and
`t301-worker-check` PASS. Smoke PASS (with `?demandWorkers=1` to fit the cores). Not checked in a
browser: the 3-worker path (a cluster last solved with its market apart, re-keyed and then kept
apart), for want of cores; the Node check covers 1 worker only.

## Corrections to earlier notes

notes/T-1847.md section 5 and TASKS.md's T-1850 line say that recomposing the market dirties
every cluster. It does not (Node check above). What dirties far clusters is the edit's noise in
their own lines' run times and trains an hour.
