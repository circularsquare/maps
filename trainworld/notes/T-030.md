# T-030 notes: water mask in the city pack

Track at level 0 cannot be built over water, and bridges and tunnels over or under water cost 2x
(SPEC 6.1, 6.4). The pack now carries a water mask: a 25 m raster over the city's bounding box,
stored as runs per row. Layout and lookup: notes/T-004.md, "Water mask". Code: `pipeline/water.py`
(sources, rasterizer, encoding), `build_city.py` (`build_water`), `read_pack.py` (independent check
and lookup), `look_water.py` (the image, `T-030.png`).

## Source: TIGER for M1, OSM later

- **Used: TIGER/Line 2020 AREAWATER** for every county within ~30 km of the box (77 counties in
  NY, NJ, CT, PA, RI, MA; 30,237 polygons, 40 MB of zips), which covers rivers, bays, sounds and
  reservoirs and the sea out to the state line (3 nautical miles), **plus the open sea**: the box
  minus the TIGER 2020 state polygons, which end at that same line. One request per county, no
  processing beyond a union per row.
- **Not used: OSM water polygons** (osmdata.openstreetmap.de, coastline-derived sea) plus OSM
  inland water (Overpass `natural=water` and riverbanks). This is the global route and the one
  M3 needs, but the sea file is a single 906 MB zip with no way to fetch a region, and inland
  water from Overpass for a 280 x 290 km box is a large query whose multipolygons need assembling.
  The TIGER route took an hour to build and check; the OSM route is a follow-up (T-041) and
  must reproduce the checks below.

## Rasterizing

Pixel = water when its centre is inside a water polygon. A scanline fill per polygon (even-odd,
so holes and islands come out right) gives runs per row directly; runs from different polygons
(TIGER splits water at county lines, the sea overlaps AREAWATER at the 3-mile line) are merged
per row. No raster library: numpy only, ~12 s for New York.

**Water narrower than ~50 m is dropped**: a morphological opening with a 2 x 2-pixel square
removes creeks, ditches, canals and ponds under about two pixels across. A railway crosses those on
a culvert or a short bridge at grade; with them in, every 15 m creek would force a viaduct and two
200 m ramps, and pixel-centre sampling would catch such creeks only here and there anyway. The
opening never adds water and leaves the shoreline of anything wider where it was. Rivers that
stay: the Harlem River, Newtown Creek, the Bronx Kill, the Hackensack and Passaic. Rivers that go
in places: the upper Bronx River, the Gowanus Canal (both ~20-30 m). Raw: 370,557 runs; after
the opening 266,401.

## Size

| | |
|---|---|
| Grid | 11,410 x 11,647 pixels of 25 m (285 x 291 km: the boundary's box plus 2 km) |
| Water | 40.2% of the box (most of it the Atlantic and Long Island Sound) |
| Runs | 266,401 |
| In the pack | 1.11 MB raw (row index 47 kB, run bounds 1.07 MB), about 0.62 MB gzipped |
| Build | ~12 s, plus the AREAWATER downloads once |

A 50 m grid would be 0.67 MB raw; 25 m was kept because the shore is where track gets built (the
Hudson Line runs on the riverbank) and pixel-centre sampling puts the shore within half a pixel.

## Checks

`read_pack.py` checks the encoding (row offsets even and increasing, runs sorted, disjoint, not
touching, inside the grid, the shipped water share) and 18 places by the exact lookup in
notes/T-004.md: water at the Hudson off 34th St, the East River at the Williamsburg Bridge, the
Harlem River at 155th St, the Upper Bay, Jamaica Bay, Grassy Bay, Newark Bay, Long Island Sound,
Kensico Reservoir, Raritan Bay and the Atlantic off Rockaway; land at Times Square, Roosevelt
Island, Governors Island, Central Park, Hoboken, JFK and Staten Island. Interior test points for
Jamaica Bay and the Harlem River were taken from the TIGER polygons themselves (my first guesses
landed on marsh islands).

`T-030.png`: the whole box and close-ups of Manhattan with the East River, Roosevelt and Governors
Islands (Liberty and Ellis Islands show as land too); the Harlem River, Randalls Island and Rikers;
Jamaica Bay with its marsh islands and JFK; Newark Bay, the Kill van Kull and the Arthur Kill. The
overview shows the Hudson, Long Island Sound, the Great South Bay, the Raritan and the reservoirs
(Kensico, the Croton system, Wanaque, Round Valley, Candlewood). Manhattan's piers come out as land.

## Left over

- The mask is the pack's bounding box only; outside it the lookup says land. Fine for one city;
  intercity track (M2) needs the world pack's water, which is where the OSM route belongs.
- Small islands under ~50 m stay land inside water (the opening removes thin water, not thin
  land), which is right for piers and breakwaters.
