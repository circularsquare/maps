# T-090 soft station reach, and a simple drive time

Done 2026-10-09 by the demand agent (batch 6). The 2.5 km edge of a station's reach is gone.
A station loses riders because the walk makes the whole trip worse than driving: the walk's
perceived cost steepens after 10 minutes, and driving now has a speed that depends on how long
the trip is and how dense its ends are, so it decides where rail stops paying. Two bounds remain,
for computing only, set where almost no rail is left. Recalibrated on the real New York network;
the recompute is 15-25% slower (inside the 1.5x allowed).

## The model (SPEC 4.3)

**The walk to and from stations** (`Params::access_cost`): 15 km/h along a path 1.3x the straight
line, so 5.2 real minutes a straight km. The first 10 minutes (1.9 km) weigh 2.0 a minute, every
minute after that 8.0.

| straight line to the station | 0.5 km | 1 km | 1.5 km | 2 km | 2.5 km | 3 km | 3.5 km | 4 km |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| real minutes | 2.6 | 5.2 | 7.8 | 10.4 | 13.0 | 15.6 | 18.2 | 20.8 |
| perceived minutes | 5 | 10 | 16 | 23 | 44 | 65 | 86 | 106 |
| odds against driving, vs beside the station | 77% | 59% | 46% | 31% | 11% | 4% | 1.4% | 0.5% |

Why steeper after 10 minutes: Anita's "a 10-minute ride to the station readily, a 30-minute one
rarely", and so that the draw fades out instead of stopping at a line. A gentler far weight (4)
left a long tail into Manhattan (driving there is slow and parking 40 minutes, so a 6 km walk
still won 13% of trips) that only a hard bound could stop, which put the edge back where the
player can see it (below). Piecewise linear like the wait (first 5 minutes at 1.37, then 0.4):
simple, two numbers.

**Driving** (`Params::place_min_per_km`, `drive_min`): road = 1.3 x straight line; the first and
last 2 km (at most half the road each) on local streets, the rest on main roads. Each place has
`f = 1 / (1 + density / 5000)` (density = commuters living and working per km² of land in its 2 km
zone), local street speed `15 + 25 f` km/h and main road speed `15 + 65 f` km/h: 40 and 80 in open
country, 24 and 39 in Brooklyn (8,600/km²), 18 and 23 in Manhattan (35,000/km²). The middle of a
trip runs at the mean of its two ends' main-road minutes a km. Then x 1.33 (congested driving),
plus 3 minutes to park and the destination's parking cost, unchanged.

| trip | new | before (flat 28 km/h) |
|---|---|---|
| 25 km between open suburbs | 27 min | 70 min |
| Brooklyn to Midtown, 10 km | 30 min | 28 min |
| 3 km inside Manhattan | 13 min | 8 min |

Mean drive minutes (+3 to park) over the gravity's commutes by county of residence, against ACS
car commute minutes (B08136 / B08301; the model's are all commutes, ACS's only those who drive;
`scratchpad drive_times.py`, same formula):

| county | ACS | flat 28 | new |
|---|---|---|---|
| New York | 37.1 | 24.6 | 26.3 |
| Kings | 37.5 | 36.6 | 33.3 |
| Queens | 36.1 | 41.0 | 34.1 |
| Bronx | 33.7 | 43.1 | 37.3 |
| Hudson | 31.1 | 33.1 | 28.2 |
| Bergen | 27.2 | 52.8 | 36.4 |
| Nassau | 30.7 | 60.5 | 40.6 |
| Suffolk | 29.1 | 74.6 | 42.4 |
| Westchester | 28.6 | 67.3 | 42.9 |

The suburbs are still 1.3-1.5x ACS: the gravity's suburban commutes are 20-30% too long (T-034
section 2, T-072); the road speed is not bent to absorb that.

**Bounds** (computational, not rules): stations are looked for within 4 km straight line
(`walk_cutoff_m`), and a pair of places is offered rail only if its two walks together cost at most
80 perceived minutes (`pair_walk_max`). What they cut:

| | real network | hand-made | lone Manhattan line |
|---|---|---|---|
| rail trips starting 3.5-4 km from the station (last half km) | 0.0% | 0.1% | 0.1% |
| rail trips starting 3-3.5 km out | 0.9% | 1.5% | 3.0% |
| rail trips with walks of 70-80 together (last 10 minutes) | 0.3% | 0.5% | 2.3% |
| riders lost to both bounds (against 8 km and no pair bound) | under 1% | under 1% | 4.3% |

The lone line is the worst case: driving into Manhattan is the worst there is, so its draw lasts
longest. Without the bounds 1.5% of its riders would come from 3.5-4 km and 1.5% from 4-4.5 km;
with them its catchment ends at about 3.5 km, where its density has fallen to about a sixth of
the first km's.

**K = 6 stations per cell** unchanged: in the centre the 6 nearest are all within a km anyway; far
out a cell rarely has 6 within 4 km.

- The drive does most of the work on the suburbs: faster suburban roads took Long Island,
  Westchester/Connecticut and the outer counties from 1.5-2x ACS to about ACS. The first version
  (density only at the ends, main roads fast everywhere) made driving inside the city too quick and
  took the boroughs to 32%; slowing the main roads with density too fixed that. Length alone (no
  density) is worse still (29%).
- The far walk weight trades the tail against the bound: at 4 the lone line still drew riders from
  6 km and more (13% of them beyond 4 km), so any bound that held the pairs cut it visibly; at 8 little is
  left past 80 perceived minutes of walking.
- Rejected: a hard reach only, no pair bound (pairs 2.3x, time 2.1x); weight 4 with pairs under
  60 (the lone line's last 10 minutes held 8.5% of its riders: a visible edge); the plain mean of
  the cells' perceived walks for a subzone (rail 3.3% under one subzone per cell; the logsum mean
  0.2% over).

## Recompute time

Same machine, shared, runs interleaved before/after. "Stage" is the free-flow estimate or one
crowding round of one period; a day is five periods x four stages on one thread.

| | pairs a period | subzones | set_network | stage (median) | a day |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| hand-made, WASM (Node 22), before | 4.14M | 2,056 | 14-25 ms | 230-240 ms | 4.6-4.9 s |
| hand-made, WASM, after | 4.17M | 2,750 | 16-27 ms | 269-271 ms | 5.4-5.6 s (+15%) |
| real, WASM, before | 13.6M | 3,702 | 34 ms | 1.00-1.03 s | 19.6-20.4 s |
| real, WASM, after | 13.8M | 5,032 | 57-58 ms | 1.20-1.23 s | 24.0-25.0 s (+22%) |
| hand-made, native, before / after | | | 13-18 / 13-15 ms | 213 / 265 ms | 4.3 / 5.3 s (+24%) |
| real, native, before / after | | | 18-26 / 21 ms | 930 / 1,064 ms | 18.1 / 20.6-21.1 s (+15%) |
| lone line, WASM, before / after | 2.3k | 48 / 78 | 9 / 12 ms | under 1 ms | 10 ms |

Not measured in Chrome with the worker pool; at +15% the mode split would land about 0.55-0.65 s
after an edit on the hand-made network (T-078: 0.49-0.55 s) and the third round by about 2.2 s.

Where it went, and what held it:

- **The pair bound** is what keeps the pairs at the old count: with the 4 km reach and no pair
  bound the hand-made network had 2.3x the pairs and took 2.1x the time.
- **Station access** by scattering each station over the cells within 4 km (7-13 ms native). The
  first try searched outwards from each cell ring by ring: 52-173 ms where stations are sparse.
- **Packed destinations**: everything a pair reads about its destination (centre, drive speeds,
  parking, trips, walk, egress stations and weights) in one array in visiting order, and the decay
  and walk share for each destination zone read once per origin zone. Each cut a few %.
- What is left: 34% more origin subzones, each building its row over all stations and visiting the
  destination zones (even with few pairs to compute in each), and the drive costs a few more
  operations per pair. At the same subzones and pairs the new code is about 10% slower than the
  old.
- Destination zones sorted by their cheapest walk in 10-minute steps, zone order within a step:
  sorting by the walk alone scattered the decay table lookups and was 20% slower.

## Accuracy of subzones

`demand_bench --pack data/packs/nyc.json --exact` (hand-made network, morning peak, free flow), one
subzone per cell (40,778 cells within 4 km, 881M pairs, 43 s native) against the 2,750 subzones:
rail +0.2%, station entries 5.0% and exits 6.3% off, segment loads 3.0% (T-078: -1.8%, 5.1%, 6.1%,
2.2%). A subzone's walk to each station is the logsum mean of its cells' perceived walks at the
mode choice's scale (`-ln(mean(exp(-0.05 c))) / 0.05`); the plain mean put rail 3.3% under.

## The demand views (T-084)

Shapes and units unchanged. `stationRiders` lists every cell with riders (not truncated), so a
catchment is now about twice as many cells (the lone line's busiest station: 207 -> 406 home
cells, a few kB more); the zones at the far end stay the top 20. The map draws each cell as a disc
by the square root of its riders, so the catchment fades out with the riders. `cellModes` is
unchanged: cells beyond 4 km of every station have no rail, as before beyond 2.5 km.
