# T-3934: what the bus walking reach costs and gains (agent busreach, 2026-11-21)

## For Anita

**Why 600 m is there.** It was never a speed limit. It is "how far people walk to a bus": 400 m in the first bus
build (T-1464), cut to 300 m in T-1466 because buses carried too many (300 m plus the 100 m along the route is a
real 400 m walk), raised to 600 m by you on 11-20 to make buses stronger (T-3856, which measured no compute cost).
The walk is priced like any walk to a train, so a far home already finds the bus worse; the reach only decides who
is offered it at all. "No limit" below is 3 km, the same bound a walk to a train has (`walk_cutoff_m`). A 10 km reach
on Taipei gave the same figures to 0.2% (1,253,876 bus riders against 1,251,996), so 3 km is as far as it goes.

**Three bus-heavy community saves** (the biggest shares that let others open a copy):
"Tokyo" by harinezumi (500 lines, 135 bus lines, 1,682 km of bus; 62 lines in Tokyo), "Japan, Hongkong, Taipei" by
JCT Railways (21 bus lines in Taipei), "East China" by Ylik (7 bus lines in Guangzhou). None is mostly buses: the
biggest shares are rail networks with a lot of buses on top. Only 11 of the 461 copyable shares were read (the
server allows 30 save downloads an hour), so a mostly-bus network may well be among the rest.

- **What the limit saves is small.** Taking it away entirely costs 5-7% more time on the two big networks (Guangzhou's
  runs swing more than that, so read it as "a few percent") and about a third on the small Taipei one, where the searches are cheap and the mode choice (which grows with the reach) is
  half the work. Memory doesn't move at all, natively or in the browser.
- **What it costs in riders depends on the city.** In Tokyo and Guangzhou, where rail is everywhere, a 7-10 km bus
  gains only 3-7% at 3 km: homes far from the bus have a train closer. In Taipei, where this player's buses cover
  places without rail, a 10 km bus gains about 40% (browser and native agree). All the player's rail and bus trips
  together move by 1-2% at most.
- **1 km is a middle step**: most of the "no limit" riders in Tokyo and Guangzhou and about 60% of them in Taipei,
  for about half of its time.
- **What buses themselves cost** (reach 1 m: the stops exist but no home walks to them, so demand barely sees
  the buses): Tokyo calculates in 628-712 s instead of 1,003-1,104 s and peaks at 3,033 MB instead of 3,660 MB. So
  the Tokyo player's buses make the calculation at least 57% slower and 21% heavier at today's reach (at least:
  stops at train stations are still walked to at 1 m, and carry 1.48M riders), and the reach adds only 7% on top
  even with no limit.

**Where the time goes with many bus stops** (Tokyo, the stage times summed over the five periods):

| Stage | No buses seen (reach 1 m) | Reach 600 m | Extra |
|---|---|---|---|
| Searches towards each station (commutes and everyday) | 412 s | 612 s | +200 s |
| Mode choice over the groups of homes | 137 s | 270 s | +133 s |
| Everything else | 153 s | 205 s | +52 s |

The searches are the biggest piece: one search towards every station some home can reach, and 2,497 of this save's
7,147 stations are stops only buses serve (one every 400 m on Auto). Mode choice comes second: homes are grouped by
their nearest stations including their nearest bus stop, so every stop splits the groups along its route (Tokyo has
12,280 groups at 600 m against 9,574 without buses).

Ideas for "optimizations elsewhere", not built:
1. **Search towards fewer bus stops.** A stop only one bus serves can borrow the search of the next stop along its
   line, adding the ride between. Searching every third bus stop would cut the bus stops' searches by about two
   thirds: about 130 of Tokyo's 1,050 s (12%). The lie: a rider changing to another line at a skipped stop. About
   3-5 hours.
2. **Group homes by bus line and stretch, not by the exact nearest stop.** Pairs of neighbouring stops as one key
   would halve the extra groups: about 60-70 s in Tokyo (6%) and 10-15% in Taipei, where mode choice is half the
   time. Riders would shift a little between neighbouring stops of one line. About 2-3 hours.

## How it was measured

- **Saves.** The community list (`/trainworld/community/list`, every share) and the 11 biggest copyable saves,
  read only. Their bus lines were counted by a scratch Rust bin (`bus_census.rs`, in the scratch folder) that decodes
  the network: share 346qjhi4 (Tokyo, 135 bus lines, 1,682 km, 32 on Auto), 6quj22eb (44 bus lines, 268 km; 21 in
  Taipei), 9u2spdsb (14, 969 km, mostly two 400 km routes), jt9u79nn (21, 520 km, none 5-10 km), pt4956ep (14, 203
  km), ky42h7bz (the T-3481 player save, 7 Auto lines, 162 km, none 5-10 km), yep85pz7, vnm2fs79, dh2zude3,
  hsji4r7f (13), hc5wcpea (none). A share's save route allows 30 downloads an hour from one address; the first
  download loop hit that limit (about 450 refused requests, around 10:00 on 11-21), so only these 11 were read.
- **Why 600 m**, sources: notes/T-1445.md (the corridor: "anyone within 400 m of the route", a walking distance; its
  cost worry was the number of stops, not the reach), notes/archive/t1000-1499/T-1466.md (400 to 300 m to cut bus
  riders), notes/T-3856.md (300 to 600 m).
- **Native runs.** Each save opened in the game, the network the demand worker gets captured (T-3924's `snap.js`
  plus the layout and line names), then `demand_mem --snapshot` with a scratch override of `BUS_REACH_M` from an
  environment variable (native only), `--line-riders` (each line's boardings over the five periods, every
  population; not the shown day's figure, about 0.85x of it on most Taipei lines, so compare within a row) and
  stage timers in `DemandApi::pass` and `EverydayApi::pass`. Zones: Tokyo `tyo,tcg,gnm,yns,szo`, Taipei `tai`,
  Guangzhou `gua,fos,dog,shz,hon`; no regional market; two crowding rounds; 1 thread. The reaches of one save ran
  side by side so they shared the load. The reach-600 loads hash equals the unset run's, and 1 km and 3 km differ,
  so the override took effect.
- **Load.** The machine was over its core cap for most of the runs (other agents' checks: 14-19 of 14 cores claimed,
  my four runs among them); one run to the next swings 10-25%, so only differences within one row's side by side
  runs mean much. Guangzhou's third round ran at 8 of 14.
- **Browser.** Taipei save, one demand worker, the tree's WASM rebuilt at each reach (`agent_cargo.py wasm`).
  Riders a day as the Lines tab shows them; memory from the demand log's final solve. In the browser this save is one
  cluster of 17 cities (Japan, Hong Kong, Taipei, London); its wall times (976 s, 694 s, 357 s) followed the
  machine's load, not the reach.
- **Capacity.** Taipei Bus 11 shows 31,100 riders a day in the browser against 132,000 natively, a far bigger gap
  than other lines (most show about 0.85x). Likely its buses are full, not confirmed: `--app-order`, which holds
  riders to the buses' room, gives 72k, 80k, 102k, still well above the browser's figures, whose 17-city cluster and
  market differ from the one native zone. Bus 12 is the cleaner browser comparison.
- Nothing in the game changed. The tree's measuring edits (the reach override, `--line-riders`, the stage timers)
  are reverted and the tree's WASM rebuilt at 600 m; `bus_census.rs` is kept in the scratch folder.
