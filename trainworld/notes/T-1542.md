# T-1542: the US car on corridor runs (2026-10-27)

Analysis only, no game code changed. Proposal waits on Anita. Background: notes/T-1521.md round 3,
notes/T-1541.md (the Northeast's breakdown), notes/T-1546.md (the corridor runs and their evaluator).

Settled and kept: one world plane fare, the same money weight for every mode, 0.4 on longer trips, no
car upkeep, 15 km ride reach, fares need not match real ones, one world airport time.

**The model's "car" also stands for long-distance buses** (demand-plain.md), so its target on New

against 91: smaller than "94% against 64%" read.

## The cause, in numbers

Station zones of New York - Washington (middle income group, 1.99 minutes a dollar; `t1542_eval.py
parts nec_feed nyc-wdc built`):

| | time (perceived) | money | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rail | access 49 + egress 35 + wait and ride 210 = 294 | $85 fare = 166 | 440 |
| Car | about 300 drive (5 hours) + parking | $21 a head = 42 | about 360 |
| Plane | 87 drive + 135 airports + 25 wait + 58 flying = 305 | $117 = 234 | 539 |

On time alone rail already ties the car at the station zones (294 against about 320); **the whole gap
is money**, and the US car's money is much the lightest anywhere. Per km and per person:

| Country | car $/km | middle group min/$ | car's money, perceived min a km |
|---|---|---|---|
| US | 0.104 | 1.99 | **0.10** |
| UK | 0.199 | 2.59 | 0.26 |
| Germany | 0.203 | 2.50 | 0.25 |
| Spain | 0.169 | 3.39 | 0.29 |
| Japan | 0.232 | 3.55 | 0.41 |
| France | 0.323 | 2.89 | 0.47 |

Before T-1521 the US car cost $0.15 a km at the full weight (no 0.4): about 0.33 minutes a km, three
times today's, and the plane at $30 + 5c took half the trips. Every input that made it light is one
of the settled choices (real fuel, no upkeep, the 0.4, the US's income), plus one that is not settled:

- **US tolls are 0** in `countryIncome.json` `car` ("a few corridors"). Nationally that is about right,
  but the Northeast's interstates are tolled almost end to end: New York - Washington pays about
  $35-45 a car one way (Hudson crossing into New York, the New Jersey Turnpike, Delaware's I-95,
  Maryland's I-95 and the Baltimore tunnels; my estimate from published E-ZPass rates), about $0.10 a
  km. A stale input for this corridor, but a country figure cannot carry it.

The driving minute counts 1.0 on longer trips (1.33 locally, where driving is "congested"); that is a
choice with a reason (free-flowing motorways), not an error.

Published: New York - Washington 20/5/64 (plus 11% bus), Boston 5/4/87, Madrid - Barcelona 64/18/18,
London - Edinburgh's car small. Japan's case was not rerun (stopped to free cores); T-1546's run has
Tokyo - Osaka at 84/0/15 against 25% car published, so any heavier car minute takes it further off.

What it says:
- **Tolls alone barely move it** (98% to 94%): at 2 minutes a dollar, $20 a head is 40 minutes.
- **Heavier car minutes fix the US and break Europe and Japan**: the car goes to 1-3% on Madrid -
  Barcelona (18% published) and London - Edinburgh, and the plane, not rail, takes most of what the
  car loses on the Northeast (26% air on New York - Washington against 5%).
- **Only rail's own money gets rail to its published share**: at half the starting US fare plus the
  Northeast's tolls, New York - Washington is 23/2/75, almost exactly the published 20/5/64 + 11% bus.
  Boston stays 2/0/97 (published 5/4/87 + 4%), with the test line's 3 h 45 ride.
- So the Northeast's rail loses on money a real traveller there does not weigh as the model's do
  (Amtrak's fare is $106 and still takes 20%): its riders are business travellers whose employer pays,
  and households without a car (55% in New York City). The model has neither: three income groups,
  everyone can drive, and "car" includes the bus.

## Options, ranked by fix against complication

1. **Leave the model; read the target as car plus bus** (75% and 91%). The US car is then 23 and 9
   points high, not 30 and 12, and the cause is the settled money rules applied to a rich country with
   cheap fuel. The player meets it as: Northeast rail at the starting fare carries few longer trips; a
   lower fare fills it (half the fare gives 11% of all trips). No change, no complication.
2. **Northeast tolls through the routed drives**: `pipeline/market_drives.py` already prices each
   lattice cell's speed from road density; a toll per km on cells of tolled motorways (from OSM's
   `toll=yes`, a global source) would replace the per-country toll figure everywhere, so France's and
   Japan's tolls would come from their roads too. No new constant, but a data rebuild of every drive,
   and alone it moves New York - Washington only 98% to 94%.
3. **A heavier driving minute on longer trips** (1.2): fixes the US, breaks Spain, the UK and Japan.
   Not recommended.
4. **Car availability** (a share of travellers with no car, by home density): the real cause in New
   York, but a new factor, and in this model it would mostly move trips to the bus, which is "car".
   Not recommended.

**Recommendation: option 1, no change now.** File option 2 only if T-1508's refit or a later
country (Japan, France) needs tolls by road rather than by country.
