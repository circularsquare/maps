# T-679: a 4 minute headway should not raise capacity alerts (Anita's playtest, 2026-10-18)

Anita: "if we set headway to 4 min (which i think is what a lot of ppl will do) we get alerts for
turning on platform is full, 1 train held out of service. i think this is kinda annoying. lets
increase tolerance or perhaps have 'headway 4 min' mean like we keep headway just *above* 4 min so we
dont have train capacity issues. though i guess this might cause problems for routes with very few
trains so perhaps we only have this behavior for like high frequency lines (she typed >10min, meaning the frequent ones) and
always keep at least one train?" Related: T-625 (headway lines), T-657 (turnback capacity), her
item "the first 10 lines i build in a huge city are all immediately at capacity even if i run 4
minute turnaround" (T-657's reason).

## Why it happens (read from docs/track-and-service.md 6.2, 6.3; confirm in the code)
A headway line takes the fewest trains that keep the headway: `auto_trains` = ceil(round trip /
headway), the real headway allowed 0.5% over (DECISIONS 2026-10-17, T-625). Rounding the train count
up always makes the real headway *shorter* than typed. A 4 minute turnaround on a through platform
is a capacity of 15 trains an hour, which is exactly 4 minutes: so a typed 4:00 yields, for example,
9 trains on a 2,000 s round trip = 3:42, over the limit, and the 9th train is held out and the
platform alert fires. Any typed headway at the limit does this.

## Proposal (A), my recommendation
Clamp at the limit instead of letting the rounding cross it: for a headway line the train count is
min(ceil(round trip / typed), floor(round trip / line's shortest headway)), where the shortest headway is
the fullest platform's or junction's limit the panel already knows (SPEC 6.2). The clamp only binds
when the typed headway is at or within a rounding of the limit, so slower lines (and lines with very
few trains) are untouched, and at least one train always runs. The panel shows the real headway it
got (for example 4:07), and no alert fires for a line the clamp has made fit. A typed headway well
below the limit (2 minutes against a 4 minute limit) still warns, as now, because that is the
player asking for something the track cannot do.

Alternatives: (B) widen T-625's 0.5% tolerance to a few percent for every line (simple; changes
all lines' train counts slightly, and does not remove the alert, only moves where it starts);
(C) a fixed "high frequency only" rule (<10 minutes) as Anita sketched: the clamp A already acts only
at the limit, so C adds a threshold without adding a case.

## Decided (Anita, 2026-10-19)
- A: "yup". Build it. Give a clamped line's real headway a hover naming what limits it (the
  platform or junction, as the capacity alerts already name it).
- With it, T-625's look (Anita, 2026-10-19: "lets refuse the edit and maybe add a little shake and
  have the train count be painted red text. same should go for if we run more trains than headway
  min allows, we should paint the headway number red text"): an edit refused for cash shakes the
  control briefly and paints the train count red; a headway shorter than the track's limit (more
  trains than the limit allows) paints the headway number red. A shake is motion, but it is
  feedback Anita asked for, not a CSS transition between states: keep it short (about 300 ms), and
  the red stays while the condition holds.

## Built (2026-10-19)
- Cause as found: as read above, plus `trim_auto` drops only whole waiting trains (floor), so the
  part train past the limit stays bought and `line_held` reports it. Which limit binds varies: a
  dead-end double-track terminus pools its two platforms ((turnaround + 60) / 2 = 2:00 at the default
  turnaround); a single-track terminus or a turn on a through platform is 4:00. The test
  `a_headway_at_the_track_limit_holds_no_train_out` reproduces the old held train by hand first.
- The limit is the residual: `Capacity::line_limits`, per line and level, the most trains an hour
  with the other lines' rates fixed, as `fill` would cut it. The clamp is applied each settle round
  in `auto_counts`. The panel reads it from the tail of `TrackApi.line_held` (5 per level).
- Real save: as saved it has no headway lines (all 109 on legacy schedules), so nothing moves. With
  every line switched to 4:00 (one-off report, old vs new code): 16 lines' counts moved, high demand
  trains 2,933 to 2,934, cars needed 32,120 to 32,143; lines with part of a train held at high 83 to 81.
  Most New York lines share trunks or flat crossings where 4:00 each does not fit, so they are "well
  under" their residual limit and stay red and held, as designed; the moves among them (up as well
  as down) come from shared track refilling after a neighbour is clamped.
