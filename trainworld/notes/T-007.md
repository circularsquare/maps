# T-007 real-network test set: New York's real rail network and measured ridership

Done 2026-10-09 by the demand realism agent (batch 4). New York's real rail network (subway with
the Staten Island Railway, PATH, LIRR, Metro-North, NJ Transit rail and light rail) is now a
demand test network and a game save, and the demand model's results on it are compared with
measured ridership and ACS commute mode shares in one command. This file has the method, the
baseline comparison (before any constant changed) and what it says is off. Later tasks (T-054,
T-022, T-034, ...) rerun the same comparison; their effect is logged under "Comparison log".

### The network (from GTFS, a Wednesday's weekday schedule of each feed)

- **Sources**: MTA subway GTFS (Feb 2026, from `maps/riders/nycriders`), PATH (trillium mirror),
  LIRR and Metro-North (MTA's S3 feeds), NJ Transit rail (njtransit.com/rail_data.zip, which also
  carries Hudson-Bergen and Newark light rail). Raw feeds in `data/raw/gtfs/`.
- **Stations**: one per MTA station complex for the subway (`data/nystreets/MTA_Subway_Stations.csv`
  maps GTFS stops to complexes, the unit of the MTA's own counts); one per stop elsewhere; stops of
  different operators within 150 m merged (Penn Station, Hoboken, Newark Penn, Secaucus). Stations
  outside the city boundary (+1 km) dropped: lines to Trenton, New Haven, Port Jervis are cut at it.
  867 stations.
- **Lines**: per route, every daytime stopping pattern with at least 15% of the route's trips
  (subway, PATH; 5% for commuter rail) and 20 (4) trips a day is a line; a short turn adds its
  trips to the pattern it is a piece of; a pattern with the same ends and 80% of the same stops as
  a busier one is folded into it (the 7 local had two such patterns, which split its frequency in
  two and halved its riders); anything else goes to the kept pattern it overlaps most. 109 lines:
  31 subway, 5 PATH, 29 LIRR, 14 Metro-North, 30 NJ Transit.
- **Trains per hour**: high = mean of 7-9 and 17-19, medium = 10-16, low = 0-6, counted at each
  trip's stop nearest Midtown, both directions halved. **Run times**: per consecutive station pair
  and level, the median over every trip of the route (departure to departure).
- **Cars** (20 m game cars): A division 10 x 51 ft = 8, B division 600 ft = 9, J/Z/M/L 7, G 5,
  shuttles 2-5, SIR 5, PATH 5, commuter rail 10 x 85 ft = 13, light rail 3. The game's 160 riders
  at crush per 20 m car is about the MTA's loading guideline, not a true crush.

### Measured targets

- **Subway entries and exits per complex per hour**: MTA Subway Origin-Destination Ridership
  Estimate 2024 (data.ny.gov jsu2-fbtj), Wednesdays of October 2024, as already downloaded by
  nycriders: 4.34M trips a day. MTA's own daily count for those Wednesdays: 4.29M subway + 10k SIR.
- **Riders per subway link per hour**: nycriders' `stats.json`, the same OD routed over the GTFS
  timetable. Its segments are the stretches between local stops, with an express hop's riders on
  every stretch it runs along, so the model's hops are spread the same way before comparing.
  This is a routed estimate, not a count, but the only per-link figure there is.
- **Commute mode share by county of residence**: ACS 2019-2023 B08301 (all 21 columns; Fairfield
  CT summed from its 2020 tracts since ACS 2023 uses planning regions). "Rail" = subway or
  elevated + commuter rail + light rail, over workers less those working from home.
- **Weekday totals by operator**: MTA Daily Ridership (vxuj-8kew) for October 2024 Wednesdays:
  LIRR 268k, Metro-North 236k; PATH 211,525 (Port Authority, October 2024 average weekday, via
  Progressive Railroading); NJ Transit rail has no recent published weekday figure that was
  found (FY2018 311k boardings; ~250k is a rough guess, not a target).

**What the comparison cannot do**: the model is commutes only (SPEC 4.1's other trips are T-066),
so operator totals and station entries are all trips against commutes; the AM peak (6-10) and the
county mode shares are the fair checks. There is no bus mode, so bus riders transferring to the
subway at Times Square, Port Authority or Atlantic Av are entries in the MTA data and nothing in
the model, and commuter-rail riders changing to the subway at Penn or Grand Central are model
boardings, not entries.

## Demand changes made here (SPEC 4.3, 6.1)

Two things the real network needed and the game needed anyway, both in `sim/src/demand/`:

- **Walking transfers** (`Params::xfer_walk_m`, 300 m): a rider leaving a train may walk to any
  station within 300 m straight line and board there, at the walk's perceived time plus the usual
  boarding cost. Walk edges start only at route nodes (after a ride), so walking from one station
  to another is never itself a "rail trip". SPEC 6.1 promised this; without it two lines crossing
  at different levels could not exchange riders at all in the game (a station node takes one
  route's track). Alightings are now counted at the station the rider left the train.
- **Stations within 100 m are one demand station** (`Params::station_merge_m`, `api::merge_stations`):
  two lines' platforms built side by side or on top of each other are one station for access
  (otherwise they crowd each other out of a cell's 6 nearest) and change trains without a walk.
  Two stations next to each other on one line are never merged. Invisible to the app: results are
  per route node.
- For checks only (not exported to wasm): station entries and exits (first boarding, last
  alighting) in `Loads`, rail trips by home subzone, `DemandApi::entries/exits/rail_by_cell/
  trips_by_cell/station_map/route_nodes/params_mut`.

Test: `walking_transfers_and_merged_stations` (api.rs). The baseline below includes both.

## Baseline comparison (2026-10-09, constants as they were: rail constant 1.5, other leg 30 + 2.5x, one crowding round)

`data/work/realnet/compare_base.txt`, `T-007-base.png`.

**Totals.** The model sends 4.62M of 17.2M daily commute trips by rail: **26.8%, against 20.3% in
ACS** (3.56M rail commute trips if each rail commuter goes and returns). Walking 4.2% against
6.2%. 23% of rail trips use the other access leg.

**By county of residence** (rail share of commuters, ACS -> model):

| | ACS | model | | ACS | model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kings | 48.5% | 47.5% | Bergen | 3.5% | 15.9% |
| Queens | 36.5% | 38.7% | Essex | 9.3% | 21.6% |
| Bronx | 40.6% | 42.2% | Hudson | 21.5% | 31.6% |
| New York | 51.1% | 64.1% | Union | 5.7% | 17.7% |
| Richmond | 3.7% | 4.3% | Passaic | 1.2% | 12.5% |
| Nassau | 12.5% | 21.1% | Westchester | 16.6% | 23.8% |
| Suffolk | 5.0% | 11.0% | Fairfield CT | 6.4% | 12.3% |

The four big boroughs and Staten Island are within 2 points. Manhattan is 13 points over (ACS
has 24% of Manhattan residents walking; the model walks 4% region-wide). **Every suburban county
is 2-10x over**, by 3 to 12 points; weighted mean absolute error 6.2 points, log correlation over
the 25 counties 0.93.

**Subway, morning peak.** Entries 6-10: model 1.07M (commutes) against 1.21M (all trips). Link
loads at the busiest hour match well: the 20 busiest links carry 493k in the model against 505k
measured (log correlation over all links 0.92); the Lexington Av corridor is 30-50% high (36k an
hour against 24k at 77 St), Queens Blvd and Broadway within 15%. Station entries are worse (log
correlation 0.66, 24% of AM entries at the wrong station): the Manhattan hubs get 10-20% of their
measured AM entries (Times Sq 4k against 41k, Grand Central 5.5k against 31k, Penn 2k against
22k), which are mostly bus, commuter rail and ferry riders changing to the subway (not entries in
the model, see above) and reverse commuters; outer stations are 0.3-1x.

**Time of day.** Measured subway entries by period 28 / 29 / 31 / 9 / 3%; model 32 / 15 / 35 / 11
/ 6%. Midday is half of what it should be (no non-commute trips: T-066) and night is double (8%
of trips to work start 0-6 in SPEC 4.2: T-058).

**Commuter rail is 1.5-2x over** in trips starting at its stations (commutes only against all
trips): LIRR 417k against 268k, Metro-North 319k against 236k, NJ Transit 491k against ~250k.
PATH shows 80k against 212k, partly because its Manhattan stations merge with subway complexes
(entries labelled subway), partly a real shortfall (looked at in T-034). The fullest trains are
all commuter rail: LIRR Ronkonkoma at Hicksville 544% of crush, NJ Transit NEC at Secaucus 423%.

**The track model's run times are 35% shorter than GTFS** (sum over the 109 lines' one-way
runs): the game's train (160 km/h, 1.0 m/s², 30 s dwell, no signals) is much faster than New
York's real service. Built through the track model, the same network gets 30.8% rail instead of
26.8%. That is a game-design matter (train performance is the track model's), not a demand
constant: noted for Anita.

**Crowding converges slowly**: one MSA round moves the AM peak's rail from 1.83M to 1.64M; T-022.

### What it says is off, in order of size

1. **Suburban rail is 2-10x too high.** Two structural causes, both visible in the county table:
   - The "other" mode is priced as a car parked at the destination. For a Manhattan job that is
     40 minutes of parking cost, so rail wins almost every suburban commute to Manhattan. Real
     New Jersey commuters to Manhattan mostly ride buses (ACS bus share Bergen 9.5%, Hudson 16.6%,
     Passaic 6.6%, Essex 9.6%), and the model's rail share in those counties is close to ACS rail
     plus bus. The model has no bus option without parking.
   - The other access leg (drive, bus, drop-off to a station, 30 + 2.5x) is as easy into a
     Manhattan station as at a suburban park-and-ride: no parking at the station end.
   - Smaller: one flat 28 km/h road speed everywhere (suburban driving is faster), and a flat fare
     of 6 minutes for every rail trip (commuter rail fares are $5-15; T-067 brings the player's
     fare into demand).
2. **Manhattan residents ride rail too much and walk too little** (64% against 51%; walking 24%
   in ACS). The walk curve is zone-to-zone and gives few walkers when rail is available.
3. **Midday and night shares** (no other trips; judgement period shares).
4. **Rail as a whole** is 32% over, nearly all of it from 1 and 2: the boroughs are right.

The single-line case (below) is the same problem seen from one line.

### The batch-3 single Manhattan line (757k riders, 173% of crush in the app)

Reproduced natively (`single_line.json`: 7 stations Bowling Green to 116 St, 12.1 km, 124 s a
hop, 14/8/4 trains an hour, 10 cars): **786k riders a day, fullest train 171% of crush** (the
app's 757k / 173% differ by the exact stations and run times). **73% of its riders reach it by
the other access leg**: people from Brooklyn, Queens and New Jersey driving or bussing to a
Manhattan station, at no cost for parking there. Walk-access riders alone are ~210k a day.

## The save

`T-007-real-nyc.save` (75 KB) is a T-029 game file for "Load a file": 109 lines, 1,756 station nodes,
everything constructed, day 1 07:00, $6B, the 6,245 cars the lines need. Each line has its own
double track and station nodes at one level per line (levels chosen to keep crossings between
lines off the same level; 470 crossing points still share one), so shared trunks are drawn as
overlapping tracks and the save is not what a player would build; it is a demand test, not a
showcase. Validation (not run on load) finds 411 curve problems (the GTFS shapes' tight curves
under 100 m), 180 crossings and 200 platform crossings. Through the track model's own run times
its demand differs as above (30.8% rail). Checked by loading it through the app's own track
module in Node (gunzip, the TWG1 header, `TrackApi.load`): loads in 133 ms, all 109 lines
running. Not yet opened in the browser, where 109 overlapping lines may be slow to draw.
