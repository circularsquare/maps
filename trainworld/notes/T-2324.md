# T-2324: the walking rule's cost, the rest of T-2307

T-2307 left New York's calib solve 7.8% over T-1059's (pre-T-2265, bb48b358). This task takes more
of the walking rule's cost out of the commute lanes and the everyday pass. Every change is exact:
the calib's figures are identical to master's (163d9725) bit for bit, apart from the summary's
milliseconds, so rail trips a day are unchanged (commutes 3,993,752, everyday 7,829,049).

## What changed

- **Commute lanes** (`kernel::mode_choice_part`):
  - The row test runs eight access entries at a time (`till` rows in whole eights), and a row longer
    than eight (only rows with rides) is passed whole where its most passes (`till_mx`; f32
    addition keeps order, so the most passing means all pass).
  - A lane with refused pairs is a sliver for certain, with no weight read, where its egress
    entries that have any refused pair carry at most `WALK_SLIVER` of its weight (`bound`: each such
    entry's whole row sum times its weight, already in registers; a 0.9999 margin for rounding).
    It catches 1.89M of the 8.22M sliver lanes a calib.
  - The refused pairs' weights come from `till_er`, a row per egress station and band of `er` from
    the origin's access entries, filled as `till` is; before, each refused pair read the s x s table.
  - The refused pairs are visited by their bits (access entry, then egress entry, the old order), and
    nothing is pushed onto `lane_bad` until the lane is known not to be a sliver.
  - The walk's detour factor is found once per origin zone and destination zone (`wf_z`).
- **Everyday pass**: the refused pairs' bits first, with no branch per station pair, then the kept
  and refused sums only where a pair-direction has a refused pair, each in the pairs' order with a
  0 added for the pairs the other takes (so the same bits in the same sums).

The WASM interface is unchanged.

## Where it went (counters, one calib)

- Commute lanes: 18.8M tested, 13.7M with a refused pair, 8.2M of them slivers, 4.5M mostly
  refused, 1.0M partly; about 4.3 egress entries a lane, rows of about 7 access entries.
- Everyday: 8.4M pair-directions checked, 190M station pairs tested, 7.0M with a refused pair,
  2.2M of them slivers.
- Probe timings (rdtsc, the probe build only): the everyday check fell about a fifth (7.2 to 5.8
  G ticks), the commutes' rule block about a quarter.

## Tried and dropped

- **A home row cache in the everyday pass** (the idea in the task line): per station the home's
  row out of it and into it, refilled when the pairs reach another home. Consecutive checked pairs
  share their home only about 2.9 at a time (the activity end about 1.2), and refused pairs are
  common (7.0M of 8.4M), so the fills cost more than the table reads they saved: the check took
  9.7 G ticks against 7.2, and 8.2 with only the used part filled. The table is 1.5 MB (876
  stations), mostly in cache already.
- Filling the commute rows' walk and ride parts apart: no gain.
