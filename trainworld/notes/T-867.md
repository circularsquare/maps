# T-867: upkeep for track and stations, trains a little cheaper to run

Proposal, 2026-10-21. Anita: "think about how to add track/station maintenance and lower
train maintenance a bit to compensate". Built 2026-10-21 as proposed (one station figure, $4 a
train-km); the rule as built is in docs/track-and-service.md 6.4, see "Built" at the end.

## Today

Running costs are trains only: US$9 a train-km plus US$2 a car-km (high-speed trains 12 + 3), times
the economy's 100 (`RUN_COST_*` in `sim/src/track/params.rs`, `running_cost` in `track/wasm.rs`).
The cash drains by each hour's trains (`moneyState.runningPerHour` per demand level). Track and
stations cost money once, to construct (US$90M a km of double track times the level's multiplier,
high speed 1.5x; stations US$10-140M by level), and nothing after. The params comment already says
the US$9 a train-km "carries the crew, stations and track upkeep". That is why it is three times a
driver's real cost (research/running-costs.md, option B). So upkeep is already paid, just through
frequency. A built line that runs nothing costs nothing, and a long thin line costs little.

research/running-costs.md wrote down this change as option C and left it for later. This note
makes it concrete.

## The rule

Constructed pieces cost upkeep every game day, whether trains run or not. It drains evenly
through the day, as running costs do. Blueprint costs nothing.

| What | Upkeep a game day | A real year (game / 100 x 365) |
|---|---|---|
| Double track, per km | US$30k | US$110k |
| Quad track, per km | US$60k (two pairs) | |
| High-speed track, per km | US$45k (1.5x, as its price) | |
| Station | US$50k | US$183k |
| Junctions, flyovers, crossovers | nothing extra | |

Trains: **US$4 a train-km** instead of 9, car-km unchanged at US$2. High-speed trains go to
**US$5 + 3** (12 x 4/9, rounded). A 9-car train goes from US$27 to US$22 a train-km, and a 4-car
one from US$17 to US$12.

I picked these numbers so that the real save's whole running cost stays about the same (below),
with track and stations in the same ratio research/running-costs.md found (0.6 to 1 per route-km
and per station). They come out about a quarter of real upkeep, which matches the game's
running costs sitting under real figures on purpose.

**Same rate at every level.** Tunnels, viaducts and track over water already pay for what they
are when built (the level multiplier, the water 2x), and their real upkeep per km is not clearly
different. One rate keeps the rule easy to predict: "$30k a km and $50k a station, a day". Option
if Anita wants it: underground stations US$75k and the rest US$40k (escalators, ventilation).
That changes the real save's total by under 1%, but it adds a second station number.

**Where it breaks even.** Each km of track gets US$5 x 100 = US$500 cheaper for every train that
passes. A line with a station every km pays US$80k a km in upkeep. So it breaks even at about
**160 trains a day past each point** (both ways, about 3.3 an hour each way over 24 hours), or
about 110 with a station every 2 km. Busier lines get cheaper and quieter ones dearer.

## Measured

The real save (`T-007-real-nyc.save`, 109 lines) has 4,499 km of constructed double track (2,848 km
in tunnel, 1,651 elevated, no at-grade, quad or high speed) and 1,756 stations. Its lines run
402,875 train-km and 3,935,628 car-km on a weekday. Today's running cost, from the worker's own
`runningPerHour`, is US$1,150M a game day: US$363M train-km part and US$787M car-km part.

| | Train-km | Car-km | Track | Stations | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 363 | 787 | | | **1,150** |
| Proposed | 161 | 787 | 135 | 88 | **1,171 (+1.9%)** |

(US$M a game day.)

One caveat about the real save: every line has its own double track (`bin/realsave.rs`), so 4,499
route-km is several times New York's real route-km. A player's network shares trunks. It would
have less track for the same trains, so its total would come out a little lower than today, not
higher. For a network that shares track well, US$4.5 a train-km would hold the total better. I
would start at 4 and see.

## What changes for the player

- Sprawling, lightly used track costs money every day. A branch with a few trains a day, or a
  line built ahead of its trains, now drains cash. Under 160 trains a day it is dearer than today.
- Frequent service on a short network gets 10-15% cheaper. More trains on track already paid for
  cost US$22 a km for a 9-car train, not 27.
- Close station spacing costs something (US$50k a station a day). Today an extra stop is free
  once built.
- The cash drains at night too, at the upkeep rate, even with every line on low demand.
- Removing unused constructed track saves its upkeep. Today removing it gains nothing.

## How it would show

- **Money tab**: yesterday and today get a row, "Upkeep", under running trains. Its hover gives the
  km of track and the stations behind it and the rule ("$30k a km of track and $50k a station, a
  day"). History's cost line becomes running trains plus upkeep, with the parts in the hover. With
  the place on, the region's own pieces count.
- **Line panel**: the running cost row stays its trains (at the new rate). Its profit (and the
  Lines tab's Profit column and the Cities list) also takes off a share of upkeep. Each piece's
  upkeep is split between the lines using it by their trains a day, and each station's between the
  lines stopping there. The hover names the share ("upkeep of its track and stations $1.2M").
  Track no line uses counts only in the Money tab.
- **Station panel**: "Upkeep $50k a day" in the constructed station's hover or row.
- **Track inspector**: the stretch's upkeep a day beside its value.
- **Build tab quote**: the blueprint's price, then "then $X a day upkeep". The player sees the
  daily cost before paying, which is the point of a predictable rule.

## Building it (later)

- Rust: `UPKEEP_TRACK_KM`, `UPKEEP_STATION` in params.rs; `upkeep_cost()` per hour over
  constructed edges (route-km times 1, 2 or 1.5) and stations; `money_params` gains both; the
  train rates change. The clock worker drains it with running costs, and the ledger gets an
  upkeep field (old saves' days read 0).
- Main thread: the Money tab rows and History, line and region profit shares (per-edge trains a
  day are already in the worker's capacity data), panel hovers, the quote.
- Docs: SPEC 6 and track-and-service.md 6.4, docs/ui-panels.md Money tab,
  research/running-costs.md marked as taken up. About one agent session.

## Built (2026-10-21)

- Rust: `UPKEEP_TRACK_KM` 300 and `UPKEEP_STATION` 500 (real US$, x100), `TrackApi::upkeep`,
  `line_upkeep(hours)`, `money_params` carries both and the single and high-speed factors; test
  `upkeep_of_a_ten_km_line_and_its_shares` (10 km, 11 stations: $0.85M a day; a second line as busy
  over half: shares $0.625M and $0.225M; the first stopped: $0.4M to the network).
- Clock worker: settles `upkeepPerHour` evenly with running costs (ledgers' `upkeep`), by region
  from `upkeepShares`; `unsettleAfter` gives it back for the hours since a loaded clock; each line's
  share in `ClockLine.upkeep`.
- Real save, measured in the game after the change: running trains $948M (weekday hours 8/10/6),
  track $135M, stations $88M: **$1,171M a day**, as proposed; running lines carry all $223M (every
  line has its own track). Accrual in a run: about $2.5M every 975 game s, smooth.
- Screenshots: artifacts/trainworld/upkeep/.

Measurement scripts: the per-line split was worked from the save's edges, stations and lines read in
the page; output in `artifacts/trainworld/track-tools/t867-real-save.json`.
