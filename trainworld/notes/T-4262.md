# T-4262 The renderer takes only the changed part of the drawn network (agent uploadedit, 2026-11-25)

**For Anita.** After an edit the map's renderer used to rebuild everything it draws: every texture made anew and filled
again, ten new GPU buffers per stroke set (about 30 MB on your Asia save), the traffic marks and track lanes of every line
worked out again. Now it keeps them and writes only what the edit changed. On your Asia save the renderer's part of an
edit (its work plus the GPU catching up) went from 121 to 30 ms for a station placed, 105 to 28 ms for a station removed
and 219 to 60 ms for a line deleted: about 5% of a station's wait (91 of about 1,700 ms) and 7% of a removal's (77 of
about 1,100 ms). The map looks the same: a busy junction drew pixel for pixel the same through edits elsewhere and undos.
The price, yours to know: for 20 s after each edit the GPU holds a second copy of the drawn track and lines (about 35 MB on
your Asia save), and the drawn buffers and textures keep an eighth more room than they use, so the next edit allocates
nothing.

## Why it rebuilt everything (the code, before)

- `setNetwork` (map/renderer.ts) made every network texture anew (`texture`: a new GL texture and a zero-padded copy of
  the whole array) whenever a field was not posted as the same, and a field is posted whole when any value in it changed
  (workers/clockPost.ts `SAME_FIELDS`): a station on a line changes its phases, samples, bands and stations, so all of
  those were uploaded whole (31 MB on Asia).
- The stroke sets were patched by runs (T-1028), but into ten new buffers per set each time: 30 MB allocated on the GPU per
  edit, which was most of the GPU's work after one (a 35 MB allocation measured 15-26 ms on its own, a 35 MB copy 4-9 ms).
- `writeFull` worked out every line segment's traffic mark again (`fullAttr` over 341,116 segments, 25-32 ms) and
  `putLane` packed every train sample's lanes again (417,694 samples, 10-23 ms), for any edit.
- In the frame after: the bus lanes asked GL for the stencil's bits (`getParameter`), which waits for the GPU to finish
  everything before it (pageedit's 77-83 ms stall), the culling boxes of both sets were worked out whole (6-16 ms), and
  the Speed view's colours of every track segment were remade with a closure `map` (18 ms).

## What changed

- **Kept textures** (rendererStrokes.ts `keepTexture`, `changedRows`): each network texture keeps its GL texture, with
  room to grow by an eighth, and the array last put in it; a new array writes only the rows (2,048 texels) that differ,
  past-the-end texels kept at 0, so the texture holds exactly what a whole upload would.
- **Lanes** (`putLane`): the rows where the sample lanes, band shifts or passing places changed are packed again and only
  those rows uploaded.
- **Traffic marks** (`fullValues`): with the same inputs (edges at capacity, crowded stretches) as last time, the
  marks of the runs an edit copies are the last network's; only new segments are worked out. A piece's run always starts
  a run of marks, so they are the same bit for bit.
- **Stroke buffers** (`strokes`): where no run moved and the count is the same, the new segments are written into the
  drawn buffers and the set keeps its VAO (and, when nothing in it changed, its simplified copies and culling boxes); else
  the copies go into the last network's buffers, kept for 20 s after an edit (`SPARE_MS`, DECISIONS 2026-11-25), so an
  edit allocates nothing. Buffers are made with an eighth of room.
- **Line widths** (`a_wmul`): the demand views always hand over a width per segment, all 1 in the Line view, which was
  written whole (4 MB on Asia) after every edit and again by the next network. Widths that are all plain now count as
  plain and are not written; in the Traffic view the set keeps a copy of what its buffer holds (`wmulHeld`), the runs
  an edit copies keep their widths, and `setLayers` writes only the rows (1,024 segments) that differ.
- **Fill** (the demand views' train fill): blank until `setLayers` puts this network's, but written once, at the next
  frame if `setLayers` has not come first (`freshFill`), not blanked and then filled at every edit.
- **Frame after**: the bus layer is told whether its target has a stencil (busLayer.ts, `BusStyle.stencil`: the only
  lines touched there); culling boxes of the chunks before an edit's first change are kept (strokeLod.ts `chunkBoxes`);
  `shownSpeed` is a loop.
- `?perfOff=keepGl` (or `renderer.keepGl = false` live) takes the old path, for A/B runs; `renderer.gpuBytes` counts
  bytes sent to the GPU, copied on it and allocated there.
- `?patch=check` now also reads back the lines' `a_full` and `a_wmul`, every kept texture (with float colour
  buffers), and compares the marks, lanes and culling boxes kept against whole ones.

## What is left (not this task's to file; for the orchestrator)

- **Shifted layouts.** The stroke sets are in draw order and the per-line tables (samples, phases) in line order, so a
  line that gains a segment or a stop moves everything after it: 35 MB copied on the GPU per station edit on Asia (about
  17 ms of GPU work), and the phases' shifted middle (2.5 MB) uploaded; a line deleted uploads the shifted tail of every
  per-sample texture (24 MB). Per-line slots with room on the GPU (the shader reading a slot table) would make these
  cost the line: a stage of its own, 4-6 h.
- **Whole passes per network still in the page**: the train guard's `setNetwork` (shared track over every path entry,
  boxes over every sample: 2-14 ms warm), `syncGroups` (connectors, 2-13 ms) and the transfer bubbles (3-6 ms) when the
  stations change, culling boxes after the first change, the simplified copies of a changed set (sliced between frames,
  zoomed out), and `setLayers`' whole width rewrite when a demand view is on (4 MB on Asia).
