**CPU** (machine-wide, typeperf every 30 s, so other agents included, 16 cores): France's
second and third runs averaged 3.9 cores during regions (median 3.8, 90th percentile 5.4) and
9.4 during the joint solve (median 11.1). Of `--jobs 9`, regions used about 4: reading is one
process, the gate one browser and Vite, and builds 3 at a time for 1-3 minutes a zone. Two or
three country batches could share the machine while in their regions, but not their joins: two
batches whose rows join one group would re-merge the same packs, and both write
`world_plan.json` and the registry (the runner's lock is per process). Running regions in
parallel and one join at the end would need a join-only run mode.

## Retrospective

- **The gate is still the biggest phase** (Italy 51 of 105 min, France 68 of 165), now the
  browser check: a Vite start and one browser pass per region, and France has 21 small regions.
  T-719's speedup 8 (one Vite per batch, the browser on a sample of zones) is next.
- **Shared code moved under the run twice**: T-780's worker edit broke region 3's browser check,
  and the junctions vanished again. The run cost about 1.5 hours of rework. A batch brief should
  name who may touch `app/` and `sim/`; the browser check could also fail as a run fault (not a
  park) when every row of a region fails the same way.
- **One country's join is everyone's join** once countries touch. The France join re-solved
  Spain and Italy too (29 min). Covering countries one at a time means each later country pays
  for the whole continent's solve: batch neighbouring countries into one run, or split groups
  (T-781).
- **A truncated park reason** hid the joint failures: the runner's 600-character reason is
  mostly the member list. `gatefails.py` over the re-gate folder found them; the runner could
  print the failing members first.
- **Tokens**: no timed waits; one background wait per runner process (seven runs, two of them
  short).
