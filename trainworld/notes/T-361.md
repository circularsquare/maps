# T-361: hardware budget, stage 1 (measure and propose)

Anita, 2026-10-14: "we should not rely on using all the cores of a computer for speedups. we
should be respectful of users' hardware, and try to build the game so that it runs reasonably on
not super modern devices." Target machine: 4 cores, 8 GB.

**Today's rule** (`demandClient.ts` `poolSize`): solving workers = floor(hardwareConcurrency / 2) - 1,
between 1 and 5, plus the auxiliary worker and the clock worker. 16 threads gives 5, 8 gives 3,
4 gives 1. Worker i owns periods i, i + n, ..., so 2 workers split the five periods 3 + 2 and
3 workers 2 + 2 + 1 (4 workers would be no faster than 3).

## Proposal (needs Anita's decision)

**(a) Default worker count.** Count by cores, then cap by memory:

- by cores: 12 or more logical threads: 3; 6 to 11: 2; fewer: 1 (5 stays only through
  `?demandWorkers=5`, or a setting if wanted);
- by memory (`navigator.deviceMemory`, Chrome and Edge only, reported up to 8): 4 GB or less: 1;
  absent (Firefox, Safari): at most 2.

The target machine (4 cores, 8 threads, 8 GB) gets 2; one without hyperthreading (4 threads)
gets 1; this desktop 3. That leaves at least half of the cores free on every machine.

**(b) Memory budget**: the renderer at most about 1.5 GB for the real New York save on the
target machine, so demand WASM about 1 GB including the auxiliary worker. Two workers today reach
about 1,020 MB steady plus the auxiliary, so it fits only before a long pan (the auxiliary grows
to about 480 MB, T-301). Cheapest cuts, in order:

1. **Fewer workers** (the rule above). A few lines in `poolSize`; no risk to results (the pool
   size never changes a number). Demand WASM steady, auxiliary included: 5 workers 2,260 MB,
   3 workers about 1,460, 2 workers about 1,050, 1 worker 650.
2. **Lower a worker's set-up peak** (about 400 MB fixed): drop the old network's state before
   building the new one, size the everyday pair list and its index up front, and build the strategy
   skims in a form that reuses one buffer across periods (u16 node ids where the network has under
   65,536 nodes). Likely 80-150 MB a worker. About half a day to measure natively with the
   counting allocator (`sim/src/demand/alloc.rs`) and a day to change; low to moderate risk,
   guarded by the bit-identical checks (`checks/t325-bare-check.mjs`, the native dumps of T-119).
3. **One engine per city, not two**: the everyday layer builds its own copy of the commute
   engine (pack, zones, gravity, subzones, a network and graph per period). Sharing it saves
   perhaps 20-40 MB a worker. A day or two; moderate risk (the two engines' set-ups are tangled).

Not proposed: sharing read-only pack data between workers needs `SharedArrayBuffer`, which needs
cross-origin isolation headers that GitHub Pages cannot send, and a threaded WASM build: large,
and hosting-dependent. Splitting work by zone instead of by period does not work, since crowding
couples the zones. Capping the auxiliary worker (restart it past a size) belongs to T-301.

**(c) What the player would feel** at 2 workers (this box's figures; a slower laptop maybe
1.3-2x): the loading card stays about 34 s on the real save instead of 20 s; after an edit the
first estimate lands at about 8 s instead of 4 s and the final at about 28 s instead of 14 s
(the old figures stay on show meanwhile, T-232); frames are smoother while loading. At 1 worker:
48 s card and 43 s per edit, which is too slow for this save. Cutting memory (items 2 and 3) does
not change these times; only a cheaper solve would.

### For Anita

- The worker rule in (a), or a different trade (for example 2 everywhere, or a "use more cores"
  setting that allows 5).
- The 1.5 GB budget for the New York save on an 8 GB machine.
- Whether the loading card may go at the first estimate again (13 s at 2 workers) rather than at

- Whether to do items 2 and 3 now (about 2-3 days together) or only the rule.
