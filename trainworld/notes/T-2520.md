## The measure

Two hops are **parallel on the ground** when they share no station and no line, their bearings are within 15 degrees,
each one's middle lies within 1 km of the other's line, and they overlap along it by at least half the shorter. A pair
is **kept parallel** when both hops' main drawn direction (the octilinear direction holding most of the hop's drawn
length) is the same. Weighted by the overlap in metres. **Manhattan** is the pairs whose hops both lie on the island
(a box round it, from Times Sq) with bearings within 20 degrees of 29 east of north (the avenues).

Script: `scratchpad/diagparallel/par.mjs` (session scratchpad; it reads the checks' saves and the captured game graphs).

## Where parallels break

Manhattan's avenues run 61 degrees (maths angle), near the 67.5 boundary between the 45 and 90 directions, so any noise
flips hops between the two. Traced per stage (the 4/5/6 from Grand Central to 125 St, the avenue hops' angles):

- **A** spreads the ground's 40-80 degrees to 0-140. Hop lengths are evened (`1000 m x (length / 1000 m)^0.3`), so a
  corridor with close stops is drawn longer than the one beside it with sparse stops; where cross lines tie them
  (42 St, 14 St, 53 St, 63 St) the longer one bends to fit. Nothing held unconnected neighbours' relative places.
- **B** chooses each edge's direction alone from its angle after A, so neighbours near the boundary go different ways
  (kept parallel after B: 50% in Manhattan). The upper east side 6 was turned to 130, -5 and -36 degrees by B though A
  had it at 36-70.
- **The routing** loses more: after B 80-90% of pairs share a direction once the fix is in, drawn 62-70%. Pieces
  not within 11 degrees of a direction get two legs, and parallel pieces pick their legs separately (filed T-2529).

## What changed

1. **One scale for parallel hops** (`prep`): each hop's drawn length per real metre is averaged with its parallel
   partners' (weighted by real length), three rounds, before the room for bundles.
2. **B's direction choice** (`chooseDirs`): an edge's angle is the mean of its own and its partners' (each turned to run
   its way), so side-by-side lines choose alike.
3. **A's last three rounds** (`lsq`): each side pair (T-2290's near stations sharing no edge) has its offset across its
   real bearing held at nothing, weight 1, so parallel corridors are not sheared against each other.

## Looked outward

- Nöllenburg and Wolff (2011, mixed-integer programming): soft "relative position" keeps each edge in its geographic
  sector or a neighbour; no criterion ties separate lines together. The A term here is the same idea for unconnected
  neighbours.
- Stott et al. (2011, multicriteria hill climbing): angular resolution, edge length, balanced edge length, crossings,
  line straightness, octilinearity; no parallel criterion.
- Wang and Chi (2011, focus+context, least squares like this layout): octilinearity, length, straightness, position.
- LOOM (Bast, Brosi, Storandt 2018-2020): its topology step merges tracks of different lines that run within a distance
  of each other into one shared segment, so they are drawn as one bundle. The closest precedent; here the graph keeps
  them apart, so the coupling is soft.
- Nickel and Nöllenburg (k-linear and irregular multilinear maps): directions chosen to fit the network. A rotation of
  New York onto 45 degrees was considered and not tried: the Bronx's north-south lines would then sit near a boundary.
  https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/k-linear-metro-maps/5
