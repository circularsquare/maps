# T-1897: track prices, road crossings, long water, rural upkeep (proposal)

Proposal only, 2026-11-02; nothing built. Anita decides the numbers. Build tasks T-1928 to T-1931
(waiting on Anita). All prices are double track, US$M, flat ground unless said.

## 1. Price per level

### Now (`LEVEL_MULT`, base US$90M a km; SPEC 6.4)

| Level | -3 | -2 bored | -1 cut and cover | 0 ground | +1 viaduct | +2 | +3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Factor | 1.2 | 1.1 | 1.0 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 1.3 | 1.8 |
| US$M a km | 108 | 99 | 90 | 45 | 90 | 117 | 162 |

Added to the factor at levels -1 to +3: low hills +0.4 (US$36M), high hills +1.0 (90), mountains
+2.5 (225); water +2 at any level (180). Single track 0.6x, high speed 1.5x.

### Real world

| What | Per km | Source (year) |
|---|---|---|
| Bored metro, cheap countries (Spain, Korea), with stations | 100-150 | the game's reference, Anita 2026-10-21 (T-805), from the Transit Costs Project |
| Underground against surface, against elevated | 4x surface, 2x elevated | City of Toronto staff report (2012) |
| Elevated against at-grade light rail; subway against it | 2-2.5x; 4-6x | Flyvbjerg, via Pedestrian Observations (2012) |
| Cut and cover against bored | usually cheaper where a wide straight street is above | Pedestrian Observations, "Cut-and-Cover is Underrated" (2021) |
| High-speed line, China (much of it on viaduct) | 17-21 | World Bank (2014, repeated 2019) |
| High-speed line, Europe | 25-39 | same World Bank comparison |
| High-speed line on flat land, Nimes-Montpellier | about EUR 22 | Construction Manager (2017) |
| One road crossing grade-separated | California average 26; Melbourne A$48-66; Bay Area 255-355 | California state auditor (2007); PTUA (2008); Bay Area press (2021) |

Ordinary double track on flat open land is well under the high-speed figures, very roughly 10-25.
So today's ground price (45) is about right for a city (knockdowns, roads) and two or three times
real for open country, while bored tunnel at only 1.1x ground's double is much closer than any
source has it.

### Proposed (base = ground, US$25M a km)

| Level | -3 | -2 bored | -1 cut and cover | 0 ground | +1 viaduct | +2 | +3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Factor | 6 | 5 | 3 | 1 | 3 | 4.5 | 6 |
| US$M a km | 150 | 125 | 75 | 25 | 75 | 112.5 | 150 |
| Against now | +39% | +26% | -17% | -44% | -17% | -4% | -7% |

- Ground pays a road-crossing charge on top (section 2), which stands for the knockdowns: so ground
  is cheap in the country and dear in a city.
- Bored tunnel is now 1.7x cut and cover (was 1.1x); +2 is 1.5x a +1 viaduct and +3 2x (was 1.3x
  and 1.8x). Up and down mirror each other: +1 and -1 3x, +3 and -3 6x. No source prices tall
  viaducts against low ones; this is a game call.
- Surcharges keep their dollars, in the new units: low hills +1.5, high hills +3.5, mountains +9,
  water +7. One change of order: in high hills a tunnel (125) is now dearer than ground (112.5),
  where it was cheaper (99 against 135). Mountains: tunnel 125 < ground 250 < viaduct 300.
- Stations unchanged.

### Scenarios (US$M)

Road crossings assumed: open country 0.5 a km, suburb 4, dense centre 10 (estimates; the build task
measures them), at US$6M each.

| Scenario | Ground | +1 viaduct | -1 cut and cover | -2 bored |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 km rural line, now | **900** | 1,800 | 1,800 | 1,980 |
| 20 km rural line, proposed | **560** | 1,500 | 1,500 | 2,500 |
| 8 km through a dense centre, now | **360** | 720 | 720 | 792 |
| 8 km through a dense centre, proposed | 680 | **600** | **600** | 1,000 |
| 8 km suburb, proposed | **392** | 600 | 600 | 1,000 |
| 2 km river crossing, 0.5 km of it water, now | bridge | **270** | **270** | 288 |
| same, proposed | bridge | **237.5** | **237.5** | 337.5 |
| 20 km metro at -2 with 18 stations, now / proposed | | | | 4,680 / 5,200 |

So in a centre the viaduct or cut and cover wins, ground is a bit dearer, and a bored tunnel is the
premium choice; in the country ground is by far the cheapest. At US$10M a crossing, ground through
a centre (1,000) ties with the bored tunnel.

### Does the economy hold

Running costs and fares are untouched, so only payback times move. A 20 km rural line (6 stations,
4-car trains, 2 an hour each way for 14 h and 1 for 4 h) costs US$2.44M a game day to run (trains
1.54, upkeep 0.90) and breaks even at about 12,500 rides a day at New York's fare (US$1 + 8 cents a
km, 12 km average ride). Its build drops from US$1.35B to US$1.01B, so payback at 20,000 rides goes
from about 910 game days to 680. A 20 km tunnelled metro line costs 11% more to build. Big existing
saves are not repriced (constructed track is paid). Nothing here breaks the economy; it moves money
from tunnels toward country lines.

## 2. A charge per road crossed at ground level

**Roads in the game today.** The packs' OSM roads (T-275) are not shipped: `pipeline/routes.py` turns
them into drive factors between zones, and the geometry stays in the pipeline. The only road
geometry in the game is the basemap's zoom 14 vector tiles that the bus tool fetches
(`app/src/map/busRoads.ts`, `roadTiles.ts`, `roadGraph.ts`, T-1465): every road class worldwide,
fetched per tile on the main thread. Track prices are worked out in Rust in the worker
(`cost.rs`), which has no roads.

**Proposed way (about one agent session):**
- On the main thread, when track is drawn or reshaped, intersect its polyline with the tiles' road
  lines (trunk to minor, as the bus tool's classes, plus motorways; no service ways, tracks or paths)
  and send the crossing chainages with the edit. Each edge keeps them (like `edge_terrain`, so a
  split carries them, and a save keeps them, so the price never depends on the tile server later).
- The price counts a crossing only where the track is under half a level from the ground (ramps
  included). A divided road counts twice, which suits its width.
- The ghost's tag uses the same count while drawing ("Roads 12" with the cost); a few hundred
  segment tests per pointer move, cheap. Construct waits for the tiles under the blueprint, as it
  waits for terrain.
- Size: **US$6M a crossing** (above a gated crossing, well below a full grade separation; it stands
  for knockdowns and road works). Options: US$10M to make ground through a centre cost as much as a
  bored tunnel; motorways and trunk roads counted as 3.
- The other way, a road-density layer per zone built in the pipeline like terrain classes, is more
  work (a world pipeline pass) and less exact; not recommended.

## 3. Long or deep water

**Water data:** a yes/no mask only. Each pack has a 25 m raster; outside the packs the world's
coarse mask (1/60 degree cells: OSM sea and Natural Earth large lakes, T-1285). No depth anywhere.
Each edge already keeps its water spans (`water_spans`, every 12.5 m), so a length rule is cheap:
check the longest stretch of water in `check_edge` (`net.rs`), joining the stretches of edges that
meet at a node in the water, and refuse with a new issue.

**Real lengths:**

| Crossing | Length | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| Hangzhou Bay high-speed rail bridge | 29.2 km | bridge, building (towers topped 2026) |
| Quanzhou Bay bridge (Fuzhou-Xiamen high speed) | 20.3 km | bridge, opened 2023 |
| Pingtan Strait rail-road bridge | 16.3 km | bridge, 2020 |
| Oresund bridge | 7.8 km | bridge (plus a 4 km immersed tunnel), 2000 |
| Fehmarnbelt | 18 km | immersed tunnel (cut and cover under water), building |
| Channel Tunnel | 50.5 km, 37.9 under sea | bored, 1994 |
| Seikan Tunnel | 53.9 km, 23.3 under sea | bored, 1988 |

Bridge lengths include land approaches, so the water part is shorter.

**Recommendation:**
- Viaduct (and the bridge ground track becomes): at most **20 km of unbroken water**. Longer is
  refused; the player tunnels. That allows every rail sea bridge in service and stops the Channel,
  the Tsugaru Strait and Helsinki-Tallinn. (30 km if she wants Hangzhou Bay allowed.)
- Cut and cover under water is the immersed tube, which is real: at most about **20 km** too
  (Fehmarnbelt). Bored tunnels: no limit; their price already makes long ones rare.
- **Depth: skip.** No depth data; the deep straits are also wide, so the length rule catches most.
  If wanted later: GEBCO's global grid (15 arc-seconds, free use) could add a "deep" bit to the
  world mask, with bridges refused over water deeper than about 60 m (the deepest bridge piers, such
  as Rio-Antirio's, stand in about 65 m). A pipeline pass plus a new world file.

**Cut and cover as a player level: keep.** It is the shallow level: shorter ramps than a bored
tunnel (200 m a level), a cheaper station (125 against 150), and in the proposed table a real price
step under bored (75 against 125). It is how most old metros were built, and under water it is the
immersed tube. Removing it would also strand -1 track in saves.

## 4. Upkeep for rural lines

**Now:** US$30k a route-km and US$50k a station a game day (US$300 and US$500 a real day x 100),
same at every level; trains US$4 a train-km plus US$2 a car-km (x100). Single track 0.6x.

**Real:** main-line maintenance and renewal in Europe is about EUR 50-80k a track-km a year
(Gradevinar 2014; a CAREC 2023 talk), so EUR 100-160k a double-track route-km, about US$300-480 a
day. Metros cost US$0.5-1.3M a route-km a year (research/running-costs.md), US$1,400-3,600 a day.
So the game's track upkeep is about real for a country line and a quarter or less of real for a
metro: country lines are charged relatively more.

**Why long distance is hard:** a 100 km line with 1 train an hour each way for 16 h (6 cars)
costs about US$8.3M a game day (trains 5.1, upkeep 3.2) and needs about 9,200 end-to-end rides a
day at New York's default fare of US$9 for 100 km. The default fare is a metro rate (8 cents a km);
real intercity fares are higher per km (a Tokyo-Osaka Shinkansen ticket is about 27 yen a km, about
18 US cents), and the player can raise the 100 km point of the curve. Real rural lines mostly lose
money too, so some of this is how it is.

**Recommendation:** a small change: **upkeep by level, ground US$15k a km a day, every other level
US$40k** (stations unchanged). It follows the real gap (surface track is cheaper to keep than tunnels
and viaducts) and halves a country line's track upkeep: the 100 km line's break-even drops from about
9,200 to 7,500 rides. On the real New York save (no ground track) upkeep goes from US$223M to about
US$268M a day, about 4% more on its US$1,171M of daily costs. It does break T-867's "one rate" simplicity; leaving it
as it is is a fair choice, since with section 1's cheaper ground track rural lines already get
cheaper to build.

### Follow-up (2026-11-02): upkeep by kind of structure

Anita approved ground US$20k and other levels US$30k a km a game day (being built), and asked
whether tunnels cost much more to keep than viaducts. Short answer: yes, roughly twice.

| Kind | Real figure | Source (year) |
|---|---|---|
| Main-line track at grade | EUR 50-80k a track-km a year (about US$110-170k a double-track km) | Gradevinar (2014); CAREC talk (2023) |
| Metro above ground (elevated and at grade) | 10-20% under the average metro | Wilbrink, University of Twente thesis (2018) |
| Metro underground | up to 80% over the average metro | same |
| Toronto, anecdotal | elevated SkyTrain about C$3.7M a km a year, TTC subway about C$7M (all operations, not only structures) | forum figures, unverified |
| Bridges | about 1.75% of replacement cost a year recommended (road bridges: bearings, joints, painting, deck) | COST 345, European bridge inventory (2004) |
| Tunnels | the energy and systems are what cost: ventilation (largest in long tunnels), lighting (largest in short), pumping (subsea or wet ground), fire and safety systems, CCTV; inspections and drills on top | PIARC Road Tunnels Manual |

No source splits cut and cover from bored tunnel. Cut and cover is shallow with short access, so
pumping, escape and ventilation are cheaper; deep bored tunnels need more of all three. The tunnel
shell itself lasts a century and costs little; viaduct steelwork, bearings and joints need regular
work, which is why a viaduct costs more to keep than ground track, but less than a tunnel.

Proposed upkeep a km a game day (x100 convention as now; stations unchanged):

| Level | Ground (0) | Viaduct (+1 to +3) | Cut and cover (-1) | Bored (-2, -3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Now approved | 20k | 30k | 30k | 30k |
| Proposed | **20k** | **25k** | **35k** | **40k** |

- Rural line (20 km at ground): no change, US$0.4M a game day of track upkeep.
- Real New York save (2,848 km in tunnel, 1,651 km elevated, all counted as bored): track upkeep
  US$135M -> about US$155M a day, about 2% of its daily costs.
