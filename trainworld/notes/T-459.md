## Footprint

Lagos State's 20 LGAs whole, and Ado-Odo/Ota, Ifo and Obafemi Owode (Ogun), where Lagos's built-up
area runs on (Ota, Ifo, Mowe, Ibafo; Africapolis 2020's Lagos agglomeration). Shagamu left out
(its own agglomeration). About 6,400 km², one polygon, box 2.69-4.37 E, 6.35-7.18 N; Badagry
reaches the Benin border. OSM's "Lagos Island" relation (164 km²) takes in much of the lagoon. The
dissolved outline had one hole, lagoon no LGA covers (3.68 E, 6.52 N); `nigeria.boundary_file`
fills holes. No other pack nearby.

## Jobs: what the proxy does with Lagos

First build (`lag-build.log`): OSM's job-place signal was 81% land use (19,427 land-place units
against 1,219 errands, 577 leisure, 2,818 other job places). Each land area sat on its
representative point, so the 23 km² Dangote refinery (OSM way 732777987) put 424,711 jobs, 6.5%
of the city, in one cell at 4.016 E, 6.456 N. Other big areas: 4.3 and 3.5 km² unnamed industrial,
Apapa port 2.8, Trade Fair Complex (retail) 2.4, Ibese cement 2.1, Tin Can 1.8. With the cap and
spread (`lag-build2.log`, land 11,921 units) the top cells are Apapa, Victoria Island and Lagos
Island at 13-19k jobs each, carried by GHSL's non-residential volume.

The markets, which by reputation each hold tens of thousands of traders, get jobs only from the
population half of the blend: GHSL has little non-residential volume there (only 3,163 of 41k
cells have any) and OSM maps few stalls. Informal work stays where people live, which is part
true and part the proxy having nothing better. No published jobs-by-area figure found to check.

On the map (`lag-everyday.png`): everyday attraction (15% + 8% of jobs) sits in 891 cells, 2.2% of
the pack, holding 6.8% of the people; the top 100 cells take 36%. It gathers in Lekki Phase 1,
Victoria Island, Ikoyi and Ikeja (malls, banks, restaurants that mappers and businesses put on
OSM) and in strings along the main roads (fuel stations, pharmacies). Alimosho (2.2M people), Ojo,
Ifo and Ado-Odo/Ota are nearly blank. So shopping and social trips run from the whole city to the
formal, mapped districts: the opposite of Lagos, where most buying is in markets and at
roadside stalls near home. The everyday layer still runs (0.300 errands and 0.200 social a
resident, by construction), but trips are long (errands 6.2 km against Mumbai's 5.4; social 9.8
against 9.0) and walk shares low (errands 11.5% against 18.6%). Below about 1 place per 1,000
residents the per-cell OSM count is not a usable attraction; T-456's fallback should lean on
population or jobs there.

The test line carries 50 times the Blue Line's riders, as expected for a game line with no
competing danfo and BRT and a tunnel through the centre at 10 trains an hour: a game line
there wins what the city's buses carry now, and the BRT corridor's 200k is the right order for
one busy corridor. Nothing here looks badly wrong except the everyday attraction.

Not worth its cost: the first routes run (the rebuild made it stale; it cost a minute, since the
ways cache made the second fast); one `probe` run that died on a non-Latin LGA name before
`PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8`.

Proposals:
1. **Print the OSM signal's make-up and its largest single place in every tier 0 build** (the
   share of land use, the biggest cell's share of jobs). The Dangote lump was visible only through a
   separate script; a one-line warning in `tier0.signals` would catch it in any city.
2. **Make the land cap the default** after measuring it on Toronto and London (T-460).
3. **A country with no census usable at district level needs no module beyond the outline**:
   `nigeria.py` is 120 lines and most of it is generic (OSM LGAs by state plus extras, fill holes,
   GHSL by unit). A shared `osm_units.py` taking (admin level, parents, extras) would serve the
   next such country in minutes.
4. **Set PYTHONIOENCODING=utf-8 in the playbook's commands** (Windows consoles choke on OSM names).
5. **Brief**: right and complete. The ask for OSM counts against other packs was cheap from the
   cached CSVs and is the most useful output.
