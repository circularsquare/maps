# T-1504: why the China network's longer trips barely ride rail (2026-10-26)

Analysis only, no game code changed. The save is `t1156-all-china.save` (39.4M rail trips a weekday;
113 metro lines and 10 lines named "HSR"), run in headless Chrome on a tree's Vite. Scripts in
`notes/T-1504/`: `probe.mjs` loads the save and then runs page commands dropped in a folder
(`cmd/*.js`; the experiments are `cmd/*_head.txt` + `cmd/exp_common.txt`), `lines.py` the lines,
`market_pairs.py` the market's trips from the rows, `show_pairs.py` and `decompose.py` the explorer's
answers. Outputs in `../artifacts/trainworld/T-1504/`. "Longer riders" is the weekday view's line
boardings of the market's inter-city trips, the figure T-1373 measured (3,337).

## The causes, biggest first

1. **Fares weigh too much on long trips (T-1431's root).** Halving China's fare weight, with nothing
   else changed, takes longer riders from 3,322 to 13,129 (x4); with passages at the termini, from
   4,944 to 61,645 (x12.5). On every served long pair the distance fare is about as many perceived
   minutes as the ride itself (Beijing - Shanghai: ride 459-530, fare 503-545). Rail's dollar weighs
   China's 7.6 minutes, the car's money 3, so the same dollar counts 2.5 times more on the train.
2. **The save's service is not high-speed.** Every "HSR" line runs on ordinary track at 160 km/h
   (`lineStats`: every stretch's limit 160, high-speed track would allow 320): Beijing - Shanghai
   is 8.0 hours one way (real 4.3-4.8), Wuhan - Changsha 117 minutes (real about 70). The 6/3/2
   trains are what 16-hour round trips need for a train every 2.7 hours at the peak and 8 hours
   in the quiet: the planned wait plus the penalty for no train within the traveller's tolerance
   comes to about 150 perceived minutes a trip. Six times the trains alone: x1.5 (3,322 to 4,852);
   on top of passages, x2.1; on top of passages and the half fare, x2.0 (to 124,027). Speed was
   not tested (it needs the track rebuilt); 320 km/h would save about 200 minutes on Beijing -
   Shanghai, about what the half fare saves there.
3. **The high-speed stations are cut off from the cities.** No HSR stop is shared with a metro
   line or linked to one: the nearest metro stop is 0.3-2.5 km away at the big cities, and line
   114 (Beijing - Shenzhen) starts 43 km outside Beijing's metro. A market zone keeps only its 8
   cheapest stations (`top8` in `intercity.rs`), and in a city with a dozen metro lines those are
   all metro stations, so the HSR station is in nobody's list and no route reaches it from the
   metro. So Shanghai serves no pair at all on line 113 (Beijing, Tianjin, Jinan, Nanjing to
   Shanghai: "no rail" in the explorer), and Guangzhou - Shenzhen is 2% served, by metro.
   Linking the 7 HSR stops that are within 1 km of a metro stop: x1.5 (to 4,944), Shanghai
   served 84% of Beijing - Shanghai's trips. Unlinkable stops stay cut off.
4. **The market's totals are low on the biggest far pairs.** Beijing - Shanghai has 10,591 trips a
   day each way by every mode (21,000 both ways). Even all of them by train is 15% of SCMP's 52M
   rail passengers a year (142,000 a day, T-1431's figure for the pair; it may be the line's).
   Guangzhou - Shenzhen's 178,000 each way looks right. This caps rail, it does not make the share
   low.
5. **The market's sampling: not a cause.** With every zone pair solved (`?perfOff=chunks`) longer
   riders are 3,322, against 3,337 sampled.

The causes multiply: passages, half fare and six times the trains together take 3,337 to 124,027
(x37), and none of them alone does more than x4.

## The three pairs

Weekday, each way (the explorer counts the same both ways). Rail's perceived minutes are the
trip-weighted logsum; "rest" is what is left after the busiest station pair's access and egress,
its route's ride and distance fare and the base fare: the planned wait and its tolerance penalty,
changes, and zones farther from the station. The car's money is estimated (1.25 x the zone
distance of road, 15 cents a km at 3 minutes a dollar, two people).

Air is 0% on all three (Beijing - Guangzhou, 1,890 km, flies 99.6%). Rail of rail + car + air is
the same as rail of all here, since every trip is counted in one of the three.

Two more readings: Beijing - Wuhan's rail costs 1,039 against the car's 1,087, so it would win,
but 0% of the pair is served, because line 114's Beijing end is 43 km out. Beijing - Tianjin
(112 km, 62,206 a day) loses on every test: the car is 137, rail 374-544, most of it the metro
legs, the walk between metro and HSR, and the waits.

## Published figures

- Beijing - Shanghai high-speed line: over 210M passengers in 2019, about 580,000 a day on the whole
  line, a record day of 798,000 (Wikipedia, from the operator). For the city pair, T-1431's SCMP
  figure of 52M by train and 8.6M by air a year (86% rail of rail + air). The market's 21,000 a
  day both ways by all modes is well under any reading of these.
- China's railways carried about 4.3 billion passengers in 2024 (about 11.8M a day), most on EMU
  trains (from memory, not checked this session). The save's 10 lines carry 3,337.
- Guangzhou - Shenzhen: no clean daily figure found. The intercity line alone runs 41 all-stop and
  13 express trains a day from September 2026 (Shenzhen government), beside the Guangshen railway
  and the high-speed line.

## What would change it most, and the cost

| Change | Effect seen or expected | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| T-1431's income groups and plane at $30 (fares weigh less for the richer fifth; awaiting Anita) | the half-fare tests: x4 alone, x12.5 with passages; Beijing - Shanghai 30-67% | T-1431's estimate: the market's pair loop about 3x; no new numbers |
| A served zone's access list keeps the cheapest station of each line it can ride to (one slot of the 8 reserved per line, or a 9th to 10th entry for a line not yet in it) | lets metro-city zones reach an unlinked HSR station by a ride, as a traveller would; on this save the biggest single gain after fares (Shanghai went from no rail to 84% served when linked) | small code change in `intercity.rs` (top8) and the trip explorer; up to 10 x 10 station pairs a zone pair instead of 8 x 8, so the pair loop ~1.5x at most |
| Rebuild the save's HSR lines on high-speed track, termini linked to metro stations, Beijing - Shenzhen starting in Beijing | the test save measures the model, not its own gaps; expected well above the x37 here | one agent session in the save |
| Car money at the country's minutes a dollar like rail's (the car's dollar is 3 now) | makes the long drive dearer: Beijing - Shanghai's car +~470 minutes at China's 7.7, rail wins clearly | small change; moves inter-city car shares worldwide, so it needs T-1431's fit rerun |
| The tail's totals on very big far pairs | Beijing - Shanghai could be 3-7x more trips; caps rail's ceiling, not its share | a calibration task against national rail totals; affects every country |

My pick, in order: T-1431's income groups (already proposed), then the access-list reserve, then a
fixed-up China test save. The car money question is for Anita: it is a real asymmetry but it
moves every country.
