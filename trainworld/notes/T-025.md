## For the demand agent (T-026): the network snapshot

- **Type**: `NetworkSnapshot` in `app/src/workers/protocol.ts` (stations with x/y in pack metres,
  lines with stop order, hop times per run and demand level, dwell, trains per hour per level).
- **When**: the clock worker builds one after every service change (a line's stops or frequency, a
  line starting or stopping running, a station-to-station time moving 5 s or more; SPEC 6.5), and
  after undo and redo, debounced 0.5 s, and once at start (empty network).
- **Where**: on the main thread, `onNetworkSnapshot(fn)` in `app/src/workers/clockClient.ts`;
  main.ts hands it to `submitNetwork`. The newest is also the `networkSnapshot` signal there.
- **What is in it**: constructed stations, and only lines that run (every edge and stop
  constructed). Ids are the track model's node and line ids: stable while the object exists,
  reused after it is deleted.
- **City packs in the app**: `app/vite.config.ts` serves `data/packs/<city>.{json,bin}` at
  `packs/` beside the page in dev and copies them into `dist/packs/` on build. The clock worker
  fetches the header and only the water arrays (one Range request, 1.1 MB).

## The clock worker (`app/src/workers/clock.worker.ts`)

- Owns one `TrackApi` (sim/src/track/wasm.rs) in the pack's frame (origin from the pack header),
  with the pack's water mask and the cash (US$M; $6B at start).
- **Edits** (`EditOp` in protocol.ts): route, removeEdge, flying, addStation, stationProps,
  removeStation, construct, constructAll, constructLine, addLine, setStops, setFrequency,
  lineLook, removeLine, undo, redo. An applied edit sends the new **state** first and then the
  **edited** answer, so the `world` signal is current when the edit's promise resolves. A refused
  edit answers with problem kinds (`TrackApi.issue_kinds`), worded for the player in
  `game/issues.ts`. A Rust panic is caught and answered as a refusal, not left hanging.
- **State**: the whole network every time (edges with their drawn polylines and heights in local
  units, nodes, stations, lines with stop times per level, trains needed, delay, build cost of
  what a planned line lacks, capacity markers per level, keyframe tables). Fine for a city; the
  per-tile updates SPEC 6.5 describes are follow-up T-063.
- **Keyframes**: phase tables and profile meta as notes/T-040.md; run 0 of each line resampled
  every 50 m into local units; a line table per line id (first sample, count, length m, step m).
- **Trips and the hourly re-base**: the renderer tells the worker the epoch it is in
  (`epoch` message, once per game hour); the worker sends that hour's trips and the next hour's,
  so the next one is already there when the hour turns. After an edit it sends both again. Until
  a new hour's trips arrive the renderer keeps drawing the last hour's on their own epoch.
- **Preview**: the drawing tool asks `preview` (one request in flight, newer ones replace a
  waiting one). The worker runs the route as a trial edit (`TrackWorld::trial`: apply, measure,
  roll back), so the preview knows everything an edit would refuse: crossings, ports, platforms,
  water. Median 0.4 ms round trip on a small network.

## Blueprint and construct (T-055, SPEC 6.4)

- `EdgeData.built` and `StationData.built` in the track model (`edge_built`, `node_built`);
  saves carry them (format `TWT2`).
- `Network::built_cost`: constructed edges and stations, a junction once three of its edge ends
  are constructed, a flat crossing once both its edges are. `total_cost - built_cost` is the
  blueprint's cost.
- `LineSvc.running` = profiles built and every path edge and stop constructed. Only running lines
  load capacity, have trips and go into the demand snapshot; a planned line still gets profiles,
  so its stop times show before it is built.
- `TrackWorld::apply_charged(op, cash)`: commit, charge the increase in built cost, roll back if
  the cash is short, and **clear the undo and redo history** (final). Used for construct, removing
  a constructed edge or station, making a constructed junction flying (the flyover is paid then),
  and a longer platform on a constructed station. Everything else is a blueprint edit: free,
  undoable. Undo therefore never rebuilds or unbuilds anything for free.
- Construct comes as: everything (toolbar and Build tab), what a line lacks (line inspector, with
  the price), one stretch of track (track inspector), one station (station inspector).

## Drawing track (T-023)

- `map/tools.ts`. Click to place PIs at the level selected; a click on a node or on track snaps
  (12 px to nodes, 10 px to track) and finishes there; a click on the last point, a double click
  or Enter finishes at that point; Backspace takes a point back; Escape drops the route, then
  leaves the tool. The first click may start on a track end, a node or track.
- **Lead-in at a node** (`lead_in` in wasm.rs): a route leaving an existing node or splitting an
  edge gets an automatic PI on the node's heading (the side nearest the player's direction; away
  from the track at a track end), at `min(R_CAP tan(Δ/2), 0.3 x distance to the next point)`.
  The arc there then uses the whole first leg, so it starts at the node: the branch leaves like a
  turnout and never runs on top of the main line (which the overlap check would refuse).
- Markers: a square per placed point, radius and km/h at every curve, length and cost by the
  cursor or the refusal reason in red. Preview drawn dashed in the level's colour, red when it
  cannot be built.
- Settings in the Build tab: double or single track, flat or flying for junctions this route
  makes, platform length for new stations, blueprint total and Construct all.

## Stations and lines (T-024, T-032)

- Station tool: click on track (splits the edge) or on a node. Named after the nearest street on
  the basemap's loaded tiles within 400 m ("West 42nd Street" -> "W 42nd St"), a name in use is
  skipped (so the next stop on Broadway takes its cross street), else a neighbourhood within
  1.2 km, else "Station N". Street names exist only in tiles at zoom 13 and closer; zoomed out,
  stations get neighbourhood names or numbers (follow-up T-065). Rename by double clicking the
  name in the station inspector.
- Line tool ("New line" in the Lines tab, "Add stops" in the line inspector): click stations in
  order; the line exists from the second stop (path = fastest route, planned over blueprint
  track is fine), each further click extends it; Enter or a click on the last stop again ends.
  Stops can be taken out (x in the stops list); a line can be deleted (undoable).
- New lines: "Line N", the first palette colour no line uses, 12/8/4 trains an hour, 30 s dwell,
  3 min turnaround, as many cars as the shortest platform takes. Colour: the palette row or any
  colour (native picker) from the swatch in the line inspector.
- T-032: station names stay DOM labels in Zen Maru Gothic. To win over basemap place names, an
  invisible symbol layer (opacity 0, `text-allow-overlap`, not `text-ignore-placement`) holds the
  same names at the same spots above every basemap layer; MapLibre places top layers first, so
  colliding basemap labels drop out. Rebuilt only when stations change.

## Picking and the inspector

- A click picks a station, else a train, else a line, else a junction, else bare track. Clicking
  the line that is already selected picks the track under it (so the track inspector is reachable
  on a line). Track inspector: length, levels, slowest curve, cost, lines, construct, remove.
  Junction inspector: flat or flying (moves and delays wait for T-031's per-resource query).
  Train inspector: towards, next stop, distance along.

## Capacity markers (part of T-031)

- `TrackApi.markers(level)` per demand level in the state; `map/capacityMarkers.ts` tags each
  resource at 75% and up with the delay per train (+m:ss), amber, orange or red; the settings'
  capacity switch shows or hides them. The line inspector shows the delay over a round trip.
  Left for T-031: the junction inspector's moves, the round trip split, naming the worst spots.

## Numbers (headless Chrome on Anita's desktop GPU, small network)

- Edit round trip (setFrequency, worker applies, rebuilds state, main thread swaps the world):
  4.7 ms median, 12.5 ms worst of 10. Preview round trip 0.4 ms median, 4.4 ms worst.
- Train positions: GPU against the CPU's float64 evaluation of the same phase tables: 0.3 mm
  median, 1.1 mm worst (144 samples, `tw.checkPrecision` with `?debug=1`).
- 12.4 km line at -1 with 7 stations: US$1.53B constructed, 30m51s round trip, 8 trains at 14 an
  hour; riders from T-026: 757k a day, train share 4.4%.
