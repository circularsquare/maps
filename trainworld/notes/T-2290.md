# T-2290: a station on the wrong side of its neighbours (Fleetwood and line 8)

Rule in docs/ui-diagram.md ("The layout", Sides); figures and reasoning here.

## What Anita saw

In her New York game the diagram drew her line 8 (West Mount Vernon along the shore to South End) going north,
then east, then back south-east, round the other line's station Fleetwood, which it put east of Northeast Rye
Neck and Port Chester. On the ground Fleetwood is 1.4 km from West Mount Vernon and well west of all of them.
(Her first reading, that line 8 tangled with the high-speed line beside it, she corrected: the near-duplicate
colour made it look so. That idea is T-2292, the colour T-2291.)

## Why

A evens hops per line: each hop's length is `1000 m x (length / 1000 m)^0.3`, whatever runs beside it. Two
lines leaving the same place, one with short hops and one with long ones, come out at different scales: in
the reproduction the line through Fleetwood has eight hops of about 1.3 km from the Bronx (each kept at about
1,100 units, 0.8 of real) while West Mount Vernon is reached by three long hops (0.4 of real), and line 8's
hops of 4 to 6 km shrink to a third. So Fleetwood is carried about twice as far out as West Mount Vernon, past
line 8's first stations, and nothing in A or B ties stations that share no edge to their real bearing: the only
term between them is the push apart, which acts along whatever way they already lie. B then makes the swap
octilinear and the routing draws line 8 round it.

The same thing happens in the real save without any line of Anita's: the 2 train's end (Wakefield-241 St,
Nereid Av, 233 St) drawn north-east of Fleetwood and Mt Vernon East when it is south-west of them, 3 Av-149 St
past Melrose, Tokyo's 新古河 on the far side of 古河. Counted as each station's 6 nearest stations in reality
that it shares no edge with, drawn more than 135 degrees off their real bearing:

| | nyc | tyo | nyc-game | tyo-game |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| after B, before | 59 | 26 | 75 | 37 |
| after B, after | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| drawn, before | 63 | 42 | 71 | 50 |
| drawn, after | 10 | 16 | 18 | 13 |
| more than 90 degrees, drawn, before | 183 | 169 | 200 | 168 |
| more than 90 degrees, drawn, after | 45 | 66 | 85 | 62 |

The swaps left in the drawing come from the routing and separation (stations placed along a straight piece by
share of its length), mostly pairs a kilometre or less apart on parallel lines (Flatbush Av-Brooklyn College
and Cortelyou Rd). Not tackled: T-2293.

## The change

Sides (layout.ts `sidePairs` and a term in `lsq`): every pair of stations sharing no edge and nearer in
reality than 1.5 times either one's mean real hop is checked each round of A and B; where the drawing puts
them less than their spacing apart along their real bearing (the wrong side, or too near on the right one), a
term pulls them to their spacing along it, weight 3. Pairs already on their side are left alone, so a layout
without swaps barely moves.

Tried and dropped:
- **Evening the scale, not the hop**: each station's scale (its hops' target over real length) averaged with its
  neighbours' within 1.5 hops, two passes, and each hop's target its real length times its ends' scale. A came
  out almost free of swaps (nyc 63 to 3), but B brought them back (59) and long hops at a dense end grew, so the
  drawing was 18% wider (867 x 814 pitches against 737 x 663). The side terms alone keep the size (744 x 690).
- The side terms in the snap too (weight 2): no change worth its cost (drawn 22 to 20).
- Weight 1: after B 5 left in New York; weight 3 left none.

## The standing checks, HEAD against after

The layout is chaotic (notes/T-2255.md): any change gives every graph another sample, so the lines that already
failed on some samples at HEAD still fail on some, on others:

| | HEAD | after |
|---|---|---|
| t2160 close pairs | nyc 1, tyo 5 | tyo 3 |
| t2161 pill overlaps | tyo 3, nyc-game 1, tyo-game 2 | tyo 2, tyo-game 4 |
| t2161 hooks | none | 34 St-Penn Station (nyc), New York Penn (nyc-game) |
| t2162 interchanges unnamed at 14 px | nyc 2, tyo 1, tyo-game 2 | nyc 2, tyo 1, nyc-game 1, tyo-game 1 |
| t2160 doubling back, octilinear, rebuild | pass | pass |

The Penn hooks are T-2255's known weak spot (a four-way hub with lines ending there). Swept for a setting that
fails nothing (reach 1.25, 1.5, 2 hops; weight 2, 3; target 0.25, 0.5, 1 of the spacing): every one fails a
few of these lines somewhere, and reach 1.5 with weight 3 at the full spacing keeps the fewest swaps.

Times, Node on this machine, HEAD against after: New York 3.97 s against 3.84 s, Tokyo 6.56 s against 6.64 s
(+1%). The page's worker after: New York 3.96 s, Tokyo 5.08 s, the line 8 game 0.06 s.

Found on the way: 28 of New York's pills and 6 of Tokyo's are round stops whose two ends differ in the last
bits; the canvas drew them as a zero-length stroke, so nothing (Melrose and West Mount Vernon in the line 8
game). draw.ts now draws any pill shorter than a millionth of its width as a circle.

## The reproduction

`checks/t2290-line8-game.mjs` builds a New York game with the track model's edit API in Node and writes
`artifacts/trainworld/t2290/line8.save` (open it with the game's Load): the line through Fleetwood and on north
to Hartsdale, a feeder into West Mount Vernon, line 8 along the shore to Fairfield, a high-speed line from the
Bronx through West Mount Vernon to South End, and the Hudson line, the five from Melrose. Before, it draws as
Anita's did (line 8 north from West Mount Vernon and round Fleetwood, which lies east of Homestead Park to
Chickahominy); after, line 8 runs east and north-east with Fleetwood west of it. `t2160-diagram-layout.mjs`
lays it out as `l8` (and the game's own graph as `l8-game`, captured by diagram-game.mjs) and fails if any of
line 8's stops after West Mount Vernon is drawn west of Fleetwood or Fleetwood is on the wrong side of line 8.
Screenshots: `artifacts/trainworld/t2290/line8-before.png` and `line8-after.png`. In both, line 2 (the feeder
into West Mount Vernon) is drawn under the high-speed line from Melrose, an overlap of the kind T-2242/T-2259/
T-2260 handle.

## Looking outward

- Misue, Eades, Lai and Sugiyama, "Layout adjustment and the mental map" (J. Visual Languages and Computing 6,
  1995): a layout keeps the reader's mental map by keeping *orthogonal ordering* (which of two nodes is left of
  and above the other) and proximity. The side terms are a soft orthogonal ordering for near pairs only, along
  their real bearing instead of the two axes. Summarised in [Bandyapadhyay et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.03847)
  and [Diehl and Görg](https://www.st.uni-trier.de/diehl/pubs/GD02_LNCS.pdf).
- Nöllenburg and Wolff, [Drawing and labeling high-quality metro maps by mixed-integer programming](https://i11www.iti.kit.edu/extra/publications/nw-dlhqm-10.pdf)
  (IEEE TVCG 17(5), 2011): relative position is a soft constraint on each *edge* (its octilinear sector kept), and
  planarity a hard one on edge pairs. Neither touches stations that share no edge, which is the gap here; their
  planarity constraints would have caught the loop, at the cost of a MIP.
- Wu, Niedermann, Takahashi, Roberts and Nöllenburg, [A survey on transit map layout](https://www.ac.tuwien.ac.at/files/pub/metromapsurvey-2020.pdf)
  (CGF 39(3), 2020): least-squares and force methods keep geography with a pull toward the real places, which
  our A and B have too but at 0.01 against already distorted hop lengths, too weak to keep sides.
