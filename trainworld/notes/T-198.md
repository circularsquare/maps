# T-198: the flatter commute distance curve

Done 2026-10-12. T-185's recommendation, applied: the work gravity's curve is
**f(d) = d^-0.8 exp(-d / 30 km)** in all nine US packs (was d^-1 exp(-d / 40 km), T-162), the
border factor kept (0.23; Washington's own fit moves from 0.49 to 0.45). Anita: "not sure about
less steep commute distance curve. i think so? but we should try to consider effects this might
have for different cities with vastly different density distribution / commute pattern."

## What changed

- `pipeline/build_city.py`: `DECAY_POW_US = 0.8`, `DECAY_KM_US = 30.0`, passed to `pack_gravity`;
  Washington's `border_factor` 0.45.
- `sim/src/demand/kernel.rs`: `Params::default()` decay 30 km and 0.8 (was 40 and 1.0). The pack
  carries its curve, but `city_gravity` uses a pack's shipped gravity only when the curve matches
  `Params`; with the old default every pack would have been re-solved at opening on the old curve.
  So the default and the pipeline constant change together (both say so).
- All nine packs re-solved in place with `regions.py` (cells, zones and every other array
  unchanged; about 30 s for all nine). Backup: `data/work/pre-T198/`.

**Across density.** The nine run from New York (21.7M people, a centre with 2M jobs) to New Haven
(0.86M) and Springfield (0.70M). What the change does is much the same everywhere, with one
difference in degree:

- Job-centre residents' trips lengthen by 0.6-1.3 km in every pack and stay 9-19% short of
  LODES; centre-to-suburb commuting rises to 0.88-0.97x LODES in every pack (from 0.73-0.87x).
  Bedroom areas' trips, 2-21% too long before, barely move.
- The overall fit improves most in the small and middle packs (New Haven, Hartford, Springfield,
  Providence, Baltimore: 5-9 points of 18-35) and least in New York, where it is about neutral:
  its length and class scores were already good, and the flatter curve sends more of its
  commuters out of their own county (own county 42.0% to 40.1%, LODES 42.8%; county pairs
  misplaced 7.5% to 8.7%), while its nearest-job rank improves (3.8% to 2.8%).
- County pairs get slightly worse in most packs (0.1-1.2 points): the flatter curve moves trips
  out of the home county; the length and rank scores more than make up for it everywhere but
  New York.
- In New Haven, Hartford, Springfield and Boston the mean trip now runs 0.1-1.1 km over LODES (the
  others are still 0.3-0.9 km under). The length was not refitted per pack (one value for all, as
  T-185 recommended).

So the curve's gain is not about density itself: in every pack, residents of job-rich places take
jobs about as far down the list of nearest jobs as anyone else, which the steep near end missed.
The one hint of a density effect is New York, the most centre-heavy, where the steeper curve fit
as well. Recorded in docs/demand-worldwide.md.
