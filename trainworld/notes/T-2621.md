changed. Asked by Anita after cutnet (notes/T-2606.md): "a long distance line doesn't really care much about the
exact local shape of ridership ... shouldnt we kinda be able to compute long distance trips more quickly?". "M"

## For Anita

**Short answer: partly.** The idea works for one half of the market's time and not the other.

- **On North America the market's time is mostly the longer trips' zone pairs, and those can be coarser.**
  1.73 million zone pairs a period, each priced station pair by station pair: 68% of a pass (M). 72% of the pairs
  are over 100 km apart. Dealing a far trip's destination zones more coarsely the farther it goes (fewer, heavier
  zone pairs, each still priced on its own zones' stations) cuts the pass **1.7-1.9 times** (M), with every line
  over 10,000 riders a day within 1.5% of its riders and every line over 100,000 within 0.35% (M). Her
  Los Angeles to Chicago line moves 0.6% of its market riders.
- **On all Japan the market's time is the route searches**, one from every station over the whole network: 60%
  of a pass (M). A coarser market does not shrink those, because every station is the far end of some longer trip
  or the end of a long commute (Kanto's commutes reach 100 km). The same coarser dealing gives Japan only about
  1.1-1.2 times (M, by counts). **Japan's 3 times has to come from making the search itself cheaper**, which is
  exact work (no figure changes), not a coarser market. 72% of what a Japan search goes through is stops served by
  one line alone (M), the obvious place to look.
- **Two coarse ideas I built and would not use**: folding stations into hubs (the far trips end at a city's main
  station and carry on from there), and solving the far place pairs between a few representative zones. Both put
  20-200% errors on lines (fake changes of train at the hub; the sample zones skew rail's share), for 1.3-2 times.

So: **3 times is not reached by coarsening on either save.** North America gets about 1.8 times on the market
(about a quarter off each round, since the market is ~60% of a round there), Japan about 1.15 times.

**What a player could see wrong with the coarse dealing**: totals by line and by city hold; what gets lumpier is
where a long trip ends inside a far city. A far city's stations get their long-distance riders in lumps (one zone's
station can show twice its longer-trip riders and the next one none), so a station's far-zone bubbles look patchier
and a few small lines (1,000-3,000 riders a day) move 2-5%, tens of riders. The sampling is fixed by the zones, so it
does not flicker between solves or edits.

## 2. The options and what was found outside

**Looked at outside.** Transit node routing (Bast, Funke, Sanders, Schultes 2007; for roads: far queries pass a
few "access nodes" near each end, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_node_routing) is the hub idea; contraction
hierarchies and Transfer Patterns (Bast et al. 2010, https://typeset.io/pdf/fast-routing-in-very-large-public-transportation-networks-1l9ia5l6iw.pdf)
precompute over a timetable; our search is Spiess and Florian's optimal strategies
(https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/transb/v23y1989i2p83-102.html), as EMME and the like assign transit. Four-step
national models put long-distance demand on coarser zones joined to stops by access connectors (UK TAG unit M3.2,
https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/664f4a03f34f9b5a56adcd6d/tag-m3-2-public-transport-assignment.pdf; a
Swedish long-distance model review on access trips, https://www.transportportal.se/transportekonomi-org/WP-2020-5.pdf):
the market's 6 km zones with their access lists already are that structure. What carried over: a hub as a trip's
end works for a cost lookup but not for an assignment, which needs the route (section 3a); contraction's simplest
form, stepping along stops with one line, fits our search exactly (4). Coarser zoning for far trips, done as
sampling (destinations sampled by their trips, each sampled pair priced on its own stations, as when alternatives
are sampled in a logit), is what worked (3c).

| | what it does | NA speed | Japan speed | lines (all riders, a day) | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 3. Detail of the prototypes (all in the scratch copy, `scratch.patch`)

**(a) Hubs.** Stations ranked by lines then trains an hour; each unassigned one becomes a hub and takes the
unassigned stations within r km and t rail minutes of it (889 hubs of 1,860 on NA at 8 km and 15 minutes). A far
longer trip's destination list becomes its stations' hubs, each priced at the logsum of its stations' egress plus the
route cost hub -> station; its trips are assigned to the hub, then from the hub to each station by that station's
own strategy. Its fault: a rider who would stay on a train through the hub is made to alight and board again there,
and one who would leave the line before the hub rides on to it; the market's boardings on NA's lines moved up to
48% (line 5), 36 of 122 lines within 2%. Ending at the hub with no onward leg (the egress priced from the hub) halves
that but drops the local legs: lines over 10,000 riders up to 25% off. Tighter groups (4 km, 8 minutes) keep 1,420
hubs and no speed.

**(b) Bounded searches.** The search stops once every station that some pair reads it from is settled and the queue
is 10-30 perceived minutes past the last (the planned first wait's pooling needs the slack). Exact: every figure the
same. On NA 1,539 of 1,540 searches were bounded and still popped 10.5M nodes, as before: long trips reach every
station from far away. Worth keeping in mind only together with something that takes the far ends off most stations.

**(c) Coarser far ends** (`solved_pairs`, scratch `CHUNK_BY_KM`, `PLACE_DEAL_KM`). Today each zone's trips with
each later place are dealt over that place's zones in chunks of about one trip a day (T-780), so a zone sends a few
hundred pairs to the far places. With the chunk at `MARKET_CHUNK x clamp(d / 50 km, 1, 16)` (d the distance to that
place's first zone) a zone's trips with each place stay exact and only how they are spread over the far place's zones
gets coarser. (c2) adds: for place pairs at least 250 km apart, the dealing runs once over both places' zones (each
origin zone's run after the last, from a start fixed by the two places), so an origin zone with fewer trips to a far
place than a chunk gets a whole chunk or none, which drops the floor of one pair per origin zone and far place. Each
pair is still priced on its own zones' stations, so rail's share is unbiased (NA rail -0.0%), and the sampling is
fixed by the zones' ids: no flicker, no change when an edit leaves the served zones as they were.

**(d) Representative zones.** Each place's served zones sampled by people and jobs; far place pairs solved between
those alone. Biased: rail +8% on NA (the sample favours zones whose stations are good), lines up to three times their
riders. It did not cut searches either (near pairs still read every station).

## 4. Recommendation and stages

1. **Coarser far ends (c2)**, one stage, Rust, small (Sonnet with this note): in `solved_pairs`, the chunk growing
   with distance and far place pairs dealt over both places' zones, as prototyped; `?perfOff=farChunks` for the old
   dealing; the market's tests on sampled pairs (`sampled_pairs_keep_each_zones_trips_with_each_place` and its
   kin) restated for place pairs. **Done when**: on the NA save the market's pass is at least 1.6 times faster
   natively (pairs about half), every line over 10,000 riders a day within 2% of its riders against today's solve and
   over 100,000 within 0.5%, rail within 0.5%; Japan within the same bounds; a player looking at Los Angeles to
   Chicago sees its riders within 1%. The lie in DECISIONS.md: "a far trip's end zone is sampled more coarsely the
   farther it goes; a far city's long-distance riders arrive at its stations in lumps".
2. **Cheaper searches, exact** (a design-and-measure stage first, Opus): Japan's 60% of the pass. Candidates, none
   built: (i) stops served by one line, with no walk or complex (54% of NA's searched nodes, 72% of Japan's, M by
   `chains.py`), stepped along their line without the queue; the queue is about a sixth of a search (T-1647), so
   1.2-1.5 times on searches is my guess, more if their settle work goes too; (ii) the far-zone table a station click
   reads filled once per zone pair and station, not per station pair (spreading is 27% of NA's pass, 15% of Japan's;
   maybe half of it, G); (iii) bounded searches (exact, measured above) once (i) or (c2) leaves stations with no far
   reader. Done when: Japan's native pass 1.5 times faster at the same figures to rounding.
3. Not recommended: hubs, representative zones, geographic cuts (T-2606).

With 1 and 2 together I'd expect NA about 2-2.5 times on the market and Japan about 1.6-1.9 times (G).
