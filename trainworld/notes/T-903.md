## 1. Which zones to merge

**State now.** 620 zone rows in Europe; 278 of them are under the 0.5M floor, because Spain, Italy,
France, Portugal, Greece and T-788's first wave (Germany, the UK, Ireland, the Benelux) were planned
before the floor. Italy is the extreme: 97 zones, median 0.34M and 2,478 km², 20 under 0.2M.

**The rule: size by people and area together.** Pure population (the floor) is the wrong test for
the retrofit: rerunning `zone_groups` on the floor alone merges France into zones of 26,000 to
34,000 km² (Auvergne whole, six départements round Toulouse), makes Sardinia one 24,000 km² zone,
and joins Messina to Caltanissetta and Enna. So a zone counts as big enough when

    people / 0.5M + area / 10,000 km2 >= 1

(a 0.25M zone stands at 5,000 km², a 0.1M one at 8,000; at 0.5M it always stands). Smaller zones
join a neighbour in their NUTS-2 region as `zone_groups` does (the smallest by this size that keeps
the two within 1.5M; another region only when none is left), and only ever a zone they touch
(T-897's contiguity rule). I tried 6,000 km² as the area term too: it leaves Sardinia in four zones
and Cagliari (0.42M in 1,248 km²) alone; 10,000 gives the groups below.

Simulated on the shipped outlines and the plan's people (`sim2.py IT,FR,GR,PT --aref 10000`); the
planner on NUTS units will give the same groups within a unit or two.

## 2. What a merge costs

**Not a re-registration.** A pack is one zone's own build: its 2 km grid and coarse blocks, places,
road routes, stations, no-car and everyday rates, one outline and one origin; the joint commute
solve then runs over every zone of the country's group. No tool joins two packs, and two packs
shown as one zone would still be two zones in demand. Each merged zone is a fresh row through
`world_run.py` (build, routes, gate, join), the same unattended path as a T-788 wave, with
`--coarse 1000` as every region run now.

**Machine time** (T-779's Italy: 97 rows in about 2 hours clean at `--jobs 9`, about 4 cores used,
so about 5 core-minutes a row with its share of the gate; merged rows are 2-3 units, say 8):

## 3. Bergen

**Why it is missing.** Bergen's metro (about 0.4M) is under the plan's 1M metro threshold, so it can
only come in as a zone, and its unit, **Vestland (0.564M in 33,400 km², 16.9 a km²), is dead space**
under the 20 a km² rule (`ZONE_MIN_DENSITY`): the fjord county's empty land averages its people
down. Not a parked row and not a fault in the plan: the rule doing what it says. The same rule drops
whole units that hold real cities: Trøndelag (Trondheim), Pohjois-Pohjanmaa (Oulu), Highlands and
Islands (Inverness), and smaller ones (T-772's list).

**Fix.** A dead unit that holds at least 80% of the floor (0.4M) is a zone anyway, alone. In Europe
that is exactly four: Vestland 0.564M (Bergen), Highlands and Islands 0.474M (Inverness), Trøndelag
0.453M (Trondheim), Pohjois-Pohjanmaa 0.419M (Oulu). One condition in `plan_zones`' density test,
then `zones --countries NOR,GBR,FIN` (every built row there is kept, so only these four are new).
The rest (91 dead units, 9.0M people, all under 0.4M: Cáceres, Agder, Tartu's Lõuna-Eesti, Sweden's
north, Latvia's regions) stays T-772's question. Since T-852's coarse blocks, empty land is cheap,
and China dropped the density rule (T-897), so dropping it for Europe too is a fair later option.
