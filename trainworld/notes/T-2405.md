# T-2405: no line of the network diagram drawn under another (agent diagpar, 2026-11-04)

## Why the local was hidden

Not a stroke-order bug, and not the high-speed line's hollow stroke as such. Two causes:

1. **Two pieces drawn on one place.** An express on tracks of its own more than 35 m from the local's
   platforms does not pass the local's stations in the graph (graph.ts' beside rule), so it gets a hop of
   its own, A to B, while the local runs A, s1, s2, ..., B. The layout draws both as straight pieces from A
   the same way. Its rule for that case (turn one piece's first leg 45 degrees) gives up when every turn
   would fold a line back at A, and then the two pieces lie exactly on top of each other, each centred on
   the same line with its own single stroke. Whichever is painted later covers the other; the high-speed
   line's background stripe made it look like the local's stations sat on it. Seen in New York at Jamaica to
   Rosedale (LIRR express over the local), University Heights to Riverdale, Cypress Hills, and Grand
   Central's two bundles going north-east; in the reproduction, the purple local under the teal line from
   Melrose to Williams Bridge.
2. **One stroke per colour.** Lines of one colour shared one stroke on a piece, so an express of the
   local's colour on its own tracks (the 2 and 3 beside the 1, the Hudson express beside its local) was
   drawn exactly over it.

## What changed

- graph.ts: per edge and line, `stroke`: lines of one colour, both high-speed or both not, share a stroke
  where at least half the shorter one's track on that hop is track both run on; every other line has its
  own. Layout and drawing count and order strokes, not colours.
- draw.ts `besideShifts`: pieces leaving a node the same way are drawn as one bundle (the widest stays,
  the others beside it on their real side); then `apart` moves any piece whose legs still lie along
  another's, nearer than the two bundles' half widths, aside until they sit side by side.
- layout.ts `order`: ties in wins resolved by swap passes instead of a sort with a comparator that is no
  order where partings go round in a circle (Node and Chrome sorted two New York pieces differently).

Tried and dropped: making the express's chain pass the local's stations when another line runs between
its hop's ends (a graph-level merge). It worked for the express but widened bundles enough to push
Queens Boulevard into the LIRR bundle (34 hidden stretches in New York, against 10 before), and the
drawing-time shift does the same job for the express without changing the layout.
