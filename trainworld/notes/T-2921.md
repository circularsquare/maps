## 1. The design in one paragraph

A tram is **a third kind of track on the existing track system**, ground level by default, with its own cheap
short stops and its own cars. It draws with the track tool, follows streets by default, and shares junctions,
schedules, line panels, the boarding account and the demand search with trains, because a tram line *is* a line
on edges. Tram track and train track never join: they only cross, flat, and that crossing holds both as any flat
crossing does. So the tram network is a separate graph inside the same model, and no pathfinding changes. It is
cheap (about 1/9 of a metro all in), slow (about 22 km/h with stops), holds 240 people on a normal 40 m tram, pays
no road crossings on streets, and has no bus boarding cost.

Everything that differs between track kinds goes in one table, `TRACK_KINDS`, replacing the `hsr` flag. Maglev is
one more row.

## 2. What is there today (the parts this leans on)

- **Trains**: PI-and-arc track (minimum radius 50 m), levels -3 to +3, double track, nodes joined by heading, flat
  crossings found from geometry and charged in capacity (90 s, `CONFLICT_S`). Stations 60-400 m; cars 20 m; 160
  rated, 240 crush. Track ground price US$37.5M/km plus US$10M per road crossed.
- **High-speed track (T-817) is the template.** One edge bool (`EdgeData::hsr`, 138 uses in sim, 91 in app), a
  per-line choice (`LineData::fast`), its own fleet, its own running costs, a look in the shader. A tram does the
  same things plus a separate graph.
- **Buses** (6.7): no track; route over basemap roads (`busRoads.ts`, `roadGraph.ts`); US$1M/km; T-2904 speeds
  (0.9 of car speed, 30 km/h floor, 28 s a stop); one bus 53 rated, 80 crush since T-2919; **6 perceived minutes
  to board** (T-1576); no track capacity at all.
- **Ferries** (6.8, 6.9): ports are stations; a boat is 2 cars to demand. The lesson: a mode that looks like "a
  line whose capacity is N cars" costs demand almost nothing.
- **Street data**: the same z14 tile feed both the bus tool and road-crossing price use (`roadTiles.ts`,
  `roads.rs`). `roads.rs` keeps each edge's crossings, not saved.
- **Real-line snap** (`realSnap.ts`, `trace.rs`): polyline to fitted PIs, previewed by the clock worker, placed as
  one `add_route` with corners. Street snap is this pipeline with a different polyline source.
- **Ctrl** already means "free click, don't snap to track or nodes" in the track tool (`tools.ts` `noSnap`). Her
  Ctrl idea collides with it. Decision in section 6.

## 3. What a tram is, against a bus and a metro

Figures for a 10 km line through a dense city with a stop every 500 m (21 stops) and one vehicle every 5 minutes.
Game prices as proposed; worked by hand.

| | Bus | **Tram, 2 cars** | Metro at -2, 8 cars |
|---|---|---|---|
| Build | US$10M | **US$267M** (225 track, 42 stops) | about US$2,450M |
| Vehicles | 13 buses, US$8M | **12 trams, US$48M** | |
| Average speed | 20.5 km/h | **about 22 km/h** | about 35 km/h |
| Places an hour each way (rated) | 636 | **2,880** | 15,360 |
| Running cost per rated place-km | 11 cents | **3.3 cents** | 1.6 cents |
| Boarding cost in demand | +6 perceived min | **none** | none |

- **Over a bus**: no 6-minute boarding cost, a bit faster, and 4.5 times the places per vehicle at a third of the
  running cost per place. A bus line matching 2,880 an hour needs a bus every 66 s, and costs about US$8M a game
  day more to run (18 service hours, game money at 100x real). That pays back the track in about a month of play on
  a busy corridor, and never on a quiet one. So: bus to find the demand, tram when the bus is full. Real trams earn
  their place the same way.
- **Over a metro**: about a ninth of the money (real-world ratio metro to light rail about 6 in Europe and Asia,
  Steer), and stops close enough for short trips. It loses on speed and capacity, and the player sees that the
  moment the line is full.
- **Over ground rail in a city**: ground rail pays US$10M per road crossed (US$145-165M a km in a dense centre). A
  street tram pays none. Her "no road crossing cost" is what makes the tram exist at all in a city.

Average speed works out at 23.8 km/h at 500 m spacing and 21.0 at 400 m with 25 s stands and the existing
acceleration (1.0 up, 0.9 braking), before corners. Each sharp street corner (19 km/h) costs about 10 s, and about
1.2 a km after the turn penalty in section 6 brings it to about 22 km/h at 500 m. Real: Paris T2 23.2 km/h, French
lines with many stops 14.5, Sydney L1 20 and L2/L3 17, Melbourne 15.8, Swiss street running about 16. So this is
at the fast end of real, which is fine: trams with signal priority (the game assumes it) are that fast, and the
game does not model traffic.

## 4. Why a track kind

Considered and rejected:

- **A new mode, bus-like line with rails** (no track, drawn route). Cheapest to draw, but trams then have no
  junctions, no capacity, no flat crossings with trains, no depots or stops the player places. It is what Anita
  said she does not want, and buses already exist.
- **Trains allowed on tram track and the other way round** (the shared standard-gauge case, tram-trains). Anita:
  exclusive. It also keeps the graph separate, which is what saves the pathfinding work.
- **Traffic-signal delay per road crossed.** `edge_roads` knows the crossings, and 3 s each would give about 20
  km/h in centres and 24 in suburbs. Rejected for stage 1: run times would then depend on which road tiles have
  landed, and the save and the demand cache key would need them. Corners and short stop spacing get to the real
  range without it.
- **Single-track trams, lift-up low-floor platforms, tram-train through-running, turning loops as a special piece.**
  None earns its keep now. A balloon loop already works (6.2).
- **Tram stops without being stations**, corridor catchment like a bus. Rejected: trams have fixed stops, the
  station tool and panels work, and the walk search is what makes each stop matter.
- **Tram rows on existing rail track "converted"** by a bar. Blueprint only and only when nothing joins it; later,
  if asked.

Two kinds of tram track, because the tool makes the difference obvious and it is free in the table:

- **Street tram track**: drawn with the snap on. No road crossings. Cap 50 km/h.
- **Tram track**: drawn off the streets (snap off, Ctrl, or no road near). Pays road crossings as train track does,
  because crossing cost stands for the buildings cleared. Cap 70 km/h. In open country it is a real light rail
  reservation; through a dense block it costs like ground rail. This is the honest version of "building along a
  street is cheaper". A player can run a line partly on streets and partly on its own track; the two kinds join at
  plain nodes as regular and high speed do today. See the question at the end.

## 5. The record (the maglev skeleton)

`sim/src/track/params.rs` (app mirror in `game/`), replacing `hsr: bool` with `kind: TrackKind` on `EdgeData` and
`edge_kind: Vec<TrackKind>` on `Network`:

```rust
pub enum TrackKind { Regular, HighSpeed, StreetTram, Tram }  // later: Maglev

pub struct TrackSpec {
    family: u8,            // nodes join only within a family: 0 rail, 1 tram, 2 maglev
    v_cap_kmh: f64,        // 160, 320, 50, 70
    min_radius: f64,       // 50, 50, 25, 25
    cost: f64,             // price factor at or above ground: 1, 1.5, 0.6, 0.6
    cost_under: f64,       // below ground: 1, 1.5, 1, 1
    upkeep: f64,           // 1, 1.5, 0.6, 0.6
    road_crossings: bool,  // true, true, false, true
    ramps_over: bool,      // the auto ramp: true, true, false, false
    section_s: f64,        // SECTION_BASE_S: 70, 70, 40, 40
    conflict_s: f64,       // CONFLICT_S within the family: 90, 90, 30, 30
    platform_margin_s: f64,// 60, 60, 30, 30
    platform: (u16, u16),  // 60-400, 60-400, 20-80, 20-80 (20 m steps)
    station: StationPrice, // the rail formula, or the tram stop's flat price
    car: CarSpec,          // price, run_train_km, run_car_km, riders_scale (1, 1, 0.75, 0.75), fleet index
}
```

`hsr` reads become `kind.spec()` fields or a `net.edge_hsr(e)` helper; the `HSR_*` constants move into the table.
Mechanical. This is the point of her "skeleton": adding maglev is a row, a shader flag and a tool option. Anything
a maglev would need that a table cannot give (its own family's vehicle length, say) can wait for it.

**Family rule**: `check_node` refuses a node where edge ends of two families meet (`Issue::TrackFamily`). Everything
that creates nodes goes through the same `validate`, so drawing, stations, `branch_end`, moving nodes and joins
(`unjoin_near`) are covered. Lines stay within a family because no path can leave it, so there is no per-line check
and **no new pathfinding**. `Network::line_tram(l)` reads the kind of the line's first edge. Nothing about a line
is new in the save.

Refused on tram track: double up, quad, single track, high-speed trains (`fast`), crossovers, the Regular / High
speed switch in the selection bar.

## 6. Drawing

**The tool.** Track tool, third type in the track bar's second row beside Regular and High speed. A Build tab button
"Tram" (under the four tools with Bus and Ferry, key Y if free) picks the track tool and the type. Picking Tram sets
level 0 (level is remembered and put back on Regular), hides the ramp switch, hides Target max speed and the
beside switch, and turns the magnet into **Snap to streets** (on by default, its own remembered setting). Real tram
snap comes in stage 3, as a separate source.

**Ctrl.** Anita's idea stands: with Tram picked, holding Ctrl flips the street snap while held. In this tool Ctrl
does not also mean "don't snap to track": a tram click near tram track almost always wants the track, and a free
start is a rare need. Train drawing keeps Ctrl as it is. (`tools.ts` `noSnap`: `&& type !== tram`.)

**Street snap**, built from what the bus tool and real snap already do:

1. First click within 100 m of a road lands on it (`roadGraph` rule); otherwise free ground, drawn as plain Tram
   track.
2. Second click: the road search of `roadGraph.ts` **with a turn penalty, 200 m per turn over 30 degrees**. This
   is the one new thing in the search, and the most important decision here: the bus router has no turn cost, so
   it staircases across grids. Bus: nobody minds. Tram: every corner is a 19 km/h squeal. Measured on real tiles
   (section 10): sharp corners per km fall 40-70% (Melbourne 2.74 to 1.21, Manhattan 2.12 to 0.66) for 2-8% more
   length.
3. The road polyline goes to `Tracer` (`trace.rs`) with `r_min` 25, tolerance 10 m (real-snap's 25 m would leave the
   road) and `cap` the street kind's design radius (175 m for 50 km/h). Today `Tracer::new` does `cap.max(R_CAP)`;
   that floor goes, per kind.
4. Ghost, tag, cost from `preview_route` as real snap does; second click places `add_route` with `pis`, one undo
   step, keep placing and Shift chaining as today.
5. If the fit fails at 10 m, retry at 20 m, else a red ghost and "The streets are too tight here. Add a point on the
   way." A click where the road search fails uses the bus tool's strings.

**Minimum radius 25 m** for tram kinds (19 km/h at the game's a_lat). Citadis is 25 on line and 18 in depots
(Alstom). 25 is the real line minimum, and the fit numbers (section 10) show 25 fits 81-94% of street corners with
the turn penalty where 50 fits 59-77%. 18 buys little and looks wrong at 50 km/h street track scale.

**Junctions.** A flat junction is the existing one: a tram branch off tram track, built flat, a turnout that leaves
along the heading. Grid-city T-junctions where trams turn both ways need two curves drawn (a triangle), as rail does
today. A "both ways" junction tool is a stage 3 candidate if players want it.

**Stops.** Station tool on tram track. Platform stepper 20-80 m, default 40 (new-station length remembered per
kind). A tram stop is "a station" in every word and panel: one word per thing. A platform may sit over joints (the
existing join) and on curves, so stops need no straight stretch of street.

**Where it meets train track.**

- Clicking or snapping: train edges and stations are not snap targets while Tram is the type. No refused click, no
  surprise joins.
- A tram segment crossing train track at the same level makes a flat crossing (no auto ramp). Priced as a flat
  crossing is (0.1 km of track), it holds both lines in capacity at the 90 s train figure, so it is a visible
  cost on a busy line. This is the "collide" she asked for.
- A tram at +1 passes over; a tram at ground over a train at -2 does too. Refused where a flat crossing is refused
  today: on a ramp, or inside a platform.
- A tram stop within 50 m of a station's platform makes a complex; people change on foot up to 300 m anyway. No new
  rule.

**Roads and buses.** Bus routes cross trams freely and stop at tram stops within 200 m as at any station (bus::plan
as is). A bus on the tram street gains no new stops overall on auto spacing, since auto spacing already costs a stop
every 400 m.

**Levels.** All seven levels work, including the automatic bridge over water. Above ground is 0.6x rail; below
ground is full rail price (`cost_under: 1`) so a tram tunnel is never a cheap metro. Stops off the ground pay the
rail station formula scaled by length. Refusing levels costs more code than allowing them, and it stops the player
building the real thing (Stadtbahn tunnels, Boston Green Line, Istanbul T4). Asked at the end.

## 7. Demand

**A tram is a line, nothing new.** It counts as rail, its stops are stations, walks and catchment are a station's,
fares are the ordinary curve (no multiplier). No new demand constants, per her 2026-10-17 rule.

- **No boarding cost** at all. `Network::bus_line` is false (it tests crush = 80; a 1-car tram is 180). Evidence for
  a real preference over buses is broad but argued (Yap: in-vehicle time on trams perceived about 0.6x bus; Bunschoten
  in Delft and the studies it lists, mostly a preference; Stockholm finds none between bus and metro). The 6 minutes
  was tuned to keep buses in their place; a tram being free of it is the feature that makes it worth building.
- **Capacity** to demand: the snapshot gives a tram car 0.75 of a car: 120 rated, 180 crush, 33 seats. `CRUSH_PER_CAR` and
  friends are untouched.
- **The catchment problem**: `K = 6` nearest stations per cell. Stops every 400 m along one street can fill a
  cell's six slots with tram stops and hide the metro 900 m away. Fix: **at most 3 of the 6 may be tram stops**, in
  `build_access`'s scatter. Change-free for a network with no trams. Add to `docs/demand-worldwide.md`.
- **Done when** (a demand task needs one): on the T-007 save plus a 10 km tram along a busy bus corridor, the tram
  takes more riders than the bus it replaces did; a cell near both a tram street and a metro keeps the metro in its
  list; a demand solve grows by no more than the share of stops added. Stop there.

Fares: ordinary, no multiplier row, so no Fares tab change.

## 8. Vehicles and money (real figures, against the game's price list)

Cars are the game's 20 m length, so `CAR_LEN`, platform fits and train drawing are unchanged. A 20 m tram section:
120 rated, 180 crush (two thirds of crush, as every vehicle since T-2919), a 40 m two-section tram 240 rated. A
1-4 car stepper, so 20, 40, 60 or 80 m (long real trams reach about 55-60 m, from memory, unchecked). New lines
start at 2. As trains, a new tram line starts with no vehicles, 25 s dwell, 120 s turnaround.

| | Game | Real |
|---|---|---|
| Track, street | **US$22.5M/km** (0.6 x ground rail), no road crossings, terrain and water by the usual rule | France about €25-30M/km; European studies €20-23.5M/km infrastructure, €20-45M/km greenfield range; Germany £13-20M/km; Lund £22.7M/km; Brescia €37.7M/km |
| Track, own | same, plus US$10M a road crossed | |
| Stop | **US$2M flat at ground**, up to 80 m | my estimate: €0.5-3M, no good source found |
| Tram section | **US$2M** per 20 m | Tram about €2.5-3.5M for 30 m or so, so about US$2M per 20 m |
| Running | **US$4 a train-km + US$2 a car-km** (train rates; country scaling applies automatically) | under real, as all the game's are; tram cost per place-km around half a bus's in the right place (Buchanan, an advocacy source) |
| Upkeep track | **US$21k/km/day** (0.6x, real US$210) | real data barely exists; Tampere's maintenance alliance runs about €2.5M/yr for 16-24 km, about €100-150k/km/yr, which is ground rail's game figure (about US$128k/yr). So the 0.6 is Anita's "low maintenance", not the data. |
| Upkeep stop | **US$5k/day** (real US$50) | estimate |

Over water the bridge pays water's +2.5 as any bridge does, at 0.6x. Junctions pay their 0.25 km at tram price.
A flat crossing is 0.1 km at the dearer of the two tracks' prices. Fleet: fifth kind (`fleet_tram`), spares
shared among tram lines, same rules as buses and boats. Station upgrades at a tram stop: Waiting room and Passage
stay; Terminal, Passing tracks and Park and ride are not offered.

**Capacity** (per kind, section 5): section 40 s + v/0.6 (57/h at 50 km/h); platform dwell + 30 s (65/h at 25 s
dwell); a flat junction or flat crossing inside trams 30 s. A tram crossing a train holds at the 90 s train
figure. Dead ends 18/h per track as today, so a loop is better at very high frequency, which is true in real life.

## 9. Map, saves, real networks

**On the map.** Shader flag `F_TRAM` beside `F_HSR`: track at about 0.6 of a rail line's width, no centre stripe,
street kind and own kind alike (the track inspector tells them apart). Trams are car strokes in the line's colour,
2.4 m wide against the train's, sections run together with no gap. Stops drawn at true length from zoom 14, a small
dot above that, so 200 stops do not fill the map; tram stop names only from zoom 13. A tram line's bullet is the
train bullet.

**Saves** (Anita's only; no migration effort). Edge flags bits 8-10 hold the kind (0 falls back to bit 6, `hsr`), so
every old save loads unchanged; the `unknown` mask moves from bit 8 to bit 11, and an old build reading a tram save
says so. `node_platform` stays in the node record; the validation range is per kind. Fleet: optional `fleetTram`
header. Lines add nothing. Save level bumps by one.

**Real networks.**

- **In the basemap.** OpenMapTiles has tram lines at z14 as `transportation` class `transit`, subclass `tram` or
  `light_rail`. Checked on the real tile feed (openfreemap, 2026-11-01 planet): way-km in a 9-tile patch (a double
  track is usually two ways): Melbourne 144, Zurich 107, Toronto 88, Lyon 41, Istanbul 20, Houston 33 (light rail),
  Midtown Manhattan 7 (the Hudson-Bergen light rail). World coverage, no build work. So a real-tram layer and a snap to real tram lines both use tiles the game
  already fetches (stage 3). `basemap.ts` hides `^railway` layers today; a filtered layer brings the lines back.
- **Noritetsu tiles and `realgraph` do not carry them**; do not extend those for this.
- **Real world start networks**: `realnet.py` already imports trams (Paris T lines, Toronto 5xx) as ordinary track
  with `sep`. Stage 3: rebuild those lines as `Tram` kind, as a pipeline flag. They work as they are until then.

## 10. Checks done for this design

`scratchpad/trams/street_fit.py` and `street_turns.py` (real z14 tiles, 9 tiles a city, shortest street routes of
1.5-3.5 km, simplified at 6 m, game's fit rule: tangent at most half of each adjacent leg). These are design
numbers, not game code; the real tracer can drop or widen corners inside its tolerance, so they understate success
a little.

| City | Corners over 60 deg per km, no penalty / 200 m penalty | Corners fitting 25 m, with penalty | at 50 m |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zurich | 1.84 / 1.12 | 89% | 75% |
| Melbourne | 2.74 / 1.21 | 86% | 67% |
| Manhattan | 2.12 / 0.66 | 94% | 77% |
| Istanbul | 3.91 / 2.03 | 81% | 59% |
| Lyon | 2.36 / 1.29 | 85% | 73% |

Whole routes where every corner fits at 25 m are rare (4-46% by city, no penalty, `street_fit.py` over eight
cities), which is why the fit must be allowed to drop corners within its tolerance and retry wider. Samples: 120
routes a city in `street_fit.py`, 50 in `street_turns.py`. The two scripts are in the session's scratch folder and go
with it; they are short and quick to rewrite.

## 11. The hardest problem

**Turning street lines into valid track at the pointer.** OSM streets are noisy (carriageway jogs, junction stubs,
short legs, corners every 100-200 m), the game's track must be arcs tangent at every node with corners that fit
their legs, and the answer must come in a few hundred milliseconds while the pointer moves. The pieces exist (bus
search, `Tracer`, preview), but they have only been asked to do it for railways, which are smooth by nature. Three
things to get right: the turn penalty, the tracer's handling of dense short corners at 25 m, and the failure case
(a corner the fit cannot make). The agent building it should take Melbourne's grid, Zurich's old town and Istanbul's
peninsula as test cases.

## 13. Stages

Sizes are agent-days of one Opus or Sonnet agent as far as such things are knowable; the brief for each settles the
open questions above.

| Stage | What | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **1a** | `TrackKind` and `TRACK_KINDS` table replacing `hsr` in sim and app, save bits, capacity constants per kind, tests. Nothing visible changes. Sonnet. | about 1 day, 230 sites, mostly mechanical |
| **1b** | Tram track and tram lines end to end, drawn by hand: third type, family rule, 25 m curves, tram stops and their cost, cars and fleet, map look, demand 0.75 and the K rule, text. Sonnet with a tight brief. | about 2 days |

**The smallest fun first stage is 1a + 1b + 1c together, about 5 agent-days.** 1b alone is a playable tram but
freehand track pays road crossings, so without 1c it costs like ground rail in a city and shows little reason to
exist. Ship the three as one.

Decided (decisions for DECISIONS.md when built): the track-kind table replacing `hsr`; family rule; no boarding

## 15. Questions for Anita

1. **Does tram track drawn off the streets pay road crossings?** Her note says "no road crossing cost". My pick:

   above ground at 0.6x, below ground at full rail price, so a tram tunnel is never a cheap metro.
