## 1. Bands

"Up side" read the way the score runs: the score is trips a km², higher is easier, so up is toward easy. Medium takes
20% of its count from the bottom of easy and 60% from the top of hard (her reason fits: the reds are the plentiful
ones). In `pipeline/start_difficulty.py` the thirds became shares of the well-known starts: `EASY_SHARE` (1 - 0.2) / 3
= 26.7%, `HARD_SHARE` (1 - 0.6) / 3 = 13.3%, medium the 60% between.

Counts from the T-2690 run's scores (408 well-known starts; all 3,545 measured starts):

| | well-known easy / medium / hard | all starts easy / medium / hard | cuts (weighted legs a km²) |
|---|---|---|---|
| before (thirds) | 135 / 136 / 137 | 135 / 181 / 3,229 | easy from 10,304, medium from 6,372 |
| after (20% up, 60% down) | 108 / 245 / 55 | 108 / 548 / 2,889 | easy from 12,140, medium from 3,877 |
| the other reading (60% up, 20% down) | 53 / 245 / 110 | 53 / 328 / 3,164 | easy from 19,688, medium from 5,664 |

## 5. Map picking lag

### What is drawn (a map start, nothing built)

At the start view (zoom 1.8) nothing of ours but 3,234 zone outlines (31k vertices, `tw-city-boundaries`). Over Europe at
zoom 4: 85 MapLibre layers, of them 41 real-network layers (one per noritetsu region, 12k features), 691 zone outlines
(53k vertices), basemap roads 26 layers (53k vertices of motorways), 20 label layers; plus our custom layers (the dots,
the shade, the network). India at zoom 5: 90 layers, 5 real-network regions with 1.9k features, 724 outlines.

### Where the time goes

Scripts in the agent's scratchpad, copied into `../artifacts/trainworld/newgame/` with their outputs (`pickperf`,
`rendercost`, `handlers`, `zoomprof`). Headless Chrome on one or two pinned cores of a busy machine, so single figures
are noisy (the same run twice differs by up to 2x); compare the A/B pairs, run alternately.

- **Hovering is cheap**: 0.2-0.5 ms a mouse move. The dots' hit test is a scan of a few thousand discs; no
  queryRenderedFeatures. Not the lag.
- **Panning**: MapLibre's own frame, 4-6 ms CPU and 1-7 ms GPU a frame over Europe; app code does not show in the profile.
  Taking layers away one group at a time (Europe zoom 4, CPU / GPU ms a frame, median): everything 6.4 / 4.5; without
  the real network 4.3 / 0.8; without the outlines 7.3 / 8.5 (no gain); without basemap roads 8.7 / 7.6 (noise); without
  labels 5.8 / 4.8. The real network is the one big part.
- **Zooming** was the worst: each zoom step ran the overview's `render` over every zone, 29-37 ms a step on a free core
  (55 ms wrapped per listener), most of it `spaceDots` and remaking every zone's points and hover text. A wheel zoom
  fires many steps, so zooming in to pick stuttered; on a phone (4x slower) a step is 100-200 ms.

### Built (A/B with `?perfOff=overviewCache,realZoom` for the old behaviour, two alternating runs each)

1. The overview keeps its points while what they are made from is the same object (the market, the selection, the
   city views, the opened list, the loads, the day, the shown realms), and their dot spacing within a tenth of a zoom
   level; hover text and label text are written when needed (`intercityView.ts`).
2. The real network starts at zoom 6, not 4 (`realNetwork.ts`).

| | old | new |
|---|---|---|
| zoom listener (`build` + labels), ms a step | 15.3, 17.5 | 9.6, 8.6 |
| all zoom handlers, ms a step, Europe z4 / India z5 / China z5.5 | 20-24 / 23-24 / 20-21 | 18 / 16-17 / 14-15 |
| same, CPU throttled 4x | 28-70 / 27-78 / 86-102 | 20-28 / 18-49 / 25-38 |
| frame GPU ms, Europe z4 (median) | 7.2, 7.6 | 3.6, 4.9 |
| frame GPU ms, Europe z4, throttled 4x | 6.9, 10.3 | 4.3, 1.5 |
| MapLibre layers drawn, Europe z4 | 85 | 44 |

### Ideas, with the expected gain

1. **Labels on move** (`placeLabels`, 6-7 ms every move event at zoom 4): it walks every zone biggest first and projects
   each until 8 labels fit. Cull by the view's bounds first, or keep the biggest-first list per tenth of zoom as the
   spacing now is. Expected: most of the 6-7 ms a move. Small change; left for a follow-up since the label rules are
   subtle (T-493, T-1050).
2. **Spacing per zoom step** is still 2-4 ms each tenth of a level crossed. A precomputed spacing per zoom tenth (the dot
   set only depends on the zoom and the points) built once in idle time would make it free. Small.
3. **One simplified outline layer**: the zone outlines are 53k vertices over Europe at zoom 4 but measured cheap (no gain
   when hidden). Not worth it now.
4. **Fewer basemap layers while picking**: the 26 road layers and 20 label layers cost about 1 ms CPU a frame together.
   A picking style without minor roads would save that; small.
5. **The real network as one tileset** instead of one source per region (41 at once over Europe at zoom 6+ still means
   41 sources' tiles). A merged world pmtiles, or merging regions' tiles at build, would cut the draw calls further
   wherever the lines show. Bigger: a noritetsu build change.
6. **Points instead of shapes until zoomed in**: already the case: zones are one dot each below zoom 8.5; the shapes on
   screen are the outlines and the shade, both cheap in the measures.
7. **Hit testing on an index**: not needed; hover is under 0.5 ms.
8. **Pause what is not seen**: with nothing built there is no network to draw and no clock; a check that the overlay's
   custom layer skips its passes when the network is empty might save a little GPU. Unmeasured.

How others do it: MapLibre's large-data guide and Mapbox's GeoJSON performance note both say the same things: vector
tiles over big GeoJSON, simplify by zoom (`tolerance`), source `maxzoom` and layer `minzoom` so nothing too fine for the
pixels is drawn, and few sources ([maplibre large data](https://maplibre.org/maplibre-gl-js/docs/guides/large-data),
[mapbox large GeoJSON](https://docs.mapbox.com/help/troubleshooting/working-with-large-geojson-data/),
[geojson-vt](https://maplibre.org/geojson-vt/)). deck.gl picks on the GPU with a colour buffer and reuses the last hover
pick for a click ([deck.gl picking](https://deck.gl/docs/developer-guide/custom-layers/picking)); our CPU scan is fast
enough at a few thousand dots.
