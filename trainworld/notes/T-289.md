# T-289: Paris from tier 0 with INSEE's jobs and flows

2026-10-15. Paris (`par`) built by `pipeline/tier0.py` with two France upgrades from
`pipeline/france.py`: INSEE workers by commune of work (the `jobs` hook) and a curve fitted on
INSEE's 2022 commune flows. Population is the legal population by commune, spread by GHSL (the
`population` hook). Registered, `market: -1`, `utcOffset` 1. Filosofi 200 m population (T-384)
and the ministry's school files (T-385) are left for later.

Scripts: `notes/t289_curve.py` (curve fit), `notes/t289_jobs.py` (tier 0's proxy against INSEE),
`checks/city-browser.mjs par` (browser check; T-364's generalised `t288-browser.mjs`). Logs and
images in `../artifacts/trainworld/T-289-*`: `features.log`, `water-read.log`, `par-outline.png`,
`curve.log`/`.json`, `jobs.log`/`.json`, `par-build.log`, `par-check.log`, `par-water.png`,
`par-pack.png`, `par-places.log`, `par-routes.log`, `par-browser.json`/`.log`, `par-homes.png`,
`par-line.png`, `packs-before.sha256`/`packs-after.sha256`, `tokens.txt`.

## Decisions

- **Jobs from the flows' destination totals**, not the census's "emploi au lieu de travail"
  tables: one file serves both upgrades. It counts workers living in France (cross-border
  commuters into Ile-de-France are few). The flows give 5.99M; INSEE's RP 2023 dossier gives
  6.03M jobs in the region.
- **Curve d^-1.3 exp(-d/20 km)**, the best fit on the pack's own margins (below).
- **Water from the Ile-de-France extract only.** The box's corners reach into five neighbouring
  regions whose rivers are not in the mask; outside the outline that is the same as having no
  mask (dead space), so their extracts were not added.
- **Charles de Gaulle's O&D share 0.75** (judgement, as Pearson's); the others 1.

## Curve: INSEE 2022 flows, commune to commune

5.58M workers with both ends inside; zone-model mean 9.9 km, median 6.3 km; 22.9% work in their
home commune. Misplaced between communes (1,285 x 1,285, a fine unit, so every figure is high):

| Curve | Census margins | Pack margins | Mean km (pack) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **d^-1.3 exp(-d/20)** (applied) | 26.8% | 28.9% | 10.7 |
| Toronto's d^-1.2 exp(-d/20) | 26.9% | 28.9% | 11.2 |
| tier 0 rule, power 1.4, length to the census median (d^-1.4 exp(-d/26), d^-1.4 exp(-d/17.1)) | 26.8% | 29.3% | 9.7 |
| London's d^-1.85 exp(-d/30) | 29.6% | 30.6% | 8.9 |
| US d^-0.8 exp(-d/30) | 32.4% | 33.6% | 15.5 |

The surface is flat between powers 1.1 and 1.4. Tier 0's one-parameter rule would have done
almost as well; London's steep curve would not.

Not worth its cost: guessing water test points from memory (three of five missed and needed a
lookup); the encoding crash (a second build, cheap only because the build is a minute); running
the browser check in the foreground, which let the cache expire (the one 261k wake).

Proposals:
1. **Playbook step 5: take water test points from `<extract>.water.fgb` by name** (a few lines,
   now in adding-a-city.md's Paris paragraph), never from memory.
2. **Run the browser check in the background** and do the docs meanwhile; a foreground call over
   5 minutes costs a full re-cache.
3. **A country's flow table is enough for both jobs and the curve** where the census publishes
   every flow (France, likely others): look for that first; it saves a second source.
4. **Curve scripts make the signals cache** (as `t289_curve.py` does), so the fitted curve goes
   into the config before the first build and the city builds once.
5. **Brief**: the outline, sources and reference figures were right and quick; nothing to add.
