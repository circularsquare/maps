# T-170 demand that varies by day: the plan, and its first slice (T-260)

T-170 was a brainstorm (2026-10-12, orchestrator with Anita): kinds of day solved once and replayed,
superholidays, stadium bursts, signals. On 2026-10-13 Anita said "maybe lets do weekend demand
yeah" (her TODO: "a weekday/weekend selector on top or something"). This file is now the plan;
T-170 itself is closed into the tasks below.

## Principles kept from the brainstorm

- **Variety only from a small set of situations known in advance**, each worked out once after an
  edit and replayed for free. Nothing new every day (weather, noise), nothing that reacts to
  yesterday.
- **Anita: no random disruptions.** The timetable is deterministic.
- **Anita: no separate weekend schedules for now.** The same trains run every day.
- **Anita: a stadium is a point that generates bursts of demand, not consistently**; generated
  dates are fine. **Alerts only for places the player has built in.**

## T-260: what was built

### Two kinds: weekday and weekend

Saturday and Sunday are one kind. Reasons: Anita asked for a weekday/weekend selector; the
weekend's change from a weekday is mostly the purpose mix, which Saturday and Sunday share
(NHTS: work 0.33 and 0.22 of a weekday, social 1.57 and 1.59); the difference between them
(Sunday shopping 1.01 against Saturday's 1.42) is smaller than the weekend/weekday step, and a
third kind is a third selector button for a small gain. The code carries `DAY_KINDS` everywhere,
so T-262 adds a kind without new plumbing.

### The calendar

Game day 1, a new game's first, is a Monday (a new game starts at day 1, 07:00), so days 6 and 7 of
each week are the weekend. `day_kind_at` in `sim/src/track/params.rs` (the boarding account
reads it) and `app/src/game/days.ts` (the main thread, the clock worker). The top bar's clock shows
the weekday: "Sat 13". Calendar days run from midnight. No holidays yet (T-262).

### A weekend is worked out from the weekday's solve, not solved

Without crowding, whether a given trip takes the train and by which route depends on its period's
service only, not on how many people make it. The solve already keeps every period's loads,
boardings and alightings split by purpose (T-158), and each city's trips, rail and walking by
purpose. So a weekend's riders in period q are, for each purpose, the weekday's times a load factor:

    factor(purpose, q) = weekend rate x (weekend outward share + weekend return share)
                                       / (weekday outward share + weekday return share)

(`sim/src/demand/days.rs`). The demand client builds the weekend's views from the same period
results (`dayResult`, about 7 ms on the main thread for the real New York save, against 5-7 ms for
the weekday's own), and the queries weigh each period by the same factors in the workers (the
`_day` methods: bubbles, a station's catchment, a selection's far end and timing, longer trips,
transfers). The everyday purposes' bubbles and selections use the weekend shares per direction
directly, so they are exact apart from crowding.

The approximation: the weekend keeps the weekday's crowded route and mode choices. Fewer weekend
riders would crowd less, so weekend rail is a little low on lines crowded on weekdays. Each
purpose's rail share is the weekday's; the weekend's overall share moves only with the purpose
mix. Accepted: plausible beats calibrated, and it costs no solve.

### The factors (NHTS 2017, national)

A research subagent computed them from the 2017 NHTS public trip and person files
(https://nhts.ornl.gov/assets/2016/download/csv.zip): person-weighted trips per person on weekdays
(Mon-Fri) against Saturday and Sunday pooled, linked trips (segments joined at a change of mode),
outward = arriving at the activity, return = activity to home. `research/weekend/`:
`nhts_weekend.py` and `nhts_results.json` (every rate, period share and sample count),
`tsa_results.json`, `nts_results.json` (DfT NTS0504b, 2023-2025), and `tables.py`, which turns
NHTS's period ratios into the weekend shares below. The 84 MB NHTS download was deleted.

| Purpose | Weekend / weekday | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Work | 0.30 | NHTS out 0.28, return 0.33; England NTS 0504b commuting 0.28 |
| Shopping and errands | 1.22 | NHTS (buy goods, services, other errands); NTS shopping 1.26 |
| Social and leisure | 1.60 | NHTS (meals, recreation, exercise, visits, religious); NTS leisure 1.57 |
| School | 0.08 | NHTS, age under 18 (NTS education 0.03) |
| University | 0.22 | NHTS, age 18 and over |
| Airport | 1.01 | TSA checkpoint throughput 2025 by day of week (Saturday 0.92, Sunday 1.11) |
| Medical | 0.24 | NHTS out 0.20, return 0.28 |
| Longer trips (the market) | 1.58 | NHTS trips of 50 miles or more |

Weekend period shares are the model's weekday shares times NHTS's weekend/weekday ratio in each
period, renormalised, so the weekday's own judgement shares carry over (the model's commute is
70% in the morning peak where NHTS has 56%; the weekend moves it by NHTS's ratio, not to NHTS's
level). School, university, airport and medical keep their weekday timing: NHTS has a few hundred
weekend trips for each, and TSA counts days, not hours.

### Service

The same trains every day (Anita: no separate weekend schedules for now). That is also what makes
the weekend free: a different service would change the routes and modes and need its own solve
(T-261).

### What the player sees

- **Settings, "Demand shown for"** (T-266; T-260 had Weekday / Weekend buttons in the top bar):
  Today (default), Weekdays, Weekends, remembered per browser. It chooses what every demand view
  shows: the split, line widths and the track tag, station circles, bubbles, the inspectors'
  riders and purposes, a line's day in the per-train account, fares and running costs a day. On
  Today the views follow the clock across midnight into and out of a weekend. Line widths are
  scaled to the busiest stretch-hour of either day, so weekend lines are drawn thinner, not
  rescaled.
- **The clock's own day** drives the trains: the boarding account holds a weekday and a weekend
  account, and each game hour's trains and platforms read their day's. Fares are settled each
  hour at that day's income. The selector does not change this.
- A switch of day shows at once; bubbles do not catch up over a game day for it (T-156's catch-up
  is for a new forecast).

### Cost

- **Solving**: none added. Chrome, the real save, five workers: first estimate 2.96-3.00 s and final
  10.4-10.5 s, against 2.94-2.97 s and 10.4 s on the build before.
- **Opening**: none added.
- **Per result**: each period's message carries each city's trips by purpose (`kind_totals`,
  about 3 ms natively per period for New York's everyday pairs). The main thread builds the
  weekend's views from the results: about 7 ms a publish.
- **Boarding account**: twice the work, one account per kind of day: 77-86 ms in the clock worker
  after a new result on the real save against 35-41 ms, still in steps of at most 6 ms.
- **Weekend bubbles**: asked for when shown, never pushed: the everyday cell modes for a weekend
  take about 0.58 s per solving worker, in parallel, once per result (the weekday's are computed
  every round already). Weekend queries need the workers idle on the shown result, like stations:
  during a re-solve the weekend bubbles keep the last ones drawn.

## Kept from the brainstorm for the later tasks

### T-264 superholidays

Chinese New Year (chunyun), Golden Week, Obon, Chuseok; Thanksgiving is a small one. Mostly a
long-distance event: people leave the big cities for home towns and come back, and city commuting
falls; the flow is one-directional for days at a time. Extra kinds of day on that country's
calendar (going home, holiday, coming back), each scaling the long-distance market up, flattening
its distance decay and weighting the direction, and turning city commuting down. The market's
solve is small. Depends on the long-distance layer being redesigned (T-117, T-214) and on a
home-town pull (migration tables where they exist, e.g. China's census by province of household
registration; else big cities sending people out by population). Gameplay: a notice ahead; with
no special schedules, the challenge is capacity for the rush. Unlike T-260's weekend, these change
the market's direction, so they are its own solve or its own factors per zone pair.

### T-263 stadiums and arenas

A venue is a point with a capacity and home teams (Wikidata capacity and tenants, OSM outline).
Game dates generated per team: about 81 home games for baseball, 41 for basketball and hockey, 8-9
for American football, in the right months, days and times. Attendance (capacity x a fill share)
arrives over about two hours before the start and leaves within 30 minutes of the end, from the
whole metro with a longer reach than commutes. One destination, so one column of mode choice (the
`flows` query's shape, 10-20 ms), solved once per venue after an edit and added onto that day's
riders when a game is on. Event riders route against the day's ordinary crowding. Finer timing
than the five periods: its own minute profile feeding the boarding account directly (the
post-game rush). Shown: an icon on the venue, a notice ("Yankees at home tonight, 7 pm, 46k
expected") only where the player has built (a constructed station in the city is the natural
test; the same rule for holiday notices), the venue's bubble, and afterwards a result ("62% came
by train").

### T-265 signals by clumps

Trains interact only through shared track, junctions and platforms, so lines group into clumps by
what they share. Travel time between cities is a buffer: each clump can simulate ahead of the
others and exchange delays at the borders (conservative parallel simulation with lookahead);
recovery time at termini stops delays spreading. Only busy places need train-by-train
simulation. With a deterministic timetable each level's pattern repeats hourly, so simulate a
clump until it repeats, store it, and trains are again a function of the clock (SPEC 2.1's
keyframes); one stored pattern per kind of day. Demand keeps reading averages.
