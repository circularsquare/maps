# T-153: short-trip walking

Implemented 2026-10-11; supervisor owns app integration and shared-document closure.

## Diagnosis

Anita reported a Midtown destination bubble with origins about three blocks away
showing roughly 80% train, 16% walk, 5% drive, despite the trivially walkable journey.
The model was rail-first: rail share `r` came from a rail-versus-driving logit, then
walking was `(1-r) * w`. With the same-zone baseline `w=83.89%`, an 80% rail result
leaves only 16.78% walking. This closely explains the example without needing a
rendering error. Expensive Midtown parking made rail attractive even when walking
should already have handled most trips.

Work uses the gravity's 2 km zone-distance walking curve. Everyday purposes use
actual sampled cell-centre distance. The selected far-end commute bubbles inherit
zone walking and gravity weights plus subzone rail attribution; rail is blurred
through station flows, and far mode totals are reconciled to the selection. The UI
can combine work with school/errand/leisure purpose counts. These approximations can
shift an individual bubble, but the rail-first model was the main systematic issue.

## Correction

Reserve baseline walking first, then let rail and driving compete for the remaining
trips: `walk=w`, `rail=(1-w)*r`, `drive=(1-w)*(1-r)`. Keep the existing walking curve,
rail/road costs, fares, service and crowding. Walking is now independent of rail
service: the intended simple gameplay model, rather than a full three-way utility
model. Existing citywide rail totals will fall; no calibration was attempted.

- `kernel.rs`: commute mode-choice loads are reduced by the zone-pair non-walk
  fraction before station assignment. Legacy walking-loss outputs stay zero so the
  existing worker/query array layout remains compatible. Comments now describe
  walking as a fraction of all trips.
- `everyday.rs`: stored pair probabilities and station loads are unconditional rail
  shares after the walking reservation. Cell edges sharing a subzone pair may have
  different walking shares; disaggregate rail by each edge's non-walk budget divided
  by the pair's mean non-walk fraction. This prevents rail from taking walking trips
  back during cell/purpose queries and preserves aggregate loads. Walking counts are
  fixed baseline values; driving is the remaining nonnegative mass.
- `api.rs`: focused regression and stale-comment cleanup. Existing summary/cell/
  query conservation uses the zero loss fields without a protocol change.

The everyday 150 m all-mode exclusion is unchanged. Regional intercity demand is
unchanged. Commute zone resolution is also unchanged: a short trip crossing a zone
boundary can still inherit a lower walking baseline than a same-zone trip. This is
an explicit remaining precision limit, not a promise that every displayed 300 m
commute bubble now has the same share.

## Representative behavior

Holding the old conditional rail-versus-road result at 80%:

| Trip / representation | Before train / walk / drive | After train / walk / drive |
|---|---|---|
| 300 m same-zone commute | 80.00 / 16.78 / 3.22% | 12.89 / 83.89 / 3.22% |
| 300 m everyday exact cell distance | 80.00 / 19.41 / 0.59% | 2.34 / 97.07 / 0.59% |
| 400 m everyday exact cell distance | 80.00 / 19.25 / 0.75% | 2.99 / 96.27 / 0.75% |

The 80% example holds costs fixed to explain the correction; actual rail shares still
respond to service and fares among the non-walking fraction. Even maximally
attractive rail cannot exceed 16.11% of the same-zone commute fixture.

## Verification

`cargo test --manifest-path sim/Cargo.toml --features demand --lib demand:: -- --nocapture`
with three build jobs: **39 passed, 3 ignored, zero failures** (2.18 s test execution).
Includes existing fare/service/crowding, assignment, cells, flow query conservation,
school purpose and 150 m cutoff coverage.

New focused regressions:
- A 300 m same-zone commute retains 83.89% walking with extremely attractive rail,
  before/after crowding, and in its selected far-flow query.
- 300/400 m everyday edges sharing subzones retain 97.07/96.27% walking; their
  differing non-walk budgets sum back to pair rail loads. School/everyday purpose
  timing and far counts reconcile; crowding cannot change walking totals.

Supervisor integration rebuilt both production WASM targets and the Vite app. The nine-city
worker regression passed across all five periods with shared crowding and reconciled purpose
queries; the school-purpose production check also passed. No new real-network calibration was
performed because this change intentionally lowers rail share where walking was previously
displaced.
