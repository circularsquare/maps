# T-162: region-aware work gravity

Implements T-159's fix (notes/T-159.md section 4), 2026-10-12. Work trips between zones in
different first-level regions (US states) have their decay multiplied by a border factor k, and a
2 km square a state line cuts is one zone per state.

**Result: f(d) = d^-1 exp(-d / 40 km), x 0.23 between states**, one value for every US pack
(it was d^-1 exp(-d / 29 km) with no factor). Cross-state commuting comes down to LODES (New
York 18.3% of trips NY<->NJ to 7.2%, LODES 7.2%; Philadelphia PA<->NJ 17.1% to 7.1%, LODES 6.8%),
and the real New York network's county rail shares get closer to ACS (weighted error 3.4 points
to 2.1).

## The fit (`notes/t162_fit.py`)

The game's own gravity on the shipped zones (split by state), solved in numpy, against LODES 8
OD 2023 (S000, both ends in the pack's counties; DE and MD OD added for Philadelphia). For each
(decay_pow, decay_km), k is set per city by bisection so the share of commutes between states
matches LODES; the score is fit_decay.py's: share of trips misplaced by 2 km band plus share
misplaced between county pairs, summed over New York and Philadelphia. A coarse grid, as Anita
asked (pragmatic, no joint optimiser).

Full grid (pow 0.8, 0.9, 1.0, 1.2; 15-60 km) in `data/work/t162_fit.json`, `t162_fit_fine.json`.
The surface is flat near the top: the best shape (d^-0.9) beats d^-1 by 1.1 points of 23, so the
shape stays d^-1 and only the decay length moves (29 to 40 km). The factor shortens trips (it
removes long cross-state ones), and the longer decay gives the length back: NYC mean 18.5 km,
LODES 19.6 (before 18.7 with no factor); Philadelphia 16.3, LODES 16.2. **k: New York 0.235 and
Philadelphia 0.219 agree within 7%; 0.23 for all US packs.** T-159's 0.245 / 0.26 were at the old
29 km decay and counted only the PA/NJ and NY/NJ pairs.

The other multi-state packs, k fitted on their own at 40 km, and what 0.23 does
(`t162_fit.py --final 1.0 40 0.23 nyc phl wdc bos pvd`; between-state share of commutes):

Washington is the exception: DC is a city counted as a state. With 0.23, DC residents working in
Maryland or Virginia fall to 3-4% of DC's commuters (LODES 13.5% each), and Maryland to DC to
17.7% (LODES 23.4%). 0.23 is still closer to LODES than no factor on every measure there, so it
stays; a per-pack value (the pack format allows one) would be a Washington-only calibration.
Fairfield CT to New York also drops: CT residents working in NY 12.5% of CT's commuters against
LODES 21.5% (no factor: 35.9%); Fairfield to Manhattan 0.25x LODES (was 0.49x), Fairfield to
Westchester 0.49x (was 1.38x). The well-paid Metro-North commute is a labour-market effect the
crow-fly gravity never had.

## What changed in the code

- **Pack** (`sim/src/demand/pack.rs`, format 1, no bump; notes/T-004.md "Regions"): optional
  per-cell `region` (u8), per-zone `zone_region` (u8), header `regions` block, and
  `gravity.border_factor`. Absent means one region and factor 1, so older packs load and solve
  exactly as before (test `pack_without_regions_has_factor_one`). `with_gravity` also works on a
  finished pack: it drops the old gravity arrays and rewrites the .bin without their bytes.
- **Kernel** (`kernel.rs`): `Zones.region`; `build_zones` keys zones by (2 km square, region), in
  first-cell order, so a one-region pack gets the same zones as before. `Gravity.border` and
  `Gravity::k(z, i, j)`; the Furness loops, `Gravity::t`, `walk_trips_by_zone` (now a list of
  zones per square, `Zones::by_square`), the mode choice's per-destination-zone decay and the
  far-end loop of `selection_flows` (`api.rs`) multiply the decay by k across regions.
  `Params.border_factor` (default 1) only applies to a gravity solved without a pack's own
  (`pack_gravity --border-factor`, or the loader's fallback solve); a pack's gravity carries its
  own factor.
- **Zone positions**: `DemandApi::zone_xy` places zones that share a square at the mean of their
  own cells, so the far-end bubbles of a split square do not sit on top of each other. Everyday
  destinations (`everyday.rs`) treat two zones of one square as intrazonal (1.04 km).
- **Pipeline**: `pipeline/regions.py` computes each cell's region by spreading per-state block
  weights (people + jobs + resident job holders + a little land) over the block polygons exactly
  as the pack's own figures were spread, and takes the state with the most; `regions.py <city>`
  adds it to a finished pack and re-solves the gravity in place (3-60 s a pack). `build_city.py`
  does the same in its own spread for a new pack and passes `--border-factor BORDER_FACTOR_US`
  to `pack_gravity`. `read_pack.py` checks zones per (square, region), each cell against its
  zone's region, and the balance with the factor; it prints the trips between regions.
  `county_flows.py` applies the factor.
- **Tests**: `region_border_factor` (kernel: the border splits squares, factor 1 changes nothing,
  0.25 cuts cross-border trips, both margins hold, the pack round trip rebuilds the same trips),
  `gravity_roundtrip` (with regions), `pack_without_regions_has_factor_one`,
  `regravity_drops_the_old_arrays`.
- **Outside the US** no factor is chosen. The factor is per pack (`gravity.border_factor`); a
  future country's pipeline sets its own from its own commuting data, and a pack that sets none
  has 1, no effect. `BORDER_FACTOR_US` in `build_city.py` is the US value.

## Packs

All nine packs were re-solved in place (`regions.py`; cells, figures and every other array
unchanged), not rebuilt from the raw data, so the before/after differences are the gravity
alone. The packs as they were are in `data/work/pre-T162/` (43 MB, gitignored; delete when
no longer needed for a rollback or comparison). `data/packs/intercity.json` reads only the
packs' cell figures, which did not change, so it was not rebuilt.

The single-state packs change only through the longer decay. Each .bin grows by its one-byte
region array (0.04-0.2 MB). All nine pass `read_pack.py` (independent balance check with the
factor; cells in their zone's region). Time: the regions step about 2.5 minutes for all nine
(New York 63 s, mostly the block spread); re-solving all nine with new values about 35 s. The fit
grids took 7 and 6 minutes, each real-network day about 1.5 minutes.

Checks: `cargo test` (70 pass) and `--release --features demand` (114 pass, and the ignored
`nyc_day` opens the new pack from its shipped gravity in 21 ms); `npm run wasm` and `npm run
check`; `t066-worker-check.mjs` and `t149-worker-check.mjs` pass and rewrite their JSON
(`../../artifacts/trainworld/notes-outputs/T-066-worker-check.json`: 65.62M trip legs, 357.6k rail). Commute totals are unchanged by
construction (New York 17.22M legs a day in both real-network runs).

## Open (Anita's)

- **Outside the US**: no factor is chosen. A pack with none has factor 1. T-159's suggestion stands:
  national borders get a factor, admin-1 gets 1 until a country's own OD data says otherwise
  (Tokyo's prefectures exchange millions of commuters). Note that many admin-1 layers have
  city-states (Berlin, Vienna, Mexico City, Bangkok); Washington shows a factor fitted on states
  is too strong for those.
- Washington DC with 0.23 (above), and Fairfield CT to New York under LODES. Settled 2026-10-12
  (T-181): Washington has its own 0.49; the policy outside the US is in SPEC section 4 (national
  borders get a factor, weaker inside Schengen; admin-1 none by default).
- Center City -> Camden at 0.39x LODES, accepted for the larger flows (T-159).
