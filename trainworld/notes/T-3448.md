# T-3448 Player's NYC schematic broken into islands (agent schem, 2026-11-16)

## For Anita

Most likely the player's game has lines or stations far from New York (another city or continent). The screenshot
only shows New York. I got the same look by adding a 3-station line far away to the real New York save. Two things
go wrong on such a map:

1. **The squash.** On a map 600 km or more across, the sheet's cells and its ground raster scale with the whole map
   (finest lattice cell = the map's longer side / 1024; raster max(200 m, side / 6000, half a cell)). New York plus
   Los Angeles gives about 4 km cells, plus Tokyo about 10 km. The real New York network (866 complexes in about
   100 km) then sits in a few dozen cells, so the sheet can only shear it as a whole: Manhattan on a 45 degree
   diagonal, lines crowded, pills overlapping. Manhattan and the Harlem River are under one raster cell.
2. **The islands.** On such a map, where the water masks of the zones under the city are missing, the ground there is
   a guess, guessed land narrower than 40 km is sea (T-3362), and the city is drawn as sea with land only where each
   pill forces it (the pill plus 1.5 pitches, and strips carried to stations off their land). The worker fetched
   every zone's mask in the sheet's box at once: 233 zones for New York + LA, 2,755 for New York + Tokyo, and with
   2,755 over 50 fetches failed (ERR_INSUFFICIENT_RESOURCES). Masks are now fetched 32 at a time with one retry.

Not confirmed: that the player's game has a far network. Worth asking them, or checking their save if they shared it.

## What I'd do (design call)

- **A. One diagram per region** (recommended): split the network where groups of stations are more than about 500 km
  apart with nothing between, lay out each group on its own sheet, and show the group around the camera (or the groups
  side by side). Fixes the player's case. A long intercity line joining two cities still makes one big map.
  About 3-5 hours.
- **B. Sheet resolution by station density**: a lattice fine wherever stations are dense whatever the map's size, and a
  raster fine near networks and coarse elsewhere. Also helps continent games ("cities squished too much", your board
  note 11-16). A redesign of the sheet's raster: a day or more.
- **C. Stopgap**: on a map over a few thousand km, draw only the group of stations around the camera. About 1-2 hours;
  the rest of the network is not in the diagram.
