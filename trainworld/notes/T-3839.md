# T-3839 Schematic rivers still break and bridge (agent riverfix, 2026-11-20)

Anita, 11-20 on 8800: "still seeing some disjoint rivers occasionally. usually at the end of what is considered a river.
like near newark where the passaic and hackensack confluence. the hudson river also appears to have a landbridge across
it near peekskill/beacon also. sands point is also connected to the bronx ... also if the whole world is selected the
rivers still look super disconnected, so idk if we solved anything substantially here." Her two shots were both of
country maps (600 km across or more): New York in a large save with every city, and with US northeast chosen.

Save used: `artifacts/trainworld/t3362/na476.save` (her North America day 476, the biggest real save in the checks;
t3364 uses it). With every city it is one region 5,000 km across; US northeast chosen is about 1,000 km across.

## Causes (in order of how much they mattered)

1. **A country map's ground raster is far too coarse for a city drawn large.** `sheet.ts` reads the masks into a
   raster of max(200 m, E/6000, h/2): 2.68 km on the whole North America save, 463 m with US northeast. The drawing
   magnifies New York to about 50 m a pitch, so a raster cell is 50 pitches: the Hudson, East River and Harlem River
   were point samples of 2.7 km cells (scattered blobs). And on a country map the raster keeps only mask water the
   ground calls sea or a strait (600 m+), so the Hudson Highlands (Peekskill to Beacon, 400-800 m) were land.
2. **Water-gap pairs need two land masses.** On a country map Manhattan, New Jersey and the Bronx are one land mass
   (the raster joins them round the narrow water), and on a city map the masks' bridges join a river's banks (London
   had 2 pairs in all). With no pair the carry squeezes the water between stations nearly free, so even fine water came
   out a sliver.
3. **The cleaning splits water.** Seen stage by stage (scratch `lay.mjs` with grid dumps, `full.mjs GRID=<stage>`):
   - the thin-water pass (`sheetThin.ts thinPass`) clears a thin piece and draws a band that can fall short of the water
     at one end: Newark Bay cut off from where the Passaic and Hackensack bands end (city map), the East River at
     Roosevelt Island (country map);
   - a pill's land (1.5 pitches, more on a coarse grid) laid across a river the carry put under a station (the Harlem
     River's mouth under 112 St on the North America map);
   - the 3 by 3 majority turns a river one cell wide on a diagonal to land.
   T-3833's coast cleaning (`cleanRing`) was not the cause of any break looked at.

## What is in the game now

- `waterReal.ts` `maskPixel`: the masks' own pixel at a place (50 km buckets, a latitude table, the last mask asked
  first; 89 ns a call in random order). `sheet.ts`: a country map's raster carries it as `fine`.
- `sheetLines.ts sheetMap`: on a country map, a drawn cell covering less ground than a raster cell takes the pixel under
  it (water, or land with its zone); a land cell covering more than 75 m within two cells of water reads four more pixels
  a quarter cell round its middle, water if any is.
- Water-gap pairs (`sheetLines`): a pair on one land mass counts where the water on the straight way runs 100 m or more
  in one run and the sheet draws it 4 pitches wide or more (masks every 25 m on a country map, the raster on a city
  map). Pairs of two land masses as before. Counts: London 2 -> 119, Osaka 83 -> 95, all Japan 4 -> 337, US northeast
  0 -> 66, North America 0 -> 86. A threshold of 1.5 pitches gave Japan 1,175 pairs and a carry of 11 rounds: too slow.
- Cleaning: corner-only water joined by a side; the majority keeps a water cell with two runs of water round it;
  `thinPass` keeps a piece as it was where its bands leave what it joined in two (`thinKept`); `rejoinWater` at the end
  of the cleaning (`rejoined`).
- `riverRoute.ts` marks the cells under routed bands (joined) and the water it took out (never carved again).
