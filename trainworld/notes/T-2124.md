# T-2124 Achievements: a design for Anita (not built)

Anita, 2026-11-03: "for goals.... i guess we can maybe work on some achievements. i think we can do
some as like quantitative ones rather than binary ones. like one type of badge for connecting 2, 10,
25, or 100 cities (different tiers?), another for carrying various numbers of trips, maybe another for
busiest station daily ridership, maybe another for busiest line. each with a bar filling up. then some
binary ones for like connecting 2 continents, having a really long line, having a really profitable
line, maybe one for like high transfer to trip ratio lol. these should be relatively unobnoxious.
perhaps they pop up in bottom left corner, not super big, and fade after 5 seconds or os, and are
visible in cities panel or something via a button at the top."

Background: research/player-voices.md idea 2 (a ladder of goals; NIMBY's "set their own goals"),
research/first-ten-minutes.md idea 4 and S5 (the first line's payoff is small on screen). The share
goals (10-40% ticks, the star; T-685, T-1437) stay as they are: they are per city, these are per game.

## 2. The list

Twenty-four steps in all: four tiered achievements of four tiers each, and eight firsts.

### Tiered (a bar filling up)

| Name | Tiers | Measured as | Data | NYC / Tokyo reach |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cities served | 2, 10, 25, 100 | regions (the Cities tab's rows: a metro or a countryside zone) with a built station where a running line stops | save stations, `stationZone`, `lineStats` status and trains set at any level | 1 / 2 tiers |
| Trips a day | 100k, 1M, 10M, 30M | the World row's train trips/day, always on the average day | `demandByDay[AVERAGE_DAY].cities`, summed as `sumModes` does | 3 / 3 |
| Busiest station | 20k, 100k, 500k, 2M | boardings/day at one station complex, average day | `demandByDay[AVERAGE_DAY].view.stations`, summed by `complexes` | 3 / 3 |
| Busiest line | 50k, 250k, 1M, 2M | riders/day on one line, average day | `view.lines.get(id).ridersPerDay` | 3 / 3 |

Why these numbers: the first tier of the last three falls to a first line (100k trips, 50k riders,
20k boardings), so a new player sees one early, which is the payoff S5 found missing. The third
tier is a whole real network's (both saves reach it). The fourth is past both saves: 30M trips is
most of real Tokyo's rail, 2M boardings is Shinjuku territory (real about 3.5M), 2M riders is a
Yamanote; Tokyo's Line 1a at 1.90M sits just short of it. Cities served is the one where breadth
pays: 25 is most of Japan, 100 takes several countries.

### Firsts (once)

| Name | Rule | Data | NYC / Tokyo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Over the border | a running line with stops in two countries | each stop's zone's `country` (registry); stops in dead space have none | no / yes (Japan-Korea) |
| Two continents | a running line with stops on two continents | country to continent table (below) | no / no |
| Sea crossing | a running line whose path crosses 10 km of unbroken water, bridge or tunnel | the sim's per-edge water spans (`edge_water`, T-1930), not exported yet: stage 3 | no / yes, presumably (Japan-Korea) |
| Long haul | a running line 500 km long | `lineStats.km` | no / yes |
| Money train | one line's expected profit today (the Lines tab's Money figure) of $200M/day | fares minus running and upkeep, as `lineNet` in LinesTab.tsx | no / yes (Shinkansen) |
| Bullet train | a train reaching 300 km/h on a running line | `lineStats.stretches[].reached` | no / yes |
| Round and round | a running circle line (a loop that carries on round, T-798) with at least 10 stops | `lineStats.loop === "circle"`, stops | no / no |
| Change here | 1 change per train trip on average, over at least 100k trips/day | `view.stations[].transfers` summed over `railTripsPerDay`, average day | no (0.97) / no (0.73) |

The four of my own (Over the border, Sea crossing, Bullet train, Round and round) are things only a
world map with real geography gives, and each is a real-world feat a player knows (Basel, Øresund,
Seikan and the Channel, the Shinkansen, the Yamanote). Left out: a night line (a train at the low
level runs all night already, so it would be had by accident) and anything per city (the share
goals cover that).

**Continents** (`game/continents.ts`, new, a fixed table): each country's UN M49 continent, the
Americas split at Panama into North and South (Central America and the Caribbean North), and the
countries that cross a line split by position: Turkey's Thrace (west of the Bosporus and the
Dardanelles) Europe, the rest Asia; Russia Asia east of the Urals (about 60°E); Egypt Asia east of
the Suez Canal; Kazakhstan Europe west of the Ural river. Indonesia is Asia throughout. Cyprus is
Asia (M49). Today the cheapest two-continent line is long: Greece to Cyprus, or Indonesia to
Australia, both a bored tunnel of hundreds of km (bored tunnels have no water limit, T-1930), or
Moscow to China. Once Turkey is built (it is on her list of next countries), Istanbul has one under
the Bosporus, as Marmaray does.

**Common rules**
- **Running** means `status === "running"` with trains set at some demand level; a blueprint, a line
  with no trains or a broken one never counts.
- **Demand figures** come from final results only and never from a rough zone's (the star's rule,
  T-685, T-1223), always on the average day, so a weekend, a holiday or the Day choice changes
  nothing.
- **Network figures** (served cities, length, speed, border, continents, circle) are checked when the
  world changes; money when the hour ledger turns (once a game hour); demand figures on each final.
- **Kept once reached**, with the game second (as `goals`), and never taken back: undo, a closed line
  or a fall in riders leaves it earned. The bars show the figure now, so a fallen figure sits below
  a tick that stays lit.
- **A jump over several tiers** (a big edit, a loaded network) records each tier at that second and
  toasts only the highest.

## 3. How it shows

### The toast

Mockups: `artifacts/trainworld/t2124/desktop-panel-and-toasts.png` (two stacked, the older fading),
`toast-close.png`, `phone.png`.

- **Where**: the map's bottom left, 10 px in, in the same column as the tool notes (`#tools`) and
  above them, so the stop editor's line and a refused edit's reason push it up rather than cover it.
- **Look**: the top-centre rides note's (background, 1 px rule border, 12 px type), at most 280 px
  wide: the medal icon in the accent colour, the name in bold, and under it one muted line saying
  what was reached ("1M riders/day on Line 1a", "Japan-Korea stops in Japan and South Korea").
  No x, no sound.
- **Life**: a 150 ms rise as it appears, 5 s held, a 0.6 s fade. The pointer over it holds it; a click
  opens the Cities tab with the achievements view and ends it. Real seconds, whatever the clock speed.
- **Several at once**: stacked upward, newest at the bottom, at most three shown; a fourth waits for a
  place, shown at least 1 s after the last. More than three waiting at once fold into one toast,
  "4 more achievements", that opens the view. A new player's first line will usually bring three
  (the first tier of trips, station and line), one after another.
- **High clock speeds**: nothing is checked per frame, so speed only bunches them, and the queue
  spaces them out as above. A hidden tab (Run in background) keeps them until it is shown.
- **Tutorial**: shown as usual. The card is at the top right, the toasts bottom left; the first
  line's toasts are the payoff the first-ten-minutes walk found missing (S5). They never ripple.
- **Waits** while the start menu, the saved games panel or a confirmation is up, and is hidden by F2
  (`html.ui-hidden`).
- **Phone held upright**: the same corner of the map (the map is above the dock), one toast at a
  time, the second line cut to one line with an ellipsis; tapping it opens the view.
- **Reduced motion**: no rise, the fade only.
- **An old save** works its achievements out on load from its first final result and the world,
  quietly: everything met by then is recorded at the load's game second with no toast. From then on
  toasts as usual.

### The panel

Mockup: `artifacts/trainworld/t2124/desktop-panel-and-toasts.png` (Tokyo save, its real figures;
the firsts ticked are the ones the measurements say it earns, all "day 406" because an old save
records them on load), `panel-tick-hover.png`. The mocks are temporary DOM over the running game
(`scratchpad/t2124/shots.mjs`), no app code. Two slips in them: Trips a day shows the network's
17.7M where the build uses the World row's 17.2M, and the medal icon is a first try.

- **The button**: at the top right of the Cities tab, above the list (also above "No stations yet."):
  the medal icon and the count of steps earned, "11/24", tabular figures; hover "Achievements". It is
  `.on` while the view is open. In a sandbox there is no button.
- **The view** opens in the inspector (selection kind `achievements`), as a row of the Cities list
  opens the city view, so the list stays beside it; on a phone the inspector stands beside the tab as
  it does for cities. Title: the medal and "Achievements".
- **Tiered rows**: the name, the figure now and the next tier ("17.2M/30M"; past the top tier the
  figure alone), then a bar of four equal parts, each filling from the tier before to its own, with
  ticks between the parts in the goal ticks' look (2 px text colour once reached, 1 px muted before),
  and the four tier figures under it (muted, the reached ones in the text colour). Equal parts
  because the tiers grow by 5-10x: a bar to scale would show nothing below the top tier. No rules
  between the rows.
- **Firsts**: after a divider (a different section) the heading "Firsts", then eight rows: a 12 px
  box (the tutorial card's, ticked in the accent once earned), the name, and the day earned at the
  right ("day 406"). A first not earned yet is shown, muted, its rule in its hover: a goal nobody can
  see is no goal (player-voices idea 2). Fixed order, so nothing jumps when one is earned.
- **Hovers** (each earns its place): a tier tick names the tier and when it was reached; a busiest
  station's or line's figure names which; an earned first gives what earned it and when; a locked
  one its rule. Nothing else has one.

## 4. Saves and modes

- **Per save**, in the header as `achievements: Record<string, { at: number; of?: string }>`, keyed
  `cities:2`, `trips:1000000`, `border` and so on (`at` the game second, `of` the line or station
  that earned it, for the hover). Set by the clock worker on the page's message, as `star` is now
  (clockClient.ts posts, clock.worker.ts keeps, saveFile.ts lists the field). Keys never refer to a
  zone, so the zone-rename migration in saveFile.ts leaves them alone.
- **An old save** gets them on load (above), at the load's time.
- **Sandbox**: earns none and shows no button (Factorio's cheat commands and Cities: Skylines'
  unlimited money turn achievements off the same way). A normal game switched to sandbox keeps what
  it earned, earns no more, and loses the button with the cash; the switch's confirmation needs no
  new words. A real-network start is a sandbox, so it never earns the real network's tiers for free.
- **Undo, a restored backup, a copy**: the copy and the backup carry the header as saved; undo keeps
  them.
- **The account**: nothing yet. A later task could show a player's count on their leaderboard row.

## 6. What building it takes

1. **Rules, storage and the panel** (medium, Sonnet with this note): `game/achievements.ts` (the
   list, tiers, checks, wording), `game/continents.ts`, the checks in clockClient.ts beside the
   star's, the worker's message and the save header field, the Cities tab button,
   `ui/inspector/AchievementsView.tsx`, CSS, docs (ui-panels.md "Cities tab" and a new
   "Achievements" section, SPEC 6's points paragraph), and a check that loads both saves and
   expects the earned sets in section 1. Seven of the eight firsts. About 500 lines.
2. **The toast** (small): `ui/AchievementToasts.tsx` in `#tools`, the queue and folding, the waits
   and F2. About 150 lines.
3. **Sea crossing** (small, but it changes the WASM interface, so its own worktree): the sim gives
   each line the longest unbroken water run on its path at any level (T-1930's run counting with
   bored tunnels included), carried in `LineStats`. About 80 lines of Rust and 20 of TS.

Stages 1 and 2 can run together if 2 starts on a fake list; 3 is independent.

## 7. How other games do it (a quick look)

- Mini Metro names a few per city by delivered passengers ("Deliver 200 passengers in London",
  then 1000, then harder ones with a limit: "1300 passengers in Paris using no more than three
  lines"): tiers by count, as here. https://acheev.net/game/623678642/s/mini-metro
- Factorio: tiered counts too (produce 10k, then 1M of an item), and script or cheat commands turn
  achievements off after a warning. https://wiki.factorio.com/Achievements
- Cities: Skylines: unlimited money and the like turn them off for that city (forum reports, not
  official). https://steamcommunity.com/app/255710/discussions/0/405694115203664129
- Steam's own toast (a corner, a few seconds, icon and name) is the pattern players know; the toast
  here is the same, smaller and silent. (Not researched further.)
