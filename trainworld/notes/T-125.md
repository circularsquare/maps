# T-125: world coordinates (closes T-053)

Every position (track nodes, stations, trains, demand queries, water, saves) was planar metres in
one frame anchored at New York with a fixed `cos(lat0)` east-west scale. Anita wants the game to
grow past two cities, with intercity track between regions, so a line of several hundred km has to
have the right length, curve radius and speed, and existing saves have to load as they were.

## Design

**Two frames, one per kind of game; the frame is a fact of the save, not of the build.**

- `Equirect` (legacy): `x = R dlon cos(lat0)`, `y = R dlat` from (-73.985, 40.758). Every save made
  before T-125 is in it and keeps it for life. Nothing about its geometry, costs, money or fares
  changes. Its error is direction-dependent (east-west scale is `cos(lat0)/cos(lat)`): measured on
  straight edges from New York, the plane length against the great circle is -0.33% to Philadelphia
  (134 km), +0.81% to Boston (302 km), -0.82% to Washington (332 km), -0.22% to Pittsburgh (509 km);
  a purely east-west mile at Philadelphia is 1.2% short, at Boston 2.7%.
- `Lcc` (world frame, new games): a spherical Lambert conformal conic tangent at the same origin
  and with the same metre units. It is conformal, so a circle in the plane is a circle on the
  ground and curve radii and speed limits (`v = sqrt(a_lat R)`) are right in every direction. Its
  scale depends on latitude alone: `k = 1 + dlat^2/2` (dlat in radians from 40.758 N), the same at any
  longitude. Ground length is plane length / k.

| distance north or south of the origin | scale error |
|---|---|
| 1 degree (111 km) | 0.015% |
| 2 degrees (222 km) | 0.06% |
| 4 degrees (444 km) | 0.24% |
| 5 degrees (555 km) | 0.38% |
| 8 degrees (888 km) | 0.97% |

  Documented extent: latitude 35.8 to 45.8 N (Charlotte to Montreal), any longitude within the
  conic's reach, error under 0.4%; the measured edges above are within 0.02% (Philadelphia +0.003%,
  Boston +0.013%, Washington +0.017%, Pittsburgh 0.000%, plane length against great circle).
  Beyond that the error grows as dlat^2/2 and a game that needs more wants a second frame origin
  (T-126). The frame's origin is in the save's network bytes, so a new world is a registry entry
  with its own origin, not a code change.

**Why this and not the options in the task.**

- (a) Per-region local frames, with a region id per node. Accurate, but every edge between two
  regions needs one frame of its own, the crossing and junction tests need a common frame, the
  save format gains a field, and legacy saves (whose Philadelphia track sits in the New York
  frame) need a "region 0" that extends to 150 km at 1.2% error anyway. Several times the code for
  an error the conic already removes.
- (b) One conformal projection with a documented extent. Chosen. The whole track model is planar
  and stays planar (geometry, crossings, grid, profiles, capacity: no code touched). A
  conformal frame keeps arcs circular. The tangent conic costs nothing at the origin (scale 1.000,
  metre units unchanged) and is better than a secant one for the stated extent.
  Web Mercator (what the renderer uses) is conformal too but its scale is 1/cos(lat) = 1.32 here,
  which would make every limit and cost in the model scale by it.
- (c) lon/lat plus a local metric. Needs geodesic arcs, a metric at every point and a rewrite of
  the planar geometry. Rejected.

**Legacy saves are not converted by default.** The frame cannot be swapped under drawn track
without moving it, and the task requires identical geometry, money, schedules, fares and ledger,
so a TWT4/TWT3/TWT2 save loads into the legacy frame exactly as before (the derived geometry,
costs and service of the real NYC fixture hash identically, see Checks) and stays there. New games
are in the world frame. An explicit conversion exists (`TrackApi::convert_save_frame`,
`save::convert_frame`): each node, PI and drawn point goes through lon/lat and back onto the 1 mm
grid, ids, levels, radii, stations and lines are copied, so places stay on the ground and lengths
follow the new frame (a legacy edge through Philadelphia gets 1.2% longer). Round trip is within
2.5 mm. No UI offers it (see "For Anita").

## What changed

- `sim/src/track/frame.rs`: `Frame` (kind, origin, forward and inverse, `scale_at`),
  `great_circle`. `Network` holds its frame (`frame_kind`, `frame()`); `tile_of` goes through it.
- Save bytes: a legacy-frame network still encodes as TWT4, byte for byte as before; a world-frame
  one encodes as TWT5, identical to TWT4 except the magic. Older builds refuse TWT5 cleanly.
- Water: `cost::GeoMask` puts a pack raster (laid out in its own equirectangular frame at its
  origin) into any world frame through lon/lat, tabulated on a 2 km lattice and interpolated
  bilinearly (under 0.1 m off, 25 m cells), so a lookup is a few multiplies. The legacy frame keeps
  the affine `TransformedMask` and its arithmetic. `TrackApi::add_pack_water` replaces the clock
  worker's offset arithmetic; the worker hands over the pack's raster and origin and the track
  model rebuilds the mask when a loaded save is in the other frame.
- `TrackApi`: `set_empty_frame(world)`, `frame_kind`, `frame_origin`, `to_lonlat`, `from_lonlat`,
  `many_to_lonlat`, `convert_save_frame`.
- `app/src/game/frame.ts` (the same two frames in TS, checked against PROJ), `registry.json` and
  `cities.ts`: the one registry. It names the world, the world frame of new games, the legacy
  frame, the regional market pack and its frame, and the cities (id, name, pack origin, centre,
  zoom, market index). The clock worker (water for every registry city, the header's `cities`),
  the demand workers (packs, home city, market indices), `vite.config.ts` (which packs are
  copied), the save header check, the City tab and `cityNear` read it. Adding a region is a
  registry entry plus its pack.
- Every conversion goes through lon/lat: `worldToCity`, `cityToWorld`, `worldToMarket`,
  `marketToWorld`, `cityToMarket`. A pack keeps its own local origin; identical frames short-circuit
  (New York's pack in a legacy game is now an exact identity where it was a round trip good to
  1e-9 m).
- The clock worker reports the loaded game's frame in `ready` and `loaded`; the main thread, the
  demand client (cell caches), the commuter view and the demand workers follow it. Network
  snapshots and the demand workers' "cells" queries carry it. A game loaded in the other frame
  invalidates everything cached in world metres, as a change of focused city does.
- The regional market keeps its sites and 600 m squares in its own pack frame (the NYC equirect
  frame, which its pack was built in). Stations go in through `worldToMarket`; its answers come
  back through `marketToWorld`.
- Debug: `window.tw.frame()`, `tw.packToLngLat`, `tw.lngLatToPack`.

## Performance

- Demand solve, three workers, real network: free flow 3.50 s to 3.36 s, round three 11.21 s to
  11.34 s (legacy), 3.23 s and 11.17 s to 3.23 s and 11.18 s (with the Philadelphia line): no
  change. WASM memory per worker unchanged (156/156/137 MB). Per-worker set-up of the combined
  network 44-47 ms to 56-73 ms in this run (one sample; JS converts the stations to the market
  frame now).
- Edits (`t125-edit-timing.mjs`, 300 route previews and adds of 15-30 km with both water
  rasters, native wasm in node): legacy frame preview 0.68 ms, add 0.33 ms; world frame 0.82 ms and
  0.40 ms (+0.14 and +0.07 ms, the extra trigonometry in `tile_of`). Loading a 300-edge save 139 ms
  against 134 ms. The first version of the water mask did the trigonometry per sample: 2.25 ms
  and 1.12 ms, load 418 ms; the lattice fixed that.
- The clock worker converts each drawn point to lng/lat: 28 ns (legacy) against 174 ns (world),
  paid only for edges an edit touched (about 17 ms extra for a full rebuild of the real network's
  119,000 points).
