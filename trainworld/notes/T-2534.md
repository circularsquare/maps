## The measure

A **crossing on the ground**: two hops sharing no station and no line whose tracks (the graph's `hop` polylines; the
straight hop where a graph has none, as the game's captured graphs) cross an odd number of times, at 20 degrees or more,
at least 100 m from every end of either. **Dense** when 12 stations or more lie within 2.5 km of the crossing. A crossing
is **kept** when the two hops' drawn polylines cross an odd number of times; **loose** when it is kept counting the hops
either side along their lines too (the line crosses one station early or late). **Across**: from the point at the
crossing's share of the second hop to the first hop's drawing (or its neighbours'), in pitches: how far a city sits off
the line through it. **Through**: a crossing between a hop inside a block and one not inside that block.

## What changed

1. **Crossing ties** (`crossTies`): every crossing on the ground outside dense places. The longer hop is e1. Its terms:
   the point at the crossing's share of e2 lies on e1's line (A: its real way, weight 3; B: its direction, 10; the snap:
   its way after B, 30), and in B (3) and the snap (10) the two points at their shares are the same. The line term lets
   a city slide along a long hop drawn short, keeping its own scale; a point term alone squeezed New Haven onto the
   Shore Line's short drawn hop and skewed its lines.
2. **The blocks' stage** holds the ties between blocks (line 1, point 0.3), does not keep two tied blocks apart, and
   counts networks a crossing ties as joined, so New Haven gets no island link.
3. **Room**: a hop with two crossings or more is at least as long as the span between them at the crossed hops' drawn
   scale, plus 4 pitches each side, so a lonely hop drawn short does not squeeze the city it crosses.
4. **Sides**: each station within 1.5 km of a tie's longer hop, outside dense places, is kept on its side of it in B
   and the snap (weight 3, its half width and 2 pitches off), so New Haven's Union Station stays north-west of the Shore
   Line. Not in A: there it cost T-2520's parallel measure about 3 points in Japan.

Tried and left out: point ties at full weight (A 3, B 10, snap 30; best counts, but the squeeze above); the routing
choosing between a bent piece's two legs by the crossings each keeps (changed no choice: the losses there are stations
sliding along legs, not leg choice); sides in A.

## Looked outward

- Nöllenburg and Wolff (2011, MIP): the input is planarised, each crossing made a dummy vertex of degree 4, so the
  drawing keeps the embedding as a hard constraint. The ties here are that dummy vertex made soft and held only outside
  dense places; the line term frees its place along the longer hop.
- LOOM (Bast, Brosi, Storandt 2018-2020) and their octilinear grid-graph maps: also work on the planarised line graph
  and keep its topology; a crossing is a node there, so lines that cross on the ground always cross.
- Stott et al. (2011) count crossings as a criterion to minimise; here they are counted only against the ground's.
- Wang and Chi (2011) and Nöllenburg's "relative position" keep a node's sector against its neighbours; the sides term
  is that for a station against a long hop it is not joined to.
