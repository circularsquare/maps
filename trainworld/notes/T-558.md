# T-558: walking that competes (proposal for Anita, 2026-10-16)

Design only; nothing in the game has changed. Today a share of every trip is set aside for walking by
distance before rail and the car compete for the rest (docs/demand.md "Walking"). The proposal puts
walking into the choice itself, with its own cost.

## Decisions for Anita

1. **The form**: one three-way choice (rail, car, walk) *(recommended)*, or a nested one (walk
   against motorised first, then rail against car). On New York they differ by 0.2 points of rail;
   the nest adds a parameter no survey we have can fit.
2. **The walk's cost rises with length**: 1.5 perceived minutes a minute for the first 10 minutes,
   4 a minute after *(recommended)*, or a flat 1.5 like the walk to a station. A flat weight puts
   20% of 3-4 km everyday trips on foot (today 7%, real New York 4%) and misplaces a fifth of all walkers.
3. **What the fit holds**: each city walks as much as today, by distance *(recommended)*. Moving to
   the surveys' shorter walking curve (below) waits for the deep calibration.
4. **Homes without a car** walk at the same cost and constant as everyone *(recommended)*. Their
   long walks then go to "other" (bus, taxi, bike), up 0.35 points of all trips in New York;
   `other_asc` is left for the deep calibration.
5. **The trip explorer** shows rail's and the car's share of all trips, with walking as one number in
   a hover *(recommended)*; the line "Walking takes N% of trips this short whatever the trains do"
   goes, since it would no longer be true.

## 1. The form

Every trip chooses among its modes in one logit, at the scale the choice has now (0.05 a perceived
minute, damped past 60 car-minutes; walks never get that long):

- **with a car**: rail, car, walk (drivers who park free and those who pay for a space choose apart,
  as now);
- **without a car**: rail (walking to stations), walk, other (as now, less the set-aside walk).

**The walk's cost.** Real minutes are today's walking distance (zone centres for commutes, cell
centres for everyday trips, round water by a bridge) at 6 km/h on a path 1.3 times the straight
line: 13 minutes a straight km. Perceived: 1.5 a minute up to 10 minutes, then 4 a minute. So 500 m
costs 10 perceived minutes, 1 km 27, 1.5 km 53, 2 km 79, 3 km 131. A good station nearby can now
take walkers, and a long walk loses to a train.

**Its constant**: walking starts 3.0 ahead of the car for commutes and 3.25 for everyday trips (in
the logit's units; about 60 and 65 perceived minutes). It stands for what a short drive costs beyond
its road time (getting to the car, finding a space, the start-up), and replaces today's two centres
of the set-aside curve (1.7 and 1.9 km). One walk cost for both, two constants, the way rail has
`asc_rail` and `everyday_asc_rail`.

The regional layer has no walking (its trips are long) and does not change.

## 2. Fitting

**What it must reproduce**: today's walking by layer and distance on each city's real network. That
curve carries T-509's fit to New York's NHTS everyday walk shares and the ACS commute check, so
holding it keeps those fits. On New York (this measurement) the constants above give commutes 5.52%
walking (today 5.51), everyday trips 21.97% (21.96); everyday walking by band:

| km | <0.5 | 0.5-1 | 1-1.5 | 1.5-2 | 2-2.5 | 2.5-3 | 3-4 | 4-5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today | 98 | 94 | 84 | 61 | 33 | 15 | 5.4 | 2.1 |
| Proposal | 96 | 94 | 85 | 65 | 36 | 16 | 3.6 | 0.3 |
| NHTS 2017, New York, everyday legs | 85 | 50 | 32 | 13 | 7.5 | 6.2 | 3.8 | 0.6 |

The walk cost's shape was picked on a grid (no-rail network, each constant set to today's total):
walking in the wrong band, as a share of all walkers, is 21-22% at a flat 1.5, 6-9% at 3, 5-9% at
4 and 8-13% at 6.

**Cities**: New York now; London as the check when it is built; all six of T-509's at the deep
calibration.

**What can wait for the deep calibration**: the T-509 rail constants (rail moves +0.8% on New York,
well inside their fit), `other_asc` (other +0.35 points), and the curve itself. The surveys walk
much shorter than the game: NHTS reaches half walking at 0.75 km, the game at about 1.9 km, but the
game also has far fewer very short trips (17% of trips under 2 km against NHTS's 43%, home-based
trips only), so the two errors partly cancel; and the game walks 12% of 2-5 km trips against NHTS's
4.5%. Fixing that is a survey fit per country, not part of this change.

**What cannot wait**: the two walking constants. Without them walking jumps the moment the rule
changes.

## 3. Effect on New York

The model's own code in a private copy of the crate (as T-551): every commute pair offered rail and
every everyday pair hands its real rail and car costs to a test module, which runs today's choice
and the proposal on the same costs. Real network `nyc_real.json`, starting fares, **no change
penalty** (T-557), free-flow costs (crowding lowers rail about 7% on commutes and 10% on everyday
trips, in both alike). % of all trips:

| | Today | Proposal | Nested (0.5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Real network**: walk | 17.87 | 17.88 | 17.88 |
| rail | 14.94 | 15.06 | 14.86 |
| car | 57.74 | 57.26 | 57.53 |
| other | 9.45 | 9.80 | 9.73 |
| **No rail**: walk | 18.64 | 19.03 | 18.46 |
| car | 64.78 | 63.72 | 64.13 |
| other | 16.58 | 17.25 | 17.41 |
| Walking that rail takes | 0.77 | 1.15 | 0.58 |

Today rail takes walkers only from homes without a car; with the proposal it takes half as many
again. Rail's share of all trips by distance (today, proposal):

| | 0-2 km | 2-5 km | 5-10 km |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commutes | 4.3, 4.9 | 26.9, 27.6 | 35.1, 35.2 |
| Everyday | 2.3, 2.0 | 11.1, 11.4 | 14.8, 15.0 |

In plain terms: the totals hardly move, because the constants keep walking where it was. What changes
is where: a train near a short commute now takes some of its walkers (rail on commutes under 5 km up
3-14%), while an errand under 2 km walks rather than takes a train a little more often (walking is
judged against the train now, not only against the car). Trips to places with paid parking walk a
little less (commutes 6.6% to 6.1%) and ride a little more (54.6% to 55.0%).

Checks: commute rail from the module's choice equals the model's to 0.01 trips (4,740,251); everyday
rail at sample level is 0.2% above the model's pair level (5,643,976 against 5,632,277). With the
change penalty gone and crowding run, the model's own rail is 13.7% of all trips (T-551, with the
penalty: 12.8%).

Scripts: `notes/t558_copy.py` (copies `sim/` and adds `notes/t558.rs`; how to run is in its header),
`t558_fit_read.py`, `t558_tables.py`, `t558_nhts.py`. Outputs: `../artifacts/trainworld/T-558-nyc.json`,
`T-558-nyc-fit.json`.

## 4. Cost

Code (one build stage, a few hundred lines, about T-511's size):

- **`kernel.rs` Params**: the walk curve's `walk_d0_km`, `walk_s_km`, `everyday_walk_d0_km` give way
  to the walk's cost (10 minutes at 1.5, then 4) and `asc_walk` 3.0, `everyday_asc_walk` +0.25.
- **The gravity's no-rail split**: `walk_trips_by_zone` and `other_trips_by_zone` become one split of
  each near zone pair into walk, car and other with the car's cost, as `other_trips_by_zone` already
  does for other. Under a second when a pack opens.
- **The commute pair loop** (`mode_choice`): each zone pair's walk cost where the walk share is now;
  one more exponential per parking class and for homes without a car; "walkers rail takes"
  (`walk_lost_by_sub`) widened to everyone. `trip::pair_choice` gets the same expressions, and the
  test that the two agree to the bit stays.
- **The everyday layer**: `pass` adds the walk to each pair's choice; `add_totals` splits each sample
  with no rail. A pair holds samples at different distances, so stage 1 picks between the pair's
  mean walk cost and its mean no-rail walking share, checked against the sample-level result here.
- **`api.rs`**: walking totals and walking by cell from the new tables; the summary is unchanged.
- **Trip explorer**: `game/trip.ts` and `TripInspector.tsx`, decision 5; about 20 lines.
- **Tools**: `realnet`/`calib --set` names and T-509's fit scripts that set `walk_d0_km`.
- **No change**: the regional layer, the workers (same crate, same messages), the boarding account
  (it reads rail loads only).

**Run time**: a commute lane now costs about five exponentials, a logarithm and up to eight egress
gathers; the proposal adds one to three exponentials, roughly 5-10% of the commute pair loop (New
York's real network: 73M lanes a round), less of the whole solve. The everyday pass adds two
exponentials to each pair's access double loop, under 5%. Stage 1 measures both.

## 5. Stages

1. **Build** (one task, after Anita's decisions). Done when: on New York's and London's real networks
   walking by layer is within 0.2 points of today and everyday walking by band within 3 points;
   rail's share of all trips within 2% of today; `pair_choice` agrees with the pair loop to the bit;
   smoke passes; the commute solve on the real save is measured before and after (within +15%);
   docs/demand.md "Walking" and the mode-choice section, docs/demand-worldwide.md's entry and a
   DECISIONS line written.
2. **Deep calibration** (later, with the world's cities in): the walking constants and the cost's
   shape fitted per country on travel surveys (NHTS, England's NTS, France's EMP, Japan's person-trip
   surveys), jointly with `asc_rail`, `everyday_asc_rail` and `other_asc`; a nest only if a survey
   shows walking reacting to rail differently from the car.
