## 1. How real operators rate a car

Two numbers almost everywhere: a planning or rated load, and a crush load well above it. What differs is which one a
system calls 100%.

**The convention.** Every system has a rated load below crush, and trains run above the rated load at the peak
everywhere (New York's observed loads pass its guideline too). What is Japanese and Korean is the labelling: they rate
a car loosely (about 3 standees a m², "everyone can hold a strap") and publish peak loads as 150-200% of it, with
official targets above 100%. China rates at 5-6 a m², nearly crush already, so its 100% is about Japan's 200%. The West
plans at 3.6-5 a m² and quotes crush (6-8 a m²) as a separate figure. So "rated 100%, runs to 150-200%" is a Japanese
and Korean way of writing it, not a universal one; the two-number fact under it is universal.

**The game's 160 is a rated load, not a crush load.** 160 a 20 m car is New York's peak guideline (145 an 18.3 m car,
158 for 20 m) and Japan's 定員 (157 an E235 car), both "every seat and comfortable standing". Real crush is 1.5-2 times
that: New York's R160 about 1.65 times, London 1.3-1.4, China 1.3, Tokyo's worst lines 2.0 as run.

## 2. The game today

- A car is 20 m, 44 seats and 160 riders "at crush" (`SEATS_PER_CAR`, `CRUSH_PER_CAR`: sim/src/demand/api.rs,
  sim/src/track/boarding.rs, app/src/game/boarding.ts); a bus half a car (22, 80).
- Crowding penalty (`crowd_factors`, sim/src/demand/kernel.rs): each stretch at its period's busiest hour (1.4 times the
  mean hour at the peaks), from the larger of the seats and 0.7 of crush (112 a car): a ride minute counts
  1 + x(1 + x), x = (load - 112) / (160 - 112). At 100% it counts 3, at 120% 5.4.
- Full trains: a boarding waits for a train with room, plain minutes, up to 60 (T-188).
- The ceiling (T-1070 as T-1255's queue, `capacity_fit`): riders stay only if the trains due in the next 30 minutes
  have room up to 160 a car; the rest go by car or on foot. So no line carries more than 160 a car at its busiest hour.
- What the player sees: the train gauge is half full when every seat is taken and full at 160, with a red rim at 160;
  "Average load" is a share of 160, capped at 100%; the crowding dots are the wait for a train with room.

So a game train at "100%" is a Tokyo train at 100% of 定員, and the game never lets it go past that, where Tokyo's
busiest lines run at 160-200% every weekday morning.

**What this says.**
- **Capacity is not what holds Asia low.** Doubling every train adds 0.3% to the timetable-built Tokyo save, whose
  lines mostly run under 70% of 160 at the peak (T-1750 had the same: 92.5% of rider-segments under 70%, 240 a car
  +0.8%). Its busiest sections carry a fifth to a third of real riders (T-1750: Chuo 12.7k an hour against MLIT's 65k),
  so there is nobody for a bigger train to carry. Against T-2088's real figures: Tokyo 0.36 stays 0.36, Osaka 0.35
  becomes 0.37 (1.5x) or 0.38 (2x), Nagoya 0.42 becomes 0.43, New York 2.41 becomes 2.50 or 2.52. T-1750 put the
  real remainder in the car side of mode choice (parking at 14 minutes instead of 7: +14% Tokyo).
- **Where it binds** is the rough jpn7 network (MLIT-count services, Osaka loses 10% of its entries at the platforms)
  and a player's own network (Anita's Seoul, every line full): +4-9% there, from riders who today go another way or
  are put off by the crowding price.
- **Most of the gain comes by 1.5x.** 1.5x gives 60-85% of what 2x gives everywhere, and leaves 2x's only real
  difference in Osaka and Fukuoka on jpn7.

## 4. Proposal for Anita

The game's 160 a car is a rated load (the "100%" of Japan, Korea and New York's planning guideline), which the game
calls crush and never lets a train pass. Real trains run past their rated load at the peak everywhere; Japan and Korea
publish it as 150-200%. Recommended, as one rule worldwide (no per-country number):

- **Rated 160 a car stays 100%. A train may run to 150% (240 a car), the new ceiling** for the queue, the wait for a
  train with room and the boarding account. 150% rather than 200%: real crush is 1.3-1.65 times the rated load in
  London, China and New York and 2 only on Tokyo's worst lines; 1.5x has most of 2x's effect; and it keeps more
  reason to buy trains.
- **Riders start to mind at 100%** (the same rule as now, 0.7 of the ceiling, is 168 a car, 105%; I would round it to
  100% so the player can read it off the gauge): a minute aboard counts 1.56 at 120%, 3 at 150%. Today it starts at
  70% (112 a car) and counts 3 at 100%. Seats stay 44.
- **What the player would see**: the gauge half full at the seats and full at 100%, then a second band from 100% to
  150% with the red rim at 150%; "Average load" as a share of 160, able to pass 100%, at most 150%; the crowding dots
  unchanged (the wait for a train with room, which now starts at 150%). Buses unchanged (80 at crush; buses are settled).
- **Effect**: real Tokyo +0.2%, New York +3.8%, jpn7 +4.5% (Osaka +6%), Anita's save +4.3% (Seoul +7%); a line at the
  ceiling carries 1.5 times as many riders on the same trains, so a crowded line needs about two thirds of the trains
  to stop turning riders away. Crowding stays a challenge, later: her Seoul lines go from 10 full to 1.
  Money gets a little easier (fewer cars and car-km for the same fares).
- **What it does not do**: lift Tokyo or Osaka towards real figures. If that is wanted, the lever is the car side of
  mode choice (T-1750), a separate question.

The other choice is to keep 160 as the ceiling and only call it "full" rather than "crush" in the docs: simpler, a
little harder game, and wrong about what a real car holds.
