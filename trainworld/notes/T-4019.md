# T-4019: players see riders skip the express (investigated, 2026-11-22, agent express)

## For Anita

**Short answer: mostly not the fare.** Three things, biggest first on the Long Island game (a commute corridor with
evenly spread stops; where the express stops are the cities the order may differ):

1. **The express serves few station pairs.** A 15-stop local serves 105 pairs, a 4-stop express 6. Even when the
   express takes every rider on its own pairs (a train every 5 minutes against the local's 30), it shows 5.3k riders
   against the local's 25k. That alone makes "most riders take the local" true on most skip-stop patterns.
2. **On the pairs both serve, riders take the first train that is worth taking.** A slower train counts as worth
   taking when it is slower by less than the wait for the faster one: half the express's headway. Riders then split
   by trains an hour. So with long headways (intercity, a train every 30-60 minutes), an express 20-35% faster still
   gets only its share of trains, and a local running twice as often gets two thirds of the express's own pairs.
   This is the textbook rule for riders who turn up without looking at the timetable. Players picture riders who
   know when each train leaves, which is what jun asked for (11-21: "can't passengers board the train that reaches
   the destination first?").
3. **The fare matters where the express costs more**: high-speed trains on it, or its own fare multiple set above
   1. At the default high-speed 1.25 on the part above the base, at US fares, the extra fare on a 72 km trip is
   worth about the 15.7 minutes the express saves, so end to end drops from 100% express to 50%. At 2.0 the express
   empties (348 riders). Cho said the prices were the same, so it doesn't explain Cho's report. On a high-speed
   express the line panel says "high-speed train" under its name, but its "Fare multiple" row reads 1.00x even at
   2.0, and nothing else in the panel names the surcharge.

Not ruled out: full trains. Every run here had loads of 3-11%, so crowding never came into it; on a busy express
it would push riders to the local too.

Also: on shared double track with no passing tracks, an express behind locals every 15 minutes loses 6 of its 16
minutes, and riders from stations the express skips never change onto it (the change costs the express's half
headway plus 2 minutes; Elizabeth to Trenton is 100% local).

**The reports.** Cho (11-13, v0.3.2): an intercity line of about 15 stops and an express of 4, the express "about
20% faster", same price, "most passengers still choose the local". My Newark to Philadelphia game (below) is
consistent with it at equal fares: 77k riders on the local and 3.6k on the express. Most of the local's riders are
inside Philadelphia, on a hop the express doesn't make (point 1), and Newark to Trenton, 41 minutes by local
against 31 by express, goes 61% local (point 2). The long trips' own split there couldn't be read (the trip
explorer answers commutes only); that they follow the same rule is from reading the code. jun (11-21, 65 lines)
asks for riders who know the timetable, a request rather than a report I could reproduce. The Japanese request of
11-17 is about fares only. I didn't read their saves. Neither report gives headways, and the answer depends on
them: riders split by trains an hour only where the express saves less than half its headway (at 30/30 end to end,
and with the express every 5 minutes, the express takes everything).

**Would a player see something obviously wrong at default fares?** Yes. The trip explorer for Newark to Trenton
shows the local at 41.2 minutes and the express at 31.2, the same fare, and 61% on the local.

**What I'd do, cheapest first.** Nothing here is built. The rule changes wait for you.

- **Show the high-speed fare in the line panel** (under an hour). The "Fare multiple" row shows 1.25x (or whatever
  is set) on a line with high-speed trains. Not a rule change, but new player text for you to see.
- **Intercity trips loaded the way they are costed** (2-3 hours). A long trip's first wait is the planned one
  (at most 5 minutes plus a sixth of the headway, 30 at most) when it is costed, but its riders are loaded on the
  plain rule, at half the headway. With hourly trains the cost says express only once it saves 10 minutes, and the
  loading still splits 50/50 up to 30. Loading on the planned rule would send intercity riders to the express
  wherever it saves more than the planned wait.
- **Riders know when each train comes** (prototype and measure about 3-4 hours, landing about 4-6 more). Each rider
  takes the train that gets them there first, given when the next train of each line leaves. Transit assignment
  calls this "information at stops" (from memory, not checked here: Visum has a variant). The soft rule you turned down gave slower trains extra
  riders. This one gives each train the riders it really gets there first for, so the express gains wherever it is
  faster and runs less often. Worked out from the times above, today's split against this rule:

  | | today, express share | with timetable knowledge |
  |---|---|---|
  | Long Island, both every 30 min, 6 pairs | 58% | about 74% |
  | Long Island, local 15 / express 30, end to end (22% faster) | 33% | about 60% |
  | Long Island, local 15 / express 30, all 6 pairs | 33% | about 45% |
  | Newark to Trenton, local 23 / express 36 | 39% | about 59% |

  The Riders rows would move less: on the Long Island game with both every 30 minutes, the express from about 2.4k
  to about 3k, since point 1 stays. Where today gives the express everything just past the half-headway mark, this
  rule gives the local a few riders back (both every 30 min, 15.7 minutes saved: 100% today, about 89%). Trips are
  calculated a bit slower: the search keeps a short list of lines per station where it now keeps two sums, and the
  loading needs each line's share stored per station and destination. I'd prototype it natively before any
  promise.
- **A wiki line** on how riders choose between two trains, in "Waiting and changing", whatever you decide
  (15 minutes).

**The rule in the code.** `sim/src/demand/kernel/search.rs` `join_line`: a line with cost from boarding `d` joins a
station's pool when the pooled cost, `(weighted + f d) / (frequency + f)` plus half the pooled headway, comes out
below the current one. For two lines that means the slower joins when it is slower (minutes plus fare) by less than
the faster one's half headway; riders then split by trains an hour (`accepted`, the board bits). Long trips:
`entry_costs` works out each station's cost at the planned first wait (`planned_board_cost`), but it is only a cost:
`DestStrategy::entry` goes to the mode choice (`intercity/logsum.rs` `Columns::fill`, `re`), and loading reads the
plain strategy's board bits.

**The "with timetable knowledge" figures** are worked out, not run: waits uniform over each line's headway and
independent, each rider takes the line with the lower wait plus ride. Express share = P(W_express < W_local + saving).
Both every h with saving s < h: 1 - (h - s)^2 / (2 h^2). Local every h_l, express every h_x, s + h_l <= h_x:
(h_l / 2 + s) / h_x. Riders rows scaled from the pairs' shares, so rough.
