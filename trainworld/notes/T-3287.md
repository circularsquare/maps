## 1. What a gap is today

**The rules.** A zone row is a group of free units (NUTS-3 in Europe, census subdivisions in Canada, geoBoundaries
elsewhere; docs/zones.md 2). A free unit is **dead space** when:
- **density** (Europe and most countries): under **20 people a km2**, unless it holds **0.4M** (`DEAD_KEEP` 0.8 of
  the 0.5M floor: Bergen, Trondheim, Oulu, Inverness got in that way, T-903);
- **reach** (Canada, Russia, Australia, Brazil, North Africa): under **100,000 people within 150 km** of its centre;
- **alone**: its zone ends up under **50,000** people and can join nothing (`ZONE_ALONE_MIN`);
- scattered towns, occupied land (`LEFT_OUT`), outside Europe's box; and every country not planned yet (North Korea).

Nothing in these rules looks at where a unit lies. A fjord or forest county between two cities averages its towns down
under 20 a km2 and drops out, town and all (Kristiansand, Tartu, Daugavpils, Karlstad, Jyvaskyla, Kuopio all sit in
dead units). T-772 (someday) already named this; this task covers it.

**What a player sees in dead space**: the dark shade (no zone outline cuts it out), no name in the top bar, no place
names for stations, no trips start or end there (a line through it carries only riders going through), the world's
coarse water instead of a mask. Track and stations can be built (terrain is priced worldwide since T-2901). So a line
Oslo - Stavanger runs 150 km through an unnamed dark hole with nobody in it.

## 2. The contiguity rule

### The rule, in numbers

Run on the dead units of a country before it is zoned; a picked unit is let through the density (or reach) test and
then zoned by the usual merge rule with the other picks (floor 0.5M, cap 1.5M, partners first).

1. **Between**: the straight line between two towns of **50,000** or more (Natural Earth's populated places) that lie in
   built land, at most **300 km** apart, runs **15 km** or more over the unit.
2. **Town**: the unit holds a town of **50,000** and touches built land (so a lone Tromso does not become an island zone).
3. **Hole**: built land holds **75%** of the unit's land border, and land is at least a fifth of its whole border (an
   island with one built neighbour, Iceland, is not a hole).
4. **Rounds**: picks count as built land, and 1-3 run again until nothing new is picked (Europe: 3 rounds, then none).

Why lines between towns and not only a unit's own people: the reach rule (Canada's) asks how many people live near a unit,
which is the same question the density floor asks in a softer form. It would keep Tromso and Lulea as islands and still
leave northwestern Ontario dead, which is the land between two cities with nobody in it. The line test asks the
question Anita asked: would a line between two places a player cares about cross it.

### Over-reach, and what a player sees in each kind

- **New zone** (everything picked in Europe; North Korea): an ordinary tier 0 zone. Outline, name, bubbles at its towns,
  trips to and from its people, a place in the regional market, a water mask and place names. Thin land costs little
  since T-852's 4 km blocks: cells only where people are.
- **Pass-through land** (only for wilderness bridges such as northwestern Ontario): land that is not shaded, with a name,
  terrain, water and costs, but no trips. Today it does not exist: a zone is a pack, and dead space has none of this.
  Two ways: (a) build the strip as ordinary zones of few people (under the 50,000 floor, so a rule exception; a pack
  each, almost empty), or (b) a new light kind with an outline in `outlines.json`, a water mask and place list but no
  demand, which needs app and pipeline work (a new code path in focus, loading and the market). (a) is far cheaper.
- **Left dead** (the far north, Iceland): as now.

Things the rule does not over-reach on in Europe: Lapland, Finnmark, Nordland and Norrbotten stay dead (no 50,000 town
pair within 300 km crosses them, and Lulea, Tromso and Rovaniemi are under 50,000 in Natural Earth or touch nothing
built). Kainuu (69k on 22,600 km2, 3 a km2) and Jamtland (2.3 a km2) are the emptiest picks; both join bigger rows.
