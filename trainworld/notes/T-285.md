# T-285: a click on a demand bubble to its other end drawn

Anita (2026-10-13): "if i click on a destination it sometimes takes up to like 3 seconds to show
the homes that go there."

## How it was measured

Real clicks in headless Chrome (own profile) on the app's Vite dev server, the real New York save
(`T-007-real-nyc.save`, 5 solving workers + 1 aux on this 16-thread machine), map at Midtown,
zoom 12.3. The driver scans the screen with `twViews.hover` for the biggest Homes or Destinations
bubble (and a mid-sized one), clicks it with the mouse, and times from the `mousedown` to two
animation frames after the far end was handed to the overlay. Three clicks on the biggest bubble
and one on a mid one, for Destinations and Homes with all purposes, Work, Shopping, Social,
School (Destinations) and Shopping (Homes), on weekday and weekend; then clicks 0.1, 1.5 and 3 s
into a re-solve of the same network (`twDemand.resubmit()`). Driver and outputs:
scratchpad `agent-bubbles/clicks.mjs`, `before.json`, `after.json`, `compare.mjs`.

Timings come from `?debug=1`: `twDemand.client.qlog` (new: per query, when it was sent, and per
worker reply when the worker took it up, how long it worked, when the reply landed; the worker
stamps `qStart`/`qMs` on every query reply) and `twViews.stats.commuters` (new: `pickAt`,
`flowsAt`, `summedAt`, `farAt`).

## What a click does

1. The selection effect asks `flows(end, cells)`: `flowRail` and `purposes` to every solving
   worker, and `icSel` (longer trips, intercityView.ts) to every solving worker too.
2. When all have answered, `flows` to worker 0 (all modes over the far end).
3. The main thread adds up the workers' everyday answers per purpose (demandClient.ts `flows`).
4. The far cells go to the bubble worker (commuters.worker.ts `sum`) for every zoom level.
5. The overlay draws them on the next MapLibre frame.

they answer in parallel. The main thread had one slow sum, the everyday add-up.

## What dominated, and the fix

**`EverydayApi::selection_day` weighed every destination sample of the city before asking whether
it belonged to the selection.** For New York that is millions of samples, each with a square root,
a walk factor and a walk share: about 200 ms natively, 420 ms in each Chrome worker, for any
selection, even one cell. And it answered **dense**: three numbers per city cell per purpose,
6 x (30 + 3 x 202,337) floats = 14.6 MB from each of five workers, which the main thread then
added up cell by cell and scanned per purpose (80-360 ms).

Changed (sim `everyday.rs`, app `demand.worker.ts`, `demandClient.ts`, new `selectionMerge.ts`):

- `selection_parts` visits only the selection's samples. A purpose whose samples are laid out by
  the selected end (homes for outings, sites for schools) reads their ranges directly; at the other
  end the sample list is scanned for selected cells (a u32 compare) and only those are weighed.
  Samples are visited in the order of the whole list, so every sum is the same f32 sum. Native:
  4-19 ms against 196-222 ms.
- `selection_sparse_day` (what the worker now calls) sends only the far cells the selection's
  samples reach: per kind 30 timing values, m, m cells ascending, their rail, walk and drive legs.
  382 to 163,070 numbers against 3.7M. `selection` and `selection_day` stay, dense, built from the
  same parts (the Rust tests and the notes/ WASM checks use them).
- `workers/selectionMerge.ts` `mergeEveryday` adds the packed answers up on the main thread over
  the union of far cells, in the same order and with the same f32 arrays the dense code used.

## Left: clicks during a re-solve (proposal, for Anita's review)

A click while demand is updating still waits for the new solve's final round: 7.5-11 s on this
save with five workers, longer with fewer. Since T-232 the figures on show are the last final
forecast, but the workers have already set the new network up, so they cannot answer about the
old one. `flowRail`, `purposes`, `flows`, `station`, `icSel`, `icStation`, `transfers` and `trip`
all need `version === current` and every period at the shown round, so they get nulls and the
view asks again when the new result is published.

**Proposal: each solving worker keeps a frozen copy of what the queries read for the last final
result, and answers queries about that version from it while the next solve runs** (T-122,
widened). What has to be kept, per worker:

- Local commutes (`DemandApi`): the subzones (`sz`) and service (`svc`) the result was solved on
  (today `set_network` replaces them; hold them behind `Rc` so the copy is a pointer), and each
  held period's `SubOut` (`od` is stations squared, about 3 MB a period at 875 stations; the rest
  is per subzone or access entry, under 1 MB).
- Everyday (`EverydayApi`): `pairs` (their walk shares), `edge_pair` (one u32 per destination
  sample, rebuilt by every `set_network`; the biggest piece) and each held period's
  `probability` (two f32 per pair, about 7 MB a period at 900k pairs).
- The market (`IntercityApi`), for a selection's longer trips and a station's: each period's
  per-station rows (about 7 MB a period) and the served zone pairs' rail.

About 40-60 MB more per solving worker in New York (300 MB now). The query code takes "the state
for version v" instead of `self`; a query about the shown version reads the copy, one about the
current version the live state. A worker answers between populations, so a click would still
wait for the population pass in hand (up to about a second, typically 0.3-0.6 s). Getting under
200 ms also during a solve would mean splitting the commute mode choice (one ~0.8 s call) into
pieces that yield, or answering from a worker that does not solve (it would need its own
everyday layer and the market: about 250 MB more).

Cheaper partial steps, if the whole is too much: (a) the copy for local commutes only (small:
`sz`, `svc`, `SubOut`), so the commute far end and station catchments answer during a solve while
everyday purposes wait; (b) show the near end and the inspector's own totals at once (already so)
with the far end marked as updating rather than blank.

Other things seen along the way (not changed):

- Every click also asks the longer trips (`icSel`) of every solving worker; it is 2-4 ms a worker
  (35-49 ms on the first click of a session, when the 600 m squares are built).
- The bubble worker's sum queues behind a city bubble rebuild while the bubbles catch up after a
  result (T-156); 10 ms, not worth a second worker.
- Re-solves also come without an edit: opening a nearby zone's pack re-solves the network (T-276),
  so a click soon after panning across a zone line can land in one.
