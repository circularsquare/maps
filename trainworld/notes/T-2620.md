# T-2620: keep long lines as hard as they were after T-2619's upkeep

Agent upkeep, 2026-11-06. **A proposal: nothing here has landed.** Anita's aim, after the first brief: "in
my game with pre upscaled maintenance costs, the transcontinental railroad of us covers like half its fares
... i think current difficulty of making long distance lines profitable is already quite sufficient and we
should try to preserve it." So after T-2619, long lines should be about as (un)profitable as before, no
easier and no harder, with short trips left alone. Her first idea, raising willingness to pay through the
fare weight, is dropped ("if we just change fare/time ratio that would probably bite out of short distance
trains maybe which is okay but a weird side effect").

## Recommendation: open line keeps the old rates

Track more than 5 km from every constructed station pays the upkeep it paid before T-2619 (a route-km of
double track US$20k a game day on the ground, 25k on a viaduct, 35k in cut and cover, 40k bored); track
within 5 km of a station pays T-2619's rates (35k, 50k, 100k, 130k), and stations pay T-2619's (50k, 200k
underground).

- On her North America save every long line comes back to within a few points of its cost recovery before
  T-2619: the 3,091 km CALIFORNIA line covers 46% of its costs (47% before T-2619, 31% with it). Lines under
  100 km keep 99% of T-2619's rise (US$188M of the 190M a day).
- It is a money rule: demand, Anita's ceiling tests and every city's rail share do not move.
- Why not a demand change: the two demand candidates measured below raise long lines' fares by trip length
  and competitor, not by how much upkeep T-2619 added. They overshoot the 400-1,400 km lines (most end more
  profitable than before T-2619) and undershoot the 3,000 km one, which competes with the plane. And
  CLAUDE.md asks for a new demand factor to be weighed hard. The upkeep that hurt the long lines is upkeep
  on open line (75-93% of the 600+ km lines' new upkeep lies more than 5 km from any station, NES's 54%), so the fix sits where the
  cause is.
- Real figures (research/upkeep-france.md): French LGVs cost about US$190k a route-km a year all in, RER A
  and B over US$1M; city track is the dear kind. Open line at US$20k a game day is US$73k a year, under real
  LGVs, as game upkeep has been since T-867 so that fares can cover it.
- What building it takes (sim/src/track): each constructed edge's distance from its midpoint to the nearest
  constructed station, kept when stations are built or removed (a grid of station positions);
  `edge_upkeep_km` picks the rate set by it; the app's `edgeUpkeep` reads a flag from `edges_info`. The
  upkeep hovers gain the open-line rates (strings drafted with the change). Saves need nothing. The lie: a
  long edge counts as open line by its midpoint, its ends near stations included.

5 km is the measured middle: at 2 km long lines come back a little closer (CALIFORNIA 47%), at 10 km a
little less (45%); cities hardly change at any of the three. A variant keeping open-line bored tunnels at
60k (mountain tunnels dearer than open ground) leaves CALIFORNIA at 43%.

## Measurements

Method: each save loaded headless in the upkeep tree (Vite 6511, one demand worker, pinned to one or two
cores), weekday demand solved to its final, then each running line's fares (the game's `incomeByDay`),
running cost (trains an hour, km, cars, as the Lines tab counts it) and upkeep share (the track model's
`line_upkeep`, T-2619's rates), with the old rates worked out in the page through `stretchMoneyOf` under the
old terms (it matches `line_upkeep` to the cent at the new). Recovery is fares over running cost plus
upkeep. Script: `profits.mjs` in the agent's scratch folder (not kept). One finding on the way: a tree made
before 3527e5bd ran with no regional market at all (every market pack refused, "invalid commute between
zones", a console warning only); the figures here are with master's registry and rows, the market on.

- **A** (a long drive tires: a car's door-to-door minutes past the first 2 hours weigh 1.5) and **B** (rail
  is nicer than both: car and plane minutes past 2 hours weigh 1.33, the same as a rail minute weighing
  0.75 of theirs) were tried in a scratch build of the market's choice (`PairRule::alt` in
  sim/src/demand/intercity.rs: only the longer trips' car and plane, never the local layers or the commutes
  and outings between zones; the length damping kept on the plain car). They raise long lines' fare income by
  20-40% at the same fares (Line 2's nearly doubles), more than their riders (+5-25%), as the trips they
  win are long.
- How the market treats the car on long trips today: a drive is 15 minutes plus 1.25 x the distance at
  85 km/h, linear, with no stop for the night (LA-Chicago is 44 hours of driving counted like 44 one-hour
  drives); its money is running cost and parking split by two. So a fatigue rule has a real gap to fill;
  but beyond about 1,000 km the plane is the competitor, which is why A does little for CALIFORNIA.
- **Ceiling tests and cities**: A and B change only the market's longer trips. Anita's ceilings (A at least
  85%, B at least 50%, notes/T-636.md, checks/ceiling/t1056_ceiling.rs) are built from the local layers
  alone and do not read the market, so neither candidate moves them; cities' local rail shares do not move
  either. The rail trips added are longer trips (+0.4% of all rail trips with A); lines under 100 km gain
  0.3% in fares.
- The profit-maximising fare was not re-measured with the market on (a sweep of the high-speed fare
  multiplier ran before the market was found missing; without the market, raising it from 1.5 to 2.5
  raised long lines' fares about 5%).

## Real long-distance rail finances (a few searches, 2026-11-06)

- **Amtrak.** In FY2024 the Northeast Corridor made an adjusted operating profit of about US$268M, while the
  long-distance routes (the overnight trains such as the Southwest Chief and California Zephyr, the game's
  LA-Chicago) lost about US$635M; the company lost about US$705M on that measure, capital grants on top
  ([Trains forum, from Amtrak's monthly reports](https://forum.trains.com/t/n-e-corridor-heartbeat-of-amtrak/328901),
  [Railway Supply](https://www.railway.supply/en/amtrak-faces-subsidy-cuts-as-losses-soar/),
  [WTOP](https://wtop.com/business-finance/2024/12/amtrak-ridership-hits-a-record-high-trims-losses),
  [Eno Center](https://enotrans.org/article/amtrak-reports-smaller-losses-higher-ridership-in-fy-2025/)).
  Outside the Northeast Amtrak runs on freight railroads' track, so those losses are operating costs, not
  track upkeep.
- **SNCF.** TGVs pay SNCF Réseau track access charges of about 35-40% of the ticket (about 15% in Italy, 8%
  in Sweden), raised 7.6% for 2024
  ([Assemblée nationale question](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/16/questions/QANR5L16QE7733),
  [FNAUT](https://fnaut.fr/uploads/2024/07/2024-07-MDV-196-Rail-89-Tarifs.pdf)). The TGV business still
  covered its costs and the charges with a margin of about 12% of revenue in 2013 (29% in 2008, falling as
  the charges rose); the Cour des comptes (2014) found none of six LGVs earning its forecast return (LGV Nord
  3%, Méditerranée 4.1%), fewer than half of TGV services profitable, and advised TGVs for trips of about
  1 h 30 to 3 h ([Banque des Territoires](https://www.banquedesterritoires.fr/grande-vitesse-ferroviaire-la-cour-des-comptes-accelere-le-train-des-critiques),
  [Slate](https://www.slate.fr/story/94047/tgv-cout)). SNCF Voyageurs: revenue €19.2bn, EBITDA €2.5bn in 2023
  ([Railway Journal](https://www.railjournal.com/financial/passenger-numbers-grow-but-net-profits-tumble-for-sncf-in-2023/)).
- **China.** The listed Beijing-Shanghai High-Speed Railway Co. made about ¥11.6bn net profit on ¥40.7bn
  revenue in 2023 ([Futu, CICC's summary](https://news.futunn.com/en/post/41661469/beijing-shanghai-high-speed-rail-601816-1q24-performance-exceeded-expectations),
  [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2023-12-01/beijing-shanghai-high-speed-railway-coltd-posted-87-billion-yuan-net-profit-in-first-three-quarters-of-2023-102141385.html)).
  Commentary reports only about six of sixteen trunk lines profitable, all in the coastal east
  (Beijing-Shanghai, Beijing-Tianjin, Shanghai-Hangzhou, Ningbo-Hangzhou, Guangzhou-Shenzhen), and about
  2,300 of 45,000 km covering costs (secondhand, [Asia Times](https://asiatimes.com/2025/06/chinas-fast-growing-high-speed-railway-network-faces-reality/)).
  China State Railway Group carries about ¥6 trillion of debt (¥5.91 trillion of liabilities in 2021,
  6.1-6.2 trillion reported since), a debt ratio of about 65%
  ([SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3276871/chinas-railway-operator-brings-gravy-train-posting-profits-and-lowering-debt-ratios),
  [Business Standard](https://bsppd.business-standard.com/article/international/china-s-once-profitable-railways-records-900bn-debt-over-push-for-growth-122070800124_1.html)).

In short: a busy corridor of a few hundred km between big cities pays its way (the Northeast Corridor,
Paris-Lyon, Beijing-Shanghai); long and thin routes lose money nearly everywhere. The game today agrees:
on Anita's save, before T-2619, six of the ten 400-1,400 km lines paid and the 3,091 km one covered about
half its costs.
