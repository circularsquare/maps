# T-1283: service edits on the real save took seconds in the clock worker

## Where the time went

Measured natively with `sim/src/bin/edit_bench.rs` (the save's network, loaded as the worker
does, then headway and count edits on line 16, subway F) and temporary timers around the edit path.

Almost all of an edit's `apply` is `TrackWorld::settle_counts`: each round re-solves the whole
service twice (`update_scoped` with every line rescheduled: a 1% probe of every rate, then the
Newton step and `fill`), about 20-30 ms a round on the 109 lines. A normal edit settles in 3-12
rounds. On the real save it ran the full 80 rounds (160 passes) on load and on many edits:

- Line 63, high demand, one train: its round trip jumps between 3049.5 and 3081.3 s as its rate
  passes about 1.1796 trains an hour (a hold at a resource coming and going). No rate fits one
  train exactly, so the Newton step circles with period 5 (shortfall 0.0014, -0.0096, 0.0093,
  0.0050, 0.0027, ...) to the cap of 80 rounds, every round a pass over every line.
- Whether an edit hit it depended on where the circle stood, which is why some edits were fast
  and others 2.7 s (counts) or 5-6 s (headways: several settles per edit). The unit test
  `a_count_edit_at_one_level_leaves_the_others_to_the_bit` had such a circle too (period 2, which
  is why 80 rounds happened to return the same bits).

Inside each pass, ~95% is recomputing profiles (`profile_for`) of lines whose holds moved; within
those, ~33 `hop_time` calls per profile, most from bisecting the crawl speed for holds mid-hop.
`before_edit`, `note_changes`, `check`, derive and the capacity update are each a few ms or less.

## What changed

1. `world.rs settle_counts`: per line and level, the shortfall's turns are counted; a turn counts
   only if it has not shrunk to under half of the last one (a converging zigzag does not count).
   Within `SWING_NEAR` (0.05 trains) of the fleet, after `SWING_TURNS` (3) such turns, the line is
   settled on its positive side (trains to spare, the rate kept) and leaves the solve.
   `TrackWorld::kept` remembers those lines with their rate and round trip bits, so the next settle
   leaves them (and their level, to the bit) alone while nothing about them changed. Recorded
   simplification: the settled rate is one point of the circle rather than wherever round 80 left it.
2. `profile.rs` / `service.rs`: each run keeps its hops with no holds (`profile::Hops`, worked out
   once in `build_run`), and `run_with` copies a cached hop, shifted, for every hop no hold falls
   in. Identical to 1e-15 relative; about 20% off profile time natively. The hold hops (crawl
   bisection) are the rest and were left alone: a different root finder would move figures within
   its 0.1 s tolerance.

T-1171 (unbuilt lines quoted in every state) is not part of this cost: it lies in `buildState`,
outside `apply`, and the real save has no unbuilt lines. Left open.

- hop cache alone: every figure equal to 1e-15 relative.
- after load: train counts unchanged everywhere; line 63's high-demand rate 1.1788 -> 1.1697
  trains an hour (0.8%, the circling line, its round trip 0.11 s shorter, its delay 0.15% less);
  13 other lines on shared track move by less.

- smoke: 12,188,990 rail trips a day on the real save; t1276's check passes (same counts, 32/23/9

## Left for later

- Each settle round still re-profiles every line at the active level twice. Probing only the
  unsettled lines, or reusing the first round's slope, would cut rounds' cost but moves figures
  within the solve's tolerance; not done.
- The crawl bisection in `profile::run_with` (up to 40 full hop evaluations per mid-hop hold).
