# T-3202: two trains on a two-station single-track line pass through each other

State 2026-11-11 (agent meet, from passing's diagnosis and first build): done, option 1 (draw-only),
on by default. Option 2 (a timetable that schedules each meet) is T-3219. No rider or money figure
moves. Rules: docs/ui-map.md "Taking single track in turn" and "Places at a line's end".

## Cause

Nothing in the timetable or the drawing keeps a line's own two directions apart on single track.

- `Service::lattice` puts run 1's departures at `off = (run 0's trip incl. turnaround + split x spare) % headway`.
  With a count line, `headway = round trip / trains`. On a symmetric two-station line with an **even**
  number of trains, the round trip is two equal halves, so `off` is 0: both ends send a train at the
  same moment. (Odd counts give half a headway.)
- The capacity solve charges the single-track stretch's queue (Kingman, two streams) as a hold at the
  stretch's entry, which is at the stop. 2 km, 2 trains: each trip stands 435 s at its first stop
  (dwell plus the hold), runs 93 s, and stands its 180 s turnaround at the far end; trips 708 s apart.
  So both trains stand ~7 min at their stations, then both run at once and meet mid-line: exactly
  Anita's description.
- `spacing.rs` (T-1925) only spaces a line against **other** lines; a count line's own `off` follows
  from the round trip anyway.
- The train guard (T-1965, T-1966) held trains behind the train ahead on their own track and at flat
  crossings; opposite directions on one single track are different track keys, so nothing held them.

## What was built

- Sim: `TrackApi.guard_singles()` (additive WASM): the capacity's two-way single-track sections, each
  as its edge list (`sim/src/track/wasm/service.rs`), carried as `ClockState.singles` and
  `NetworkBuffers.singles`.
- `TrainGuard.holdOnSingles` (`map/trainGuard.ts`): per candidate, the next stretch of its run it has
  not cleared; its time there from leaving the stop before it to coming to rest at the stop after it
  (stops within 300 m of the stretch's ends). First come first served; one on the stretch keeps it;
  one the other way that would overlap stands at its stop until 5 s after the other is in. Same way
  never conflicts. At every zoom, and every train of a line on single track is a candidate once the
  line is in view (the one coming the other way is usually far outside the view; T-1965's 150 px
  margin left it out, so zoomed in on one end nothing was held). `?perfOff=singleWait` or
  `renderer.guard.singles` turns it off.
- Side by side at a station on single track: `network.ts` `singleSplits` raises the train lane
  (`sampleLane`) to 1 along each such platform (a dead end's from the end inward), easing over 60 m
  (`SINGLE_SPLIT_LEAD_M`), so trains there are drawn on their own side as on double track once its
  tracks part (zoom ~16.7 up). At a line's end on single track `terminalEnds` now gives two places
  (`single`), the crossover 60 m past where the sides join, and each arrival takes the place free
  longest, so a train keeps its side through its turnaround and the one it meets is on the other.
  The guard's place hold ignores a train standing for single track (it would wait for the arrival
  it is holding back: a deadlock to LAG_MAX).
- A guard bug found on the way: the next-trip lookup (`nextIndex`, a held train's lateness carried to
  its train's next trip) was keyed by line and departure only. With `off` 0 both ends' trips leave at
  the same second, so the lateness went to the train at the other end, and the late train was drawn
  twice at its end (its arriving trip, still late, and its next trip, on time) for up to ~30 s. Now
  keyed by run too (the other run first, the same run for a loop).

## The 273 s lag (explained, fixed)

passing measured a longest lag of 273 s, longer than the 93 s run. The stretch's end time was found as
"the first stand near the far end", but a run's last stop has no stand phase: the trip's last phase
brakes to rest and stays there through the turnaround. With no stand found it fell back to the trip's
end, so the turnaround (180 s) counted as time on the single track: the waiting train stood 93 + 180
until the other's turnaround was over, 273 s with the 5 s, and stood on top of it at the stop all that
while. `singleSpan` now takes the end of a braking phase that stops the train as a stand. Longest lag
is now 93 s (the other's run), at 60x and 600x.

## Left as they were

- Out of view (and past 150 px), trains of lines with no single track are still not held (T-1965's
  known lie).
- Opposite directions of *different* lines on two-way single track are held on screen now too, but
  `spacing.rs` still spaces them by when they reach the section's entry (at opposite ends), not by
  whether their times on it overlap; where they overlap a lot, the drawn waits are long.
- A held train that departs after a long frame stall jumps forward by the stall (its lag is only
  re-applied while the other is registered); seen once at 60x after a 20 s game-time hitch.
- Flat crossings wait only from zoom 14 (T-1966, by design).
- The track itself is still drawn as one line through a single-track station: T-3212 (notes/T-3212.md).
