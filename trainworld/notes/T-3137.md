# T-3137: double track as two tracks (audit and model)

Agent singletrack, 2026-11-10, stage 1b of the single-track work (after notes/T-3135.md). Anita's words are on the
TASKS.md line: double track has been simplified into one object, she wants it robust, and possibly two objects, but a
player must never delete one half of a double track. Flyover code read in `.tw-trees/flyover2`, stop order in
`.tw-trees/stoporder`, the rest in the main tree.

## How double track is held today

One **edge** is one alignment (the centre line: PIs, radii, levels) with `tracks` 1 or 2 (`EdgeData.tracks`,
`Network::edge_tracks`). Double track's two tracks are never stored: they are "4 m apart, trains keeping right"
(T-319), so each direction of the edge is one directed track. A path entry is `edge << 1 | dir` (`net.rs`
`path_edge`, `path_dir`); for double track, `dir` *is* the track. A **node** is a point with edge ends (`Ports`:
each end's side, 0 against the node heading or 1 along it, and its rank right to left on that side, `derive_node`). A
move is any end on side 0 with any end on side 1. Quad track is two double edges side by side. The app draws the two
tracks 2 m either side of the centre line in the shader.

Running side: the sim has `Network::right_hand`, but every `new TrackApi(..)` in the app passes `true`
(`clock.worker.ts` 801, `realRoute.worker.ts` 541/558), and the app's drawing assumes keep-right with no flag. So
left-hand running exists only in sim tests. Not part of this task, but any model change should keep the one flag
and have the app read it, or drop it.

## Models

### (a) Join side on single ends, inferred way (notes/T-3135.md)

A stored join (left or right track) on each single end at a junction; the run's way inferred from its joins; `dir_ok`
and `directed` read the way; `track_pos` stays; the app draws the shift from the join.
- Simpler: small, touches few files, no save change beyond 4 flag bits.
- Harder: it is a third patch on items 3, 5 and 24, and leaves moves implicit. Whether a move exists is decided by a
  per-edge flag plus `track_pos`'s rank arithmetic, so the next feature that needs "which track" (a crossover within a
  double, a single platform beside a double, a single-lead junction, wrong-line working) patches again.
- Performance and save: no change; 4 bits per edge.
- Flyovers: unchanged, joins added. Crossovers: unchanged.
- Size: about 2.5 agent-days.

### (b) Two objects: each double is two directed single tracks

Every double piece stored as two single-track edges, each with its own alignment (or the shared one plus an offset),
own nodes at junctions, turnouts and diamonds as real track; a grouping record so the player still places, edits,
deletes and prices the pair as one.
- Simpler: everything at nodes becomes plain graph: flyovers are just tracks, crossovers are just tracks, single-lead
  junctions, scissors, wrong-line running and per-track geometry (spreading round an island platform) come free.
- Harder: nearly every system changes. Path entries (lines, saves, service keyframes, boarding, the demand
  snapshot) move to track edges; capacity's sections and junctions are rebuilt on many more nodes (a double Y becomes
  several nodes and internal diamonds, and `find_crossings` must classify shallow-angle crossings 4 m apart at every
  turnout, where today's Bad-crossing rule would misfire); stations span two track nodes; the grouping layer must keep
  every edit whole (her constraint), which is the kind of re-grouping quad already needs (items 13, 29) applied to all
  track. The drawing would either keep drawing from a shared centre line (so the two objects are a fiction to the app)
  or draw each track, doubling strokes.
- Performance and save, NYC (`T-007-real-nyc.save`: 1,647 edges, all double, 8,293 PIs; network 128 KB raw, 75 KB
  gzipped with lines): with a shared alignment plus a group id, about +10 KB raw; with an alignment per track, about +75
  KB raw (+35 KB gzipped, estimated). Route search states stay about the same (each track one direction), but junction
  nodes and moves grow 2-4x, and crossing detection pairs grow 4x for double over double. Runtime is likely fine; the
  cost is the work and the risk.
- Flyovers: become ordinary track (the legs are single tracks off one track). Crossovers: ordinary track.
- Old saves: a load-time split of each double into two tracks and a remap of every line path. Doable, but saves are a
  nicety.
- Size: about 20 agent-days, staged over data model and migration, routing and lines, capacity, stations and turning,
  drawing and editing as one piece, flyovers/crossovers/quad/double-up/beside/rebuild/import, and a long tail of checks.
  High risk of quiet errors in capacity and demand for weeks.

### (c) Tracks inside the piece: lanes, and explicit moves at nodes (recommended)

Keep the edge as the one object the player places, edits, prices and deletes (so one half can never be deleted: there
is nothing to select). Make its tracks explicit inside it, and make nodes work on tracks:
- **Lanes**: every edge has `tracks` lanes, derived, never stored: single track one lane at offset 0 (both ways, one way
  or inferred); double two lanes at ±2 m, each one direction by the running side. One function owns this
  (`Network::lanes(e)`), so no other code derives offsets or running sides again.
- **Lane ends at nodes**: `derive_node` gives each edge end's lanes their lateral position across the node (the end's
  own lateral offset plus the lane's). This replaces `track_pos`'s rank arithmetic (item 5) with real positions in metres.
- **A move table per node**, derived on each edit: which arriving lane end may continue onto which leaving lane end.
  Double to double: the lane running that way, as today. A single end joined to a track (the T-3135 join, stored per
  end) connects to that track only; if its run is two-way it also connects to the other track, which crosses the
  joined one (a single-lead junction, Anita's OK'd terminus case). An unjoined single end (old saves) connects to all,
  as today. `find_path_side`, `junction_crosses`, `build_nodes`' moves, `move_conflicts` and the turning check all read
  the table; crossing pairs come from lane positions, so the badge, capacity and the train guard agree by construction.
- **Path entries stay `(edge, dir)`**: on double track the direction still names the lane, and on single there is one
  lane, so lines, saves, keyframes, boarding, stop order and the demand snapshot do not change.
- **The way** (T-3135's inferred direction) stays: a run's way from its joins, `oneway` the player's override with 3 =
  pinned both ways. It now also decides the extra connection above, rather than being a side effect read by `dir_ok`.
- **The app** gets lanes and joins from the sim and draws every single end at its lane's offset (replacing
  `legShifts`' guess), snaps the pointer to a lane, and draws the ghost per segment from the sim's lanes.

- Simpler: every "which track" question has one answer in one place; the junction rule is real geometry; later
  features become table entries: a crossover inside a double (turning at a station without a terminal could become a
  real move with its own crossing), a single platform beside double track, per-lane passing tracks. Quad could later
  become one four-lane edge, which would retire `twin_at`, the corridor bundling and `orderOverlapping`'s guesswork.
- Harder: routing's inner loop goes through the table (one more lookup per move); `derive_node` grows; a parity check
  is needed to prove an all-double network gives exactly today's paths, resources and badges.
- Performance: the table is small (at most `MAX_PORTS` ends x 2 lanes), built on edit for touched nodes only; route
  search state count unchanged. Save: 4 bits per edge for joins, as (a). NYC: no measurable change expected; the parity
  check measures route search time on it.
- Flyovers: legs join lanes at J and J3, so their drawing and crossing status come from the model; `legShifts` goes.
  If flyovers are reworked later (Anita: "we might wanna change how flyovers work"), they work in lanes from the start.
- Crossovers: the T-338 diagonals (between two edges, quad pairs) stay as edges; a crossover within one double becomes
  possible as a table entry, not built here.
- Old saves: load as today (joins 0 = connect to all); nothing to migrate.
- What it does not do: independent geometry per track (an island platform spreading the tracks, different radii), and
  wrong-line running on double track. Both can grow from it later (lane offsets per edge; a lane bit in the path entry).
- Size, in agent-days:
  - **A** T-3134 ghost lanes (0.25; unchanged from T-3135).
  - **C1** lanes, lane ends, move table; routing, `junction_crosses`, `build_nodes`, `move_conflicts` on it; parity
    check on the NYC save and all tests (2-3, Opus, own worktree: the WASM interface changes).
  - **C2** joins, the way, `set_route_join`, joins in `route_op_as`/`branch_end`/`lay_flyover`, lines rerouted when a
    run's way changes (1.5).
  - **C3** app: lanes and joins in the snapshot, drawing ends at lane offsets (retire `legShifts`), pointer snaps to a
    lane, ghost from lanes, chevrons on the ghost (1.5-2).
  - **C4** the track inspector's switch (the way, 3 = pinned) and docs, wiki, DECISIONS (1).
  - About 7-8 in all, against 2.5 for (a) and about 20 for (b).

### Recommendation

**(c)**. Everything that breaks is at nodes, and (c) fixes nodes properly: tracks become explicit where they meet,
in one place, with real positions, while the piece stays one object, which is exactly her "no deleting one half".
(b) buys per-track geometry and wrong-line running, which nothing asks for yet, at about three times the work and a
long risk tail in capacity and demand; (c) leaves the door to both open. (a) is the quickest and fixes what she saw,
but it is the kind of shortcut she asked us to stop adding.

## T-3134's ghost (stage A)

Independent of the model: the sim reports each preview segment's track count (route, quad's express pair, flyover
legs), and the app draws per segment, in the ghost, the held segments, the real-network snap's local ghost and node
drags. Under (c) the same per-segment lanes come from `Network::lanes`. It can go first and land alone (about 2
hours; the plan is in notes/T-3135.md "Build plan" A), so v0.2.2's other fixes are not held on it.
