# T-1750: what holds Tokyo's real network down (2026-10-28)

Written answer only; no code, constant or save changed. Question (after T-1742): Tokyo's real-network save
(`data/packs/real/tyo.save`) carries 0.4-0.5 of published rail trips and New York's 2.5 times; Anita's guess was
that real New York trains are slow and crowded and Tokyo's fast, so a Tokyo import that drew slow trains would
explain part of it. No per-place rail factor (Anita, 2026-10-28); lean towards Tokyo correct, game easier.

## Answer

**The four things asked about (speed, crowding, service limits, access) together move Tokyo's rail trips by about
5% at most. They cannot be the 2x.** The rest sits in the demand rules (mode choice against the car), which treat
Tokyo like any other city. Measured on a native copy of the demand solve, game's Tokyo service as it stands = 100:

| Cause in Tokyo | What is wrong | Rail trips if fixed | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat crossings between the saves' lines (service limits) | 26 same-level crossings are full at 40 trains an hour; 20 of 133 lines lose over 20% of their trains, 41% of the riders are on them; the holds add 5-13 minutes one way on the central lines | +1.6% (about +0.26M of 16.1M) | **biggest of the four, and the one a player can see** |
| Default peak service below real (calib_tyo.py TIERS) | requested 17-22 trains an hour at the high level; real peaks 24-30 | +1.4% more (peak x1.25, at most 30) | small |
| Crowding: the room limit and the crowding price | the limit turns away 0.28% of line boardings; 92.5% of rider-segments run under 70% of crush | +1.1% (limit 0.1%, price 1.0%) | small |
| Crush load 160 a car | MLIT's 100% is about 143 a car, so 160 is 112%; real peaks are 150-200% | +0.8% at 240 a car | none to speak of |
| Run times | game's trains match real ones on the lines checked (see below); the one real gap is the missing rapid/express services | 15% faster everywhere is +4.5%, so express on the long suburban lines is about +1-3% (estimate, not run) | small |
| Transfers between the saves' line pieces | 2.01 boardings a journey against about 1.4 real | +4.1% if a change cost 0 minutes instead of 2 (upper bound) | small to medium |
| **Not in the four: the car side of mode choice** | commutes: rail 31% of trips, drive 52% in the game; census says rail 52% | park_min 7 to 14: **+13.8%**; w_drive 1.33 to 1.6: **+8.7%** | **the real remainder** |

So the biggest cause is not one of the four. Of the four, the flat crossings are the biggest (and the only one with
an obvious fix). Your guess is right for New York and wrong for Tokyo: New York's game trains are 8-38% faster
than the GTFS timetable (slowing them to it takes 6% off New York's rail trips); Tokyo's are not slow at all.

## How it was measured

- **Game side.** `data/packs/real/<city>.save` loaded into `?new=1&opened=all&paused=1` on Vite 6196, one demand
  worker, waited for the weekday's final demand (227 s Tokyo, 133 s New York), then dumped: line stats (run time per
  stop and demand level, stretch top speeds, dwell, holds), the service the demand solved with (`view.lines[].tph`
  and `cars`), per-period loads, boardings and arrivals (`arrive` is the riders who would board if trains had room,
  `board` what the room limit keeps), the capacity markers. Script `notes/T-1750/dump_real.mjs`
  (`CITY=tyo OUT=<json> node dump_real.mjs`); dumps in `../artifacts/trainworld/t1750/{tyo,nyc}_dump.json`.
- **Effect sizes.** There is no switch for crowding or the room limit in the app, and the save cannot be edited
  here, so the effects come from the native `calib` bin (T-509; built 2026-10-24, so it lacks later demand changes:
  T-1520's logsum and the like). It takes a network JSON, so I made variants of `data/work/realnet/<city>_real_track.json`
  with the game's solved service put in (`make_variants.py`): **G** = tph as solved, run times from the dump with the
  holds in, cars as run; **X** = G with the crossing cuts removed (requested tph, run times from the low level, which
  has no holds); **R** (New York) = G's service with the GTFS medians as run times. `run_calib.ps1`,
  `run_calib4.ps1`, summary `../artifacts/trainworld/t1750/calib_summary.txt`. One thread each, 40-100 s a run.
  The calib total for G is 17.6M rail trips against the game's 16.1M, so sizes are right to the direction and
  roughly to the factor, not to the percent. The Tokyo network is the pre-T-1303 build (stations and census lines
  the same as the save; the geometry is not).
- **What the Tokyo save is.** Not a timetable. `pipeline/calib_tyo.py trips` makes it from the 2015 census's 141
  lines: every train stops at every station of its line, no rapid or express, no Shinkansen; peak trains an hour by
  tier of the busiest segment (24 for 150k riders and up on commuter lines, 18 for metro at 30k, and so on);
  midday and early hours are fractions of it. Run times in the save are the game's own physics, not those of the CSV.
  The game's high demand level runs 06:00-20:00 (params.rs `PERIOD_LEVEL`), so midday runs the peak service.

## 1. Speeds

The game's run time includes its capacity holds (the flat crossings, below); "no holds" is the same line at the
low level, where nothing is held. Real figures: Yamanote, Chuo Rapid's length, MLIT's FY2023 loads are from
Wikipedia and the ITmedia write-up of MLIT's table; **the other real times are from my memory of the operators'
timetables, good to about 10% and not re-fetched** (the pages I tried did not give them: JA Wikipedia's text was
cut, tokyu.co.jp and odakyu pages came back without times).

| Chuo Rapid (Tokyo-Fujino) | 34 | 66.3 | 80 / 76 | about 68 (rapid to Takao 55, then 14 local) | 49 | 160 | 20% | the rapid's stations, but every train stops at all 34 and dwells 30 s; real is slower between stops than game, faster overall |

| Sobu local (Chiba-Mitaka) | 39 | 60.0 | 89 / 76 | about 92 | 41 | 158 | 21% | holds add 13 minutes one way |

What the game does to a Tokyo train: top speed is the train's 160 on the long stretches and the stops are too close
to reach it on most (122-147 on the dense lines); acceleration is 1.0 m/s2 to 60 km/h then 0.5, braking 0.9 (a
plain kinematic model of those bands, `kin.py`, lands 3-15% under the game's low-level time, the rest being stop
margins); curves as drawn limit nothing (no stretch in the save has a curve limit); dwell is the fixed 30 s, 20-28%
of the run time. A cap of 100 km/h on top speed would change a Tokyo line by 0-7%: top speed is not a lever.

Where the game and the real timetable part is the **pattern**, not the physics: the saves have one all-stop pattern
per line, and riders on the long suburban lines in real life take the express (Odakyu, Den-en-toshi, Toyoko, Keio,
Keikyu, Sobu rapid) that is 20-35% quicker. Elasticity from the calib runs: all run times -15% is +4.5% rail trips
(+15% is -4.3%). If express serves a third of the trips and saves a quarter of the time, that is about +2%.

**New York for a fair comparison.** The save carries GTFS medians, so the real time is a real timetable:

| NY line | Stops | km | Game min (high / no holds) | GTFS median min | Game / GTFS | Top speed reached |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 (Lexington express) | 28 | 32.1 | 53 / 48 | 69 | 0.77 | 160 |
| 5 | 36 | 38.5 | 67 / 62 | 87 | 0.77 | 160 |
| 6 (Lexington local) | 32 | 20.3 | 50 / 45 | 50-54 | 1.00 | 100 |
| 7 | 20 | 16.5 | 35 / 31 | 38 | 0.92 | 134 |
| LIRR Ronkonkoma Branch | 13 | 81.1 | 50 / 49 | 81 | 0.62 | 160 |
| Metro-North Hudson | 22 | 52.3 | 54 / 53 | 72 | 0.74 | 160 |

Across the 60 busiest New York lines the game's run time is 0.74 of the GTFS one (summed end to end). Subway
locals match (the 6 at 1.00); expresses and the railroads take 23-38% less time than the timetable. The game has no signal timers, slow zones, trains behind
trains or padding, and a New York express runs 160 where the real one is held to 80-90 mph. A speed cap does not
reproduce it (120 km/h adds 0-1% on the subway, 4-15% on LIRR and Metro-North). The effect: New York with GTFS run
times has **6% fewer rail legs and 9% fewer operator trips** (R against G). So Anita's guess holds for New York, at
that size.

## 2. Capacity and crowding

- **Room limit.** `board` against `arrive` across the day: Tokyo turns away **0.28%** of line boardings (0.09M of
  32.5M), New York **1.55%**. Of Tokyo's rider-segments at the busiest hour, 92.5% run under 70% of crush (where
  the crowding price starts), 6.2% at 70-85%, 1.2% at 85-100%, 0.1% over; New York 78.5 / 13.3 / 5.1 / 3.1%.
  Tokyo's fullest lines against crush (160 a car x trains an hour x cars): Sobu local 98%, Tozai 93%, Keihin-Tohoku
  92%, Oedo 91%, Chuo 90%, Ginza 89%; the Yamanote 79%.
- **Why the loads are low.** Tokyo's busiest section hour in the game is a fifth to a third of the real one: Chuo
  12.7k riders against MLIT's 65k for Chuo Rapid Nakano-Shinjuku (FY2023, 28 trains of 10 cars, capacity about
  40,000 an hour, 158%); Hibiya 18.8k against 45k on Minowa-Iriya. Crowding cannot remove riders that are not
  there.
- **Crush scale.** MLIT's 100% is 40,000 / 28 trains / 10 cars = 143 riders a car, so the game's 160 crush is 112%
  on MLIT's scale (44 seats a car, the price starts at 70% of crush = 112 a car). Real Tokyo peaks run 150-200%,
  Yamanote 125-131% (2023). A rule at 240 a car (150%) puts Tokyo at +0.8%, New York at +6.0%.
- **Crowding off** (limit and price both, `--no-cap --set crowd_alpha=0,left_behind=0`): Tokyo +1.1% (+0.6% more
  on the X variant), New York **+8.3%** (limit alone +3.2%). New York is the city the crowding rules bite on.
- T-560 (crush load per country from stock figures) would be a per-country number; it would do nothing for Tokyo.

## 3. Service limits

- **Trains an hour at the high level, requested against what the game ran** (peak, both numbers per direction):

| Line | Requested | Ran | Real peak | Cars (ran / real) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yamanote | 21.5 | 14.6 | about 24-26 | 11 / 11 |
| Keihin-Tohoku | 16.8 | 6.3 | about 26 | 10 / 10 |
| Chuo | 16.6 | 8.8 | 28 (MLIT FY2023) | 10 / 10 |
| Sobu local | 19.1 | 10.9 | about 24 | 10 / 10 |
| Tozai | 20.8 | 10.9 | about 27 | 10 / 10 |
| Marunouchi | 21.5 | 5.6 | about 26 | 5 / 6 (18 m cars) |
| Ginza | 22.5 | 17.2 | about 30 | 5 / 6 (16 m cars) |
| Hibiya | 22.0 | 14.4 | 27 (MLIT FY2023) | 10 / 8 |
| Oedo | 15.0 | 6.9 | about 20 | 7 / 8 |
| Den-en-toshi | 21.5 | 21.7 | about 27 | 10 / 10 |
| Toyoko | 22.0 | 22.4 | about 24 | 10 / 10 |
| Odakyu | 16.9 | 16.9 | about 28 | 10 / 10 |

  Real peak figures other than Chuo and Hibiya are my recollection of the timetables, +-15%.
- **The 2.5-minute ceiling does not bind.** Of the 43 edges at capacity at the high level, 41 are limited by a
  flat crossing at 40 an hour (one crossing every 90 s) and 2 by a section at 26.7 an hour. Platforms (30 s dwell +
  60 s = 40 an hour) and sections (70 s + v/0.6: 25 an hour at 160, 28 at 130) bind only if the requested rate is
  raised to the real 28-30. Chuo at 28-30 would meet the section rule.
- **What binds is the flat crossings.** All 52 full resources at the high level are kind 5 (flat crossing; 26 sites,
  two directions each) of the 128 crossing markers; New York has 9 sites full. `realnet.py assign_levels` gives each
  line one level out of a palette of -3..-1 and +1..+3 by greedy colouring of the crossing graph; central Tokyo's
  lines cross more than six levels can separate, so some cross flat. Progressive filling cuts every line through a
  full crossing until it fits, and the crossing's wait (about 227 s a train) is baked into the run times.
  Result, high level: 20 of 133 lines are cut by more than 20%, carrying 41% of the line riders; train-km an hour
  requested 45,226, ran 41,008 (-9%). New York: 1 of 111 lines (the 5, 10.1 to 6.9).
- **Size.** Removing the cut and the holds: **+1.6%** rail trips in Tokyo. Raising the peaks as well (x1.25, at
  most 30): +3.0% in all. The demand model is not very sensitive to service at these rates (the wait at 10 against
  21 an hour is 3 minutes against 1.4); what the cut does cost is visible in the game, a Marunouchi at 5.6 trains
  an hour and a Yamanote at 14.6.

## 4. Access, named only

- **Transfers.** 2.01 line boardings a journey (real about 1.4): the saves cut a real line into pieces and branches.
  A change costs `transfer_min` 2 plus the wait. At 0 minutes: Tokyo +4.1%, New York +1.3% (and line boardings
  +17%, which is the same riders counted more).
- **Car against rail.** The weekday's modelled commutes in Tokyo (calib): 31.1% rail, 16.4% walk, 52.5% drive. The
  2020 census (table 17-2, in `calib_tyo.py targets`): rail 51.8% of commuters and students 15+ by home
  municipality. The retired T-509 fit had 43%. The levers sized above are global constants, so they move Tokyo
  2-3 times as much as New York: `park_min` 7 to 14 (the parking base): Tokyo +13.8%, New York +6.9%; `w_drive`
  1.33 to 1.6: Tokyo +8.7%, New York +4.2%. These are demand rules; a change needs the T-636 ceiling tests
  (rail of rail+car A at least 85%, B at least 50%) and Anita's word. I did not run those.
- **Not looked at:** walk to station catchments, the pack's road speeds (`road_city_kmh` 16.5).
- The `--areas` score of the calib bin could not be used: the pack's cell count changed since the file was made
  (64,300 against 61,431).

## Proposed fixes and what each does to New York

| Fix (no per-place factor) | Tokyo | New York | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. No flat crossings in the real saves**: levels per hop rather than per line, or a real-network flag that skips the crossing resource, or the same-level crossings left as flyovers. Applies only to saves built from real networks; a player's own crossings keep their rule | +1.6% trips; central lines get their trains and 5-13 minutes back | at most +1% (9 sites, 1 line cut); not run | small to medium: `realnet.py assign_levels` or `realsave`, rebuild and ship the 7 saves |
| **2. Raise the Tokyo peak tiers** (24 to 28; the high shape so that the hour mean is the peak) | +1.4% | none (GTFS) | small; the section rule caps at about 25-28, so Chuo at 28 meets it |
| **3. Rapid and express patterns for the big Tokyo lines** | about +2% (estimate) | none | large; no timetable source without an ODPT account (calib_tyo.py says so) |
| 4. Leave crush at 160 | none | none | none. A raise to 240 gives New York +6.0% and Tokyo +0.8%: easier everywhere, but it pushes New York further over and does nothing for Tokyo |
| 5. Slower run times worldwide (+15%, e.g. dwell 30 to 45 s) | -4.3% | -3.3% | one constant in params.rs; against "easier game" |
| 6. A top-speed cap | 0-7% on a line | 0-1% subway, 4-15% railroads | not worth it: the New York gap is not top speed |
| **7. Car cost: `park_min` and `w_drive` as global constants (Anita's call)** | **+14% / +9%** | +7% / +4% | one value each, but they are demand rules; run T-636's ceilings first |
| 8. Transfers at 1 minute rather than 2 | +2% | +0.6% | one value; same note |

Fixes 1 and 2 are the ones that hold Tokyo's own network right, and cost New York nothing. Only the car-side
constants can close a gap of this size, and they push New York's overshoot higher in absolute terms (it would go
from 2.5 to about 2.8 with `park_min` 14 and `w_drive` 1.6 together; a guess from multiplying the two effects).
Whether "Tokyo correct" is worth that is Anita's.

## Limits

- calib is the 2026-10-24 build, one thread, the pre-T-1303 network; G's 17.6M against the game's 16.1M. Sizes
  are indicative; the signs and rough factors are not in doubt.
- Real timetable times other than Yamanote (Wikipedia) and the MLIT FY2023 loads (ITmedia's write-up of MLIT's
  table; the PDF `002013572.pdf` is FY2025 and carries only the averages: Tokyo 143%) are from memory. The
  conclusion (game speeds near the real all-stop times) holds at +-10%; a line-by-line fit would need ODPT.
- NY GTFS medians are departure-to-departure, so they include the dwell; the game's `fromStart` does too.
- Crowding "off" sets the price and left-behind waits to zero and drops the room limit; the app has no switch.
- The saves were not edited and `data/packs` was not written.

Scripts and outputs: `notes/T-1750/`; `../artifacts/trainworld/t1750/` (`tyo_dump.json`, `nyc_dump.json`,
`calib_summary.txt`, `calib_progress.log`). The script paths point at my scratchpad folder; adjust to rerun.
