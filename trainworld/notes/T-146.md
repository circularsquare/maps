# T-146: station complexes

Anita wants a station/complex inspector toggle and corresponding aggregated station bubbles. Group platforms when close to touching; define the geometric threshold in implementation notes. T-147 is separately deferred: bottom-left station-info mini-map, per-line traffic and strongest platform-to-platform transfer flows, capped for hubs with many lines.

## Implemented 2026-10-12

**Rule.** A platform is the straight segment of its length through the station node along the
save's track heading (`StationInput.heading`, `length`). Two stations are linked when those
segments come within `PLATFORM_GAP_M` = 50 m, or when their centres are within
`DEMAND_MERGE_M` = 100 m (the demand kernel's `station_merge_m`). A complex is the single-linkage
closure. Levels are ignored. Consecutive stops of one line are not excluded (the demand merge
excludes them; for display, two stops whose platforms nearly touch are honestly one place).

Why 50 m: platform boxes visibly touch at about that distance at zoom 14-15 (minimum box width
~8-9 px, 3.7-7.3 m a pixel at New York's latitude), and real double-track island platforms are
10-15 m wide, so 50 m between centre lines is about a street between platform edges. Why the
100 m clause: without it, two side-by-side stations 60-100 m apart would be one station for
demand (shared catchment, free transfer) but two complexes, and the station inspector would
show two copies of one catchment. With it, every demand station lies inside one complex, which
also makes the complex catchment sum exact (below).

**Derived, not stored.** `buildComplexes` (game/complexes.ts, pure, grid of cells as wide as the
farthest linkable pair) runs on the main thread from the world's stations; `complexes`
(game/complexView.ts) is a computed signal that returns the same object while no station's
id, position, heading or length changed. No save, worker or WASM change.

**Complex view** is `display.complexView` (remembered with the other display settings). The
station inspector shows a Station / Complex `Segmented` switch only when the station's complex
has more than one member. Complex view lists the members (name, line chips, boardings; click
selects the member and stays in complex view), the lines serving any member with their summed
boardings at member stops (all occurrences), and the total. Platform stepper and
Construct/Remove are station-only and hidden in complex view. Settings has "One circle per
station complex" under Station demand circles (same setting).

**Catchment and longer trips for a complex.** `stationRiders(id, top, members)` and
`intercityStation(id, members)` ask the workers for every member and sum the answers, counting
each demand station once: members the kernel merged answer with bit-identical numbers, so the
client fingerprints each answer (FNV over the float bits) and skips repeats. Worker replies are
now taken in period-mask order (`byMask`) rather than arrival order, for both the per-member
fingerprint and the intercity sums; before that, identical answers summed in different orders
and were counted again (first browser run: complex home riders 10,375 against 2,499). Results
carry `scope` (member ids joined by commas); the inspector sections and the map discs match on
it. `selectedScope` (a computed string) keeps the station and intercity effects from re-asking
on unrelated display changes.

**Map circles.** `complexCircles` (map/demandLayers.ts) gives each multi-station complex one
circle on its first constructed member, scale from the summed boardings plus alightings, and a
per-station offset to the mean position of the constructed members. The renderer has a new
`a_halo` instance attribute (vec2, local units) used only in the halo pass; boxes and picking
are unchanged. Blueprint stations neither hold nor place a circle.

## Known limits

- Only the selected station's box was enlarged in complex view; since T-163 every member's is.
- Members of one complex often share a name (the real New York save names every platform alike),
  so the member list repeats names; line chips tell them apart.
- Demand does not use complexes: transfers beyond the 100 m merge still walk (300 m limit), so a
  long chained complex like Penn Station is several demand stations (T-161).
- A curved platform is taken as straight along the node heading.
