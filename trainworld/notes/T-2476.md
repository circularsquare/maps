# T-2476: how long a city's demand takes to show, worst case first (2026-11-05, agent loadwait)

Anita: "i still find myself waiting too long for cities demand to load sometimes :( what is our expected worst case
wait time? when does it happen and how long?"

## The worst case

**Selecting a zone in another cluster than the zone in view while a big save loads waits for the whole load:
Okinawa on the all-of-Japan save (45 zones, 2 workers) showed nothing for 18 minutes (1,095 s), because its small
cluster is solved after Japan's main one and its figures come only with that solve's final.** Panning Okinawa into view
instead showed it in 12.6 s: the zone in view goes first (the load's solve took it as the cluster ahead). On saves the
size of Anita's Tokyo (20 zones) the same kind of wait is 22-37 s, for a zone in the zone in view's own cluster.

## Workers

The pool is `poolSize()` in `workers/demandClient.ts`: 3 solving workers at 12 or more logical cores, 2 at 6-11, 1
below or on a phone, tablet or device of 4 GB or less, and at most 2 when the browser reports no memory (Firefox,
Safari). A typical laptop (8 threads) gets **2**; Anita's machine (16) gets 3. A game load adds the focus pool (up to 2
more workers, none on a small device). So the rows below are 2 workers (the typical player) and 3 (Anita), with the
default focus pool; each page had 5-7 cores claimed in `cores.py` (2 workers: 5, 3 workers: 6-7), and other agents'
jobs ran meanwhile, so the same case wandered 20-40% between runs (Tokyo's final 55-96 s); gaps within one run are the
steady figures.

## How it was measured

Headless Chrome on the tree's Vite, a fresh profile each case (no demand cache: cold). Two paths:

- **reload**: the save imported once into a slot in that profile (its demand cache not written), then the page loaded
  with `?slot=` as the saves panel does, the zone opened (selected in the city view) as soon as the page was up, 4-9 s
  after the navigation. This is the player's own path.
- **in game**: the save imported into a running page, as T-2453 measured, for the cases that start from a loaded game
  (after its final, after an edit, a pan).

"Figures" is the city view's figures with the network's rail in them (a zone first opens with its no-rail figures);
"graph" is the city view's trip-length graph; "settled" the solve's final. Times from the opening. Scripts and log: `artifacts/trainworld/loadwait/` (`scen.mjs`
one case, `batch.mjs` a list of them, `scen.log` every run).

## Warm

A reload of an unchanged game shows its cached result at once (the Vancouver save: figures at the page's start, its
graph 8.9 s later, since the workers answer no question about a cached result before the fresh solve). The Tokyo save
missed the cache in both tries, so its reload paid the whole cold load: `cacheLog.miss` was "zones" (the reload opened
at New York's view, which went in the solve's zones), then, with the view's `#` kept, "none" (no result stored for the
reload's network hash, though the first load wrote one, 1.32 GB by `storedSize`). Either the slot's network changed
between the write and the reload (the settle moving run times, T-1850) or the harness's import-then-reload differs
from play; not followed further here. Worth a look: if a player's reload of a big game misses the cache, every reload
is the cold row above (1-1.5 minutes on Tokyo's save), and a 1.3 GB write per final is a lot of disk.

## What could cut it

Built (small, no settled figure changed, checked to the bit):

1. **The zone in view takes the cluster ahead's rounds when no focus pool shows it** (`selectedRound`, as T-2453 did for
   the selected zone; `?perfOff=viewRound` off). On a phone or 4 GB device (no focus pool), or when the focus pool
   stopped (seen once here, in a run that hit `ERR_NETWORK_CHANGED`), the zone in view showed its no-rail figures until
   its cluster's final. Tokyo save imported, no focus pool: 2 workers 79.4 s -> 43.8 s, 1 worker 96.1 s -> 50.2 s.
   Settled figures `beea2924ff1e1ad8` in all four runs.
2. **The graph comes with round 0's figures** (`?perfOff=lengths0` off). On the player's path the focus pool's round 0
   is shown on its own (no other view yet), so the shown view's round changed with every round, and the graph's
   question, keyed by it, never found the answer asked ahead; the graph came a round late. A question that waits for a
   round is now kept by its solve and round alone, and a focus solve of the selected zone is asked of its round 0 as it
   is sent. Tokyo in view: graph 12.4 s after the figures -> 2.6 s; Seoul: 12-13 s -> 0.1 s; New York: 17-20 s -> 5.5 s.
   Settled figures `45a60840983f51a2` before and after.

Not built, with an estimate each:

3. **A selected zone in another cluster goes ahead of the big cluster during a load** when the focus pool already shows
   the zone in view (T-2459, now with this measurement: Okinawa 18 min -> seconds; the same for any island, or a
   country beside a big one). The hold would have to count the focus pool's final. About a day, with checks.
4. **The focus pool takes the selected zone after the zone in view** (T-2453's item 4): Seoul 25-37 s -> about 15 s
   (the focus pool finishes Tokyo at about 25 s; a second focus zone of Seoul's size is about 10 s on its own). Half a
   day. T-2484.
5. **The small device's rough solve holds the opens for its free-flow round**: with no focus pool the load's whole solve
   was sent at 23-28 s against 7-9 s with one (Tokyo save), and the settled figures came about 15 s later. Letting the
   opens go on while the rough solve runs would cost the rough round itself some seconds. Measure first; small.
6. **After an edit** the cluster's other zones keep their old figures until the whole solve (20-30 s on Tokyo's save, all
   of Japan 19-26 minutes). That is what an edit costs and is shown as "before your last change"; nothing to cut here
   without a new kind of solve.
