# T-4403 Calculate trips on the GPU (scope, Gemini read-only, 2026-11-27)

Brief: artifacts/trainworld/gemini/gpu-scope-brief.md. Shares of solve time are from notes/T-4057.md profiles; the hours are Gemini's estimate.

### Could players' GPUs calculate trips?

#### 1. What the solve does, step by step (and where the time goes)
For each of 5 time-of-day periods (split across 1–5 WebAssembly workers, [demandClient.ts](app/src/workers/demandClient.ts#L246-L287)), the solver runs 1 free-flow pass plus up to 3 crowding rounds ([demandClient.ts](app/src/workers/demandClient.ts#L136-L151), [kernel.rs](sim/src/demand/kernel.rs#L1-L20)):
1. **Zone-to-zone trips & station access (once per city / edit):** Commuter volumes between 2 km zones are pre-solved offline and shipped in each city pack ([gravity.rs](sim/src/demand/kernel/gravity.rs#L6-L28)). On each network edit, cells are matched to their nearest stations/bus stops and grouped into access subzones. *Tiny share of a load (~1–2%, estimated from [perf-survey.md](notes/perf-survey.md#L132-L136)).*
2. **Route search (station-to-station times):** For every destination station, a reverse graph search finds the best mix of lines, transfers, walks, and first-arrival waits ([docs/demand-kernel.md](docs/demand-kernel.md#L115-L332)). *Usually the biggest cost:* **40%** on real NYC, **72%** on a big Tokyo save, and **82%** on bus-heavy Detroit ([notes/T-4057.md](notes/T-4057.md#L27-L85)).
3. **Mode choice (train vs. car vs. walk):** For every subzone pair (commutes), sampled errand/school/airport trips (everyday), and intercity pairs (market), computes whether people walk, drive, or ride rail—and which stations they use. *Second biggest:* **36%** commutes + **11%** everyday + **5%** market on NYC; drops to **19%** on Tokyo and **~3%** on Detroit ([notes/T-4057.md](notes/T-4057.md#L27-L85)).
4. **Loading riders onto lines:** Walks each destination's route strategy backwards to tally segment loads, boardings, alightings, and transfers. *Small:* **~6%** on NYC, **~5%** on Tokyo/Detroit ([notes/T-4057.md](notes/T-4057.md#L33-L79)).
5. **Crowding, left-behind queues & capacity fit:** Turns segment loads into crowding penalties and full-train wait times, averages loads across rounds, and caps final boardings at train crush capacity. *Negligible:* **<0.4%** of solve time ([notes/T-4057.md](notes/T-4057.md#L36)).

#### 2. Which steps suit a GPU?
- **Route search — Poor fit** (`skims_extend` / `DestSearch::run`, [search.rs](sim/src/demand/kernel/search.rs#L756-L795)): Priority-queue graph search with heavy branching (transfer bars, leaf-station shortcuts, platform walks, late-line fixes, fare zones). Running many destinations in parallel on a GPU would need huge per-thread scratch memory and suffers severe thread divergence.
- **Commute & everyday mode choice — Good fit** (`station_exp` [mode.rs](sim/src/demand/kernel/mode.rs#L35-L47), `mode_choice_with` [mode.rs](sim/src/demand/kernel/mode.rs#L327), `EverydayApi::pass` [everyday.rs](sim/src/demand/everyday.rs#L1335)): Millions of independent pairs doing nearly identical float math (already vectorized with 8-wide CPU SIMD in `lanes.rs`). The catch: it only accounts for **~47% on NYC**, **~18% on Tokyo**, and **~3% on Detroit**.
- **Market pass — Mixed/poor fit** (`IntercityApi::pass_window`, [pass.rs](sim/src/demand/intercity/pass.rs#L87)): Streams destination windows to avoid allocating giant station-by-station tables; only ~1–5% of solve time on profiled saves (larger on all-Japan, [perf-survey.md](notes/perf-survey.md#L314-L316)).
- **Loading & crowding — Poor fit** (`assign` / `DestAssign` [assign.rs](sim/src/demand/kernel/assign.rs#L69-L185), `left_behind` & `capacity_fit` [stalls.rs](sim/src/demand/kernel/stalls.rs#L38-L201)): Sequential topological walks and per-line train queues; also already tiny (~6% combined).

#### 3. What using the GPU from the browser would take
- **Separate shader codebase:** Browsers run GPU compute via **WebGPU** using **WGSL** shaders, not Rust. Rust-to-WGSL toolchains are immature, so the mode-choice rules (walk rule, ratio curve, parking, fare bands, ferry fans) would have to be rewritten and kept in sync in WGSL alongside Rust.
- **Data copies every round:** Each crowding round (4 passes × 5 periods), the CPU-built station-to-station cost tables (tens to hundreds of MB on big saves, [notes/T-4057.md](notes/T-4057.md#L83-L84)) would be uploaded to GPU buffers, and station-pair trip totals read back for CPU assignment.
- **Browser/phone support & fallback:** WebGPU works on desktop Chrome/Edge and newer Android Chrome, and recently landed in newer Safari/iOS and Firefox, but older iPhones (e.g. iOS 18 Safari in [load-reports-2026-11-27.md](notes/load-reports-2026-11-27.md#L24)), low-end Android GPUs, and some drivers still lack it or hit driver limits (exact live player share is not measured in the repo). The full Rust/WASM path would have to stay forever as a fallback.

#### 4. Who it would help
- **Not the players currently hurting:** In [load-reports-2026-11-27.md](notes/load-reports-2026-11-27.md#L44-L68), 7–9 of the failed loads are **out-of-memory crashes** on phones and 4 GB laptops running 1 worker, and the 20–50 minute loads are 1-worker big saves ([notes/T-4343.md](notes/T-4343.md#L10-L43)).
- **Shared memory makes OOM worse:** Phones and integrated-GPU laptops share one RAM pool between CPU and GPU; staging extra GPU buffers increases peak memory and risks more crashes.
- **Mainly helps desktops that are mostly fine:** A desktop with dedicated VRAM and a NYC-style rail save could trim ~30–40% off solve time (mode choice only), while big bus-heavy saves like Detroit or Belgrade (~80% route search, [notes/T-4025.md](notes/T-4025.md#L128-L141)) would barely speed up at all.

#### 5. Verdict
**Not worth building.** The slowest part of big solves (40–82%) is a branching graph search that GPUs are bad at, while the players suffering crashes and long waits are memory-starved phones and 4 GB laptops where GPU buffers would hurt more than help. Even the smallest useful version—offloading just commute `mode_choice` to WebGPU/WGSL with a CPU fallback—would take roughly **60–100 hours** of agent work (estimating, plus ongoing maintenance to keep WGSL matching every demand-rule change) for a desktop-only ~20–35% speedup on rail-heavy saves and near-zero gain on bus-heavy or mobile ones.
