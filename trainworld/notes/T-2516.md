## Built (diagwater3, 2026-11-05, on T-2497's uncommitted work in `.tw-trees/diagwater2`)

The rule as built is docs/ui-diagram.md "Water"; the choices are in DECISIONS.md under T-2516.

- **Ground** (`waterReal.ts`): seas labelled into bodies (connected on the ground); rivers followed out of the 3 km
  tiles as their flood reaches new ones (to 12 km from a station; `open` ends past that or out of the masks); free
  ends within 400 m of another river bridged to it (New York's Raritan was two rivers 204 m apart at a bridge), within
  1 km of the sea taken to the sea; each river end at the sea knows its body; `waterWay` finds the shortest way by
  water on the coarse grid. Arcs carry a `river` id.
- **Sea first** (`waterSea.ts`): the drawn sea is rasterised back into regions, each with the body its cells' votes
  found (`grid`), so rivers can be drawn into the right one.
- **Rivers** (`water.ts`): only crossed rivers, each whole (crossed arcs, joins, nearest mouth, wide mouths within
  25 km); one routing pass that may cross lines at a cost (no more gaps, no `MAX_FALSE`, no stub rule); a crossing run
  serving two side-by-side hops; mouths into their body's drawn sea; ways on by water past undrawn seas; channels
  between pieces of one sea; narrow dirty branches and narrow dirty rivers left out; rivers reaching no sea and no open
  end left out. Band 2 -> 2.34 pitches.
- **Colour** (`draw.ts` `WATER_SHARE`, `view.ts`): the map's water 55% of the way from the background, both themes.
- **Checks**: `t2169-water.mjs` now reports, per drawn river, its pieces (arcs touching end to end or meeting one region
  of drawn sea), its ends by kind (sea, way, river, open, free, dry), the drawn sea's regions joined by rivers, and
  each ground sea drawn in how many pieces; two new PASS lines (every river one band; every end at the sea drawn into
  it). A band crossing its own river's hop a second time where it says so counts as `ownTwice`, not a failure.
  `t2169-shots.mjs` colours each river differently in debug pictures and paints the new water colour.

## How published schematic maps run rivers (from memory; no pages fetched)

- **London's Tube map** (Beck, 1933 on, and TfL's today): the Thames is the one geographic feature kept, as a single
  band of constant width, pale blue, at 0/45/90 degrees with rounded corners and far fewer bends than the real river.
  Lines cross it wherever they need to, and the band is placed so stations sit on the right bank more than it follows
  the river's course; small rivers (the Lea, the Wandle) are left out. It runs on to the map's edge both ways.
- **Paris** (RATP's plan): the Seine as one band through the whole map, crossed freely, also run off the edges.
- **New York**: Vignelli's 1972 diagram drew the water as flat areas that did not match the land, and was
  criticised for it; the MTA map and the Kick Map keep the rivers and harbour as wide continuous water, Manhattan an
  island.
- What this took: one band per river, constant width, few bends, crossed by lines wherever the layout needs, always
  continuous and running to the sea or off the map; small rivers left out. Hence the cost-based routing (a smooth band
  crossing a line beats one winding through the gaps) and dropping rivers that stop short.
