# T-869: what the game still computes that a player would hardly notice (2026-10-21)

Anita, 2026-10-21: "ask again about how we handle sparse areas / super small trips... what cases are
we still computing that are probably not that noticeable and could be merged or eliminated without
much impact?" Survey only; nothing in the game changed.

**Method.** Native, on a scratch copy of the sim crate at HEAD 940303b (before T-854's edits), with
counters added in the copy only: the commute pair loop counted by the zone pair's trips, the everyday
pairs by distance, per-period timing by layer, and the existing `prune_trips` setting (the pair loop
already skips zone pairs under it; default 0) and an everyday distance floor made settable. A weekday
as `calib` runs it: the real New York network (`data/work/realnet/nyc_real.json`, 111 lines, 876
stations; the real save's network), commutes and everyday purposes, free flow and three crowding
rounds per period, one core. Base run twice, one run at a time: 51.7 and 54.6 s (so about +-5%).
The market's far-zone rows and corridors from the real save's own network (109 lines, captured by
T-780, `t780nyc`) against today's registry (742 zones, 1,196 places). A countryside Europe network:
a 6-stop line through Romania's `aad` (as T-850). Summaries in `../artifacts/trainworld/t869/`;
scripts were scratch.

**Already merged or dropped** (not repeated here): sparse cells and the countryside zone grid
(T-850b, T-852: approved, owned by another agent); market zone pairs under a trip a day dealt in
chunks (T-780: all of Japan 12.4M pairs to 3.1M); corridors only for pairs with trips (T-789);
everyday slots only where there is a budget (T-395) and samples scaled to trips (T-397).
**Already not computed**: walking-only pairs. Cells with no station within 4 km are in no subzone, so
no rail pair loop sees them; pairs whose two walks pass 80 perceived minutes are skipped before they
are visited; everyday pairs exist only between subzones. Their walk and car shares come from the
gravity once, not per period.

## Where a solve's time goes (New York, native, one core)

| | free flow | crowding rounds | share of the 52 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| commutes, 5 periods | 16.0 s (2.8-3.6 s a period) | most of the 32.0 s | about 90% |
| everyday, 5 periods | 1.4 s (0.26-0.30 s a period) | | about 3% |
| opening, network set-up | 1.5 s, 0.7 s | | 4% |

Rounds by period: 10.6, 7.7, 10.4, 3.3 and 0.002 s (night has no crowded segment, so its rounds
are skipped). In the countryside the solve is nothing: `aad`'s line takes about 1 ms a period per
layer and its rounds under 0.1 ms; its cost is opening (656 ms) and memory, which is T-850/T-852's.

## The list, ranked by gain

### 1. Commute zone pairs with tiny flows (the pair loop)

(a) For each origin subzone, the commute pass visits every destination zone within the walk bound
and every subzone of it: 172 million subzone pairs over a weekday's five periods (free flow), again
in every crowding round. Most carry almost nothing, because the gravity spreads a little to every
zone pair.
(b) 36.8% of the pairs belong to zone pairs under 0.01 trips in the period, together 0.18% of the
trips; 48.9% are under 0.03 (0.54% of trips); 62.9% under 0.1 (1.7%). Measured, solve time (the
cost that matters: load to final and edit to final):

| skip zone pairs under (trips a period) | weekday solve | commute rail | station entries moved / worst | line boardings worst | hop loads worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (today) | 51.7-54.6 s | | | | |
| 0.01 | 41.1 s (-23%) | -0.02% | 0.01% / 0.6% | 1.7% | 3.5% |
| 0.03 | 38.6 s (-27%) | -0.08% | 0.03% / 1.8% | 2.5% | 5.6% |
| 0.1 | 35.8 s (-33%) | -0.28% | 0.12% / 5.3% | 6.4% | 13.2% |

(c) At 0.01 nothing a player could see moves: 0.02% of commute rail riders go, no station's entries
move more than 0.6%. At 0.03 the worst station moves 1.8%. The worst hop at 0.1 (13%) is a
lightly used one.
(d) Simplest: set `prune_trips` (the code is there, `kernel::mode_choice`) at 0.01 or 0.03. Better:
deal the skipped trips in chunks as T-780 does for the market, so each origin zone keeps its trips
with each destination's area and the -0.02% becomes noise around zero instead of a loss. The skip is
per period, so the quiet periods (night: 5% of trips) prune more of their pairs, which suits them.
(e) Risk low at 0.01. The trips skipped are long, thin commutes; they lose rail only (the trip total
is unchanged, they count as car or walk). T-854 removes the car-free second logsum from each pair,
so a pair gets cheaper but the share of pairs skipped, and so the share of time saved, stays about
the same; its rail floor would give skipped pairs at most 2% of 0.18% of trips. In the browser, with
2 workers, the same share should come off New York's 30-35 s to final and off each edit (not
measured in WASM).

### 2. The evening period re-solved though midday has the same trains

(a) Five periods are solved; midday (10-16) and evening (20-24) run the same trains (medium level),
as the two peaks do (high). Evening is 15% of commute trips and 6.6 s of the 52 s (13%).
(b) Measured: the commute rail share by home cell differs between midday and evening by 0.23 points
on average (23.7% against 23.9% overall); between the peaks 0.75 points. What differs is crowding
(midday's rounds ran, so some trains are over seats) and the purpose and direction mix: station
entries' mix differs 12.8% between midday and evening, so the evening cannot be midday scaled; it
would have to be midday's choices applied per purpose and direction to the evening's trips.
(c) A player would see the evening's crowding computed from midday's choices: on the real save its
rail share would move about 0.2 points per home cell.
(d) Keep midday's per-pair choices (or its station-pair tables per purpose and direction) and load
the evening from them, with the evening's own crowding check: re-solve it only when one of its
segments goes over seats. Night (low level, 5% of trips, 5.7% of commute rail, 0.9% of everyday
rail) costs 3.0 s (6%) and is already skipped in rounds; item 1 trims it most.
(e) Risk moderate: per-period storage and a rule for when the shortcut holds. Measure first.

### 3. Stations' far-zone rows in the market (per solving worker)

(a) Each period keeps, for every station, the market's rail riders starting and ending there by far
zone (`st_pat`, one f32 an entry), for the inspector's far-end bubbles.
(b) On the real save's network: 695,130 entries a period, 2.7 MB, about 13.5 MB a worker for five
periods, beside a pattern of the same length. 85-98% of entries hold under 0.0001 riders a period
(midday: 592,399 entries with 4 riders of 2,309); 94% under 0.001 (26 riders, 1.1%). Grows with
stations times served zones.
(c) Dropping entries under 0.001 riders a period keeps about 6% of them; a station's far-zone
bubbles lose about 1% of their riders, spread over hundreds of zones a rider or less each.
(d) Drop entries under a floor after the pass, folding their riders into a per-place remainder so a
station's totals stay exact (the inspector's places stay right; only zone bubbles of a hundredth of
a rider vanish).
(e) Risk low; memory, not time (the market's pass is 0.25-0.66 s a period here).

### 4. Corridors sent for pairs with no rail (T-790, already filed)

(a) Every period's result carries the market's corridors (zone pairs with trips), commutes and the
place-pair mode shares.
(b) Today: 719,400 place pairs, 274,898 with trips, **6 with rail** on the real save. T-789 measured
5.3 + 2.7 + 4 MB a message at 577 zones and 5-13 ms of main thread to decode each, 16-17 messages an
edit; with pairs growing as zones squared, about 1.7 times that now (an estimate from the counts, not
measured).
(c) Nothing a player sees comes from the empty pairs; they are fixed for a given set of zones.
(d) T-790 as filed: send the fixed part once and only the railed pairs per period. Worth raising
from P3 as zones grow (Europe's countries are still joining).
(e) Low.

### 5. Market rows' air lists (part of T-777's download)

(a) `market-rows.json` lists, per zone and per airport its people use, every place that airport
flies to directly. Zones sharing an airport repeat its list.
(b) Today 2.98 MB, 742 rows; 68% of it is these lists (486,000 place entries in 2,173 lists, only
615 distinct). Each solving worker parses it whole: 24 ms in Node, +28 MB of heap. Grows with zones
times places.
(c) Nothing changes for the player.
(d) Store each airport's list once and reference it: 2.98 to 1.68 MB (-44%), the same rows.
(e) Low; a format change in `market_pack.rs` and the reader.

### Not worth doing

- **Very short everyday trips.** Pairs under 500 m are 1.1% of everyday pairs and 1.4% of its trips,
  under 1 km 3.1% and 6.4%; the everyday pass is 3% of a solve, so skipping them saves about 0.03%
  of the time. And they are not rail-free: under 500 m they take 0.4% of everyday rail (a 4% rail
  share there, likely subzone pairs whose mean distance hides spread-out homes and shops), under
  1 km 3.8%, with one station's entries moving 20% when they go. Under 300 m there are none (the
  150 m exclusion and the subzone grain already remove them).
- **Market zone pairs with tiny flows**: done (T-780).
- **The countryside's solve**: about 1 ms a period; its cost is the pack's memory and opening
  (T-850b, T-852).

## What I'd do

1. **T-874** commute zone pairs under 0.01 trips a period dealt in chunks (or skipped) in the pair
   loop: about a fifth off every solve and edit on the real save, rail -0.02%, no station over 0.6%.
   Smallest change, biggest gain.
2. **T-875** the evening from midday's choices, measured first: up to 13% of a solve, about 0.2
   points of rail share per home cell; only if T-874 is not enough.
3. **T-876** far-zone station rows under 0.001 riders folded into a per-place remainder: about
   12 MB a solving worker on the real save, more as zones are served.
4. **T-877** market rows' air lists once per airport: the rows file 3.0 to 1.7 MB, exact.
