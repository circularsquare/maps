# T-3627: car money weighed like fares, a trial (2026-11-17, carmoney)

From T-3622 (notes/T-3622.md). Inside a city a fare dollar weighs 4.4 x (90,000 / GDP per head)^0.5 minutes,
by country (US 4.40, Japan 6.96, Malaysia 11.52, Mexico 11.20, Brazil 12.75, India 25.39), while a dollar of the
car's ownership money ($2.10 a trip plus $0.1365 a km of road, the code's figures) weighs 3 everywhere. Anita,
2026-11-18: "i think car cost should be changed to scale the same way as fares. lets test out how big of a change
that is. dont necessarily want to do it. but want to see."

Nothing landed. The demand change is `notes/T-3627.patch` (sim only); a separate display fix for the
between-cities train hover is `notes/T-3627-display.patch`.

## The options

- **A**: the car's ownership money weighs the fare weight exactly (US 4.40, India 25.39 minutes a dollar).
- **B**: the same scaling anchored on the US: 3 x the fare weight / the US's (US 3.00, UK 3.75, Japan 4.75,
  Malaysia 7.86, Mexico 7.64, Brazil 8.70, India 17.32).
- **C** (Anita's restatement, no demand change): today's numbers read as "a car costs fewer dollars in poorer
  countries, and every dollar in a city weighs the fare weight": the car's prices are today's times 3 / the fare
  weight, so $2.10 a trip and $0.1365 a km become, in US$:

| | fare weight | per trip | per km | a 10 km trip |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| US | 4.40 | $1.43 | $0.093 | $2.36 |
| UK | 5.50 | $1.15 | $0.074 | $1.89 |
| Japan | 6.96 | $0.90 | $0.059 | $1.49 |
| Malaysia | 11.52 | $0.55 | $0.036 | $0.90 |
| Mexico | 11.20 | $0.56 | $0.037 | $0.93 |
| Brazil | 12.75 | $0.49 | $0.032 | $0.82 |
| India | 25.39 | $0.25 | $0.016 | $0.41 |

  Under A every country's car costs $3.47 for that 10 km trip; under B $2.36 (the US's C figure) everywhere.
  So all three are "car money at the fare weight" with different car prices: A at US prices plus 47%, B at US
  prices, C at prices that scale with income to the 0.5, as the default fares and the trains' running costs do.

The ownership minutes a 10 km trip pays: today 10.4 everywhere; A US 15.2, Japan 24.1, Malaysia 39.9, Mexico 38.8,
Brazil 44.2, India 88.0; B US 10.4, Japan 16.4, Malaysia 27.2, Mexico 26.5, Brazil 30.1, India 60.0.

## Which car costs are already per country

- Inside a city: the ownership money ($2.10 a trip, $0.1365 a km) is one figure worldwide. Paid parking (destination
  and home) is in minutes, already times (GDP per head / $35,000)^0.5 below $35,000 (T-2278): parking prices
  follow income and a dollar weighs the fare weight, so parking already works the way C describes. Congestion
  charges (Manhattan, central London) are real dollar prices at 3 minutes a dollar.
- Between cities: the car's running cost a km is per country (countryIncome.json `car`, fuel and tolls: US $0.104,
  Japan $0.232, Malaysia $0.096, Mexico $0.205, Brazil $0.157, India $0.118), split between 2 people, and weighed at
  the travellers' weight (`travelWeight`). Between cities there is no ownership cost.

So scaling ownership by the fare weight (A or B) would stack on nothing else per country inside a city; C says the
ownership money is already priced per country, implicitly.

## Intercity: why 3.55 and 3.58 (fixed, display only)

Kuala Lumpur to Seremban, Singapore dollars shown: the train's hover had "fare $6.56 x 3.55", the car's
"running $4.92 x 3.58". The choice weighs both at the same thing: each income group's riders weigh money at the
country's fare weight times their group's factor (Malaysia's groups average 0.4035, `travelWeight` 4.65 minutes a
US$, 3.58 a S$). The train's hover showed instead the route search's weight, `marketFareWeight`: the curve's weight
times a flat `LONG_TRIP_MONEY` 0.40 (and the fare scales' tenths), 4.62 a US$, 3.55 a S$. The rail cost itself uses
the groups (`fare_d` in intercity/setup.rs), so the hover's fare term was a little off and "choice of routes"
absorbed the difference.

The fix (`notes/T-3627-display.patch`, app/src/ui/inspector/MarketTrip.tsx `trainEquation`): the fare term alone
at the travellers' weight (`travelWeight` of the start's country), as the car and plane rows have it; every other
term as before. The evidence is the code trace above (`fare_d`); the played remainder moves too little to prove
anything. Played on my Vite, Singapore dollars shown:

- Kuala Lumpur to Seremban, before: "Walk 59.1 + wait 6.1 + ride 21.2 + fare $6.56 × 3.55 + choice of routes 63.6
  = 173 min"; after: "... + fare $6.56 × 3.58 + choice of routes 63.5 = 173 min"; car "running $4.92 × 3.58".
- T-3622's cross-border case (Singapore start, Johor Bahru to Singapore), after: "Walk 163.9 + wait 2 + ride 2.4 +
  fare $1.63 × 3.58 + choice of routes 64.5 = 239 min", the fare still the Fare row's $1.63, same 239 min.

Effective times unchanged; no demand change. String: only the weight in the hover changes ("× 3.55" to "× 3.58"
here). Changelog: none (too fiddly for a player).

A separate oddity, not looked into: "choice of routes" is +63 minutes on both trips. The hover says it is "the time
saved by having several ways to go", so it should be zero or negative; here it adds an hour to a single-line trip.

## One minutes-per-money figure for a place: what it takes

1. Between cities, train against car and plane: the fix above (all three at `travelWeight`).
2. The car hover's two ways ("running $4.92 x 3.58" and "Each $1.30 counts as 4.6 minutes"): write the line in the
   shown currency's own unit, rounded from the same number, e.g. "Each S$1 counts as 3.6 minutes" (a round local
   amount where one unit is tiny: "Each ¥100 counts as 3.1 minutes"). Display only. Proposed, not applied.
3. Inside a city, transit against car: C does it as display only. The car's money is shown at the fare weight and
   its dollars at today's prices times 3 / the fare weight (`CAR_MIN_PER_USD` in game/trip.ts, TripInspector.tsx,
   becomes the country's `fareWeight`, and the dollars shown follow). The one place C's restatement doesn't hold:
   the congestion charges are real prices ($9, £18), which would then read about a third lower in New York and
   45% lower in London unless they are also converted at the fare weight (a small demand change, two places).
4. City against intercity: they differ on purpose, the longer trips weighing money at about 0.40 of a local trip's
   (`LONG_TRIP_MONEY`, T-3239 option A). Kuala Lumpur shows 8.86 a S$ inside the city and 3.58 between cities.
   One figure across both would be a demand change; I'd keep the rule and say so in the between-cities hover
   (its line already says "on average over the people making these trips").

## Recommendation

C: keep today's demand, restate the rule as "car costs scale with income to the 0.5, like fares and the trains'
running costs, and every money inside a city weighs the fare weight", and change the city hovers to show it (item 3),
plus items 1 and 2. Display alone gives the consistency she asked for; the ceilings pass today without either
option; and A or B is a large demand change (rail up 20-43% in the poorer cities under A, driving down 20-37
points, mostly to walking rather than the train). Under C every car figure shown in the US drops by about a third
($2.10 a trip shown as $1.43), since the US's fare weight is 4.4, not 3.

The deciding question is what "car" stands for in a poorer city. Real car fuel is nearly flat across countries
($0.70-1.08 per 10 km above; Malaysia's subsidy apart), and C's India car ($0.41) matches a scooter, not a car. If
"car" there is mostly motorbikes and scooters (as in Delhi, Jakarta or Ho Chi Minh City), today's numbers are about
right and C is the answer. If it should be a car, B is the answer: $2.36 per 10 km everywhere, about 3 times a car's
fuel in India, the same multiple the US has today; A is dearer still. Whether a player would find A or B obviously
wrong isn't shown: Mumbai at 17-24% driving is defensible, and Kuala Lumpur at 46-55% looks low for a city where
most people drive, but I have no mode-share source for either here.

## Method

- Trial code (`notes/T-3627.patch`): `Params::car_w`, the car's ownership minutes a dollar, set at pack load from
  the pack country's GDP (`kernel::car_w_of`, where paid parking's income factor is set: api.rs and cross.rs) and
  read by `car_own_min` / `car_own_km_min`; the market's cross-zone commutes and outings (intercity/setup.rs) take
  the home place's weight. `CAR_FARE_RATIO` picks the option (1 A, -1 B, 0 today); natively `TW_CAR_RATIO`
  overrides it (measurement only). Not covered: congestion charges (left at 3 minutes a dollar), the app's
  hovers (`CAR_MIN_PER_USD` still divides by 3, so a trial build's car dollars are wrong). The weight follows the
  pack's country, where the fare weight follows the trip's (the same country nearly everywhere).
- Ceilings: `checks/ceiling/t1056_ceiling.rs` copied to the scratch folder with the fare at `Params::fare_w`
  (set to the pack country's fare weight) and wired into everyday.rs for the run only; 8 packs x 3 options.
- Cities and the real save: three demand wasm builds (today, A, B) from the same tree, swapped into my Vite
  (port 6193), one headless Chrome a city, weekday demand final, `twDemand.days()[0].cities`. Scripts in the
  carmoney scratch folder (`measure.mjs`, `run_ceil.py`, `sum_runs.py`, `market.mjs`, `arith.py`).
- Tests (from trainworld/sim, 2 cores): `cargo test --release` 621 passed; `cargo test --release --features demand
  --lib` 814 passed at today's weight (`TW_CAR_RATIO=0`) and 814 passed with the patch as saved (option A): no
  test pins the car's weight on a country's pack. App typecheck clean with the display patch.
- Advisor called twice (before the plan, before the recommendation). It moved the weight from `set_fare_weight`
  to pack load (the everyday layer caches its parking at construction), made B exactly US-anchored (the leak test),
  added the poorer packs to the ceilings, narrowed the display fix to the fare term and added the cross-border
  replay, and turned the recommendation into the "what does car stand for" question.
