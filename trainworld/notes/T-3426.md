# T-3426: long-distance trips barely depend on feeders

Anita, 2026-11-16: "some people have noted that long distance train use doesn't actually seem to depend on feeders
anymore. i've noticed this too which is kinda concerning, im pretty sure this used to not be the case. why is this?"

## Cause

The regional market (longer trips, commutes and outings between zones) prices a site's way to the stations
it can walk to as **the mean** of their weights (`add_access`, sim/src/demand/intercity/api.rs; docs/regional.md 5.3
"its walks (the mean of their weights at 0.10 a minute)"). Each walkable station gets exp(-0.10 x walk) / n, n being
how many stations the site can walk to (up to 6 within 3 km). Since T-718 (2026-10-19, ab4b4506) the market chooses
station pairs at the station choice's scale and reads a weight w as -ln(w) / 0.10 perceived minutes (`rail_mix_by`,
k = th / beta). So the 1 / n becomes **ln(n) / 0.10 minutes on every station the site walks to**: 6.9 minutes with
two stations, 13.9 with four, 17.9 with six. The station-pair logsum gives a little of that back when the stations are
equally good, nothing when one is clearly best (the inter-city terminal, reached directly).

So a feeder's stop near a zone that already walks to the inter-city station makes rail **dearer** for that zone.
Feeders help only zones they bring into reach, and in a dense city they cost the central zones, which carry most of
the trips. The local layers do not have this: they price every (station, way to it) pair by a logit on total cost
(docs/demand-mode.md "Access and egress": "a second usable station helps a little").

Worked case (the new unit test's numbers, rail from a site to the far end): the inter-city station a 10 minute walk
away and 30 minutes on: 40.0 perceived minutes. Add a metro stop a 5 minute walk away whose way on with the change takes
45: today 46.8 (car at 60 min), 46.1 (200), 44.1 (600); with the fix 39.9, 39.2, 37.2. Three metro stops: today
48.5-53.7, with the fix 34.6-39.8.

## When it changed

T-718 (2026-10-19, ab4b4506, Anita's call: "the regional layer takes T-710's route choice"). Before it, the market
weighed every station pair at the mode choice's scale, where the walk mean was neutral and many station pairs were a
bonus (the "64 near-equal pairs 40-80 minutes cheaper" T-718 fixed). T-718 moved the pairs to the station choice's
scale but kept the walk mean, which turned it into the penalty above. T-846 (2026-10-21) saw the real save's longer
trips by rail fall from 115,665 (10-15) to 4,744 and put it on T-636 and T-718 "by design"; the fix brings 90,959 back,
so most of that fall was this side effect, not the intended part of either change. The mean itself dates from the
market's first access (T-117/T-276) and was kept in T-373 to stop extra options counting many minutes at the trip's
scale; nobody revisited it when T-718 changed the scale.

What did not cause it: T-2641's far dealing (place pairs over 250 km, 2026-11-06) keeps each origin zone's trips on
average and prices each pair on its own stations; T-1506 and T-1511 add slots only for track the feeders are not on.

## Proposal (a demand rule, so Anita's)

Price each of a site's ways to a station on its own at the station choice's scale, as the local layers do: every walk
and ride adds exp(-0.29 x its perceived minutes) to the zone's weight for that station, and the zone's sums go to the
logsums' scale at the end (`add_access`, `at_beta`). No mean, no nesting composite. A second usable station then
helps a little and never hurts. Built and measured in tree `.tw-trees/feeders` (not landed): sim/src/demand/intercity/
api.rs and setup.rs, the T-373 nesting test rewritten and `another_station_never_makes_rail_dearer` added; the
intercity tests pass (52), the full cargo suites not run. Landing it also rewrites docs/regional.md 5.3's "A site's
access at each end is its walks (the mean of their weights...) nested with its zone's rides" sentence and
docs/demand-worldwide.md, and gets a changelog item.

Effect: feeders always help long-distance rail (New York - Philadelphia +22% / +103% instead of -29%); the real save's
longer trips by rail 19x (4.8k to 91k, still under T-851's ~55k real trips crossing the zone's edge plus the inside-zone
part T-846 counted), commute legs between zones +49%, all rail +0.8%. Not yet checked: her two ceiling tests and
other regions' corridors (China, Japan), which would move the same way.

Scripts (scratchpad feeders/): `feeders.mjs` (SET=lp or ny; builds the line, adds and removes feeders, measures),
`realsave.mjs`; raw outputs run2.json, run3-ny.json, run4-ny-fix.json, run5-lp-fix.json, real-old2.json,
real-new.json.

## Are the figures realistic? (round 3, 2026-11-16)

Anita: "ohh boy for feeders thats a hueg change. lets recalibrate. are the demands after we land this at all
realistic?"

Same feeders.mjs (`REAL=1`): each line run near its real service (New York - Philadelphia ordinary trains on the
high-speed track, 4 an hour; Tokyo - Nagoya fast, 12 an hour; Beijing - Tianjin fast, 8 an hour; London - Paris fast,
1.5 an hour; London - Birmingham and Edinburgh - Glasgow ordinary double track, 4 an hour), 3 feeders at the first city
and 2 at the second. Corridor rail a weekday, both ways, commute legs included, with every feeder. "Today" is tree
feeders-old (49814638, no fix), "fix" tree feeders.

| corridor | real, a day | today | fix | fix, access scale 0.15 | corridor's trips, all modes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

| Tokyo - Nagoya | 40-60k (estimate; no published pair figure; the whole Tokaido 504k a day) | 17,407 (0.3-0.4x) | 21,939 (0.4-0.55x) | 19,795 | 105,582 |

Reading:
- The fix brings the two British pairs, where a few feeders cover most of each city's people near the line, from
  about a third of real to 0.7-0.9 of it.
- New York - Philadelphia goes over (3x), and most of it is commute legs between the two places (9,782 of 16,556);
  the corridor's 385k commute legs a day are the market's known oversize there (T-276, T-379), not rail's share.
  Its longer trips alone are 6,774 against Amtrak's ~4.2k.
- Tokyo - Nagoya and Beijing - Tianjin stay well under. Three or four metro lines cover a small part of Tokyo's
  37 million or Beijing's 21 million; the real cities have whole networks feeding the terminals. The real save shows the
  same model with real coverage: longer trips by rail 4.8k today, 91k with the fix.
- London - Paris is capped by the market: 23,984 trips a day by every mode, under the real rail figure alone. The
  long-trip pull (T-3240, off until the world rebuild) is what raises it.

The one existing market-only knob, the scale the access sums are taken at (0.33), moves every corridor the same way:
0.15 takes New York - Philadelphia from 16.6k to 12.7k, and Edinburgh - Glasgow 7.2k to 5.1k, Tokyo - Nagoya 21.9k
to 19.8k, Beijing - Tianjin 23.6k to 17.3k. No single knob closes gaps that run in opposite directions, and the
gaps left come from market size and coverage, not from how much people like the train. Ceiling tests read only the
local layers; the market knob leaves them as they are (A 93.7/93.9%, B 84.1/84.8%).

Recommendation: land the fix with no recalibration. Follow-ups worth filing: New York - Philadelphia commute legs
(the corridor's 385k a day against real cross-border commuting), and checking Japan and China corridors on a save with
real-like metro coverage.
