## For Anita

**What's wrong today.** The stretched Beijing grid is real and I found its cause. When a game has several cities far
apart (a "country map", 600 km or more across), the diagram can draw a whole city **turned** by 10 to 57 degrees. A
grid whose lines run east-west and north-south then gets snapped onto the 45-degree diagonals, so it looks sheared and
stretched along one diagonal. Two Beijing games players shared both have Beijing turned 20 degrees, with Line 1 and
Line 6 running diagonally across the drawing. Games with one city are not affected (turns of 0 to 1 degrees).

Other things that go wrong, less urgent:
- Busy city centres get only a little more room than the suburbs (1.1 to 1.5 times per km), so a dense core is a
  tight knot while long branches run on with nothing on them.
- A game with cities across a whole country is mostly empty land with small cities in it (already T-2724).
- Lines that run straight on the ground step and jog in the drawing, and parallel lines jog differently (T-2529).

**Approaches.**
1. **Each city keeps its own turn** (my pick). Tell the rubber sheet that turning a city costs something, and measure
   the drawing's overall turn from the hops instead of from the far-apart cities. The cities on these maps come out
   turned 0 to 3 degrees, and Beijing reads as a grid again. It costs no extra time. About 2-3 hours to land with the
   checks. I'd switch it on for country maps only, so one-city games stay exactly as they are.
2. **Bigger city centres**: let a station's room grow with how crowded it is, so cores open up and branches shrink.
   This changes the look you tuned (the distortion dial), so it's a taste call. About 3-5 hours to try. Not mocked.
3. **A different way to draw the lines**: route every line on an octilinear grid (Bast, Brosi and Storandt's method,
   the LOOM project). Cleaner straight runs and fewer jogs, but a rewrite of most of the layout, 30-50 hours, and
   slower on big games.

## 1. How the schematic is laid out now

The default path is the rubber sheet (docs/ui-diagram.md "The rubber sheet"; `workers/diagram.worker.ts` `onSheet`):

1. **The graph** (`diagram/graph.ts` `diagramGraph`): complexes, each line's chain, edges with their strokes, each
   hop's track. Places in metres.
2. **The sheet** (`diagram/sheet.ts` `rubberSheet`): a quadtree lattice over the ground. Each triangle's map is pulled
   to the size the field asks (the stations' room, the dial at 0.5), with a cost for uneven stretch by cell kind
   (land near lines 4, other land 2, water 0.1). Solved by multigrid CG in rounds. On a map 600 km or more across, the
   cut gives empty columns and rows a narrow rest shape and the floor falls away from the networks (T-2609, T-2625).
3. **The lines on the sheet** (`diagram/sheetLines.ts` `sheetLines`): layout.ts' octilinear stages (B, the snap, the
   routing, the separation, the order) run as a rebuild from the sheet places, hop targets their sheet lengths. Then
   the carry moves the map with the stations.
4. **Drawing and names** (`draw.ts` `drawing`, `names.ts` `placeNames`).

**Where the stretch comes from.** In `sheet.ts` `assemble`, each triangle's even part is pulled toward its planned
size "at its current turn": `ct, st` are taken from the triangle's own current rotation, so a turn costs nothing. Only
the whole sheet's turn is removed (`unturn`, a Procrustes fit over every station's place). On a country map that fit is
dominated by where the cities sit relative to each other, thousands of km apart. When the cut and the floor's fall
squeeze the land between cities unevenly, the cheapest way for a city's patch to fit is to turn as a whole, and
nothing turns it back. The octilinear stages then snap the turned lines: east-west at +20 degrees becomes the 45
degree diagonal for whole stretches, which reads as a grid stretched along that diagonal. Measured on the sheet (each
city's best-fit rotation from the ground to the sheet places, `rotation` in run.mjs):

## 3. Approaches

**1. Each city keeps its own turn** (prototype: `proto/sheet.ts`, two edits to a copy of sheet.ts):
- In `assemble`, on each triangle, a term pulling its turn (row 3 of `ROWS`, the even part's rotation) to none, with
  weight `rotW` times the cell's share of land near lines (`cellK0`); `rotW` 16. It is one more Gram block, so the
  solver is unchanged.
- In `unturn`, the turn measured from the hops (each hop one vote, the angle from its ground direction to its sheet
  direction) rather than from every station's place, so the cities' own orientation is kept, not the line through
  far-apart cities.
- Both are needed: the term alone (old `unturn`) leaves every city turned the same 12-14 degrees on the Beijing game.
- Results above. Sheet time the same within noise (Beijing game 1.6 -> 1.8 s, all Japan 4.3 -> 4.6 s, Tokyo-heavy
  game 5.1 -> 4.0 s). Room for cores about unchanged (`scales.py`: dense-to-sparse ratios within a few percent, except the densest 12 stations of the Shenzhen game, 1.41 -> 1.65).
- What can go wrong: a squeeze that was a turn becomes an uneven stretch: Bergamo (39 stations, in the Italy game) 1.05 -> 1.44 by
  affine fit, a small Seoul group 1.14 -> 1.26. All Japan changes shape (closer to the ground, but she approved
  today's). One-city maps have nothing to unturn but their details move (New York's core is redrawn differently,
  neither clearly better). So the proposal is country maps only (the sheet's own 600 km switch), one-city games
  unchanged bit for bit.
- To land (stage 2): the option in `SHEET`, both edits behind `far`, t2580-sheet-water, t2160, t2405 hidden and t2289
  on all Japan and the player games, figures in these notes, the rubber sheet paragraph in docs/ui-diagram.md, a
  DECISIONS line. **About 2-3 hours.**

**2. Bigger city centres (core zoom)**: make each station's room grow with its local density (e.g. room times
density^p) in the sheet's field, so a dense core is drawn 2-3 times bigger per km than suburbs, as the Tube and most
metro maps do (the "focus + context" idea in the metro-map literature). Fixes failure 2. Risk: it is the distortion
Anita tuned in T-2556 (dial 0.8, then 0.5, "a little less distorted"); long branches get shorter; country maps' cut
interacts. Cost at run time none. **3-5 hours** to try and mock; her taste call first.

**3. Lines routed on an octilinear grid** (Bast, Brosi and Storandt, "Metro Maps on Octilinear Grid Graphs", EuroVis
2020; the LOOM project's `octi`): place stations on grid nodes and route each hop as a shortest grid path, greedy with
an approximation that runs in a fraction of a second for a metro map in C++. Would replace B, the snap, the routing
and the separation (most of layout.ts) while keeping the sheet, the carry, pills and names. Fixes failures 4 and the
open T-2529, T-2538, T-2293. Risks: a rewrite (LOOM is GPL, so our own code), grid resolution against dense cores, big
country maps (2,000-6,000 stations) likely 5-30 s in JS, every standing diagram check to redo. **30-50 hours**, not
before 1 and 2. Nöllenburg and Wolff's mixed-integer program gives the best maps but takes minutes to hours per map,
so not for the browser; Batik et al.'s shape-guided layout (2023) reports 2 s to 7 minutes before grid routing.

**Rejected: aspect balancing.** Scaling the finished drawing so its box matches the real aspect does not help: the
box aspect is already right (cities 1.0-1.1 of real by affine fit) and the problem is a turn; and scaling one axis
breaks the 45-degree runs.

Others: no transit game with a built-in automatic schematic was found (Subway Builder, NIMBY Rails, Transport Fever;
Cities: Skylines has player mods such as CSLMapView). Earlier references: notes/T-2146.md.

Sources: Bast, Brosi, Storandt 2020 (https://ad-publications.informatik.uni-freiburg.de/EuroVis%20octi-maps.pdf);

## For Anita

**What's wrong today.** The diagram finds each river on the ground as a lot of short pieces, cut wherever a creek,
a dock or an island channel joins it. Each piece is drawn on its own, with round ends, and pieces shorter than about
two river widths are left out. So a long river like the Thames comes out as a row of separate rounded pieces with

   it shows in the busy centre too (the old layout before the rubber sheet did this). It would fix the centre of

3. **Draw small rivers no line crosses** as thin bands too. Today they only show where they're wide enough to come

## How water is drawn now (the sheet path)

With the water button on (docs/ui-diagram.md, "The rubber sheet", "The map" and "Narrow rivers"):
- **Area water**: the ground's water (the zones' 25 m masks, read on a 50 m raster on a city map) is carried through
  the sheet onto a grid over the drawing (`sheetMap`). Each station's pill and 1.5 pitches round it are land. Thin
  water is redrawn as bands (`sheetThin.ts` `thinPass`), and water under 40 square pitches is dropped (`dropSmall`).
  The sea and lakes come from this.
- **Narrow rivers** (`sheetThin.ts` `riverBands`, city maps only): the river finder (`waterReal.ts` `realWater`, 25 m
  cell) traces rivers some hop crosses as **arcs between junctions**. Each arc is carried into the drawing, moved onto
  its crossings, pushed off stations, and drawn as a band **only where the grid's area water is narrower than the
  band** (`covered`), **cut at every station it cannot clear** (line 443: a station within the band's half width and
  a pill ends the piece), cut again where the fitted band crosses a station, and **dropped when shorter than twice
  its width and crossing none of its arc's hops**. Every piece gets round ends.

## Why rivers become strings of blobs

On the real London save (864 stations), the river finder gives 144 arcs, 115 of them on rivers a hop crosses. Only
29 are drawn. Most of the rest are short arcs between junctions on the Thames (2-5 points each) that fail the stub
rule. So each drawn arc is a separate band with round ends, and the arcs between them are missing: a row of rounded
pieces. Where the river widens enough to be area water, the band stops and the area takes over, and that area is
itself cut by the pills' land and the 40 square pitch rule: more rounded pieces. Turning off only the station cuts
and the `covered` handoffs changes almost nothing (tried); joining the arcs into courses is what closes the gaps
(45 courses on crossed rivers, 17 drawn, each long).

In the London centre the joined Thames is drawn, but it runs right under the District and Circle and other lines
that follow the Embankment (they are carried to the same place), so it doesn't show. That needs routing (approach 2).

## The prototype (approach 1)

In `riverBands`, before the per-arc loop: each river's arcs joined into courses (start from the widest arc not yet
used, grow from each end along the widest unused arc of the same river at that node; the course's width the arcs'
mean). Then each course is drawn whole: no cut at stations (it narrows to `minW` and passes under the pill), no
`covered` handoff (inside wide area water the band is the same colour, so it doesn't show). The stub rule is kept.

Figures: London rivers drawn 29 arcs -> 17 courses, map time 1.2-1.5 s either way. What can go wrong: a band passing
under a pill reads as a station in the river, and t2580-sheet-water checks that no station is under a band, so the
landed version should keep a short square gap at such a station (a bridge) instead of a round-ended stop, and only
where it truly cannot clear it; a band through wide area water may show its edge where the area is narrower than the band; the river's meanders
are kept by the fit (the Thames west of the centre wiggles; a fit with a larger tolerance would straighten it).
To land: the water checks (t2580-sheet-water: 0/45/90 degree edges, no stairs, stations on land and under no band,
the Harlem River drawn) on New York and all Japan, plus London, figures here, the "Narrow rivers" paragraph in
docs/ui-diagram.md.

# Approach 2 mocks: bigger city centres (schem2, 2026-11-12)

Anita: "ah yes i think we can try bigger city centers. i do think distortion is a tad low right now." Mocks only,
nothing in app/ changed. Base: the schem tree's sheet.ts (city-turn fix), copied to the scratchpad as
`schem2/proto/sheet.ts`; the try is `schem2/proto/sheetZoom.ts`, run by `schem2/mock.mjs`.

**The change tried.** Each station's ask (`sSt`, the larger of its hop-length and room asks) is multiplied by
(median d4 / its own d4)^p, where d4 is its 4th nearest station's distance, kept within 0.6 to 3 (5 for the stronger
one). So a station in a crowded core asks for more room and a lonely branch stop a little less, and the field smooths it.

# T-3364 An inland network drawn as an island (schem2, 2026-11-12)

Anita, 2026-11-12, on a Nottingham-only game: "it looks lke nottingham is on a round island lol cuz we shrink away all
the land arorund it. i think in this case it would probably be better to just not draw the water. not sure what
criteria would make sense here."

**Why.** Not a far coast pulled in. The sheet map's grid (`sheetMap`) is the box round the carried lattice, and the
lattice ends a margin past the stations (8 km or 8% of the diagonal); its edges bend with the sheet. Every grid cell
outside the carried lattice is `OUT`, and the coast tracing counts `OUT` as water (`wm[c] = D === 0 || D === OUT`). So
every network sits inside a ring of made-up sea along the sheet's edge. On a synthetic Nottingham network (two tram
lines and two rail lines, `schem2/nott.mjs`) 13% of the grid is `OUT`. London, New York and Tokyo get the same ring;
it is just less striking where real sea is nearby (`t3364-london-before-after.png` left: London ringed by sea).

**Rule proposed.** No distance or share criterion needed: draw water only where the ground has water. Past the
sheet's edge, each cell carries the ground on from the nearest cell on the sheet, at that cell's own scale, and asks
the ground's sea there (the same `GroundSea` the worker already has). So Nottingham shows no sea at all, and a coastal
city's sea runs on past the edge as the real coast (New York's Sound and the Atlantic, Tokyo's Pacific, the Thames
estuary and the Channel south of London). Prototype `schem2/outfix2.py` -> `proto/sheetLinesOut2.ts`,
`SEA_OUT=1 PROTO_LINES=proto/sheetLinesOut2.ts node water.mjs nyc,lon,tyo out` and `node nott.mjs out`. Images:
`t3364-nottingham-before-after.png` (open first), `t3364-london-before-after.png`, `t3364-nyc-before-after.png`,
`t3364-tokyo-before-after.png`.

Tried first and dropped: `OUT` taking the nearest cell's wetness (no ground lookup). It ran a lake or a pier at the edge
on as a straight strip to the grid's edge (New York's west, London's south), the T-3362 fault again.

Left for landing: a few one-cell slivers where neighbouring edge cells carry at different scales (a thin line of water
at New York's top, London's east edge), to clean with the thin-piece pass or a smoothed scale; on a country map, past
the far grid's own edge the ground is a guess too, so there count it as land (after T-3362's fix). Pass the sea to
`sheetMap` from the worker, the water checks (t2580-sheet-water on New York and all Japan), a new check that an inland
