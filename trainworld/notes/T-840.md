Found in T-826: `checks/t488-money-days.mjs` timed out waiting for midnight.

## Cause

The hold is right: it ends with the imported game's own final round, the same moment the card
goes. The check's condition `view().final && !client.waiting` was met by the **blank game's**
final result, still shown while the save's first round ran (a solve dispatched clears `waiting`
before anything of it is shown). So the check began timing with the whole solve still ahead of it;
it passed while the real save solved within its 3 + 30 s (about 20 s in T-243) and fails now the
solve takes 40-65 s on this busy machine (T-793's note). Not introduced by T-793: no cluster ahead
applies to this save in view of New York, and the release path is T-347's `holdDone`. No
before-T-793 worktree was needed.

Found on the way: the bar sat at 99% for 49 s. The save's network arrived while the blank game's
load was still in hand (its hold had not ended: New York not yet open bare), so the import joined
that load, whose best share (0.96) was measured against the blank game's tiny solve; the bar never
goes back. A player importing or opening a slot in the first seconds after a new game would have
seen exactly this; a load started later starts a load of its own.

## Fix

- `checks/t488-money-days.mjs` also waits for `!clock.held`.
- `demandClient.submitNetwork`: a whole-game load whose network differs starts the load in hand
  over (`job` fresh, best 0). After it the bar climbs 0.01 to 0.99 over the 50 s and the card goes
  with the hold (run 2: hold and card both at 50.7 s).
