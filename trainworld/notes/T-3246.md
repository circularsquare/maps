# T-3246: income groups for local trips (proposal, 2026-11-21, agent incomes)

## For Anita

**The idea.** Each local trip (commutes, everyday trips, attractions) comes from three groups by income: the
poorest fifth, the middle three fifths and the richest fifth, from the World Bank income shares the longer trips
already use. The middle group weighs money exactly as everyone does today. The poorest fifth counts each dollar
1.76 times as heavily in the US (Japan 1.62, UK 1.52), the richest 0.58 times (0.67, 0.66): income to the power
-0.5, the longer trips' rule, so no new numbers. That factor goes on the fare and on the car's money alike
(running cost, parking, congestion charges). Today the car's money counts 3 minutes a dollar and fares 4.4 in the
US; both keep those weights, times the group's factor. Putting them on one weight, as you asked on 10-26, would be
a separate change that moves every city. I used fifths, not your 20/70/10, because the table comes in fifths.

**What a player would see.** The totals barely move: rail trips -0.4% in New York at the starting fares, +0.4% in
Tokyo. Commuter rail gains about 1% (LIRR +1.1%, NJ Transit +1.3%) and the subway loses 0.6%. Doubling every fare
loses 19.2% of rail trips, against 18.4% today. What changes is who rides. The poorest fifth take the train 8%
more than average and the richest 10% less, since the car's running cost and parking weigh more on the poor than
the fare does (LIRR +20% and -14%). Rail riders would be 22% low income, 60% middle and 18% high. Every
neighbourhood has the same income mix, so that split is the same on every line: safe to show for a city, odd for
a line. Only C changes which line anyone takes, so "some take the express, some the local" (the line's aim in
LINES.md) needs C.

**The options.** Solve time is measured natively, against a run of the same batch, for New York (the real save's
network, starting fares) and Tokyo (the real network, 1,454 stations, the size of the iPhone crash saves). These
are percentages, not phone seconds. Memory is the solve's heap peak.

| Option | New York | Tokyo | Memory (NY / Tokyo) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Each group chooses train, car or walking, its extra fare read from trip length on the fare curve | +25-34% | +26% | none |
| A2. A, with the Money tab's fare estimate from the middle group | +15-24% | +8-10% | none |
| B. A with each station pair's own fare, so the groups also feel line multiples, zones and transfer fares | +27-32% | +27% | +6 / +16 MB |
| C. Each group also its own route choice | about 3x (not run: each stage's time added up) | about 3x | about +90 / +200 MB (table sizes) |

A, A2 and B carry the same riders at one fare curve, within 0.05%; B differs only once a player prices lines
differently. Two groups (poorest fifth and the rest) were no cheaper than three within the runs' spread.

**Your ceiling tests** hardly move: A New York 91.9% -> 91.8%, London 91.5% -> 91.5%, Houston 86.2% -> 86.1%
(85 needed); B 82.9% -> 82.9%, 83.2% -> 83.2%, 72.8% -> 72.8%. Paris and Tokyo not run.

**My pick: A2**, if you want the split shown in the city panel; without showing it, groups change too little to be
worth a tenth to a quarter more solve time. A2 adds nothing to memory, which phones can't spare. What it
simplifies: the poorer and richer fifths feel a line's own fare multiple only as the middle group does, and the
Money tab's estimate while dragging a fare comes from the middle group. C is the only way to the express-or-local
split, and at 3x and +200 MB on Tokyo I'd not build it now.

## Method (2026-11-21)

Prototype in tree `tw/incomes`, behind `Params::t3246` (0 is today's code, bit for bit: the calib outputs match
the untouched build's except timings), not for landing: `sim/src/demand/t3246.rs` and marked blocks in
`kernel/mode.rs`, `everyday.rs`, `kernel/search.rs`, `kernel.rs`, `api.rs`, `bin/calib.rs`. Settings: `t3246` 1 is
A, 2 is B, 3 is A2; `t3246_lo`, `_lo_f`, `_hi`, `_hi_f` the tail groups' shares and factors on today's weight;
`t3246_km` groups only past that length. Each group's train cost is the middle group's plus (factor - 1) x the
trip's expected fare, base included; its car cost plus (factor - 1) x the car's money (running cost, parking paid,
cordon, home parking), and its paid parking times the factor. The station pairs' shares stay the middle group's
(T-1520's RailMix, here in local mode choice). A takes the expected fare from the straight line x 1.15 on the
fare curve; B from a stations x stations table of each pair's expected distance fare under its strategy
(`trip_km_on` over `rn_fare`, plus the curve's bend), read through a second row per origin as `ab_row`. In A and B
the tails' fare sensitivity steps one perceived minute as the middle's does (strictly it should step factor times
that); A2 leaves it to the middle group. Ferry fans choose at the middle group's costs.

Runs: `calib` (`nyc_real.json`, `tyo_real.json`), 3 crowding rounds, 4 periods, one core each, `--set
fare_w=4.4 --curve 0,1.5,20,3.5,1000,248.5` for New York (the game's starting curve, US weight), Japan at 6.0 and
the curve x 0.6. calib has commutes and everyday trips with no market and no attractions, so its rail totals
(11.48M New York) are not the save's in-game figure. Scripts in `notes/T-3246/` (`runs.py`, `effects.py`,
`ceil.py`, `ceil_one.py`, `ceil_read.py`, the ceiling copy `t3246_ceiling.rs`), every run's summary lines in
`notes/T-3246/results.txt`. Machine load moves times 10-20% between batches, so each figure is against an off run
of the same batch; the route search, which the groups don't touch, is the noise check (within 1 s).

sum of every stage per group (search, exp table, mode choice, assignment, both layers): 61.9 of 62.4 s in New
York, 101.2 of 102.0 in Tokyo, so three groups about 3x; not run.

10.29M all high (-10.3%; LIRR -14.1%, subway -9.8%), 11.48M today. Fares doubled (base $3, curve x 2): rail
-18.4% today, -19.2% with groups (two full solves each). 20/70/10 built from the fifths (low 1.98, high 0.66 on a

a city or zone panel is three running sums; by line or station it needs each group's loads (an extra stations x
stations table and assignment per group: about +9% time each from the stage times, +3 MB New York, +8 MB Tokyo).

Ceilings: `checks/ceiling/t1056_ceiling.rs` copied with the groups' money on rail's fare and the car's money,
the walking rule on (the A1056/B1056 rows); today's figures match demand-plain.md's.
