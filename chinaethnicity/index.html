About

Each dot is people counted in China's 2020 census, coloured by nationality (minzu), the officially recognised ethnic group recorded for every person.

Zoomed out, the map draws one circle per administrative unit instead: a county, or a prefecture or a province where counties would overlap. The circle is divided by the nationalities living in that unit and its area is proportional to its population, and it sits at the mean position of the unit's people rather than in the middle of its boundary. Hovering gives the name and the breakdown. Zoom in and the dots come back. The three words at the bottom left set which of the two you see.

are drawn from table 1-4, population by region, sex and nationality, in each province's own 2020 census yearbook, which gives every county. Each table was checked against the national yearbook's copy, and every province matches it in every column.

The census gives how many people of each nationality live in a county, not where in the county they live. Inside a county every group is spread the same way, using the ASPECT population grid (Ju et al. 2025), which places the same census's township counts on 100 m cells. Development zones, which the census counts apart from the districts around them, are placed in the counties of their city where ASPECT finds more people than those counties' own census rows.

2000 county tables from the 2000 Population Census Data Assembly, digitised in the China Census collection on the Harvard Dataverse. Boundaries from DataV GeoAtlas, clipped to areas China administers. Basemap by OpenFreeMap, © OpenStreetMap contributors.
