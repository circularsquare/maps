Religion Dot Map

Every dot is 1,000 people, the same number for every group, so two colours can be compared by counting. Rings mark a body that is present but too small to reach one dot, or that reported congregations without a membership count — a ring says only "here", never how many.

Dots are placed at random within census tracts, not at anyone's address. Tracts hold roughly 4,000 people each, so this places people about where they live without using any population data — but it says nothing about where inside a county a group lives.

The two countries are not measured the same way, and the border shows it. The US figures are membership rolls — the 2020 U.S. Religion Census (ASARB) asks congregations who is on their books, and 48.6% of Americans appear on anyone's. The Canadian figures are self-identification: the 2021 census asks the person, and 53.3% say they are Christian. Those are different quantities, and self-description is always the larger one. Any step you see at the 49th parallel is mostly that, not a change in religion.

Canada also reports "no religion" directly — 12.4 million people, its largest single answer. The US data cannot produce that number at all: its residual means "on no membership roll", which is not the same thing.

Canadian categories below the province level are derived — the census publishes 168 religions by province and 25 by subdivision, never both, so the fine categories are split out proportionally. Where a group is too small to reach one dot it is rolled up to its parent rather than dropped or invented.
