## The detail

**The sources** (`sources.md` §11aq, "Africa swept a second time" in `queue.md`):
- **Burkina Faso:** the 2006 census gives exact counts by **45 provinces** (13 regions above
  them), matching UNSD to the person.
- **Mali:** the 2022 census gives percentages by **20 régions**, with Catholic and Protestant
  split.
- Both have insurgencies that target Christians and animists (the USCIRF Sahel update of October
  2025, cited in ask 017 for Chad).

**Anita asked: "can we not do the mapping to get good sides of both?"** Only partly. The shares in
a unit add to 100%, so drawing the Muslim share at the fine grain also reveals, at that grain,
where the non-Muslims are. A mixed-level build can blur which minority someone belongs to, but
not whether they are a minority. Hiding that means coarsening the whole unit.

**What does give both sides is mixing by place, not by religion:**
- **(a)** Published grain everywhere: 45 provinces, 20 régions.
- **(b) Recommended.** Published grain where it is calm, and the coarser tier inside the regions
  where attacks on Christians and animists are documented. The builder identifies those from USCIRF
  and ACLED and records the list and its date.
- **(c)** Coarser everywhere: Burkina at 13 regions; Mali has no coarser official tier worth using.
- **(d)** Hold both.

In every option, dots inside a unit follow population density, not where members of a religion
live. That is already the case on the whole map.
