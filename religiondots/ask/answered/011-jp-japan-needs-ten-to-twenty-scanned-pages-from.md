## The detail

**The source.** NHK ran two different surveys and the famous one is the wrong one. 日本人の意識
(every five years, 1973–2023) is national and asks about practices, which is what the earlier scout
checked. **全国県民意識調査** is a separate instrument NHK fielded twice, in 1978 and 1996, at 900
targeted respondents in **each of the 47 prefectures**, and it asked *which* religion or sect:
Tendai/Shingon, Pure Land, Zen, Nichiren, Soka Gakkai, other Buddhist, Shinto sects, Christianity,
other, plus "I have no faith". About 600 answered per prefecture, so ≈28,000 nationally, and it
breaks below prefecture into roughly 150 sub-prefectural regions.

**What it would give the map.** Japanese Buddhism split into five schools by self-identification at
47 units. Nothing on this map splits Buddhism that way for any country, and the existing Japan
verdict (§11q) rests on the Agency for Cultural Affairs roll, which reports 175M adherents in a
126M country because shrines return their parish populations. This is the other basis, and on it
31.2% of the country claims a faith and 64.0% says it has none.

**Why it is stuck.** The figures live in データブック全国県民意識調査 1996 (NHK放送文化研究所 編,
日本放送出版協会, 1997.6, ISBN 4-14-009278-5, 87+761pp, ¥25,000 new). Its contents list
III-9「宗教観」 and then 結果表（都道府県別・基本属性別） for every question, which is what the 761
pages are. The National Diet Library has scanned it and both sibling volumes, and all three are
**国立国会図書館内限定公開** — viewable only inside the library building. The catalogue states that
the personal-transmission service excludes people living outside Japan, so the scan is no use from
here.

The only public rendering is a stacked bar chart published as a GIF on 社会実情データ図録 図録7770.
English Wikipedia's 47-row "Organised religious affiliation in Japan by prefecture (1996)" table is
that chart read off by eye and cites nothing else; a third of its cells say `~2%` or `~0`, so it
cannot be used.

**What would unblock it, cheapest first.**

1. **An NDL remote copy of the religion pages.** The one table needed is 47 rows; ten to twenty
   pages covers it with the question wording and the sample sizes. NDL's copying service takes
   requests from overseas but needs a registered user, which means your name and a small fee.
2. **A secondhand copy of the book.** It is held in nearly every prefectural library in Japan and
   the series turns up on 日本の古本屋 (`kosho.or.jp`), the Japanese used-book marketplace. **I could
   not price it** — that site renders its listings in JavaScript and came back blank, and per your
   standing rule I stopped rather than working around it. Shipping a 761-page book from Japan to
   photograph twenty of its pages is the expensive version of option 1, but it also brings the ~150
   sub-prefectural regions and the 1978 wave. There is a cheaper popular edition of the same survey,
   現代の県民気質 (日本放送出版協会, 1997.11), which has the religion chapter written out as prose but
   is unlikely to carry the 47-row table.
3. **Somebody physically in Tokyo or Kyoto.** The NDL scan is free to read on site.

**What you would be buying.** A 1996 vintage, which is older than anything drawn here except
Russia's Arena atlas, and a country where two thirds of people answer that they have no faith, so
only about a third of Japan's population lands in a named category. Against that: 47 units, a real
sample behind each, and the only self-identified Buddhist-school geography that exists anywhere.

**What you are not being asked.** Whether Japan is worth drawing at all, and whether the answer set
maps cleanly onto the taxonomy. Both are mine and I will take them if the pages arrive.

## Access checked 2026-09-14 by the supervisor

Option 1 above says an NDL remote copy "needs your name and a small fee". **That understates it.**

- **Registration needs an ID that shows a current home address.** The accepted list on
  `ndl.go.jp/en/registration/individuals_official.html` is: My Number card, driving licence with
  address, residence card, special permanent resident certificate, student ID with address and
  date of birth, resident registration copy, or a public-authority ID with address. **Passports are
  not on it.** Where the ID lacks an address, a utility bill or addressed mail from the last three
  months may supplement it. The page does not say whether a foreign address or bill qualifies.
- **A registered-user card is required to enter the Tokyo Main Library.** In-person applicants get
  it on the spot at the counter, so a visit stands or falls on the ID.
- **The in-library-only scan can be remote-copied when the pages are known.** The NDL Collaborative
  Reference Database entry on using 館内限定公開 material outside the library
  (`crd.ndl.go.jp`, id 2000021179) names 遠隔複写 for cases where 閲覧したいページ等が明確. That
  needs official registration too, so registration is the one wall on every NDL route. Whether the
  PDF-download variant accepts in-library-only items is unclear from its help page.
- **On site, digitized material prints at the Printout Counter** for 17.60 yen per black-and-white
  page, "with some exceptions" that the fee chart does not list. The copyright limit for a
  monograph is half the main text, so twenty pages is well inside it.
- **The physical book is in 83 university libraries** per CiNii Books, NCID `BA31359333`, including
  Tokyo Metropolitan University, the University of Tokyo Faculty of Law library, and Kyoto
  University's economics library. Each sets its own rules for outside visitors; none were checked.

The pages to ask for are chapter III-9「宗教観」 and the 結果表 for the religion question.
