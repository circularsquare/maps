## 1. Nothing asks religion

- 2003 census form: no religion column; 2003 and 2010 final results print none (§11ao). Since the
  2020 e-census the population is read off registers (Royal Oman Police civil status, Ministry of
  Health vital statistics), which hold no religion.
- Oman has never been in the Arab Barometer (`sources.md` line "Comoros, Djibouti, Somalia, Syria,
  Oman, Emirates: no rows anywhere"). No WVS, ISSP or Pew survey found.

## 3. Compiler figures and the sect record

- **Pew 2020, Oman** (4,522,497): Muslims 81.787%, Christians 8.071%, Hindus 9.531%, other 0.351%,
  unaffiliated 0.140%, Buddhists 0.112%, Jews 0.007%. Not the 85.9 / 6.4 / 5.7 in circulation, which
  is the CIA Factbook's.
- **US State Department 2023** (ecoi.net copy; state.gov 403): 95% Muslim, 45% Sunni, 45% Ibadi, 5%
  Shia, of everyone; Hindus, Buddhists and Christians 5%, "almost all" foreign workers.
- **Sect, all national**: Peterson 2004 (MEJ 58:1, p.32 n.1) about 45% Ibadi, 50% Sunni, under 5%
  Shia and Hindu, "indigenous Omani population"; AEI (Majidyar 2013) three-quarters Ibadi, Shia 5%;
  CIA note 45/45/5 of citizens; Badr al-Abri (2023, uncited, from a TV programme) 77% Sunni, 21%
  Ibadi, 2% Shia; maarefhekmiya.org 100,000-201,000 Twelver Shia "according to varying sources".
- **Sect geography, descriptive only**: Peterson, Southern Oman (MEJ 58:2, p.254): "Dhufaris are
  entirely Sunni", nearly all Shafi'i. Northern Oman: Ibadis in the interior heartland; Sunnis
  elsewhere in the north and the "overwhelming majority in far eastern" Oman; Baluch (Batinah coast,
  Muscat, Matrah) uniformly Sunni; Lawatiyya (Ja'fari Shia) mostly Matrah, some in Saham, Barka,
  al-Masna'a, al-Khabura; Baharina under a dozen families in Muscat; 'Ajam in Muscat and Matrah.
- **Searched, nothing below the nation** (2026-09-15): English and Arabic web searches for sect
  shares by governorate or wilaya; Valeri's *High visibility, low profile* (ResearchGate 403); Review

- **§14**: 15 July 2024, gunmen attacked the Imam Ali (Shia) mosque in Wadi al-Kabir, Muscat, during

## 4. The construction, decided

**Omanis**: all 2,984,793 on `islam`, per wilaya. No Ibadi/Sunni/Shia split: ask 043.

**Expatriates**, per governorate, three parts, each at its own national nationality mix:
- **male workers** (Table 8-4) at the mix of male workers in Tables 17-4 and 18-4;
- **female workers** at the mix of female workers. The sexes differ: Christians are 1.80% of the
  male mix and 33.57% of the female;
- **dependants**, the governorate's expatriates less its expatriate workers (474,353; 172,658 men,
  301,695 women), at the mix of mid-2018 population less 2018 workers by nationality: India 87,725,
  Pakistan 39,503, Bangladesh 32,185, Egypt 18,706, Tanzania 2,753, Sri Lanka 2,138, Philippines
  1,347, Uganda 698, Nepal 691 (other nationalities 49,277, at the named mix). Christians 3.88%.
  The 2018 population is mid-year and the worker series probably not, so rows for shrinking groups
  (Bangladesh, 2018 to 2019) may be high.
- Each wilaya's expatriates take their governorate's composition.

Residual cells, bars set before any religion figure was computed:
- **Other nationalities, women** (59,371): Uganda, Indonesia, Ethiopia and Nepal, the four the 2018
  table named and the 2024 tables do not, at their 2018 female counts (55,832, 94% of the cell).
- **Other nationalities, men** (94,516): the named men's mix. Named men in 2018's four: 12,290; the
  2022 worker series names Nepal and Yemen (32,040, both sexes); neither reaches half the cell.
- **Other Arabs** (government sector, 3,008): Pew's All Middle East-North Africa row.

**Religion**: Pew 2020 through `taxonomy/origin_religion.py`; Muslim branches folded to `islam`;
`other.om` new (4,871). **India's Hindu share 55.38%, not 79.37%**, so the layer's Hindus equal Pew
2020's Oman 9.531% of the register (502,122); the removed Hindus are on `islam`. Same rule and
evidence as Saudi Arabia (Pew, *Faith on the Move*, 2012, pp.21-22). On India's own row the layer
would draw 684,460 Hindus, 1.36x Pew. **Myanmar's 33,059 workers (98.9% women) stay at Pew's
Myanmar row**; nothing found says who they are, and in Saudi Arabia `Burma` meant the Rohingya.

**Witness** (`CHRISTIAN_BAND` 0.2-2.0, written after a scouting sum over the largest nationalities
gave about 0.3): 133,118 Christians, 2.527% of the register against Pew's 8.071%, **0.31**. Pew and
the State Department disagree by more than that: the State Department's non-Muslims (5%) are fewer
than Pew's Christians alone. Not corrected, printed only: Buddhists 60,559 against Pew's 5,905
(10.3x; Myanmar women 29,116, Sri Lankans 20,439), unaffiliated 1,406 against 7,390, Jews 50 against
373.

**Result**: 86.327% Muslim (Pew 81.787%, State Department 95%); 720,302 non-Muslims: Hindu 502,123,
Latin Catholic 69,970, Buddhist 60,559, Protestant 52,312, Sikh 16,477, other.om 4,871, Coptic 4,175,
Ethiopian Orthodox 3,590. Christians 3.76% of Muscat governorate, 1.66% of Musandam. Against one
national mix for everyone, the sex and dependant split moves Christians into Muscat (+2,971) and
Ad Dakhliyah (+1,462) and out of Dhofar (-3,472) and Al Wusta (-854).

**The ruling's test** (Algeria: foreigners on a national mix where the non-Muslims are small or
predictable): not small (13.7% of the country). Placement rests on the register's expatriate count
per wilaya and its worker counts by sex per governorate; the unpublished part is the nationality
mix within each part per governorate. Judged predictable enough at wilaya grain, as Saudi Arabia;
said in `note_public`; no ask.

## 5. Geography and placement

- **Boundaries: geoBoundaries gbOpen OMN ADM2**, 61 wilayat traced from NCSI's *Oman National
  Geographic Structure* (2020); licence "Other - Direct Permission" (WMgeoLab, April 2020), citation
  of geoBoundaries required. Names joined by hand (`UNITS`). Sinaw and Al Jabal al Akhdar (register
  wilayat newer than the layer) merged into Al Mudaybi and Nizwa by their GeoNames seats. **GeoNames
  has two `Sayq`**: the plateau town in Nizwa and a village in Al Kamil wa al Wafi; the seat is
  matched on `Al Jabal al Akhdar` only.
- **COD-AB not used.** `cod-ab-omn` (GADM lineage; 11 ADM1, 49 ADM2) disagrees with the register's
  governorate for 6 of 61 wilayat (Al Kamil wa al Wafi and Jaalan Bani Bu Hasan in North instead of
  South Sharqiyah, Al Jazir in Dhofar, As Sunaynah in Adh Dhahirah, Al Musanaah in North Batinah,
  Dama wa at Taiyin in Muscat), and its governorate areas are off: Ash Sharqiyah South 6,376 km2
  against this layer's 12,033, Al Buraymi 4,810 against 7,463, Dhofar 127,841 against 98,871. The
  register over NCSI's own density map (yearbook p.31) gives about 12,000, 8,100 and 99,900, so the
  wilayat layer is the current geography.
- **Kontur `OM`** (2023-11-01): 4,682,509, 0.889 of the register; 552 hexes outside every wilaya,
  all within 5 km, snapped; none dropped. Rank witness over 61 wilayat +0.837, best of 20,000
  shuffles +0.468. **16.7% of Kontur's people in a different wilaya from the register.** Per
  governorate over the national ratio: Ash Sharqiyah South 0.71 to Dhofar 1.91 and Al Wusta 2.83.
  Desert wilayat read far over their counts (Al Mazyunah 49.8, Muqshin 32.0, Thumrayt 5.6, Hayma
  5.4); Salalah 0.58, Sur 0.61. **Not calibrated**: the dots follow the register per wilaya, so
  Kontur's error moves dots only inside a wilaya (Al Mazyunah's 11,117 go where Kontur's 492,000
  are). No GeoNames seat of 20,000+ lost.
- **No Kontur block at the density cap** (`python kontur_cap.py om`: no stops).
